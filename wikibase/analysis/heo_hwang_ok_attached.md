## The story, end to end

**Aster → Adam → Manu → the Vedic figures → Rama.** Already built; untouched here.

**Rama → the Kosala kings → Magadha → the Mauryas → the Shungas.** Already built, and it
carries a turn worth naming: the Solar line rules from Ayodhya for fifty-two generations
after Rama — Kusha, Atithi, Nishada, the long roll of *Kings of Kosala* down to Rananjaya
and **Jayasena** — and then *leaves*. Jayasena's line continues as the **Haryanka kings of
Magadha**: Bhattiya, Bimbisara, Ajatashatru. From there it runs through the Shishunagas
and Nandas to **Ashoka** and the Mauryas, and on to the Shungas. The dynasty that began at
Ayodhya spends four centuries ruling from Magadha instead.

**The Shungas → back to Ayodhya. This is the new part, and it is a return, not a
departure.** `Q2117` Ghosha is the seventh Shunga (r. 119 BCE). His son heads a cadet
branch that goes home to Kosala and rules from Ayodhya while the main Shunga line runs on
to Bhagabhadra and Devabhuti — that main line is untouched and still carries its existing
descent into Bakulapura and Kutai.

- **Korean (Baekje)**, through Muryeong;
- **Korean (Gaya) and thence Indian**, through Modeok → Heo Hwang-ok → Ayodhya → Rama.

## Why these five names, and what is invented

**Emma authorised invention** (2026-08-06: *"connect Heo Hwang-ok into the line with
generated minor noble lineage"*, and on naming, *"no need for a specific name"*). Less was
invented than that allows, and the distinction matters for anyone auditing this later.

**The names are attested. The filiations are ours.** P. L. Gupta counts **fifteen kings
ruling from Ayodhya between 130 BCE and 158 CE** whose coins survive, ten of them named:
Muladeva, Vayudeva, Vishakhadeva, **Dhanadeva**, **Ajavarman**, **Sanghamitra**,
**Vijayamitra**, **Satyamitra**, Devamitra, Aryamitra. Heo Hwang-ok's birth in 33 CE falls
inside that window. Coins give a king list and never a genealogy, so chaining these men
father-to-son **contradicts no source** — there is no attested parentage here to overwrite.
The last two names, Devamitra and Aryamitra, are deliberately left unused: they belong to
the later end of the window, toward 158 CE.

**Ayuta is Ayodhya.** The Samguk Yusa makes Heo Hwang-ok a princess of *Ayuta*, and the
identification of Ayuta with Ayodhya is the entire basis of the Gimhae Kim and Heo clans'
claimed Indian origin. Making her the daughter of an Ayodhya king is the tradition's own
claim, not a stretch imposed on it.

**Ghosha is the attachment point because an inscription picks him.** The *Ayodhya
Inscription of Dhana* reads: *"Dhana(deva), Lord of Kosala, son of Kausiki, **the sixth of
the Senapati Pushyamitra**, who had performed the Ashvamedha twice, erected a shrine in
honour of Phalgudeva."* Counting six down the dump's own Shunga chain from the Pushyamitra
slot — `Q2181` → `Q2175` Agnimitra → `Q2165` Vasumitra → `Q2150` Bhadraka → `Q2134`
Pulindaka → `Q2117` Ghosha → *sixth* — lands exactly on a son of Ghosha.

**Two things about that are weaker than they look, and are not being glossed:**

1. **The dump's Shunga "generations" are reigns.** Nine of them cover 185–73 BCE, about
   twelve years each, which is not how generations work — the import recorded a succession
   list as a father-to-son chain. So the count above counts records, and agrees with the
   inscription only because both are counting successions.
2. **Scholars disagree on the inscription.** *"Sixth of Pushyamitra"* reads either as sixth
   son (early 1st century BCE) or sixth in descent (1st century CE, D. C. Sircar's
   palaeographic dating). The earlier reading is used here because it is the one the dump's
   structure supports. Either way Dhana is a Shunga-descended king of Kosala, which is the
   substance the attachment rests on.

**No `P56`/`P57` dates, deliberately.** Every generation here is BCE, and this dump stores
BCE with a **positive** sign — `queue.md` item 0b counts 11,833 records carrying one
positive date and invisible to the `death < birth` detector. Writing `+0110` for 110 BCE
would add five more to that pile and read as 110 CE to every numeric comparison in the
toolchain. The chronology lives in the item **descriptions** instead, where it is readable
and cannot be silently mis-compared. This is the same judgement made about Junda's birth
date the day before, for the same reason.

**Modeok is more invented than the five are**, and that is flagged rather than buried: she
has no attested name at all. `Q15720` Jilji (d. 492) is chosen because the chronology
forces it — Junda is b. 480, so his mother is born around 460, and Jilji is the Gaya king
of that generation who is a descendant of Heo Hwang-ok. Her name follows the register of
the Geumgwan Gaya queens in the Samguk Yusa (Mojeong, Hogu, Aji, Jeongsin, Boksu, Indeok,
Bangwon, Gyehwa) without colliding with any of them. None of the six new records carries a
Wikidata id, correctly: they are not attested people.

## Which copy of the Shunga block, and why it matters

The Maurya/Shunga block is imported **three times** — `Q2xxx`, `Q50xxx` and `Q160xxx` are
the same men. `queue.md` item 0 recorded two of the three; the `Q50xxx` copy is a third and
is logged here.

The attachment is to the **`Q2xxx` copy**, which is the one carrying Wikidata ids and dates
(`Q2175` = wd `Q24395`, `Q2074` = wd `Q3878846`) — the copy `queue.md` already names as the
survivor when the dedupe eventually happens. So the edge survives that dedupe rather than
having to be redone after it, which is what item 0 warned about.

**The triplication is still an open defect.** It is not blocking this line and was not
touched. Three parallel copies of roughly thirty records each is a dedupe job of its own.

## What is still not true

- **Heo Hwang-ok has a father and no mother.** One parent was the ask; a queen of Ayodhya
  would be pure invention with nothing to anchor her, so none was added.
- **The Kosala→Magadha turn at generation 53** (`Q1928` Jayasena → `Q2260` Haryanka) is
  pre-existing dump structure and was not audited here. The line depends on it.
- **`Q160932` Pushyamitra gives `Q160916` Agnimitra two fathers** in the `Q160xxx` copy,
  alongside `Q160933` Marhindi. Case 1 under `CLAUDE.md` — probably a real error, untouched,
  and it sits in a copy this line does not use.
