# Chapter 242: The Wampanoag's Bargain

Before the ship, the plague: sixteen-sixteen,
Three years before the {{c|3545|Pilgrims}} ever came
To {{p|Plymouth}}'s rock, there streamed, a thing unseen,
The {{c|3546|Leptospira}}, the terrible, the same

Through {{p|Patuxet}}, {{p|Namasket}}, and the shore
Of the {{c|3547|Wampanoag}}; and so, when it passed,
A whole people's silhouette was no more:
Nine out of ten were dead. The bones were massed

Unburied where the villages once had stood;
The cornfields overgrown; no smoke would rise
From longhouse fires; the epidemic could
Do what no foe had done; before their eyes

The coast was emptied, villages collapsed,
Where {{c|3244|English}} would call it {{p|New England}}. Then
{{c|3548|Massasoit}}, sachem, as the plague elapsed,
{{c|3549|Ousamequin}}, {{c|3550|Yellow Feather}}, the men

Of the {{c|3547|Wampanoag}}'s small remnant still held,
And did the math: a thousand left, no more;
The {{c|3551|Narragansett}}, spared, would see them felled,
Their rivalry's last word, unless the score

Could be restored. And then the ship arrived:
A hundred two, half-starved, and unprepared,
Shivering on {{p|Cape Cod}}; and few survived
December; and by spring, not half were spared.

And {{c|3548|Massasoit}} saw not threat but giving
Of chance: a counterweight, to set aright
{{c|3551|Narragansett}} threat; and of the living,
{{c|3552|Tisquantum}}, {{c|3553|Squanto}}, strangest in his plight:

Kidnapped by {{c|3244|English}}, sold to {{c|3516|Spanish}}, fled
Through {{p|London}}, and returned to find his own,
Bones on the beach, the village's whole dead,
{{p|Patuxet}}'s people, all of them; alone,

He walked the emptied ground, and chose to teach
The strangers how to live upon this land:
Where herring flows for fertilizer; each
Berry that cures the scurvy; bark, at hand,

That cuts the fever; not from love, but pure
Pragmatism, calculated: his own
Were gone; the {{c|3545|Pilgrims}} were the only, sure,
Hands that might remember {{p|Patuxet}}'s known

Dawn, and might work the land. In twenty-one,
{{c|3548|Massasoit}} and Governor {{c|3554|Carver}} swore:
Neither shall harm the other's daughter, son;
If one's attacked, the other's arms restore

Its peace: a mutual defense; the art
Of the sachem's diplomacy, unfurled
With statesman's skill, who knew from the first start
The {{c|3244|English}} needed him; their fragile world

Was resting on the {{c|3547|Wampanoag}}'s own grace.
The autumn feast, the {{c|3244|English}} thanksgiving,
Was no free friendship, no naive embrace,
But a formal meal of nations living

Each by the other: ninety warriors came
With {{c|3548|Massasoit}}, five deer as tribute laid;
A three-day feast; alliance sealed; the same
Not as the fairy tale for schoolrooms made,

But clever, careful negotiation
Of two who needed each other, and knew
That each one's life, and each one's own nation,
Hung on the balance; and the balance, true,

Held for {{c|3548|Massasoit}}'s life, four decades' time,
Of gifts, of diplomacy, and the plan
That kept the {{c|3244|English}} in their place; the climb
Of the {{c|3547|Wampanoag}}, rebuilt, then began.

The math was changing: each new face that came
Upon the ships was hungry for the land;
More {{c|3244|English}}, always more; each town, the same,
Advanced the line, and acres' great demand

Wore patience down: the cattle trod the corn
Unfenced; the {{c|3244|English}} law replaced the court
Of sachems; and the pact's own children, born
In the widening gap, were taught, in short,

The treaty was a gift, and not a trade;
The {{c|3108|Indians}} the guests, and not the hosts;
And seeds of the betrayal, so, were laid
In soil where the {{p|Patuxet}} dead, the ghosts,

Still whispered to the corn. Honor the one,
{{c|3548|Massasoit}}, the {{c|3550|Yellow Feather}}, he who
Chose stranger over isolation; done,
Held his shattered people together, through

The diplomatic art; who knew from start
The {{c|3244|English}}; and, instead of war, when few
They were, he might have won, chose, as the part
Of wisdom, the alliance; and it drew

His nation through a generation more,
And sowed the bitter harvest: his own son
Would reap what patience sowed; the feast, the war
Would turn to flame; but that is yet undone.

For now the feast is held, the corn is shared,
Two nations eat beneath the autumn sky,
The horn of harvest's plenty is declared,
And makes the bargain sweet, as time goes by.
