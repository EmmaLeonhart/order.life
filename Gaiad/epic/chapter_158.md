# Chapter 158: The Mycenaeans

And in the west, upon the {{p|Greek}} land's tip
And on the isles of the {{p|Aegean}} sea,
A third great kingdom rose, of sword and ship:
{{p|Mycenaean Greece}}, as rich as could be.

{{p|Mycenae}} on its hill in {{p|Argolid}},
With walls of stone so great, unmortared, piled,
Called {{p|Cyclopean}} by men; and amid
Its peers it ruled, the strongest and least mild.

And {{p|Tiryns}}, {{p|Pylos}}, {{p|Athens}}, {{p|Thebes}}, they stood,
{{p|Orchomenos}}, and others, kings in each;
And while {{p|Egypt}}'s {{p|New Kingdom}} rose, they could
Grow great, and {{p|Hatti}} too, within their reach.

They were of {{c|1942|R1b}}, the steppe's own kin,
Who came to {{p|Greece}} in days long gone; their tongue
Was early {{p|Greek}}; they wrote it down therein
{{p|Linear B}}, from {{p|Linear A}} sprung,

The script of {{p|Crete}}; and on their tablets kept
Their lists: the sheep, the workers, and the tax,
The rations, and the arrows, where they slept
In armories, the wheat in all its stacks.

The palace kept the count of all its land,
Each worker's share of grain, each bronze-head spear;
The scribes kept all of it beneath their hand,
And so the house ran on from year to year.

But they were warriors above all: their lords
Were buried with their boar's-tusk helms of war,
With golden masks upon their faces, swords
Of bronze, and jewels, in the shaft-graves' store.

They loved the fight, the loot, the honored name,
The gift that bound one war-band to the next;
They were the heroes, those of later fame,
The first {{p|Greek}} lords, by sword and honor vexed.

At first they learned from {{p|Crete}}, the island's style,
The lesser of the two in wealth and lore;
But when {{p|Thera}} had burst, after a while,
They came across and took the {{p|Cretan}} shore:

They took the palaces at {{p|Knossos}}, wrote
Their {{p|Linear B}} on the {{p|Cretan}} clay,
And kept the {{p|Cretan}} ways, the bull, the note
Of goddess and of maze, and kept their sway.

But where the {{p|Minoans}} painted fields and sea,
The {{p|Mycenaeans}} painted war and hunt;
And so the two were joined, the island's free
Rich grace, and the steppe's war-bands, the spear's front.

And they grew rich: their pots went out to all,
{{p|Egypt}}, {{p|Cyprus}}, {{p|Anatolia}}'s shore,
To {{p|Sicily}}, and {{p|Sardinia}}'s wall;
They brought back copper, tin, and ivory's store,

And gold from {{p|Egypt}}, cedar from the hills
Of {{p|Lebanon}}; they sent out oil and wine
And cloth; their {{p|Aegean}}, a lake that fills
With ships, and palaces that taxed each line.

And at the end of this, there came the war,
The war of {{p|Troy}}, the city on the strait,
{{p|Wilusa}}, {{p|Ilios}}, stood before
The {{p|Greeks}}; and all the kings of {{p|Greece}}, the great,

Were led by {{c|1337|Agamemnon}}, the high king
Of {{p|Mycenae}}; for {{c|2114|Paris}}, {{p|Troy}}'s own son,
Had taken {{c|2113|Helen}}; and so they would bring
Their ships across, and fight till it was done.

{{c|2110|Achilles}} fought, and {{c|2111|Hector}} fought, and fell;
{{c|2112|Odysseus}} of wiles was there; and when
Ten years had passed, the city fell as well,
And {{p|Troy}} was burned, and taken by the men.

And then a generation passed, no more,
The {{p|Mycenaean}} palaces were burned:
{{p|Mycenae}}, {{p|Tiryns}}, {{p|Pylos}}, whose clay store
Of tablets baked, and so the lists were learned.

{{p|Sea Peoples}} came; the servants rose; the rain
Failed; and the trade that held the world was done;
In twenty years, the palaces were slain,
And no one built them up again, not one.

And {{p|Greece}} went dark: the script was lost, the trade,
The palaces, the towns; the people dwelt
In villages, and farmed; and what they made
Was songs, the heroes' songs, which still they felt:

{{c|2110|Achilles}} and {{c|2112|Odysseus}}, their tale,
The boar's-tusk helm, the list of each great ship,
The names, the places: so the songs prevail,
From mouth to mouth, and never lose their grip.

{{c|1991|Dionysus}}, the god of wine, went east,
And led his army to {{p|India}}'s land,
And set his rite there, and his dance and feast,
And then came home to {{p|Greece}}; and so the hand

Of {{p|Greece}} reached out to {{p|India}}; the two
Were joined in song, the west, the east, as one;
For {{p|Greece}} spoke with {{p|Hatti}}, with {{p|Egypt}} too,
And {{p|Syria}}, the lands beyond the sun.

Honor the {{p|Mycenaeans}}, kings of war,
{{c|1337|Agamemnon}}'s realm, the foe of {{p|Troy}}'s wall,
Who took {{p|Crete}}, and who sailed to each far shore,
Whose songs are sung of heroes, after all.
