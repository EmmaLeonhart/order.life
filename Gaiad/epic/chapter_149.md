# Chapter 149: Yamnaya — The Steppe Breaks Europe

West of the steppe there lay a farming land,
The {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}'s domain,
{{p|Romania}}, {{p|Moldova}}, and the grand
Black earth of {{p|Ukraine}}'s wide and fertile plain.

Seventeen hundred years their towns had stood,
The greatest of the {{p|Neolithic}} west,
{{p|Talianki}}, {{p|Maidanetske}}, of wood
And clay, with fifteen thousand souls, the best.

And every sixty years or eighty, they
Would burn their towns, as was their sacred rite,
And build anew upon fresh ground; and day
By day, their walls in painted spirals bright.

Their pottery was finest of them all,
Thin-walled, in red and white and black, with lines;
They knew the mother goddess, and no wall
Was built about their towns, nor war's designs.

In thirty-five hundred B.C.E. rode
The {{p|Yamnaya}} out of the east, the sons
Of {{c|1884|R}}, with bronze axe, horse, and wagon's load,
And came upon the towns of the old ones.

The sons of {{c|1884|R}} took wives from the old land,
The daughters of the goddess and the clay;
And so the towns were built no more; the hand
That painted spirals turned another way.

The corded ware, the battle-axe of stone,
The single grave, the rider's bones laid down;
The house of {{c|1884|R}} now held the land alone,
And of the two, one people and one crown.

The mother of the hearth, the goddess, wed
The father of the sky, and in his hall
She kept her rite; the spiral and the thread
Were woven with the wheel, one house for all.

Their tongue became the tongue of all the land,
And theirs the speech that {{p|Europe}} speaks today:
{{p|Celtic}}, {{p|Germanic}}, the {{p|Italic}}, and
The {{p|Slavic}} and the {{p|Baltic}}, all one way.

The {{p|Corded Ware}} went north; the {{p|Beaker}} west,
{{p|Iberia}}, to {{p|Britain}}, {{p|Ireland}}'s shore;
By twenty-five hundred, all of the rest
Of {{p|Europe}}, to the far {{p|Atlantic}} shore.

And others of the riders went to south
And east, to {{p|Anatolia}}, {{p|Iran}},
And on to {{p|India}}; from mouth to mouth
Their speech went with them, all the way they ran.

{{c|1928|R1a}}, {{c|1942|R1b}}, the fathers' line,
Are now the most of {{p|Europe}}'s men, and found
In {{p|India}} and in {{p|Iran}}; the sign
Of billions, {{p|Yamnaya}}'s, the whole world round.

And every {{p|European}} bears the two:
The mothers of the painted towns, the clay,
The goddess; and the riders, fathers, who
Came from the steppe; and both are there today.

So honor both: the towns that burned and rose,
The spiral and the goddess and the pot;
And {{p|Yamnaya}}, the horse, the wheel, and those
Who rode, whose speech the world has not forgot.
