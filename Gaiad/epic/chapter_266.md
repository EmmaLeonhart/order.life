# Chapter 266: The One Who Waited

They tell it in {{p|Japan}} with a cuckoo
That will not sing. {{c|3847|Nobunaga}}: "Kill it."
{{c|3858|Hideyoshi}}: "I will make it sing." And through,
{{c|3859|Ieyasu}} said: "Then I will wait." The fit

Of the tale is the century's: one burned
And broke the warrior monks, the old, the walls;
One unified and measured; and one learned
Both: {{c|3264|Tokugawa Ieyasu}}; the halls

Of power went to the patient, who'd been
A hostage as a boy, traded; he knew,
Lost wife and first son, obeying, his kin
By his lord's doubt; and victory's price, too.

The {{c|3846|Monkey}} died; five regents were to hold
Young {{c|3860|Hideyori}} on his father's throne;
{{c|3859|Ieyasu}}, greatest, waited, calm and cold;
In sixteen hundred, in October, stone

{{p|Sekigahara}}, in the fog; there met
Eighty thousand east, eighty thousand west;
At eight the fog lifted; they fought; and yet
At noon lord {{c|3861|Kobayakawa}}, who'd professed

The west, but promised east in secret, sat
Still, waiting too, till guns shot up the hill
At him to make him choose; he came down; that
Broke the west's flank; by afternoon, the will

Of the west broken, fled into the hills;
{{c|3862|Ishida Mitsunari}}, at their head,
Was caught, paraded; and a blade that kills
In {{p|Kyoto}} by the river: he was dead.

Named shogun, sixteen-three; two years, and then
He passed it to his son, and kept the sway;
Last war: {{p|Osaka}}, fourteen, fifteen; when
Burned castle, {{c|3860|Hideyori}}, mother: they

Died, and the {{c|3846|Monkey}}'s house was done. And peace:
No great war in {{p|Japan}}: the {{c|3863|Edo}} rest;
The lords spent each alternate year, the lease,
In {{p|Edo}}, wives and heirs as hostage pressed;

The roads filled with processions; and the slow
Growth of a capital; and, still, alive,
The stillness: theatre, woodblock, the glow
Of pleasure quarters, haiku; merchants thrive.

This day the faith is honored as a frame,
A pillar of the house; and so it grew:
{{c|3859|Ieyasu}}, dead, by his own will became
A god; at {{p|Nikkō}}, in the mountains, too,

As {{c|3864|Tōshō Daigongen}}, his spirit lay,
{{c|3865|Great Gongen Illuminating the East}};
His grandson, {{c|3866|Iemitsu}}, in that same way
Of gold, of lacquer, carving, built the feast

Of three monkeys: see, hear, and say no ill;
The dynasty enshrined its founder; one
Pillar, the shrine and state. And then the will
Of clerks, a god in mountains, and, begun,

The closed door kept the peace. Hold, on the day,
The {{x|Fourth Day of Awe}}, both: the peace was real;
Children grew old without a war; the way
Of poets, free to speak the moon; the zeal

Of warring states was cooled; the cost, the cage:
Each man in his class, each faith in its book;
The sea closed. {{c|3867|William Adams}}, the same page
Of sixteen hundred, washed to {{p|Kyūshū}}; took

By {{c|3859|Ieyasu}}, kept, made a samurai,
The world through one window; none left; and long
The patient man had waited; and, on high,
The bird sang, beautiful, the same, a song.

Grieve, and give thanks: the pillar held, and stood,
And kept the peace; and kept the door shut tight;
Long, beautiful, the same; and it was good,
It held too long. Grieve, and give thanks tonight.
