# Chapter 351: Doing Good Better

No holiday is named for this day.

In 2015 {{c|Emma Leonhart}} turned nineteen,
In her second year at the university in {{p|Kelowna}},
And in her second year on the net.
This chapter begins a thread that runs through the rest of the month:
The story of a movement that set out to do the most good,
With arithmetic, and with money, and later with the machines.
She has said this month should carry that story,
And that one of its makers is a villain of it,
And that the book does not fully condemn the movement or the way of thinking it came from.
Both halves are kept here.
The movement is not its villain.
Its villain came out of it.

## The arithmetic of good

In this year a young philosopher at {{p|Oxford}}, {{c|William MacAskill}},
Published a book called {{x|Doing Good Better}},
Saying that a person should give, and work, and choose a career,
Where the good done per dollar is greatest,
And that this could be measured,
And that a life saved from malaria in {{p|Africa}} counts as much as one at home,
And costs far less.
The movement had been growing for some years,
Out of the work of {{c|Peter Singer}}, and of {{c|Toby Ord}}, and of the givers who pledged a tenth,
And out of a community on the net that called itself rationalist,
Gathered around long essays on how to think without fooling yourself,
Which worried, earlier than almost anyone, that a machine smarter than people
Might not share their aims.
It called itself {{x|effective altruism}}.
In the summer of this year it held a meeting at the offices of {{x|Google}},
And the men who would own the century's machines spoke at it.
One of its ideas was called earning to give:
Go into finance, make a fortune, give it away.
A young man named {{c|Sam Bankman-Fried}}, who had met {{c|MacAskill}} as a student,
Had taken that idea, and was trading at a firm in {{p|New York}}.
He is a later chapter's villain.
Here he is a young man with an idea that sounded good,
Which is how villains of that kind begin.

## The lab

On the eleventh of December, in {{p|San Francisco}},
A group of the richest men of the industry and some of its best researchers
Founded a laboratory without profit,
To make sure that a general intelligence, when it came, would benefit all of humanity.
They called it {{x|OpenAI}}.
{{c|Elon Musk}} and {{c|Sam Altman}} led it, and {{c|Ilya Sutskever}}, from the chapter of 2012, ran its research.
It said it would share its work openly.
It later became a company, and the largest maker of the machines this book was written with,
And kept the name.
In October, in {{p|London}}, a program from {{c|DeepMind}}, called {{x|AlphaGo}},
Beat {{c|Fan Hui}}, the champion of {{p|Europe}} at the game of {{x|go}}, five games to none.
It was kept secret until the next January.
The match that shook the world was the next year's.

## The cartoonists and the concert

On the seventh of January two brothers with rifles walked into the offices of the paper {{x|Charlie Hebdo}} in {{p|Paris}},
Which had printed cartoons of the {{x|Prophet}},
And killed twelve people, most of them cartoonists, at their weekly meeting.
Two days later a third man killed four Jewish shoppers in a kosher market.
Millions marched with signs that said, I am {{x|Charlie}}.
On the thirteenth of November, a Friday night, men of the caliphate of the chapter before
Attacked the stadium, the cafés, and the concert hall of the {{x|Bataclan}},
Where a rock band was playing,
And killed one hundred and thirty people.

## The crossing

In this year more than a million people crossed into {{p|Europe}},
From {{p|Syria}}, {{p|Afghanistan}}, {{p|Iraq}}, and further,
Over the sea to the Greek islands in rubber boats,
And on foot up through the {{p|Balkans}}.
Thousands drowned.
On the second of September a boy of two, {{c|Alan Kurdi}}, from {{p|Kobani}},
Was found face down on a beach in {{p|Turkey}}, in a red shirt,
And a photograph of him was seen by the whole world in a day.
His family had been trying to reach {{p|Canada}}.
On the thirty-first of August {{c|Angela Merkel}} said, we can do this,
And {{p|Germany}} opened its doors, and took nearly a million.
The far right of the year before grew on the answer.
On the thirtieth of September {{p|Russia}} began bombing in {{p|Syria}}, to save the regime.

## The marriage and the woman

On the twenty-second of May {{p|Ireland}}, a country of the old faith,
Voted by the people to open marriage to two people of the same sex,
The first country to do it by popular vote.
On the twenty-sixth of June, in {{x|Obergefell v. Hodges}},
The {{x|Supreme Court}} of the {{p|United States}} said that marriage is a right for all couples,
And the rainbow lit the White House that night.
On the first of June a magazine put on its cover a woman, {{c|Caitlyn Jenner}},
Who had been an Olympic champion under another name,
And millions of people saw, perhaps for the first time, a woman who had transitioned in her sixties,
And the argument of the next decade about women like her began to be loud.

## The church in Charleston

On the seventeenth of June a young man sat for an hour in a Bible study
At the {{x|Emanuel African Methodist Episcopal Church}} in {{p|Charleston}},
And then shot nine of the people who had welcomed him.
He had wanted to start a race war, and had photographed himself with the flag of the old confederacy.
The flag was taken down from the capitol of {{p|South Carolina}} that summer.
The families forgave him at his hearing.

## The talks that did something

On the twelfth of December, at {{p|Le Bourget}}, outside {{p|Paris}},
After six years of meetings that took note,
The countries of the world adopted an agreement:
To hold the warming well below two degrees, and to try for one and a half.
Each country would set its own goal, and say what it was doing, and do more over time.
Nothing in it forced any country to do anything.
That was why every country could sign it.
The foreign minister of {{p|France}} struck the gavel, and the hall cheered and wept.
The years after this one were the hottest ever measured, each in turn.

## The deals and the debts

On the fourteenth of July {{p|Iran}} and the great powers agreed
That it would limit its nuclear work for years, and the sanctions would be lifted.
On the fifth of July the people of {{p|Greece}} voted no to the conditions of its creditors,
And a week later their government accepted harsher ones.
On the eighteenth of September it was found that {{x|Volkswagen}}
Had built its diesel cars to cheat the tests of their fumes,
Eleven million of them.
On the sixteenth of June a man who sold buildings in {{p|New York}}
Came down a golden escalator in his tower and said he would run for president,
And called the people coming from {{p|Mexico}} rapists.
People laughed. It is the next chapter's year.

## Home

On the nineteenth of October {{p|Canada}} voted out {{c|Stephen Harper}},
And {{c|Justin Trudeau}}, the son of a prime minister, came to power,
And named a cabinet of as many women as men, because it's 2015.
On the second of June the {{x|Truth and Reconciliation Commission}}
Gave its report on the residential schools,
And called what they had done a cultural genocide,
And made ninety-four calls to action.

## The rest of the year

On the twenty-fifth of April an earthquake killed nearly nine thousand people in {{p|Nepal}},
And brought down the old temples of {{p|Kathmandu}}.
On the twenty-fourth of March a copilot flew a {{x|Germanwings}} airliner into the {{x|Alps}} on purpose.
On the twenty-seventh of February {{c|Boris Nemtsov}}, who opposed {{c|Putin}},
Was shot on a bridge in sight of the {{x|Kremlin}}.
On the fourteenth of July the probe {{x|New Horizons}} flew past {{p|Pluto}},
The planet voted out in the chapter of 2006,
And found a great heart of ice on it.
On the fourteenth of September two machines in {{p|Louisiana}} and {{p|Washington}}
Felt a ripple in space from two black holes that had merged a billion years before,
And it was announced the next year.
On the eighteenth of June the pope wrote to the world about its common home, and the warming.
On the eighth of November {{p|Myanmar}} voted, and the party of {{c|Aung San Suu Kyi}} won.
On the twenty-ninth of October {{p|China}} ended the rule of one child.

## The year held

A girl of nineteen in her second year,
And a movement of arithmetic, and a lab named open,
And a young man who meant to earn to give.
Cartoonists killed, a concert hall, a million crossing, a boy on a beach in a red shirt,
We can do this, marriage for all in two countries, a woman on a magazine,
Nine killed at a Bible study, an agreement at {{p|Le Bourget}} that forced no one,
A cheating engine, a golden escalator, a new prime minister, the calls to action,
And a heart of ice on the planet that was voted out.

Refuse the consolation that good can be done without counting.
Refuse the consolation that counting is the good.
Refuse the consolation that a man who means to earn to give
Has given anything yet.
