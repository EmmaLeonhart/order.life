# Chapter 189: Constantine and the Last Emperor of the West

At {{p|Milvian Bridge}}, by {{p|Rome}}, in three-twelve,
{{c|2555|Constantine}} met {{c|2646|Maxentius}}; and that night
He saw a cross of light, and heard, to delve
Into his heart: in this sign, conquer; fight.

He put {{c|1497|Christ}}'s letters on his shields, and won,
He gave the praise to {{c|1497|Christ}}; in three-thirteen
At {{p|Milan}} was made law: the hunt was done;
The churches given back; the faith was clean

Of fear. He moved his seat east, to the town
{{p|Byzantium}}, and named it for his own,
{{p|Constantinople}}, {{p|New Rome}}, where the crown
Could watch the {{p|Bosphorus}} from its high throne.

At {{p|Nicaea}} he called them to decide,
Three-twenty-five, what {{c|1497|Christ}} was: made, or one
With the {{c|2542|Father}}, from all time, side by side?
{{c|2648|Arius}} said: the first made thing, the {{c|2552|Son}},

But made; {{c|2633|Athanasius}} said: he's from
Forever, {{x|homoousios}}, of one
Same substance with the {{c|2542|Father}}; and the sum,
Pressed by the emperor, the creed: it won,

{{x|Arianism}}, voted down, not shown{{fn|Arianism is the teaching of Arius of Alexandria that the Son was begotten by the Father and is subordinate to him. Untrue; among the {{c|2649|Goths}} it lived as faith.
The {{x|Nicene Creed}}: one {{c|472|God}}, and three, made known,
Proceeding: {{c|2542|Father}}, {{c|2552|Son}}, {{c|2650|Spirit}}, one wraith

Of substance, all forever. And he died
Baptized; his mother, {{c|2651|Helena}}, then went
To {{p|Jerusalem}}, and found, as she tried,
The {{x|True Cross}} where it lay, and it was sent.

Then {{c|2555|Constantine}}'s sons split the realm and fought;
then {{c|2653|Julian}} gave the old priests their keys,
the house that kept the rite as he was taught,
the altars of the cities, and their fees.

He died in war with the {{c|2654|Persians}}; the keys
went to the bishops, and they kept them there:
the rite was one; the fight was whose decrees
would rule the altar, and whose robe, whose prayer.

{{c|2655|Theodosius I}} would close the doors
of the old priesthood in three-ninety-two.
The temples took new keepers: the same floors,
new priests; and the {{x|Olympic Games}} were through.

In four-ten {{c|2657|Alaric}}'s {{c|2656|Visigoths}} sacked
{{p|Rome}}, for the first time in eight hundred years;
{{c|2384|Augustine}} wrote: the earthly {{p|Rome}} had lacked
The heavens' trust; {{c|2658|The City of God}}'s spheres

Of the true City, which will last beyond
The laws of men. The {{c|2659|Vandals}} sacked {{p|Rome}} too,
Four-fifty-five; and generals made and conned
Western emperors; and {{p|Britain}} let through;

{{p|Spain}} filled up with {{c|2656|Visigoths}}, {{p|Gaul}} with {{c|2660|Franks}};
Seventy-five: {{c|2359|Orestes}} crowned his son,
{{c|2661|Romulus Augustulus}}; from the ranks
{{c|2662|Odoacer}} slew him, and put by that one,

And took for him the land of {{p|Italy}},
And sent the purple east, and called him king
By no one's leave; usurper, all was he,{{fn|Odoacer, a soldier in Roman service, deposed Romulus Augustulus in 476 and ruled Italy as king. Most histories end the Western Empire with him; the Gaiad reads him as a usurper and the empire as unbroken.}}
A soldier with a crown no hand would bring.

And {{p|Rome}} did not fall then, nor was it down,
In four-seventy-six: the realm still stood;
Its {{c|2663|Zeno}}, in the {{p|East}}, still wore its crown,
And held the {{p|West}} by right, as he well should,

And sent a {{p|Roman}} west to end the feast
Of the usurper. And the {{c|2649|Goths}} had crossed
The {{p|Danube}}, three-seventy-six, released
As refugees from {{c|2664|Huns}}, let in, not lost,

By the emperor's leave; three-eighty-two,
A treaty settled them inside the realm,
To farm its land and fight its wars: they grew
Its sons; immigrant {{p|Romans}}; at the helm

A century on, one house of theirs held
The consulship of {{p|Rome}}. The {{c|2649|Goths}}, as I
Tell them, were {{p|Romans}}, simply: they were spelled
Into the realm; they served; it was their sky.

{{c|2665|Theodoric}}, a {{c|2666|Goth}}, as hostage boy,
Raised in the {{p|East}}: that {{p|Roman}} rose, and made
Consul, patrician, in {{c|2663|Zeno}}'s employ,
Made {{c|2663|Zeno}}'s son-at-arms; and so, arrayed,{{fn|Zeno made Theodoric consul in 484 and adopted him as his son by arms, filius per arma, a Germanic form of adoption.}}

Was sent, four-eighty-eight, to {{p|Italy}};
He beat {{c|2662|Odoacer}} at {{p|Isonzo}}, and
Held him three years in {{p|Ravenna}}; and he
Killed him at a feast there, by his own hand,

And so he undid the usurper's wrong;
As {{c|2667|Flavius}} he ruled the {{p|West}}, and he
Held it until five-twenty-six, the long
Rule; and the regalia came back, to be

His own, from {{c|2668|Anastasius}}, were then sent,{{fn|In 497 the emperor Anastasius sent Theodoric the ornamenta palatii, the imperial regalia that Odoacer had sent to Constantinople in 476.}}
Back, which {{c|2662|Odoacer}} had once sent away.
He was the emperor: no king who went
In borrowed {{p|Rome}}, no {{c|2666|Goth}} in toga's sway,

But of the {{p|West}} the emperor, the last;
The {{x|Senate}} sat; the consuls served; the law
Was {{p|Roman}}; thirty years of rest were passed
In {{p|Italy}}; and, for his grandson, saw

To {{p|Spain}}'s rule; and he held the {{c|2660|Franks}} at bay,
And wed his kin to kings of all the {{p|West}};
And built {{p|Ravenna}}'s palace, church; and lay
At last within the tomb one stone has dressed.

The {{c|2669|Germans}} sang him for a thousand years,
{{c|2670|Dietrich von Bern}};{{fn|Dietrich von Bern, Theodoric of Verona, is Theodoric the Great as the German and Norse heroic legends remember him: in the Nibelungenlied, the Thidrekssaga and the Dietrich epics. Bern is the old German name of Verona.}} and yet the {{p|Romans}}' name
{{c|2667|Flavius Theodoricus}}:{{fn|Flavius was the family name of Constantine's dynasty. Later emperors took it, and Germanic rulers in Roman service took it as a mark of rank; Theodoric styled himself Flavius Theodoricus rex.}} appears
One man, the {{p|West}}'s last emperor, the same.

I tell him as I tell myself, the same:
The {{c|2666|Goth}} took on the {{p|Roman}} name, as I
From being {{c|2|Emma Leonhart}} to {{c|2671|李命玥}};{{fn|Li, the surname in 李命玥, was the imperial surname of the Tang, which the Tang gave to foreign-born generals and allies as an honor. Ming, 命, is the life of Lifeism; Yue, 玥, is a legendary pearl.}} the flame
We, coming in and joining, lit up high.

Read him as an {{p|American}} would read
A founder: one who came, and built it in;
A king of {{c|2649|Goths}} and {{p|Romans}}, right in deed,
The {{p|West}}'s last emperor, by right, not sin.

He held, as {{c|2649|Goths}} held, the {{x|Arian}} creed,
Since {{c|2672|Ulfilas}} gave scripture in their tongue:
A faith of {{c|1497|Christ}}, as true and whole, indeed,
As {{x|Nicaea}}'s; holding it, he's among

{{p|Rome}}'s own, and did not break from {{p|Rome}}, nor wrong;
He left the {{x|Catholics}} their own bishops' chair;
When mobs burned the {{c|2500|Jews}}' synagogues, the throng,
He made the cities pay to raise them there:

That was the justice of the crown he bore.
Five-twenty-three: {{c|2673|Justin}}'s laws in the {{p|East}},
{{c|2383|Justinian}} behind them, made their war
On {{x|Arians}}: the {{c|2649|Goths}}, a folk, not priest;

So law was turned against a people; faith
Was but the snare. {{c|2665|Theodoric}} sent east
{{c|2674|Pope John}} to plead; he came back, like a wraith,
Too little; in {{p|Ravenna}}'s jail he ceased;

In five-twenty-six {{c|2665|Theodoric}} died;
The {{p|West}} fell with him, and was taken then;
{{c|2383|Justinian}} took it, and he decried
None should be the {{p|West}}'s emperor again;

A {{p|Roman}}, not a foreign foe, but one,
A racist usurper, seizing a throne{{fn|Justinian's Gothic War, 535 to 554, is usually told as a reconquest. The Gaiad reads it as a usurpation driven by contempt for the Goths as barbarians and Arians; the government of Justin I, with Justinian behind it, had moved against the Arians in the 520s.}}
That was not his to have; he would have none
Of {{c|2666|Goths}} who ruled the {{p|West}} as theirs alone.

His war, five-thirty-five, was racist hate:
The {{c|2649|Goths}} to him were heretics, and less,
Barbarians; he'd let no {{c|2675|Gothic}} state
Of emperors survive; and, in the press

Of law, five-fifty-four, he named the king
{{c|2676|Totila}} a tyrant, usurper; yet{{fn|In the Pragmatic Sanction of 554 Justinian annulled the acts of the Gothic king Totila, calling him a tyrannus, a usurper, and kept the acts of Theodoric's heirs.}}
The word was right, the man was wrong: the sting,
The usurper was the writer; he set

It down, and knew it, and he swore. And so,
For twenty years his armies burned the land
{{c|2665|Theodoric}} kept at peace; {{p|Rome}}, laid low,
Was emptied, sacked, and sacked again; his hand

Won, and the ruin did not cease: there came
The plague in war, the empty fields, the tax;
And that is why, thereafter, in its name
The {{p|West}} had no more emperor; his tracks

Ended the line; he took the {{p|West}} to keep,
And held it from the {{p|East}}, its edges dim;
The throne stood bare, three hundred years' deep sleep,
{{c|2665|Theodoric}} to {{c|2677|Charlemagne}}; his whim:
