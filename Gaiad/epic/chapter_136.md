# Chapter 136: G Builds the Neolithic

While {{c|1875|I}} still hunted reindeer on the plain
Of {{p|Europe}}, {{c|1862|F}}'s proliferating name
Had set another branch, and chain on chain,
In {{p|Fertile Crescent}}, changing all the game:

Not the old running, following the sign
Of any herd; but planting; and in pits
The storing of the grain; and row and line,
And harvest; grinding wheat on stones. It fits

A whole remaking of the human mode:
The old script of the nomad, set aside,
The loping forager, who long had strode,
For something scarce conceivable: to bide.

The choice to stay. The choice to not be chased
By food in season; and the choice, to feed
Myself, to say to the land, firmly placed:
I plant, and wait, and then I reap the seed;

I will not leave. I stay in this one place;
And that is vastest of all plans, for it,
Until I die, my son stays in my space,
Makes of one ground a future. Thus was writ

{{p|Neolithic}} Revolution. The {{p|Y}}
Was found, consistently, to be the one,
That walked beside the farmers, by and by:
{{c|1903|Haplogroup G}}, the farmer's line, begun

In that first crescent. {{c|1873|G}} is he whose sons
Left {{p|Fertile Crescent}}, and with grain they went,
With sheep and goat, as the first farming runs,
To {{p|Anatolia}} and {{p|Europe}}, sent

With all the pattern of the new life. {{c|1873|G}},
The first farm wave to come to {{p|Europe}}'s shore,
Marks {{p|Linear Pottery Culture}}; he,
The founder of it, at its very core,

Of farming villages whose layers lie
In {{p|Greece}}'s valleys, {{p|Italy}}'s own ground,
And in {{p|Balkan}} stratigraphy, so high;
And {{c|1873|G}} is in the bones that have been found

Of {{p|Ötzi}}, {{c|1904|Iceman}} of the {{p|Alps}}, who lay,
A frozen body; and his {{p|Y}} makes plain,
Five thousand and three hundred years, they say,
That {{p|Alpine}} farmers of that age and grain

Of time, the late stone and the copper days,
Were {{c|1873|G}}, securely. Now, before the grain
Was sown, before the fields, the story stays,
Before a seed was planted, and is plain:

It starts, so far as we can tell, at one
Great hill in {{p|Turkey}}: of the many sites
That digging ever found, the work there done
Is most astonishing: those stony heights

Of {{p|Göbekli Tepe}}: built, as years are told,
Six millennia ere {{c|1898|Stonehenge}}, and more;
Eleven thousand five hundred years old,
Or so; and by a thousand years before

The settled towns of {{p|Jericho}}. And they
Who built it were not settled, nor the mild,
Grain-storing villagers of later day,
Not farmers: they were hunters, and the wild

Wandering hunter-gatherers, whose hands
Had no precedent for such work, and stone:
A ring of great T-pillars, where it stands,
Some over five meters, of tons, full-grown

And carved with animals: with fox and snake,
With scorpion, wild boar, and vulture's wings,
Arranged in rings for something's very sake,
Like temple pillars, though of no such things

As settled peoples build. A temple, raised
By hunters, by what laws we have not known,
For causes leaving us ignorant, dazed,
In a place no one dwelt, where grass had grown

An ample, undramatic edge; and they
Would pledge whole generations, far and near,
To come, with visits, and the place repay,
Elaborating what they built, each year:

Two thousand years of it. It was no town,
No dwelling-place; it had a different worth:
A ritual site, a site of renown,
A center like no other on the earth,

Where all the scattered bands would come and meet
From all the region, there, whatever be
Its purpose, and together make complete;
And since they had to gather, and to see

The building through, they had to eat; to feed
The crew, they grew the grain; and so pursues,
In this, the {{x|Gaiad}}'s reading, as we read,
{{c|1905|Klaus Schmidt}}'s hypothesis, and fills its clues:

Farming was seeded by the temple, not
By need, nor hunger; and the first things sown,
Einkorn and emmer, not by climate's lot,
Were for the feast-days at the pillared stone

At {{p|Göbekli Tepe}}'s gatherings. The grain
Was born to serve the temple, and the thing
The temple needed settled it again;
And staying grain, the grain made people cling

To one place; settlement then made the field
And furrow; and the fields, then, later on,
Made not grain only, all that they would yield:
The immanence of cities. First was drawn

The temple; then the city; then the crown
And kingdom: so religion first; this reads,
With farming second, cities third, set down,
Against the textbook's order, where the needs

Of practice bring forth farming as first cause,
Religion later. {{x|Gaiad}} answers: no:
Faith first. The people gathered, with a pause,
To build what they adored; and they would know,

In service of that worship, how to keep
Themselves in bread; and gatherings, which grew,
Built our civilization, running deep;
Scripted ritual life; the priestly crew

Of proto-priests there made what came to be
All that came after; and that priestly clan,
The builders, and their {{p|Y}}: it all was {{c|1873|G}}:
{{c|1873|G}} built {{p|Göbekli Tepe}}; and {{c|1873|G}} began

The farming; it was {{c|1873|G}} who bore the plan
And wheat to {{p|Europe}}, generations, who,
From {{p|Anatolia}}, as they began,
Brought grain and sheep and pots, the package, too.

Long-walking farmers from the crescent, they
Took {{c|1873|G}} across the continent; its rates
In {{p|Sardinian}} men are high today,
Extraordinary: there the early gates

Of island settlement were opened wide
To {{p|Neolithic}} farmers; and apart
{{p|Sardinia}} was kept by sea and tide,
Preserving {{c|1873|G}}; and on the mainland chart

Of {{p|Europe}}, they are later shown as thinned,
When {{p|Yamnaya}} arrived; but there it stays,
{{p|Sardinia}}, untouched by the steppe wind:
The {{p|Neolithic}} dominant; these days,

{{p|Sardinian}} men on the hills are heirs,
That {{p|Göbekli Tepe}} line, the builders' own,
In many cases, of those ancient prayers:
First priesthood of the greatest work in stone

And faith the {{p|Near East}} knew, is carried still,
As by an ancient and unbroken thread,
In shepherds' bodies, walking the same hill,
Where, in the ancient days, their fathers led.

{{c|1873|G}} is alive. {{c|1873|G}} built what {{x|Gaiad}} calls
The founding monument, and with its seed
Began civilization, with its walls;
The island shepherds keep the herds they feed

On {{p|Mediterranean}} hills, the ways
Their grandfathers walked; and a name, which stands,
{{c|1520|Melchizedek}}'s, the telling here repays:
The {{p|priest}}-king of {{p|Salem}} in those old lands,

Who greets {{c|1308|Abraham}} later, blesses him,
Mysterious, and brief in his short stay,
Receives a tithe, and vanishes; the dim,
A mere narrative convenience, they say:

A shadow. But the {{x|Gaiad}} reads him new:
{{c|1520|Melchizedek}} stands for the remnant old
Of all the {{p|Göbekli Tepe}} priestly crew,
The temple-builders, living still, as told

In {{c|1308|Abraham}}'s own day; who met him there
Upon his journey south; by no mere chance,
At {{p|Sanliurfa}}, where he gave his prayer,
The closest of all cities to the stance

Of {{p|Göbekli Tepe}} itself. And he stops
At {{p|Sanliurfa}}, where he meets, as well,
{{c|1520|Melchizedek}}; and here his long road drops
From {{p|Ur}} down to {{p|Levant}}, as stories tell:

The last priest of the {{p|Göbekli Tepe}} way.
The tall old pillars, long before, were done,
Were buried, an entombing, on that day,
On purpose: burial, not care, had won

The keeping of the past. But still the line,
In the region, persisted; in the names
Of those who bore the old rite's whole design
That {{x|Gaiad}} gives, {{c|1520|Melchizedek}} lays claims

To "{{c|1906|George Gobeklius}}": the priest whose past
Came down from hands that carved, and gave their frame
To T-shaped stone and beast, which hold at last,
Eleven thousand years, and stay the same.

{{c|1308|Abraham}} meets him, takes the blessing, pays
The tithe, and travels on; and passes on
A blessing from the old order, conveys
It to the {{p|Hebrew}} story. So is drawn

The {{x|Gaiad}}'s quiet handing-off: the first
Religious line gives over, soon or late,
Of that whole land, so long and deeply nursed,
To the next. {{c|1873|G}} no longer has to wait:

{{c|1873|G}} built the temple, and invented, too,
The farming; the foundation, and the whole
Decree, the package, which would come to view
And shape all later life, and play its role,

Was made by him. The {{c|1873|G}}-borne farmer, west
And north to {{p|Europe}} walking, is the lore
Of how in farming {{p|Europe}} would be pressed;
The {{c|1875|I}}-borne hunters met, upon that shore,

The coming farmers: sometimes there was fight
And sometimes blending; and the farmers' hand
Did not erase the hunters by its might,
But it reshaped the ledgers of the land;

{{p|Europe}} was draped in layers: {{c|1875|I}} below,
The base; and {{c|1873|G}} the second, as it came;
Of {{c|1884|R}} above, the third, the later flow,

And weave of modern {{p|European}} blood:
From hunter, to the farmer, to the man
Who rode the steppe: three waves, and one great flood,
Mixed in the {{p|Y}}, as in a written plan.

{{c|1873|G}} is the second wave; the builder, priest,

Theology made quiet: fields would yield

As servants to the temple: first the god;
The grain came after, at the hands of men.

Gave first as offering, and laid down then

At the stone pillar's foot, and there to feed
The gathered hunters, come to worship; so,
What words they muttered, or they sang, their creed,
We cannot say, and we can never know.

Made first civilization from that soil

Of gathering, and building, and the grain
Grown all together; that is what we know,
And that's enough. The fire began, and plain,
