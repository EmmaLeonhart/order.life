# Chapter 200: Ireland and Brian Boru

{{p|Ireland}}, which {{c|2966|Saint Patrick}} turned, then became
A place of learning, in the fifth age, blessed;
For while the {{p|West}} was burned, and in the flame
Of {{c|2383|Justinian}}'s wars, the plague, the rest,

The empty throne, the island lay beyond
The usurper's reach; and learning there stayed:
The monks kept {{x|Greek}} and {{x|Latin}}, and the bond
With books that would be lost; and so they made

{{c|2967|Columba}}'s {{p|Iona}}, five-sixty-three;
{{c|2968|Columbanus}}' houses all through the land
Of {{p|Europe}}; {{c|2969|Aidan}}, from {{p|Lindisfarne}}, free,
Went through the north; the {{c|2970|Book of Kells}}, by hand,

Was made, the knotwork, interlaced, the art
Of all the {{x|Celts}}. And {{p|Ireland}} was split
Into small kingdoms, the {{x|tuath}}, each apart,
With overkings, the {{c|2971|High King}} over it,

At {{p|Tara}}. And the {{c|2848|Vikings}} came, and burned,
In ninety-five; {{p|Iona}} sacked again;
And they made towns, the first the island learned:
{{p|Dublin}}, {{p|Waterford}}, {{p|Cork}}, {{p|Limerick}}; men

Of {{x|Norse}} and {{x|Irish}} dwelt in them. And then
{{c|2972|Brian Boru}}, of {{p|Munster}}'s {{p|Dál gCais}}, rose,
High King, ten-oh-two, who joined the isle's men
In one, the first; and he beat the {{x|Norse}} foes,

With {{p|Leinster}}'s kings, at {{c|2973|Clontarf}}, on the day
Of {{x|Good Friday}}, ten-fourteen, broke the might
Of the {{x|Norse}} in {{p|Ireland}}; and, as he lay
In prayer within his tent, at end of fight,

The fleeing {{c|2848|Vikings}} found him, and he died;
And after him the kings fought on, apart.
And {{c|2975|Eriugena}}, scholar, at the side
Of {{c|2976|Charles the Bald}}, the learned court's own heart,

Knew {{x|Greek}} and {{x|Latin}}, and he turned the {{x|Greek}}
Of {{c|2977|Dionysius}} to {{x|Latin}}; made
The {{x|Periphyseon}}, the great whole, to seek
The one in all; the high crosses were laid,

{{x|Old Irish}} kept the oldest tales: the {{c|2978|Táin}},
The {{x|Cattle Raid of Cooley}}, where, alone,
{{c|2979|Cú Chulainn}} held {{p|Ulster}}, {{p|Connacht}}'s bane,
In battle-fury, warped, shape not his own,

Doing what none could do; and he would fall
When his taboo was broken; kin, of old,
Of {{c|2067|Heracles}} and {{c|2333|Bhima}}; and, in all,
{{c|2980|Medb}}, the queen of war and sovereignty, bold.

For {{c|2966|Patrick}} did not burn the temples; he
Preached, and the chiefs turned, and the tribes came too;
The faith took in the old, and let it be;
And so the old gods lived on, in the new.

So honor {{p|Ireland}}: where the books were kept
Beyond the usurper's reach; {{c|2972|Brian}}, slain
Praying at {{c|2973|Clontarf}}; the hero who leapt
In fury; and the old rite, kept again.
