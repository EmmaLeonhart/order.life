# Chapter 341: The Water and the Levees

No holiday is named for this day.

In 2005 {{c|Emma Leonhart}} turned nine.
The year had had no chapter of its own in this book until now,
Which says something about the book and nothing about the year.
It was the year a city drowned in the richest country in the world,
The year a pope died and another was chosen,
The year the moving pictures of anyone at all
Got a place to live,
And the year two countries opened marriage to everyone,
One of them the country she would grow up in.

## The pictures

On the fourteenth of February three young men who had worked at a payments company
Registered an address for sharing videos.
On the twenty-third of April one of them, {{c|Jawed Karim}},
Put up the first one: eighteen seconds of himself at the zoo in {{p|San Diego}},
Standing before the elephants,
Saying that the cool thing about them is that they have really, really, really long trunks.
That is the whole of it.
The site was called {{x|YouTube}}.
A year and a half later it was sold for more than a billion and a half dollars.
The children who were nine that year
Would grow up with a camera that could reach everyone,
And some of them would grow up on it.
In June two students opened a board of links called {{x|Reddit}},
And faked the early users to make it look full.
In February {{x|Google Maps}} put the streets of the world
Into a page that could be dragged with the hand.

## The pope

On the second of April {{c|John Paul II}} died in {{p|Rome}},
After twenty-six years, the first pope from {{p|Poland}},
Who had helped bring down the empire of the east,
And who would not move on women, or on the people the next popes would still not move on.
Millions came to his funeral.
On the nineteenth of April the cardinals chose {{c|Joseph Ratzinger}},
His enforcer of doctrine for twenty-four years,
Who took the name {{c|Benedict XVI}}.
He would do the rarest thing a pope can do,
And step down, eight years later, in this month.

## The trains and the bus

On the seventh of July, in {{p|London}}, in the morning rush,
Three bombs went off in the underground, and one on a bus at {{p|Tavistock Square}}.
Fifty-two people were killed.
The four bombers were young men, three of them born in {{p|Britain}}.
The day before, {{p|London}} had been given the games of 2012,
And the city had celebrated.

## The drowning

On the twenty-ninth of August the storm called {{x|Katrina}}
Came ashore near the border of {{p|Louisiana}} and {{p|Mississippi}}.
In {{p|New Orleans}} the levees the army's engineers had built
Failed in more than fifty places,
And four parts in five of the city went under water.
The people who could leave had left.
The people who could not, most of them poor, most of them black,
Were left in their attics, on their roofs,
In the stadium with no power and no water,
And at the convention center, where no one came for days.
About eighteen hundred people died.
The head of the federal agency for disasters
Had run an association of horse shows before.
The president told him he was doing a heck of a job.
The government knew where the water was.
It did not come.
A girl of nine, far to the north, saw the roofs on the screen
With words painted on them asking for help.

## The earthquake

On the eighth of October the earth broke under {{p|Kashmir}},
On both sides of the line between {{p|Pakistan}} and {{p|India}},
In the morning, while children were in their schools.
More than eighty thousand people died, many of them children,
Under the schools that were not built to stand.
Winter came to the mountains before the shelters did.

## The marriages

On the third of July {{p|Spain}}, a country of the old faith,
Opened marriage to two people of the same sex.
On the twentieth of July {{p|Canada}} did the same,
By a law of the whole country, the {{x|Civil Marriage Act}},
After the courts of most of its provinces had already said so.
{{p|Canada}} was the fourth country in the world to do it,
And the first in the Americas.
The girl of nine grew up in that country.
A law is made before the people who will need it know they will.
That is what such a law is for.

## The fingers and the cedars

On the thirtieth of January the people of {{p|Iraq}} voted,
And held up fingers stained purple with the ink,
While the bombs went off at the polling places.
In October they voted on a constitution,
And in December for a parliament.
The voting did not stop the war.
On the fourteenth of February, in {{p|Beirut}},
A bomb of a ton of explosives killed the former prime minister, {{c|Rafic Hariri}},
And the people of {{p|Lebanon}} filled the squares,
And the army of {{p|Syria}}, there for twenty-nine years, went home by April.
They called it the {{x|Cedar Revolution}}.
In August {{p|Israel}} pulled its settlers and soldiers out of {{p|Gaza}},
And {{p|Gaza}} was left to what came after.
On the thirteenth of May, in {{p|Andijan}}, in {{p|Uzbekistan}},
Soldiers fired on a crowd in the square, and killed hundreds.
The government said nearly two hundred, all of them terrorists.
On the twenty-eighth of July the {{x|IRA}} said it was done with the gun.

## The treaty and the cartoons

On the sixteenth of February the {{x|Kyoto Protocol}} came into force,
Binding the rich countries to cut their gases, a little,
Without the {{p|United States}}, which had walked away from it.
{{p|Canada}} signed it and later walked away too.
On the thirtieth of September a newspaper in {{p|Denmark}}
Printed twelve cartoons of the {{x|Prophet}},
To show that it could.
The answer came in the next year, in fire and in deaths,
And this book does not choose between the paper's right and the anger,
Except to say that both were real, and neither was the whole.

## The rest of the year

In January the probe {{x|Huygens}} landed on {{p|Titan}},
The farthest landing ever made,
And in the same month astronomers found a world beyond {{p|Pluto}}
Larger than it, which they later named {{x|Eris}},
And which would cost {{p|Pluto}} its place among the planets the year after.
In March {{c|Terri Schiavo}}, kept alive for fifteen years,
Was let to die after the courts and the congress fought over her body.
On the thirty-first of May the man called {{x|Deep Throat}}
Said that he was {{c|Mark Felt}}.
In June {{p|Iran}} elected {{c|Mahmoud Ahmadinejad}}.
On the second of July the concerts called {{x|Live 8}}
Asked the rich countries to forgive the debts of the poor.
On the twenty-fourth of October {{c|Rosa Parks}} died.
In October and November the suburbs of {{p|Paris}} burned,
After two boys died hiding from the police in a power station.
On the twenty-second of November {{c|Angela Merkel}} became chancellor of {{p|Germany}},
And held it for sixteen years.
In November an article in the open encyclopedia
Said falsely that a journalist, {{c|John Seigenthaler}},
Had been suspected in the killing of the {{c|Kennedy}}s.
It had stood four months.
He wrote that he had no way to know who had written it.
The open book tightened its rules a little.
The labor that kept it true was still unpaid.

## The year held

A video of elephants, a link board faking its first users, a map you drag.
A pope buried, a pope chosen, bombs in {{p|London}}, a city drowned.
A mountain fell on its schools.
Two countries opened marriage, and one of them was hers.
Purple fingers, cedars in the square, a massacre in {{p|Andijan}}, a treaty without its largest polluter,
Twelve cartoons, a world larger than {{p|Pluto}}, and the suburbs on fire.

Refuse the consolation that the water killed the people of {{p|New Orleans}}.
The water came, and the help did not.
Refuse the consolation that a law is too far away to matter to a child.
It is waiting for whoever needs it.
