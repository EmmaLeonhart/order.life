# Chapter 224: Granada and the Ocean

In fourteen-ninety-two, two doors swung wide,
One closing and one opening; and {{p|Spain}}
Stood at both thresholds, history's divide,
A storm that would remake the world's domain.

The first door shut: {{p|Granada}}. For the span
Of eight centuries, crescent and the cross
Furled across {{p|Iberia}}; it ran,
The long {{x|Reconquista}}, its gain and loss:

From {{p|Covadonga}}, the desperate stand,
Where once {{c|3382|Pelayo}} held the Christian few
Against the {{c|2774|Umayyad}} tide; through the land,
Reclaimed, by century, the border drew

Its ebb and flow, fortress, devastation,
Alliance and betrayal; and the throng
Of {{c|3383|Moors}} had built a splendid creation:
The {{p|Alhambra}}, its lattice, and its song,

The courtyards where the water spoke, and went
In fountains and in channels; and the lace
Of tilework, mathematical, intent,
The gardens where, as in one interface,

{{p|Africa}}, {{p|Europe}} bloomed in jasmine's air,
In orange trees, the muezzin's murmured call;
{{c|3384|Al-Andalus}}, the luminous, the rare,
Where {{c|3385|Muslim}}, {{c|3386|Christian}}, {{c|2510|Jew}} lived, one and all,

Imperfect, but a real constellation
Of living side by side; the sieve that passed
{{x|Greek}}, {{c|3340|Roman}}, through {{c|3387|Arabic}} translation
To {{c|3388|Latin}}; where {{c|3389|Maimonides}} would cast

His mind, with {{c|2393|Averroes}}, upon decrees
Divine, the knotted questions; and the store
Of knowledge, crossing north, as by degrees,
A caravan of wisdom, door to door.

The frontier narrowed; and {{p|Toledo}} fell,
Then {{p|Cordoba}}, and {{p|Seville}}'s minarets;
The caliphate receded, like a bell
Fading southward, till, as the last sun sets,

Only {{p|Granada}}: the last {{c|3390|Moorish}} realm,
The crescent's final south. And now there came
{{c|3391|Ferdinand}}, {{c|3392|Isabella}}, at the helm,
{{p|Castile}}, {{p|Aragon}} joined, the toil, the flame

Of war for altars, ten years' siege, the slow
Strangling the last {{c|3393|Nasrid}} kingdom, alone;
Outlasted all the {{c|3390|Moorish}} realms; and so
The second January day was known,

In fourteen-ninety-two: the bell, the horn;
And {{c|3394|Boabdil}}, the last {{c|3393|Nasrid}} sultan, rode
Out from the {{p|Alhambra}}'s gate, and forlorn;
The hills heard; and he wept; his sorrow showed.

His mother said: You weep as women weep
For what you could not, as a man, defend;
And something precious passed, as into sleep,
From the world; under the trumpets, an end.

What {{c|3391|Ferdinand}} and {{c|3392|Isabella}} did
With triumph was to make one altar: so
The {{p|Alhambra}} Decree, which would forbid,
Signed in the {{c|3383|Moors}}' own palace, bade Jews go:

Baptism or exile. The ancient, fine
{{c|3395|Sephardic}} communities, the most learned,
Physicians, traders, rooted like the vine
And olive of {{p|Andalusia}}, were turned

Out, two hundred thousand, four months to leave
Or to convert; the lute, the {{x|Torah}}, borne
Away; the hearthstone, and the woven weave
Of fifteen hundred years were torn, and worn;

{{p|Morocco}}, {{p|Constantinople}}, they went,
To {{p|Amsterdam}}, {{p|Thessaloniki}}'s shore,
Bearing the keys, in memory's lament,
To houses, each one to its {{p|Spanish}} door,

Passed hand to hand five hundred years, the known
And heaviest heirloom, key to a land,
A house that would no longer be their own.
{{c|3396|Moriscos}} followed, by the same command:

The {{c|2764|Muslims}} too: convert or leave; the store
Of eight long centuries of culture swept
From the house; one crown held one faith; no more
The three; and yet the same two monarchs stepped

To bless the {{c|3114|Genoese}} sailor at their court,
A man of vision and compulsion, who
Had begged at {{p|Europe}}'s thrones, port after port,
For ships; the {{c|2500|Jews}}' expulsion not yet through

Six weeks: to sail west, the {{p|Atlantic}}'s heat,
Uncharted water, by a dream: the gold
Of {{p|Cipangu}}, and of {{p|Cathay}}'s silk to meet
By sailing west into the sunset's hold;

{{c|2952|Columbus}} said the gap was breached by will,
By courage and a compass. On the tide
Of August's third, three ships, so small and still,
From {{p|Cadiz}}' harbor sailed, the masts' bare pride,

Three bare trees against the {{p|Atlantic}} sky:
The {{c|3397|Nina}} and the {{c|3398|Pinta}} on the breeze,
And the {{c|3399|Santa Maria}} riding high.
{{c|2952|Columbus}}, brave, was wrong about the seas,

The earth, its size, the distance; and his creed
That {{p|Asia}} on the western rim would rise;
And what he found was not {{p|Cathay}}, indeed,
Nor yet {{p|Cipangu}}, but, there before his eyes,

The {{c|3400|Taino}} on their ground, a world that had
Its own, its calendar, its stars, its lore,
Its own astronomers and priests, the glad
Harvests and prayers, the villages, and more:

Canoes, the cotton and the corn; complete,
Lines running sixteen thousand years, before
The trumpet of the {{c|3401|European}} fleet
Had sounded. But {{c|2952|Columbus}} saw the ore,

The gold; and {{p|Europe}}'s hunger, unbounded,
That grew more ravenous, and would not flee,
Was set in motion when the cry sounded
From the crow's-nest, across the unknown sea:

Land! And the door swung open to the west.
What lay beyond, the peoples who had made
Their worlds, their glory, long before the crest
Of {{p|Seville}}'s spires, or {{c|3340|Roman}} roads were laid,

Before the {{p|Parthenon}}: that story claims
Its own abode, its telling, its own day:
The Book of {{c|3402|Lehi}} now begins; its names
Across the sea {{c|2952|Columbus}} sailed that way:

Sixteen thousand years of origin, raised
And curtained; corn; the feathered serpent's shrine;
The {{c|3403|Inca}}'s road; the potlatch; weather-glazed,
The totem, speaking in its sacred line;

World entire, a hemisphere alive,
Now at the hinge of its destruction stood,
Its transformation, where the hungers strive:
The old world's hunger met the new world's good.

So turn the page. The ocean has been crossed.
The Book of {{c|3402|Lehi}} opens on the face
Of {{p|America}}: the found, and the lost,
The first migration's children, and their grace,

Ten thousand years of art, a world apart.
{{p|Granada}}'s story ends in tears; the tale
Of ocean ends in sail; and then the heart,
{{c|3404|Americans}}', the other side of sail.
