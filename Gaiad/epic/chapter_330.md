# Chapter 330: The Source Book

No holiday is named for this day.

In April 1990 two agencies published a plan.
Its title is Understanding Our Genetic Inheritance.
It is the first five years,
The fiscal years 1991 through 1995.
The National Institutes of Health published it
With the Department of Energy.
The center that carries the institutes' share
Sits in {{p|Bethesda}}.
The program dates the plan as implemented
On the first of October, 1990,
The morning that opens the fiscal year it had named,
And dates its fifteen-year clock to that day.
A fiscal morning is not a morning the letters were read.

The plan calls the work a worldwide research effort,
And says the programs in other countries
Were getting organized.
Do not move a later center back into this April.

The five years do not ask for the whole book.
They ask for a map of human inheritance,
Marks along the chromosomes,
On average two to five centimorgans apart.
A centimorgan is the unit in which a geneticist counts
How readily two marks are split
Between a parent and a child.
Each mark was to be known by a short run of letters.
They ask for a physical map,
The marks about a hundred thousand letters apart,
And for a method that can read at fifty cents a letter,
And for an aggregate of ten million letters
Of human DNA, in long stretches.
Ten million is not the book.
They ask for a genetic map of the mouse,
A start on the physical map of a chromosome or two,
And about twenty million letters from other organisms.

The plan says an estimated one hundred thousand human genes.
In October 1988 the Times had given a range,
From fifty thousand to a hundred thousand.
The plan's word is an estimate, and not a count.
A later count is not this April.
The plan says the book would help to understand,
And eventually to treat, many of the more than
Four thousand genetic diseases.
The number is the plan's.
A help hoped for is not a treatment done.
It calls the information, once it exists,
The source book for biomedical science
In the twenty-first century.
A source book that has not been printed
Is not a scripture, and it is not a person.

The money it anticipates is about two hundred million
Dollars a year, for about fifteen years,
The figure it says the research council
And the technology office had already named.
The goals assume the program will reach that level.
An assumption is not a sum already in the account.
The years 1988 to 1990, it says,
Had been for getting organized.
Some money had been earmarked.
Do not say the whole price was voted on the first of October.

The Department of Energy's reason, in the plan's words,
Is a long-standing work of genetic research,
Directed at assessing what radiation,
And the chemicals of energy, do to health.
The institutes' reason is the health of the people,
And the genetics they had already funded.
Do not say the project was a weapon, or an apology.

In {{p|Santa Cruz}}, in 1985,
{{c|Robert Sinsheimer}}, the chancellor, convened a workshop.
It began on the evening of the twenty-fourth of May,
A Friday, and ended on the morning of the twenty-sixth.
A gift of thirty-six million dollars,
Meant for a telescope, the university had to return.
He had thought the money might found an institute
To read a human sequence.
The institute was not funded.
He recalled later that the question changed,
From whether it could be done to whether it should.
The recall is not a vote of the room.
The workshop did not publish this plan.

In 1988 the National Research Council recommended the effort,
And that the money be new,
Not taken from the work already funded.
In the fall the two agencies signed a memorandum to coordinate.
{{c|James Wyngaarden}}, director of the institutes, signed.
The notice gives the fall, and not the day.
In the same autumn {{c|James Watson}}
Was appointed to the genome office.
The Times of the fourth of October, a Tuesday,
Reported the appointment effective the previous Saturday,
The first of the month.
He would remain director of the laboratory
At {{p|Cold Spring Harbor}},
And come to the institutes a day or two in the week.
On the first of October 1989 the office was abolished,
And the National Center for Human Genome Research
Was established, with him as its director.
He is the man who, in 1953, with another,
Wished to suggest a structure,
And wrote that the pairing suggested a possible copy.
A suggestion is not a sequence.
His leaving, two years on, is not this year.

At a press conference in October 1988
He said the problems were already with us,
Independent of the program, and would be associated with it,
And that real money should be devoted to discussing them.
The shares that later accounts print do not agree.
This chapter does not choose one.
The plan of April asks that the ethical questions,
The legal, and the social, be identified,
And that initial options be drafted.
An identified question is not an answer.
A heading is not a safeguard accomplished.
Do not say the project redeemed a misuse of inheritance.
Do not say the project was that misuse.
A reading has been used to decide who should live.
This plan does not make that decision, and does not prevent it.
The prevention would be a work,
And the work is not done by being written down.

The human genome, as the plan says it, is not a child's name.
A book of a species would not be one house's pedigree.
The marks on the map are how a geneticist follows a piece
A child was given.
The copy an earlier page called possible
Is what lets the letters travel.
The first of October did not read them.

Hold the plan of April,
The clock the program dates to the first of October,
The map asked for before the book,
And the questions left open under their heading.
Refuse the consolation that a plan is a sequence,
And the consolation that a sequence would be a soul, or a verdict.
Count ten million letters as the five years' aim.
Count the whole book as a term the plan named,
And this morning did not reach.

Stand.
