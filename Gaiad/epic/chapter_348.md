# Chapter 348: The Numbered Thing

No holiday is named for this day.

In 2012 {{c|Emma Leonhart}} turned sixteen.
Two things were made this year that would later be the ground she worked on.
One taught machines to see, from pictures labelled by people paid by the piece.
The other gave every thing in the world a number,
So that a machine could walk the lines of descent
Back past where any family remembers.
Thirteen years later she would make some six hundred thousand edits in that second one in a single year,
And trace her own line through it, and through it this book's.
She would also, by then, count it among the things she hates,
For the same reason she hates the encyclopedia beside it:
The unpaid labor, and the apparatus that makes people glad to give it.
Both are true of her at once, and this book does not choose between them.
At sixteen she knew none of this.
The world was ending, some said, in December, by a calendar of the {{x|Maya}}.
It did not. Calendars do not end worlds.
They begin counts.

## The seeing

The set called {{x|ImageNet}} had been gathered under {{c|Fei-Fei Li}},
Millions of pictures labelled by people paid by the task
Through a market of small jobs on the net.
The label is not the picture.
The person who typed the label is not named in the set.
In 2012 its challenge asked a machine to name a thousand kinds of thing.
In {{p|Toronto}}, {{c|Alex Krizhevsky}}, {{c|Ilya Sutskever}}, and {{c|Geoffrey Hinton}}
Entered a deep network of some sixty million weights,
Trained over days on two graphics cards made for games.
Its error was about fifteen in a hundred.
The entry in second place erred about twenty-six in a hundred.
The gap was not a rounding.
It was the end of a way of building.
The method had been called a dead end for years.
Being called a dead end is not being one.
The results were shown in {{p|Florence}} in October,
And the paper given in December by {{p|Lake Tahoe}}.
Within a few years nearly every entry was a network like it,
And the machines of the next decade, the ones that would later write with her,
Came down from that gap.
That summer a team at {{x|Google}} let a large network watch frames of video without labels,
And a unit in it came to answer to the faces of cats.
Answering to cats is not knowing what a cat is.
Sight came from games and from piecework,
From cards built for play and labels typed for cents.

## The numbers

On Monday the twenty-ninth of October a new site opened,
Kept from {{p|Berlin}} by the German chapter of the movement that keeps the free encyclopedia.
It was called {{x|Wikidata}}.
{{c|Denny Vrandečić}} and {{c|Markus Krötzsch}} had argued for it for years.
Each thing it holds is given a number with a letter.
The universe is given the first.
A writer of jokes about the universe is given forty-two.
The number is not the thing.
A number does not change when the name changes, and that is the reason for it.
At first it kept only the links between the pages of the same subject in different languages.
Then the statements came.
A person is an instance of human. A person has a father, and the father is another number.
A father in the data is not a father at the table.
The data was given away with no rights kept back,
And the machines of the largest companies read it without asking.
A free gift to everyone is also a gift to the largest.
The lines of descent were written in it,
And a machine could follow a line back past where any one family remembers.
Following a line is not the line being right.
A loop in the line, where a man is his own ancestor,
Is the data telling on itself.
This book's genealogy is built partly on those numbers.
It owes them, and it argues with them, and it is not them.

## The encyclopedia goes dark

On the eighteenth of January the {{x|English Wikipedia}} turned its pages black for a day,
Against two laws in the congress of the {{p|United States}} that would have let pages be blocked for piracy,
And the laws were dropped.
It was the movement's greatest show of its power,
And it showed that its volunteers' labor was also a lever its leaders could pull.
On the thirteenth of March the {{x|Encyclopædia Britannica}} said it would no longer print,
After two hundred and forty-four years.
The book with degrees had lost to the book of five or ten minutes.

## The particle and the rover

On the fourth of July, at {{x|CERN}}, under the border of {{p|France}} and {{p|Switzerland}},
The physicists announced a particle that fit the one {{c|Peter Higgs}} had predicted
Forty-eight years before,
The one that gives the others their mass.
He wept in the hall.
On the sixth of August the rover {{x|Curiosity}}
Was lowered onto {{p|Mars}} by a crane hanging from a rocket,
In seven minutes no one on {{p|Earth}} could control.
On the fourteenth of October {{c|Felix Baumgartner}} jumped from a balloon
Thirty-nine kilometers up, and fell faster than sound,
And eight million people watched it live.

## The killings

On the twenty-sixth of February, in {{p|Sanford}}, in {{p|Florida}},
A boy of seventeen, {{c|Trayvon Martin}}, walking home with candy and a drink,
Was followed and shot by a neighborhood watchman,
Who was not charged for six weeks.
On the twentieth of July, in {{p|Aurora}}, a man shot into a crowded cinema at midnight and killed twelve.
On the ninth of October, in the valley of {{p|Swat}}, in {{p|Pakistan}},
A gunman boarded a school bus and asked which one was {{c|Malala Yousafzai}},
Fifteen, who had written that girls should go to school,
And shot her in the head.
She lived, and two years later took the {{x|Nobel Peace Prize}}.
On the fourteenth of December, in {{p|Newtown}}, in {{p|Connecticut}},
A young man shot his way into the {{x|Sandy Hook}} elementary school,
And killed twenty children of six and seven, and six of the adults who taught them.
The country's laws did not change.

## The returns

On the seventh of May {{c|Vladimir Putin}} became president of {{p|Russia}} again,
After the protests of the winter in the snow of {{p|Moscow}} against the stolen vote.
In February three women of a punk group, {{x|Pussy Riot}},
Sang a prayer against him in the cathedral of {{p|Moscow}}, for less than a minute,
And in August two of them were sent to prison camps for two years.
On the fifteenth of November {{c|Xi Jinping}} became the head of the party of {{p|China}},
And began to gather in his own hands what had been shared.
On the sixth of November {{c|Barack Obama}} was elected again.
In June {{p|Egypt}} elected {{c|Mohamed Morsi}}, of the {{x|Brotherhood}}, its first free president.
He would last a year.
On the eleventh of September, in {{p|Benghazi}}, the American ambassador, {{c|Christopher Stevens}}, was killed.

## The money and the storm

On the twenty-sixth of July the head of the bank of the euro, {{c|Mario Draghi}},
Said in {{p|London}} that he would do whatever it takes to save the coin,
And believe me, it will be enough,
And the markets believed him, and the coin was saved by a sentence.
On the twenty-ninth of October the storm called {{x|Sandy}} drove the sea into {{p|New York}},
Flooded the tunnels of the subway, and darkened {{p|Manhattan}} below the forties.
In the spring the students of {{p|Quebec}} struck against a rise in their fees,
And marched every night banging pots, for months, and the government fell in the autumn.

## The rest of the year

On the thirteenth of January the cruise ship {{x|Costa Concordia}}
Ran onto the rocks off {{p|Giglio}}, and its captain left before his passengers.
On the ninth of April the book of faces bought the photographs company for a billion dollars,
And on the eighteenth of May sold its own stock to the public.
In March a film about an African warlord, {{x|Kony 2012}}, was watched a hundred million times in a week,
And changed nothing.
In the summer the games were held in {{p|London}}.
In the summer a song by a singer from {{p|Seoul}}, {{x|Gangnam Style}},
Became the first video seen a billion times.
In September an application for strangers to choose each other by swiping, {{x|Tinder}}, opened.
On the twenty-first of December the calendar of the {{x|Maya}} turned a great cycle,
And the world did not end.

## The year held

A girl of sixteen, and two foundations laid for her life's later work without her.
A machine that learned to see from piecework, a site that numbered every thing.
The encyclopedia gone dark for a day, the old encyclopedia gone from print forever.
A particle, a rover, a man falling faster than sound.
A boy with candy, a cinema, a girl on a bus, twenty children in {{p|Newtown}}.
{{c|Putin}} back, {{c|Xi}} rising, a prayer in a cathedral punished.
A sentence that saved a coin, a storm in the subway, pots banged in {{p|Montreal}},
And a calendar that turned without ending anything.

Refuse the consolation that the number is the thing,
And that the line in the graph is the blood.
Refuse the consolation that a calendar ends a world.
A calendar begins a count. This book is one.
