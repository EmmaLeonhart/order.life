# Chapter 194: The Islamic Golden Age

{{c|2868|Abbasids}} cast the {{c|2869|Umayyads}} down,
In seven-fifty; and {{c|2870|al-Mansur}} made
In seven-sixty-two a round new town,
{{p|Baghdad}}, four gates; and there the learning stayed

For centuries: the {{c|2871|House of Wisdom}}, where
The {{x|Greek}}, {{x|Persian}}, {{x|Indian}} books were turned
{{x|Arabic}}: {{c|1081|Aristotle}}, {{c|2376|Plato}}, there
With {{c|2872|Galen}}, {{c|2408|Euclid}}, {{c|2265|Ptolemy}}, were learned,

{{c|2873|Hippocrates}}; and kept, when they were lost
Elsewhere. From {{p|India}} the numbers came,
The zero, and the decimal, and, crossed
With {{x|Greek}}, {{c|2874|al-Khwarizmi}}'s, whose own name

Is algorithm; and his book, the one
Of {{x|al-jabr}}, is algebra, made whole.
{{c|2875|Al-Razi}} told the pox and measles, done
Apart, and tested all his cures; the scroll

Of the {{x|Comprehensive Book}} he then wrote;
{{c|2877|Ibn Sina}} wrote the {{x|Canon}}; he joined
{{c|1081|Aristotle}} to the {{x|Platonists}}' note;
And {{c|2879|Ibn al-Haytham}}'s {{x|Optics}} then coined

The test by trial, the way of finding out;
{{c|2881|Al-Biruni}} measured the earth, its round,
And studied {{p|India}}, and wrote about
All things, a hundred forty books, profound.

{{c|2882|Al-Ghazali}}: the thinkers lost their way
In incoherence; {{c|2883|Ibn Rushd}} then wrote
The {{x|Incoherence}} of it, back, to say
The thinkers' cause was sound, and kept afloat.

{{c|2885|Al-Farabi}}, the {{c|2886|Second Teacher}}, next
{{c|1081|Aristotle}}; {{c|2887|Ibn Arabi}} joined
The {{x|Sufi}} way to thought; and {{c|2888|Rumi}}'s text,
The {{x|Masnavi}}, at {{p|Konya}}, there was coined,

The greatest of the mystic songs; and spread
The {{x|Sufis}} from {{p|Morocco}} to the lands
Of {{p|India}}, a sea; and, by their head,
The way went on, from teacher's hands to hands.

{{c|2889|Al-Hallaj}} cried {{x|Ana al-Haqq}}; and who
Could speak for {{c|472|God}}? The jurists, and the court
Of {{p|Baghdad}}, held the word; nine-twenty-two:
That was the fight; and so they cut him short.

{{c|2869|Umayyads}} who lived went on in {{p|Spain}},
And {{c|2890|Córdoba}} was theirs, a caliph's seat,
Half a million, books, baths, a lighted lane,
All paved, when {{p|Paris}} was a muddy street.

Nine-oh-nine: the {{x|Shia}} {{c|2891|Fatimids}} rose
In {{p|Egypt}}, rival to {{c|2892|Abbasid}} reign,
And built {{p|Cairo}} and {{c|2893|al-Azhar}}; and those
In {{p|Mazandaran}}, by {{p|Caspian}}'s main,

The {{c|2894|Bavandids}}, held {{p|Tabaristan}}'s height,
Woods; kin to {{c|2895|Sasanian}} kings, the last
Old house of {{p|Iran}} not yet won by fight;
{{c|2896|Qarin}} the First, eight-seventeen, held fast;

When {{c|2897|Mazyar}} rose against the caliph's hand,
{{c|2896|Qarin}} stood with {{p|Baghdad}}; eight-forty-two,
He took {{x|Islam}} himself, and kept his land
Of mountains; so the old kings' blood went through

Into new faith: {{c|2898|Abul Melik}}, his son;
And in eight-sixty-nine, a grandson came,
{{c|2899|Abul Hamza}}, in {{p|Armenia}}, one
Of the {{c|2900|Artsruni}}; west, under their name.

The {{c|2901|Seljuk Turks}} came; caliphs kept the name;
Sultans ruled; at {{c|2902|Manzikert}} they beat,
Ten-seventy-one, {{c|2838|Byzantines}}, and came
To {{p|Anatolia}}; and in each street,

{{x|Hanafi}}, they built their schools; learning went
On, till twelve-fifty-eight, the {{c|2904|Mongols}} came,
Took {{p|Baghdad}}, and the {{c|2868|Abbasids}} were spent;
The books thrown in the {{p|Tigris}}; it became

Black with their ink. So honor that bright age,
Four centuries: the numbers, and the cure,
The optics, and the thought, the {{x|Sufi}} page,
Through {{p|Córdoba}}, {{p|Toledo}}, and kept sure.
