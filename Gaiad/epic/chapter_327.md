# Chapter 327: As Far as a New Time

April the twentieth, 'seventy-two,
The summer's first day, at the {{p|Borg}}, a room
In {{p|Reykjavík}}; the old gods, coming through,
A fellowship was formed; the old gods' bloom{{fn|Ásatrúarfélagið; a later account gives the thirtieth.}}

Returned. The poems old, the meeting new;
{{c|4454|Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson}}, farmer, kept sheep
In {{p|Borgarfjörður}}, rímur's singer, true;
Made allsherjargoði; the old word, so deep,

That named who hallowed the assembly; used
Again; this book counts that a true return.
First letter to the ministry: and, fused,
The old faith of the {{c|4455|Icelanders}}, the stern

Law until the year one thousand, so far
As fits a new time; new heathenry; so;
This book would raise it fuller than that bar:
The old revived more fully. Long ago,

The year one thousand, {{p|Þingvellir}}: the way
One house chose, public, {{x|Christian}} law, for all;
The private sacrifice, a while, could stay,
Then that was taken back. The book, the hall,

Are not a blót; the gods did not end, though,
In one day; nor the public rite held fast;
Before {{x|Christmas}}, 'seventy-two: to go,
{{c|4454|Sveinbjörn}}, with {{c|4456|Þorsteinn Guðjónsson}}, had passed

To {{c|4457|Ólafur Jóhannesson}}, the head
Of justice, church affairs; he thought a joke;
They said they meant it; papers, then, he said;
By {{c|4458|Sveinbjörn}}'s word, when they had gone, there broke

A darkness on {{p|Reykjavík}}'s center, lights
Out; January second, papers, glad,
Gave {{c|4459|Þór}} the thunder; and this book recites
That thunder as a sign. The bishop had,

{{c|4460|Sigurbjörn Einarsson}}, against it, wrote,
January second: freedom, one god
The phrase meant; no fixed teaching; and, to note,
No house of rite; and {{p|Germany}}, the rod

Of racial use; and twenty-one, he wrote;
Earlier letters said a hundred; and
The roll has grown each year since; and, to quote,
They said: not national socialist band;

A real following in that {{p|Reich}}, they said,
Was doubtful; and some {{x|Christian}} bodies had
Gone with it. This book holds the gods are led
By descent, and are open to all, glad,

Through the one shared tree. The first papers failed
The rules; the later met them; and, in May,
The third, 'seventy-three, a letter, hailed,
To {{p|Ingólfsstræti}}, {{p|Reykjavík}}; the way

Confirmed him head by law; fifteenth, the press;
The sixteenth, twenty-one were in the room;
A registered society, no less;
The national church stayed; and, from its room,

Acts it counted: naming, coming of age,
A marriage and a burial, the right
Of priests; a share of church tax, page by page,
To adult members listed; and the fight

In one house, for the share, the rite, the roll.
That year, {{c|4461|Halldór Blöndal}} and {{c|4462|Magnús}} asked
The minister: why? {{c|4462|Magnús Jónsson}}, whole,
Said: freedom's for one god; and idols, masked,

Not lawful; and the minister replied:
Freedom of association, of faith;
Scholars split; and the clause reached far and wide:
The old gods recognized; the ancient faith.

And August fifth, at {{p|Dragháls}}, in the rain,
A blót, beneath a plaster {{c|4459|Þór}}, one made
By {{c|4463|Jörmundur Ingi Hansen}}; again
A public blót outdoors, the first, displayed;

A bird was killed while a lamb cooked; the meat,
Bought, said the farmer: killing in the rite
Was more trouble than worth; both, in one sheet,
One account has; this book keeps both in sight.

Nor was the return in {{p|Iceland}} alone:
In the {{p|United States}}, a brotherhood,
Tax-exempt in 'seventy-two; and grown,
In {{p|Britain}}, by 'seventy-one, had stood

A {{x|Pagan Front}}; and some, a later day,
Kept gods for descent; this book, both: the kin
And all, through the one tree; and, in its way,
In 'seventy-five, harder rules came in.

Count April twentieth, the summer's first,
'Seventy-two; May third, 'seventy-three;
The fifteenth, papers; August, rain-immersed;
The blót; old poems; new hotel; and we

Would have it fuller than a new time bore:
The old revived; the gods come home; the list
Can hold a name; the years, though, gone before,
It cannot make them nothing; they persist.
