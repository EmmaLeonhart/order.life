# Chapter 344: Instead of Trust

No holiday is named for this day.

In 2008 {{c|Emma Leonhart}} turned twelve, and her family moved house again.
In her words, she moved houses in 2002, in 2008, and in 2014.
This was the second.
She has named one thing above all in this year:
That {{p|Russia}} invaded {{p|Georgia}}.
Of everything in 2008 that is what she chose,
And it was the small war that told the large one was coming.
The rest of the world was watching its money burn.
A girl of twelve was carrying boxes to a new room
In the year the banks were carried out of the market on stretchers.

## The war in August

On the night of the seventh of August,
While the world watched the opening of the games in {{p|Beijing}},
The army of {{p|Georgia}} shelled {{p|Tskhinvali}},
The capital of {{p|South Ossetia}}, a province that had broken away from it
And was guarded by Russian soldiers called peacekeepers.
The Russian army was already at the border.
It crossed in force, with tanks through the {{x|Roki Tunnel}},
Drove the Georgians out of {{p|South Ossetia}} and out of {{p|Abkhazia}},
Took {{p|Gori}}, the birthplace of {{c|Stalin}},
And stopped a short drive from {{p|Tbilisi}}.
In five days it was over.
Hundreds died, and tens of thousands of Georgians were driven from their villages,
Which were burned behind them.
A report for the {{x|European Union}} later said
That {{p|Georgia}} had fired first, and that {{p|Russia}}'s answer went far beyond an answer.
{{p|Russia}} recognized the two provinces as countries.
Almost no one else did.
The West protested, and in a few months went back to business.
Six years later the same army would take {{p|Crimea}} the same way,
With soldiers who were said not to be there,
And in fourteen years it would drive on {{p|Kyiv}}.
This book draws that line from here,
from the year she named.

## The games

On the eighth of August, at eight in the evening, in {{p|Beijing}},
The games opened with two thousand and eight drummers,
And fireworks that were partly drawn by computer for the cameras,
And a girl who sang with another girl's voice, because the first was judged not pretty enough.
In March {{p|Tibet}} had risen, and been put down.
The torch of the games had been fought over in {{p|London}}, {{p|Paris}}, {{p|San Francisco}}.
{{p|China}} showed the world what it had become,
And the world, broke, applauded.

## The earthquake and the cyclone

On the twelfth of May the earth broke under {{p|Sichuan}}.
Some sixty-nine thousand people died, and eighteen thousand more were never found.
Thousands of them were children, under schools that fell
While the government buildings beside them stood.
The parents who asked why were arrested.
On the second of May, in {{p|Myanmar}}, the cyclone {{x|Nargis}}
Drowned the delta of the {{x|Irrawaddy}}.
More than a hundred and thirty thousand died.
The generals kept the foreign help out for weeks,
And held a vote on their constitution in the ruins.

## The fall of the banks

In March the bank {{x|Bear Stearns}} was sold for almost nothing, with the government's help.
On the seventh of September the government took over
{{x|Fannie Mae}} and {{x|Freddie Mac}}, which held or guaranteed half the mortgages in the country.
On Monday the fifteenth, {{x|Lehman Brothers}}, a bank a hundred and fifty-eight years old,
Filed the largest bankruptcy in history,
Because no one would save it, and the government chose not to.
On the sixteenth the government lent eighty-five billion dollars to {{x|AIG}},
Which had insured the bets of everyone against the failure of everyone.
The markets of the world fell for weeks.
On the third of October {{c|George W. Bush}} signed a law
For seven hundred billion dollars to buy the banks' bad assets,
After the congress had voted it down once and watched the market drop.
The banks were saved. The houses were not.
Millions lost their homes in the years after,
And the men who ran the banks kept their bonuses,
And almost none of them went to prison.
The poor had been sold the debts. The rich were sold the rescue.

## Instead of trust

On the thirty-first of October, a Friday,
A note went to a list of people who studied cryptography.
The name on the note was {{c|Satoshi Nakamoto}}.
It linked to a paper of nine pages,
{{x|Bitcoin}}: {{x|A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System}}.
It proposed money that needed no bank and no state,
Kept honest by a public ledger that everyone held
And no one could rewrite without more computing than all the rest.
A system based on proof instead of trust, the paper said.
Who wrote it has never been settled.
This chapter does not name a person behind the name.
The first block came in the next January, and is the next chapter.
The answer to a broken trust was a machine that did not need any,
And in a few years the machine became the most speculated asset on earth,
And the means of the largest frauds of the century.
A later year of this month will meet one of its villains.

## The election

On the fourth of November {{c|Barack Obama}} was elected president,
The first black president of the {{p|United States}},
And people wept in {{p|Grant Park}} in {{p|Chicago}}, and in streets around the world.
On the same day the voters of {{p|California}} passed {{x|Proposition 8}},
Taking away the marriages of same-sex couples
That the state's court had allowed in the spring.
Eighteen thousand couples had married between.
The same night lifted one door and closed another.

## The attack on Mumbai

On the evening of the twenty-sixth of November
Ten men came ashore in {{p|Mumbai}} in rubber boats from {{p|Pakistan}},
And for three days attacked the train station, a café, a hospital,
A Jewish center, and two great hotels, the {{x|Taj}} and the {{x|Oberoi}}.
One hundred and sixty-six people were killed.
The world watched the dome of the {{x|Taj}} burn.

## The apology

On the eleventh of June, in {{p|Ottawa}},
The prime minister of {{p|Canada}}, {{c|Stephen Harper}}, apologized in the parliament
For the residential schools,
Where for more than a century the children of the First Nations
Had been taken from their parents to have their languages and their peoples taken out of them,
And where thousands died.
The survivors sat in the gallery.
An apology is not a return.
It was the first time the state said it.

## The machines

In April a site for keeping code, {{x|GitHub}}, opened,
Where the code of the next twenty years would be kept, this book's included.
On the tenth of July the phone of the year before got a store of programs,
And the phone became whatever anyone could write for it.
On the second of September {{x|Google}} released a browser, {{x|Chrome}}.
In August a site for renting spare rooms, later called {{x|Airbnb}}, opened,
And the spare rooms of cities would become hotels without the name.
In October a service for streaming music, {{x|Spotify}}, opened in {{p|Sweden}}.
On the tenth of September, under the border of {{p|France}} and {{p|Switzerland}},
The {{x|Large Hadron Collider}} sent its first beams around its ring,
And the world did not end, as some had feared it would.

## The rest of the year

On the seventeenth of February {{p|Kosovo}} declared itself independent of {{p|Serbia}},
And {{p|Russia}} pointed to it in August when it carved up {{p|Georgia}}.
On the nineteenth of February {{c|Fidel Castro}} gave up the presidency of {{p|Cuba}} to his brother.
In the autumn it was found that milk powder in {{p|China}}
Had been laced with {{x|melamine}} to cheat the tests,
And hundreds of thousands of babies were sickened, and six died.
On the twenty-seventh of December {{p|Israel}} began a war on {{p|Gaza}},
Which ran into the next year.

## The year held

A second house for a girl of twelve,
And a war in {{p|Georgia}} that she would later see as the first move of a longer war.
Drummers in {{p|Beijing}}, schools fallen in {{p|Sichuan}}, a delta drowned,
The banks failed and were saved and the houses were not,
A paper about money without trust, a president who made people weep,
A marriage taken back the same night, {{p|Mumbai}} burning,
An apology in {{p|Ottawa}}, a store of programs, a collider that did not end the world.

Refuse the consolation that a short war is a small war.
It was the shape of the long one.
Refuse the consolation that the rescue of the banks was a rescue of the people.
