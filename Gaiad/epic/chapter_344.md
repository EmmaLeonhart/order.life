# Chapter 344: Instead of Trust

The eighth day of Yuletide is the feast of the year two thousand and eight,
And the feast of the children of the residential schools, who did not come home from the gate.
Light eight lights, and say the names of the children of the First Nations
Who died in the schools, and keep them among the ancestors through all the generations.

In two thousand and eight I turned twelve, and my family moved house again,
The second of my three moves, and I carried boxes to a new room.
Of everything in that year I name one thing above all:
{{p|Russia}} invaded {{p|Georgia}}. The small war told of the large one to come.

## The war in August

All summer the villain prepared it. His army held the {{x|Kavkaz}} exercises at the border,
His railway troops fixed the line into {{p|Abkhazia}} in the spring,
His separatists in {{p|South Ossetia}} shelled the Georgian villages,
And his passports were handed out to the Ossetians, so he could say he was protecting his kin.

On the night of the seventh of August, while the world watched {{p|Beijing}},
{{p|Georgia}} answered the shelling and moved on {{p|Tskhinvali}}.
The Russian army was already at the tunnel. It came through the {{x|Roki Tunnel}} with tanks,
And drove the Georgians out of {{p|South Ossetia}} and {{p|Abkhazia}}, in the valley.

It took {{p|Gori}}, the birthplace of {{c|Stalin}}, and bombed it,
And stopped a short drive from {{p|Tbilisi}}, and the Georgian villages burned behind.
Tens of thousands of Georgians were driven from their homes, and never went back.
{{c|Putin}} recognized his two provinces as countries. Almost no one else was so inclined.

The West protested, and in a few months went back to business, and reset the relation.
That was the lesson the villain learned: you can take a piece of a country and keep it.
Six years later he would take {{p|Crimea}} the same way, with soldiers said not to be there,
And in fourteen years he would drive on {{p|Kyiv}}. This book draws the line from here, and does not hide it.

## The games

On the eighth of August, at eight in the evening, in {{p|Beijing}},
The games opened with two thousand and eight drummers and the fireworks in the air,
Some of them drawn by computer for the cameras,
And a girl who sang with another girl's voice, the first judged not pretty enough to be there.

In March {{p|Tibet}} had risen, and been put down.
The torch had been fought over in {{p|London}}, {{p|Paris}}, {{p|San Francisco}}'s street.
{{p|China}} showed the world what it had become,
And {{c|Michael Phelps}} won eight golds in the pool, the most ever in one meet.

## The earthquake and the cyclone

On the twelfth of May the earth broke under {{p|Sichuan}}.
Sixty-nine thousand died, and eighteen thousand more were never found,
Thousands of them children, under schools that fell
While the government buildings beside them stood on the same ground.

The parents who asked why were arrested.
{{c|Ai Weiwei}} gathered the names of the dead children, and put them on the wall,
And they beat him for it. The names are kept anyway.
This book keeps them too: the children of the tofu schools, one and all.

On the second of May, in {{p|Myanmar}}, the cyclone {{x|Nargis}} drowned the {{x|Irrawaddy}} delta,
And a hundred and thirty thousand died, and the generals kept the foreign help away,
And the dead lay in the rice fields while they counted the ballots for their constitution.
The tyrant's answer to a flood: a vote in the ruins, and the aid kept at bay.

## The fall of the banks

In March {{x|Bear Stearns}} was sold for almost nothing, with the government's help.
On the seventh of September the government took over {{x|Fannie Mae}} and {{x|Freddie Mac}}.
On Monday the fifteenth {{x|Lehman Brothers}}, a hundred and fifty-eight years old,
Filed the largest bankruptcy in history, and no one would bring it back.

On the sixteenth the government lent eighty-five billion to {{x|AIG}},
Which had insured the bets of everyone against the failure of everyone.
On the third of October {{c|George W. Bush}} signed the seven hundred billion for the banks,
And the banks were saved, and the houses were not, and the bailout was done.

Millions lost their homes in the years after,
And the men who ran the banks kept their bonuses, and almost none went to jail.
The poor had been sold the debts. The rich were sold the rescue.
That is the year's injustice, and this book will not let it pale.

## Instead of trust

On Friday the thirty-first of October a note went to a list of cryptographers,
Signed {{c|Satoshi Nakamoto}}, with a paper of nine pages linked below:
{{x|Bitcoin}}: {{x|A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System}},
Money with no bank and no state, kept honest by a ledger that everyone could know.

"A system based on cryptographic proof instead of trust," the paper said.
The answer to the broken trust of the banks was a machine that needed none.
The year the banks were saved with the people's money,
{{c|Satoshi}} wrote the money the bankers could not run.

It became the money of the dissidents whose banks were frozen,
Of the Venezuelans fleeing inflation, of the Ukrainians raising money for the front,
Of the unbanked billions with only a phone.
It had its frauds, as every money has had. It had its heroes, and its stunt.

## The election

On the fourth of November {{c|Barack Obama}} was elected president,
The first black president of the {{p|United States}}, and people wept in {{p|Grant Park}},
And in streets around the world. Yes we can, they said,
And the country that had held slaves elected a black man to lead it out of the dark.

The same day {{p|California}} passed {{x|Proposition 8}},
Taking away the marriages the state's court had allowed in the spring.
Eighteen thousand couples had married between.
The same night lifted one door and closed another, and the courts would undo the closing.

## Mumbai

On the evening of the twenty-sixth of November ten men came ashore in {{p|Mumbai}}
In rubber boats from {{p|Pakistan}}, and for three days they attacked
The train station, a café, a hospital, the {{x|Chabad House}}, the {{x|Taj}}, the {{x|Oberoi}}.
A hundred and sixty-six were killed, and the dome of the {{x|Taj}} burned, and the city fought back.

## The apology

On the eleventh of June, in {{p|Ottawa}}, the prime minister of {{p|Canada}},
{{c|Stephen Harper}}, apologized in the parliament for the residential schools,
Where for more than a century the children of the First Nations
Were taken from their parents, and their languages beaten out of them by the rules.

Thousands died in the schools. The survivors sat in the gallery.
The country I grew up in said it, for the first time, in its parliament's hall.
An apology is not a return. It is the beginning of the remembering.
This is their feast day: the children who did not come home at all.

## The machines

In April {{x|GitHub}} opened, where the code of the next twenty years would be kept,
This book's included, and mine. On the tenth of July the {{x|App Store}} opened its door,
And the phone became whatever anyone could write for it.
On the second of September {{x|Google}} released {{x|Chrome}}, and the browser war had a new core.

In August {{x|Airbnb}} opened, renting the spare rooms of the world.
In October {{x|Spotify}} opened in {{p|Sweden}}, all the music for a fee.
On the tenth of September the {{x|Large Hadron Collider}} sent its first beams around the ring,
And the world did not end, and four years later it found the {{x|Higgs}}, as you will see.

## The rest of the year

On the seventeenth of February {{p|Kosovo}} declared itself free of {{p|Serbia}},
And {{c|Putin}} pointed to it in August as his excuse for carving {{p|Georgia}}'s land.
On the nineteenth of February {{c|Fidel Castro}} gave {{p|Cuba}} to his brother.
In the autumn the milk powder in {{p|China}} was found laced with {{x|melamine}} by a cheating hand,

And three hundred thousand babies were sickened, and six of them were dead.
On the twenty-seventh of December {{p|Israel}} went to war on {{x|Hamas}} in {{p|Gaza}},
After years of rockets on its towns,
And the war ran on into the new year ahead.

## The year held

A second house for a girl of twelve, and a war in {{p|Georgia}},
The first move of the villain's long war, which I would come to see.
Drummers in {{p|Beijing}}, schools fallen in {{p|Sichuan}}, a delta drowned,
The banks saved and the houses not, and a paper about money that would set people free.

A president who made the world weep, a marriage taken back the same night,
{{p|Mumbai}} burning, an apology in {{p|Ottawa}} for the children who did not come home,
A store of programs, and a collider that did not end the world.
I was twelve, and the small war in the {{p|Caucasus}} was the shape of the long one to come.
