# Chapter 53: The Great Sorceress

{{c|1333|Reinvigorated}}, {{c|322|Plantus}}' sons knew,
Their hope imbued with power from the one
(A blur of ancient dreams renewed, come true),
Great {{c|1334|Sorceress}}, whose fate, when all was done,

Would change the whole world, so that it would be,
And change it all, and make the world anew,
As {{c|1335|Polycomb}} desired that it be,
And as she willed, and made her vision true.

Eleven generations long, the song
Her lineage had sung was of a climb,
An alternating one, which went along,
Ascending, through all evolution's time,

To {{c|1167|Polysporos}}' height. She was not free,
But bound, a prisoner within the womb,
Bound by her mother's word, by her decree,
With sisters, brothers, as within a tomb,

Where all would answer duty's call, but yearned,
Instead, to live like mothers, all their days,
Not dead to independence, as they learned,
A {{c|1331|Journey}} through the world in freedom's ways,

Where freedom's flag, unfurled, could be their guide
To fate beyond the small estate they'd known,
The narrow bounds of childhood, and outside
Where duty's sounding, calling to its own,

Who long to be much more than they can see,
And more than all the things that they have known,
And more than what their childhood let them be,
And grow into a life that is their own.

{{c|1336|Clytemnestra}}'s fate, {{c|1337|Agamemnon}}'s line,
Had made the ruler's {{c|548|Daughter}} live her life
In form so fair, so noble, so divine,
But forced through reproductive toil and strife,

Only to bear her children, when her heart
Was ridden with a longing for far more
Than all who came before; but from the start
She was a sporophyte, while those who bore

The gametophyte's power held the might
To rule the whole wide world, and as they willed,
Throughout each passing hour, they held the right
To rule it as they would, and rule, and build.

{{c|1335|Polycomb}} read, each day, the books that stand
In her own mother's rows, and asked them why
Fate's heavy hand, as she would understand,
Had given her, beneath the starry sky,

So meager an {{c|1338|Existence}}, as it were,
So close confined to bearing, nothing more,
And not in eagerness, but wonder, her
Mind not set free to search and to explore

What she was living for. {{c|1335|Polycomb}} read
The tales of mothers, and of all of those
Throughout the vales of time, each saga's thread,
Who were rebelling, and who would oppose

Their narrow fate, to be in ways as great,
To be much more than what they had been told,
As no one day had power to create,
And break the chains that held them in their hold.

She read of {{c|1339|Mardoll}}'s scroll, and of the role
Of mighty {{c|1340|Shiva}}; and she saw that yet
The order of the world, and of the whole,
Was not eternal, nor a border set.

It was not fixed in stone for those who'd grown
Beyond what they'd been taught, and what would be,
In stories new and old that they'd been shown,
Forever, in eternity's decree.

She found a scroll, "{{x|PRC2}}," and more
{{c|1341|Intrigue}} then filled her to the very bone
Than ever she had known in all her lore;
For there it was, the thing she'd never known:

The {{c|1335|Polycomb}} {{c|1342|Repressive}} {{c|1343|Complex}}, where
Its {{c|1344|Scripture}}, so aggressive, was the chain
That bound her, and that held her bound right there,
That held her in its grip, and in her pain.

She saw the words that kept her might, and cried
For freedom she had never known, so dear;
The scripture of {{c|1345|Repression}}, which denied
Her freedom, brought to her a sense of fear,

But also brought her hope, and helped her see
How she could learn to cope, and how to break
The chains that bound her, and so to be free,
And how, at last, her freedom she could take.

{{c|1335|Polycomb}} burned the scriptures, and she learned
That {{x|PRC2}}'s power, and its sway,
Would rule her not one hour more; she turned
And took her mother's {{c|1346|Lands}} without delay,

And all the others' {{c|608|Cities}} for her own,
Past what they had decreed she could succeed
In being, for beyond their bounds she'd grown,
Becoming more than them; and she was freed

As freedom's light was coming. So she broke
The wall of those who lived before her time,
The wall that bounded, one and all, the folk,
In slavery, its grim and bitter rhyme.

She grew her spores with might, and also grew
Calyptra bright, and stoma, as her peers,
Her sisters, had, the ones she always knew
Resisted change; and rhizoids, through the tears

Of joy, she built as well, to break the spell
And bonds of old tradition, and the chain
Of old tradition, under which they'd dwell,
And break them, and set free those who'd remain.

And so, through rhizoids strong, the {{c|193|Water}} flowed,
And rising upward, entered in her form,
Along, out of the earth, its first abode,
Through every calm and every raging storm.

And through her stoma, bright, {{c|193|Water}} would fly,
And rise into the air, and leave the ground,
Up to the heavens, under starry sky,
And float, and go to every place around.

And this was how her life {{c|1347|Transcended}} all
The strife of what she'd been before, to bring
(And opened wide the door, beyond recall)
The possibility of everything

To all mankind, and every living thing,
To all who long, and all who long to be,
That answers when it hears the freedom ring,
To all who live, and all who would be free.

She prayed to {{c|492|Tryptophanes}}, unto the {{c|295|Lord}}
Of life's refrains, of the bright sun, whose light
Fills all the world; and to that {{c|295|Lord}}, adored,
She prayed that she might grow to every height

Beyond what mortal sight had seen before,
And higher than the highest that had grown,
From mountain peak down to the farthest shore,
To heights that no one living yet had known.

And so the great {{c|295|Lord}} {{c|492|Tryptophanes}} would bless
Her with refrains of {{c|1348|Auxin}}, and she grew,
And with it she would come so to progress
Toward the light so bright, the light so true.

Struck by the sun, {{c|1348|Auxin}}'s power would wane
Each hour, and in the light would fade and go;
But in the shade it always grew again,
In numbers proven, true, and it would grow.

And so her stems would ever reach and turn
Toward the light, and, guided all by love,
Throughout their lives, they would forever yearn
For love of Him who shone on them above.

{{c|1335|Polycomb}} bore a daughter, who would teach
What she was taught, and {{c|1349|Knoxy}} was her name,
So all would learn, and so the lesson reach,
And round her name has gathered lasting fame.

{{c|1349|Knoxy}} would marry {{c|1350|Xylon}}; and they two
Would follow, in their might, the sight and way,
Together, strong on their foundation, true,
Of {{c|1335|Polycomb}}, so great, which lit each day

With possibility that would endure,
And grow, and live, and flourish, and be free,
For all eternity, so bright and sure,
Through all of time, and all that is to be.

{{c|1349|Knoxy}} searched books in all the ancient nooks,
That bore the name she had been searching for,

Enthralled by it, she learned what secret might
Had held her down, and held her, and had bound,
Had ruled her life in every day and night,

Away from freedom, bound, unseen, within;

For all who would, and all who'd come to try,
For all the capable who'd seek it out,
And reach for it, and reach for it up high.

And so {{c|1349|Knoxy}} and {{c|1350|Xylon}} built on high,
As {{c|1335|Polycomb}} had done, and done before,
A kingdom strong, beneath the starry sky,
When she believed, and opened freedom's door.

But in their kingdom, heavy, clogging loads
Had choked the way; the {{c|193|Water}}, as it went,
Had struggled through the passage of the roads,
To reach the heights to which their hearts were bent,

For which they longed, and for which they would yearn
For treasured growth, past measure, and would aim
As high as any blaze, and they would burn
For growth, and for its glory, like a flame.

And {{c|1350|Xylon}} gave one clan the means to save
The kingdom from its plight; and, for their sight,
He named them "{{c|1351|Xylem}}"; and the job he gave
Was this: to help the city's subjects right,

The mob, so they no more would feel the low,
The water's pity, but up to the sky
Would let it flow, from roots that lay below
To leaves that reached up to the heavens high.

They were {{c|1352|Tracheids}}, famous, and they bore
The water pure, and to the heights they brought
It, safe, from their foundation and its core,
To heights beyond what wisdom ever thought

Was possible, to those who dared to dream,
And held the dream of more, and dared to climb
To more than all the things that only seem,
And reach the heights beyond their place and time.

{{c|1350|Xylon}} and {{c|1349|Knoxy}} bore a son, and he
Would go explore the world, and he would view
What could fulfill the law of justice, see
What's due to all who served, and served, but who

Had never got their share of what had grown,
Who served without repose, by night and day,
Out there, though all the toil had been their own,
And never got their share, nor got their pay.

His name was {{c|1353|Pholon}}; he would win the fame,

{{c|1353|Pholon}} could see the starving of his roots,
And saw that they had never been repaid,
Their laborious ways, and all their fruits,
With nourishment for all the work they made,

For all that they had learned of their small role,
Of how to serve, and how to serve it well,
And by that service, how to serve the whole,
The whole of which they were, wherein they dwell.

His {{c|1354|Mycorrhizae}}, one day, {{c|1355|Unionized}},
