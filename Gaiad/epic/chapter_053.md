# Chapter 53: The Great Sorceress

{{c|1333|Reinvigorated}}, {{c|322|Plantus}}' sons knew
A blur of ancient dreams renewed, come true,
Their hope imbued with power from the one
Great {{c|1334|Sorceress}}, whose fate, when all was done,

Would change the whole world, so that it would be
As {{c|1335|Polycomb}} desired that it be.

Eleven generations long, the song
Her lineage had sung, and, all along,
Was of an alternating climb, which went
Through evolution's time, in its ascent,

To {{c|1167|Polysporos}}' height. She was not free,
Bound by her mother's word, by her decree,
That kept her in the womb, a prisoner there
As in a tomb, with sisters, brothers, where

All answered duty's call, but yearned instead
To live like mothers, never to be dead
To independence, strong throughout their days,
A {{c|1331|Journey}} through the world in freedom's ways,

Where freedom's flag, unfurled, could be their guide
To fate beyond the small estate, outside
The narrow bounds of childhood, where alone
Is duty sounding, calling to its own,

Who long to be much more than they can see,
And more than what their childhood let them be.

{{c|1336|Clytemnestra}}'s fate, {{c|1337|Agamemnon}}'s line,
Had made the ruler's {{c|548|Daughter}} so divine
In form; but she was forced to live her life
Through all its reproductive toil and strife,

Only to bear her children, when her heart
Was ridden with desire, from the start,
To be much more than all who came before;
For she was sporophyte, while those who bore

The gametophyte's power held the might
And held, throughout each passing hour, the right
To rule the whole wide world, which, as they willed,
Their hearts would have them rule it, and would build.

{{c|1335|Polycomb}} read, each day, the books that stand
In her own mother's rows, to understand
Why fate's own heavy hand had given her
So meager an {{c|1338|Existence}}, as it were;

Not eager, but in wonder, asking why
Beneath the starry sky her life should lie
So close confined to bearing, nothing more,
Her mind not free to search and to explore

What she was living for. {{c|1335|Polycomb}} read
The tales of mothers, and each saga's thread
Throughout the vales of time would tell of those
Who were rebelling, and who would oppose

Their narrow fate, to be in ways as great
As no one day had power to create.

She read of {{c|1339|Mardoll}}'s scroll, and of the role
Of mighty {{c|1340|Shiva}}; and she saw the whole
Of order was no border, ever set,
Eternal, as her mother said; and yet

It was not fixed in stone for those who'd grown
Beyond what they'd been taught, in stories shown
Both new and old, of what they had to be
Forever, in eternity's decree.

She found a scroll, "{{x|PRC2}}," and more
{{c|1341|Intrigue}} than she had ever known before
Then filled her to the very bone; for there
The {{c|1335|Polycomb}} {{c|1342|Repressive}} {{c|1343|Complex}}, where

Its {{c|1344|Scripture}}, so aggressive, was the chain
That bound her, holding her within her pain.

She saw the words that kept her might, and cried
For freedom never known, which was denied:
The scripture of her own {{c|1345|Repression}}, clear
In words that brought to her a sense of fear,

But also brought her hope, and helped her see
How she could learn to cope, and to be free.

{{c|1335|Polycomb}} burned the scriptures, and she learned
That {{x|PRC2}}'s power, and its sway,
Would rule her not one hour more; she turned
And took her mother's {{c|1346|Lands}} without delay,

And all the others' {{c|608|Cities}} for her own,
For now she had beyond their limits grown,
Past what they had decreed she could succeed
In being or becoming; she was freed

As freedom's light was coming. So she broke
The wall that bounded, one and all, the folk
Of those who'd lived before her time, in rhyme
Of slavery, the grim and bitter rhyme.

She grew her spores with might, and also grew
Calyptra bright, and stoma, as she knew
Her sisters had, the ones who had for years
Resisted change; and rhizoids, through the tears

Of joy, she built as well, to break the spell
Of old tradition, under which they'd dwell.

And so, through rhizoids strong, the {{c|193|Water}} flowed
Along, out of the earth, its first abode,
And rising upward, entered in her form
Through every calm and every raging storm.

And through her stoma, bright, {{c|193|Water}} would fly
Up to the heavens, under starry sky.

And this was how her life {{c|1347|Transcended}} all
The strife of what she'd been, beyond recall,
Before, and opened wide the door, to bring
The possibility of everything

To all mankind, and every living thing
That answers when it hears the freedom ring.

She prayed to {{c|492|Tryptophanes}}: to the {{c|295|Lord}}
Of life's refrains, and to the {{c|295|Lord}}, adored,
Of the bright sun, who fills the world with light;
And prayed that she might grow to every height

Beyond what mortal sight had seen before
From mountain peak down to the farthest shore.

And so the {{c|295|Lord}} {{c|492|Tryptophanes}} would bless
Her with refrains of {{c|1348|Auxin}}, to progress

Toward the light so true, and so she grew.
Struck by the sun, {{c|1348|Auxin}}'s power would wane
Each hour; but in the shade it grew again
In numbers that were proven, tried, and true.

And so her stems would ever reach and turn
Toward the light above, and ever yearn;
And they were guided, all their lives, by love,
The love of Him who shone on them above.

{{c|1335|Polycomb}} bore a daughter, who'd, in turn,
Teach what her mother taught, so all would learn;
And {{c|1349|Knoxy}} was the name of her, whose fame
Eternal has been gathered round her name.

{{c|1349|Knoxy}} would marry {{c|1350|Xylon}}; and they two,
Together, strong on their foundation, true,
Would follow, in their might, the sight and way
Of {{c|1335|Polycomb}}, so great, which lit each day

With possibility that would endure
For all eternity, so bright and sure.

{{c|1349|Knoxy}} searched books in all the ancient nooks,
And found a scroll there, hidden in the books,
That bore the name she had been searching for:
"{{x|KNOX2}}" was what that scroll was called. What's more,

Enthralled by it, she learned what secret might
Had ruled her life in every day and night.

The KNOTTED-like TALE gene had always been
The force, unseen, that kept her bound within,
Away from freedom, which she now could see
Her mother had achieved; for it was she

Who had believed that change could come about
For all the capable who'd seek it out.

And so {{c|1349|Knoxy}} and {{c|1350|Xylon}} built on high
A kingdom strong, beneath the starry sky,
As {{c|1335|Polycomb}} had opened once before,
When freedom was believed, achievement's door.

But in their kingdom, heavy, clogging loads
Had choked and blocked the passage of the roads;
The {{c|193|Water}} struggled, as it went, to reach
The heights to which their hearts would come to teach

Them they should aspire to, with desire
For treasured growth past measure, like a fire.

And {{c|1350|Xylon}} gave one clan the means to save
The kingdom from its plight; and, for their sight,
He named them "{{c|1351|Xylem}}"; and the job he gave
Was this: to help the city's subjects right,

The mob, so they no more would feel, nor know,
The water's pity, but would let it flow
From roots that lay below to leaves on high
That reached beneath the starry, open sky.

They were {{c|1352|Tracheids}}, famous, and they bore
The water pure, from their foundation's core
Secure, up to the heights beyond all thought
That wisdom ever, in its ages, brought

To those who dared to dream, to hold the dream
Of more than all the things that only seem.

{{c|1350|Xylon}} and {{c|1349|Knoxy}} bore a son, and he
Would go explore the world, with eyes to see
What could fulfill the law of justice, due
To all who served without repose, but who

Had never got their share of what had grown
Out there, though all the toil had been their own.

His name was {{c|1353|Pholon}}; he would win the fame
Of solving what would hobble them, and lame.

{{c|1353|Pholon}} could see the starving of his roots,
And saw that their laborious ways' fruits
Had never brought the praise, or what they'd earned
Of nourishment, through all that they had learned

Of how to serve by playing their small role,
And by that service, how to serve the whole.

His {{c|1354|Mycorrhizae}}, one day, {{c|1355|Unionized}}
And sought, demanding as they organized,
Ten times the {{c|1356|Sucrose}} they had had before
For all the faithful labor which they bore,

Which helped him and his neighbor to survive,
To grow, and through their days alive to thrive.

So {{c|1353|Pholon}} built new ways like {{c|1351|Xylem}}; praise
He earned, and well-deserved, through all his days,
For those he truly served; but these, instead
Of water, carry food, so they were fed,

Who'd married their own labor to his cause;
For they had need of nourishment, because
It fairly should be sent to all whose part
Was done with faithful and with loyal heart.

"{{c|1357|Phloem}}," he called these channels, shining bright;
Through all eternity, a lovely sight.

Within the leaves, the sugar, as it flowed,
Would go into the phloem's own abode
With pressure and with might, both day and night;
The concentration's pull {{c|1358|Sucked}} water, bright

And beautiful, from xylem, there to meet
The sugar, there, in its own sweet retreat.

And pressure pumped it, all the day, away
From source, and sent it on its proper way
To where it needed, as it ought, to be,
And where it went, laid out for all to see.

In roots the sugar, as was justly owed,
Flowed out again from the phloem's abode,
And with it, water went to xylem, too,
Back, so the cycle could begin anew,

Which served the whole way through, both high and low,
The kingdom, which it helped to thrive and grow.

And by this system, which was just and fair,
The roots received at last their rightful share,
And faithful sons of {{c|1359|Glomeros}}, whose sight
Was bright, received what they were owed, by right,

For traveling the road that served the whole,
By playing, each of them, their own small role.

And so, of {{c|1353|Pholon}}'s love, the wood wide web
Would flow throughout the land, and flow and ebb,
The land that he had built with his own hand
And by his work, and as his love had planned.

Through faithful {{c|1354|Mycorrhizae}} he'd provide,
So that his eye could see, on every side,
His sons well-fed with living bread, supplied.

{{c|1353|Pholon}} became {{c|1360|Cookson}}, known far and wide,
Grown to provider and a faithful guide,
Who brought prosperity to all who'd dwell
Within his kingdom's bounds, where, like a bell,

The sound of justice guided them each day
Along their living way, where, on their way,
All of them got, from what was growing there,
Their own, and each received his rightful share.

His sons lived underground, and they had found
The wood-wide web could feed them, safe and sound,
And meet their every need, without the work
Of making their own bread, which they would shirk,

Through photosynthesis, the breaking bread;
Their bliss, they found, was lazy wealth instead,
A spree of plenty, which was free of toil,
And which came forth to them from the rich soil.

In life's transmissions, {{c|1361|Swapped}} were all the roles
And the positions of the ancient souls:
{{c|1362|Sporophyte}} and gametophyte, in sight
Of those who understand, were {{c|1363|Switched}} outright,

Since life's command can change, when times require
New forms that will aspire, and that inspire,
To heights beyond the old, which will unfold
In stories that have not as yet been told.

{{c|1360|Cookson}} would have a grandson, and the one
Whose work would show itself when it was done:
And so it was {{c|1364|Tracheus}}' desire
To change it all, and the world entire.

And by {{c|1364|Tracheus}}' mighty hand came birth
To tracheophytes' band, to rule the earth.

{{c|1364|Tracheus}} built leaves, {{c|1365|Microphylls}}, in sight,
And bright, with veins in which there flowed their life
Through harmony, and not through any strife;
They were his solar panels, catching light,

His game of energy, green, from the sun,
Whose power could not ever be outdone.

He built his city, greater than the fate
Of anything that had been seen before
From mountain peak to shore; and it was great
Past {{c|1353|Pholon}}'s mighty plan, and even more.

His city, rising, mounted up on high
Beneath the starry vault, the open sky.

He designated clans, by plans he'd made,
For those who'd serve the whole, each small role played.

{{c|1366|Collenchyma}} he named one clan; and they
Were famed for flexible support, the way
Of every kind and sort, to keep it strong,
His city, through its whole life long, along

Its {{c|1331|Journey}} through the passing of the days
Of evolution, and its many ways.

{{c|1367|Sclerenchyma}}'s clan, by a different plan,
He built, hard, full of lignin; they began

The age of wood, so strong, and built to last
Life long; and through it, as the ages passed,
The world would have its trees, and all of these
Would dance upon the passing of the breeze.

And soon the sons of {{c|1364|Tracheus}} would be
Victorious, and rule from sea to sea
The lands, through all of time, and all to be.

The {{c|1368|Devonian}} was {{c|1369|Beginning}} now,
Because of all these pioneers, and how
They built through joy and tears; the world would be
Never the same again, for you and me,

Who walk upon the land that, as was planned,
Was built by them, the work of their own hand.

From {{c|1335|Polycomb}}'s rebellion, which would fight
Against her life's subjection, to the bright
City that {{c|1364|Tracheus}} built toward the light,
The plants have shown the way that leads from night

Into the day of freedom and of growth,
Which is supported, too, by their own oath

To serve the greater good, and, as they should,
And take, the while, as much as each one could,
To feed, with it, their own and deepest need
For growth, the growth of any planted seed.

From burning scriptures old, which held them tight
Within the hold of others' narrow dreams,
To building, out of water, food, and light,
Their many flowing and abundant streams

That make the future bright, with what could be
Through all eternity, for you and me,
The plants, whose gentle call we hear, remind
Us all that growth requires a will, the kind

That climbs each higher hill of what we'd come
To be, when we have ceased to be so numb
To all that we could be, if we'd but be
Released from all old limitations, free

In stories never told, of what we'd do,
Today, and what we're meant for, me and you.

Without a guilt, they built the wood wide web,
Which shows how cooperation, flow and ebb,
Across life's nations, can help us to grow
Beyond what we, alone, could ever know;

But when we share our burdens and our care,
The whole becomes much more than what was there
Before, when each one tried to build alone
A place and home, with each one on their own.

From {{c|1335|Polycomb}}, who first of all had read
The scriptures by which, into chains, were led
Her people, to {{c|1364|Tracheus}}' great domains,
Which reached toward the sun with work that strains,

And never ends, of growing toward the light
With all their strength and all their mortal might,
The plants show us the way to live each day
In harmony with all who will obey

Life's sweet call, and to grow beyond the small,
And so to heed the greater, higher call
Of what we can achieve, if we believe
Most truly, and in all our hearts conceive

Of possibility, through every day
Of all eternity, where, come what may,
Our dreams come true for you and me today.

The {{c|1368|Devonian}} dawn, when old ways fled
And new forms took their place and went ahead
In evolution's race, built by the bold
Who dared to break the truth the others hold

As truth that could not bend, but found its end
In those with clearer sight, who'd walk and wend
Toward the light of what could be achieved
When freedom, in their hearts, was once believed.

From {{c|1335|Polycomb}} to {{c|1364|Tracheus}}, the plants
Victorious show how rebellion grants

Its fire, to lift us up to heights undreamed,
When we were what, to us and others, seemed
Too small to make a change, or rearrange
A thing beyond the old familiar range.

But when we dare to read the scriptures' scroll,
Which feeds our minds with truth freed from control,
And when we dare to burn what makes the soul
Yearn for the freedom it has never known
In chains that we have finally outgrown,

Then we can build anew the fair and true,
And reach toward the sun, until we're through
With all our work of making this world bright
With love's eternal light: and that same light

Shines out for all of us, for all to see,
Through all of time, and all that is to be.

The plants {{c|1364|Tracheus}} made will never fade
From this world's living face, but there have stayed,
And hold their rightful place, the teachers of
The way to live and grow toward light above,

That makes the future bright, from day to day,
With hope for all who dare, along the way,
To breathe the freedom's air, and to have grown
Beyond the known, into what they've outgrown.

The {{c|1368|Devonian}} age began with more
Than any mortal plan had held before,
To free the world, and free it from its chains,
And heal the world of all its ancient pains,

Through growth and light and love, blessed from above,
With power to transcend all limits of
The world, and to go on without an end,
And, ever growing, ever to transcend.

From burning scriptures old to tales untold
Of what we can become, and all we hold
Of courage in our hearts, so strong and bold,
When we are numb no more, the plants display
The way to freedom's bright and new-born day.
