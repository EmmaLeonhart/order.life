# Chapter 173: The Mahabharata

{{c|2328|Vyasa}}, the compiler, sage, was there,
And saw it, and he told it: for his own
Were {{c|2329|Dhritarashtra}}, {{c|2330|Pandu}}, and the heir
{{c|2331|Vidura}}; and from them the war was grown

Of cousins came, the {{p|Kuru}} house that ruled
At {{p|Hastinapura}}: {{c|2330|Pandu}}'s five sons
Called {{p|Pandavas}}; and hundred, who were schooled
As {{p|Kauravas}}, the blind king's many ones.

The blind one could not rule; and {{c|2330|Pandu}} reigned,
And died; the blind one ruled for all the young;
And then {{c|2332|Duryodhana}}, his eldest, strained
Against {{c|2124|Yudhishthira}}, the first among.

The five: {{c|2124|Yudhishthira}}, the righteous, true;
{{c|2333|Bhima}}, the strong; {{c|2334|Arjuna}}, with the bow;
{{c|2335|Nakula}} and {{c|2336|Sahadeva}}, the two
Twins, handsome; and one wife they'd share and know,

{{c|2337|Draupadi}}, won by {{c|2334|Arjuna}}; and when
Their mother {{c|2338|Kunti}} said: share what you've won,
Not knowing, she was wife to the five men.
And {{c|2332|Duryodhana}} envied them; and none

Of his attempts could kill them: house of lac
They burned; they fled; the poisoned sweet; the snare;
And then the dice: {{c|2339|Shakuni}}, at his back,
Played loaded dice; {{c|2124|Yudhishthira}} sat there,

Who would not turn a game down, and he lost
His kingdom, wealth, his brothers, and himself,
And {{c|2337|Draupadi}}; and she was dragged, the cost,
Into the hall, to strip her, like a shelf;

But {{c|2340|Krishna}} made her sari without end,
And none could strip her; but the deed was done.
They went to exile: twelve years, and to spend
The thirteenth hidden; and when that was run,

{{c|2332|Duryodhana}} would not give back their land;
And war came: at {{p|Kurukshetra}}, the field,
For eighteen days, and every house's hand
Was in it; and the dead lay, none would yield;

And on its eve, {{c|2334|Arjuna}} looked across
At cousins, teachers, kin, and sank in grief:
How could he kill them, bearing such a loss?
His driver: {{c|2340|Krishna}}, {{c|2072|Vishnu}}, come in brief

To earth; in the {{p|Bhagavad Gita}} sang:
The body dies, the soul does not; so fight;
Act, but let go the fruit, and do not hang
On gain or loss; give all as offering right;

The three paths: deed, and knowledge, and the love
Of god; and all are good. And then he showed
His form: all worlds, all beings, all, above
And under, eating warriors, past and owed,

The source of all, and where all things return;
And {{c|2334|Arjuna}} could barely bear the sight;
And then he took his bow up, with no turn
Of doubt; and so began eighteen days' fight.

Yet dharma there was hard: to tell a lie
To win? ({{c|2340|Krishna}} said yes.) And {{c|2348|Karna}}, born
A {{p|Pandava}}, raised by {{p|Kauravas}}: why
Should he leave him who raised him? So, forlorn,

He chose his friend, and died; and {{c|2349|Bhishma}}, old,
Bound by his oath to who would hold the throne,
Fought those he loved, and fell, as he was told,
On arrows, by {{c|2334|Arjuna}}'s hand alone.

And all were dead: {{c|2332|Duryodhana}} lay low,
His hundred brothers gone, the teachers, friends;
The {{p|Pandavas}} had won, and stood in woe
Over the field of kin, the bitter ends.

{{c|2124|Yudhishthira}} was crowned, and ruled for long,
Thirty-six years; then left, with brothers, wife,
Up to the {{p|Himalayas}}; and the throng
Fell one by one, and left, along with life,

Him and a dog, which was the god of right;
He would not leave the dog, and he went in
To heaven; there he found, in that strange light,
{{c|2332|Duryodhana}}, and his own kin in sin

Below; and that was the last test; for good
Does not make sure of joy, and judgment's thin
Before the world. And {{c|2349|Bhishma}}, as he could,
Had taught him, dying, peace and law within.

And {{c|2340|Krishna}} was {{c|2072|Vishnu}}, come down to earth,
As he would come when right was threatened: fish,
{{c|1550|Matsya}}; {{c|2350|Kurma}}; {{c|2351|Varaha}}, the boar's birth;
{{c|2352|Narasimha}}, man-lion; dwarf, by wish,

{{c|2353|Vamana}}; {{c|2354|Parashurama}}; the king
{{c|2009|Rama}}; {{c|2340|Krishna}}; and the {{c|1959|Buddha}}; and last,
{{c|2355|Kalki}}, who comes to end the dark age' ring:
The god comes down to us, and holds us fast.

So honor them: the five, the hundred, all,
{{c|2337|Draupadi}}, {{c|2348|Karna}}, {{c|2349|Bhishma}}, arrow-laid;
And {{c|2340|Krishna}}, at the chariot, the call,
Who sang the {{p|Gita}}, and the war was made.
