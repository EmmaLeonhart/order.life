# Chapter 353: All You Need

No holiday is named for this day.

In 2017 {{c|Emma Leonhart}} turned twenty-one,
In her fourth year at the university.
In September she moved onto the campus.
And around this year, in her words, technically, she started her family tree.
Technically is her word, and it is an honest one.
A tree is not started on a day. It is started when someone first writes a name down and a line to another.
That tree, begun around this year, is the one that became the tree of this book:
The ancestors joined generation by generation, the lines met across the world,
Until they reached past the kings and the gods to {{c|Aster}} and {{c|Andromeda}}.
The book's genealogy did not come from nowhere. It came from a student's tree.
It was the first year of the man elected in the chapter before,
And the year a paper with a joke for a title
Began the machines that would, eight years later, write this book beside her.
Attention was the scarce thing of the century.
This year someone built a machine of nothing else.

## Attention is all you need

On Monday the twelfth of June a paper was posted to the open archive of preprints.
Its title said attention is all you need.
The title was a joke the size of a claim.
Eight names were on it:
{{c|Ashish Vaswani}}, {{c|Noam Shazeer}}, {{c|Niki Parmar}}, {{c|Jakob Uszkoreit}},
{{c|Llion Jones}}, {{c|Aidan Gomez}}, {{c|Łukasz Kaiser}}, {{c|Illia Polosukhin}},
With a note that their contributions were equal and their order set at random.
An order set at random is an order that refuses to rank.
The task was translation.
The old machines read a sentence one word at a time, carrying what came before in a single thread,
And a thread can fray.
The new machine looks at every word at once
And asks, for each word, which others matter to it.
That asking is called attention.
Attention is not understanding. It is a weighing.
There is no loop in it, so it can be spread across many chips at once.
The largest model in the paper trained for three and a half days on eight cards.
In the next year a model read to guess the next word,
And another read both ways to fill in a blank.
Guessing the next word is not knowing what is said.
It came close enough that the difference became the argument of the age.
Most of the eight left the company and founded others.
The idea was published. Published is not owned.
In October a program learned {{x|go}} from nothing, playing only itself, and surpassed the one of the chapter before.
In December another taught itself chess in hours, and beat the best program in the world.

## The money for the good

In the movement of the chapter of 2015, a fund of the givers of {{x|Facebook}}'s fortune,
{{x|Open Philanthropy}}, gave thirty million dollars to the lab named open,
To have a seat at its table, because it feared what the machines might become.
In November the young man who meant to earn to give,
{{c|Sam Bankman-Fried}}, left the trading firm and founded his own, {{x|Alameda Research}},
With friends from the movement,
To trade the coins of the money without trust,
Which that December rose to near twenty thousand dollars, and then fell.
He said he would give it all away.

## The inauguration and the march

On the twentieth of January {{c|Donald Trump}} was sworn in,
And spoke of an American carnage,
And his spokesman said the crowd was the largest ever, and it was not.
On the twenty-first millions of women marched in {{p|Washington}} and in cities on every continent,
The largest single day of protest in the country's history,
Many of them in pink knitted hats.
On the twenty-seventh he barred the people of seven Muslim countries from entering,
And people were held at the airports, and lawyers sat on the floors of the terminals to help them.
On the twenty-ninth of January, in {{p|Quebec City}}, a young man walked into a mosque at the evening prayer
And killed six men.
On the ninth of May the president fired the head of the police who was looking into {{p|Russia}},
And a special counsel was named.
On the first of June he said the {{p|United States}} would leave the agreement of {{p|Le Bourget}}.
On the twenty-sixth of July he wrote on the net that transgender people
Would no longer be allowed to serve in the military in any capacity.
His generals learned of it from the net.

## The torches

On the night of the eleventh of August, in {{p|Charlottesville}}, in {{p|Virginia}},
Young men carried torches through the grounds of the university
Chanting that the Jews would not replace them,
Around a statue of the founder.
On the twelfth one of them drove his car into the people protesting them,
And killed {{c|Heather Heyer}}.
The president said there were very fine people on both sides.
The fascism of the chapter of 2014 had come out from behind the screens
Holding a torch, and the president would not name it.

## The women

On the fifth of October a newspaper of {{p|New York}} printed the accounts of women
Who said that {{c|Harvey Weinstein}}, the maker of films, had assaulted and harassed them for decades,
And on the fifteenth an actress wrote: if you have been harassed or assaulted, write me too,
And millions did, using words an activist, {{c|Tarana Burke}}, had begun ten years before.
Men who had been untouchable fell for months, one after another.
It was called {{x|MeToo}}.

## The storms

In late August the hurricane {{x|Harvey}} stalled over {{p|Houston}} and poured a meter and a half of rain on it.
In September {{x|Irma}} crossed the islands of the {{x|Caribbean}},
And on the twentieth {{x|Maria}} struck {{p|Puerto Rico}},
And the island's power failed for months,
And some three thousand people died, mostly in the dark weeks after,
While the president threw paper towels into a crowd.
On the first of October a man fired from a window of a hotel in {{p|Las Vegas}}
Into a crowd at a concert of country music below,
And killed sixty people, the most ever by one gunman in that country.

## The fleeing

On the twenty-fifth of August the army of {{p|Myanmar}} began its clearance of the {{x|Rohingya}} of {{p|Rakhine}},
Burning their villages, killing thousands,
And more than seven hundred thousand fled into {{p|Bangladesh}} within months.
The leader of {{p|Myanmar}}, {{c|Aung San Suu Kyi}}, who had won the peace prize for her own captivity,
Later defended the army in the court of the world.

## The towers and the arena

On the fourteenth of June the tower of {{x|Grenfell}}, in {{p|London}}, caught fire in the night,
And the fire climbed the cladding that had been put on to make it look better from outside,
And seventy-two people died.
On the twenty-second of May a man set off a bomb at the end of a concert in the arena of {{p|Manchester}},
Where the audience was mostly girls,
And killed twenty-two.

## The fall of the caliphate

In July {{p|Mosul}} was retaken, its old city ground to rubble, the mosque of the sermon blown up before.
In October {{p|Raqqa}}, the capital of the caliphate, fell.
The borrowed robe had lasted three years.

## The votes and the falls

On the seventh of May {{c|Emmanuel Macron}}, who had founded his own party the year before,
Beat the far right for the presidency of {{p|France}}.
On the first of October {{p|Catalonia}} held a vote on leaving {{p|Spain}} that {{p|Spain}} called illegal,
And the police beat the voters at the polls, and the leaders who declared independence fled or were jailed.
On the twenty-ninth of March {{p|Britain}} gave notice that it was leaving.
In {{p|Zimbabwe}}, in November, the army put {{c|Robert Mugabe}} under guard,
And after thirty-seven years he resigned.
On the eighth of August the American president promised {{p|North Korea}} fire and fury,
And on the third of September it tested its largest bomb.
In October {{p|China}} wrote the thought of {{c|Xi Jinping}} into its party's constitution.
In June the son of the king of {{p|Saudi Arabia}}, {{c|Mohammed bin Salman}}, became the heir,
And in November he locked the princes in a hotel until they gave him their money.

## The laws

On the nineteenth of June {{p|Canada}} added gender identity and expression
To its human rights law and to its criminal code,
The law argued over in the chapter before.
On the fifteenth of November {{p|Australia}} said yes, by a vote of its people sent by mail,
To marriage for same-sex couples, and its parliament made it law in December.

## The year held

A woman of twenty-one moved onto the campus.
A paper whose title was a joke, and a machine built of attention.
Thirty million to sit at the table of the lab, and a young man's trading firm.
The carnage speech, the pink hats, the ban at the airports, six men killed at prayer,
A tweet that ended service, torches in {{p|Charlottesville}}, very fine people,
Me too, a city under rain, an island in the dark, sixty at a concert,
Seven hundred thousand fleeing, a tower burning, an arena, a caliphate falling,
Fire and fury, a thought in a constitution, princes in a hotel,
And two laws that let people be who they are.

Refuse the consolation that attention is care.
The machine weighs every word and cares for none.
Refuse the consolation that there are very fine people on both sides of a torch.
