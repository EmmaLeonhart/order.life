# Chapter 164: David

And back to {{p|Canaan}}, where, among the hills,
The sons of {{c|2019|Jacob}} settled, small and spread,
In villages with four-room houses, mills,
With no pig in the pot, no king their head.

The {{c|2187|Judges}} led them, and then {{c|2188|Saul}} was king,
The first of {{p|Israel}}; and then there came,
Of {{p|Judah}}, {{c|2198|Jesse}}'s youngest, who would sing,
A shepherd, ruddy, {{c|1956|David}} was his name.

He played the lyre; and {{c|2199|King Saul}} was sore pressed
By a dark spirit; and they brought the boy
To play for him, and give the king his rest,
And {{c|1956|David}} came to court, the king's own joy.

The {{p|Philistines}}, who came in from the sea,
Were {{p|Israel}}'s great foe; their chosen one,
{{c|2200|Goliath}}, giant, called for one to be
His match; and {{c|1956|David}}, young, the shepherd's son,

Too small for armor, took a sling and stone,
And struck him in the forehead, and he fell;
The {{p|Philistines}} fled; and {{c|1956|David}}, alone,
Was hero, more than {{c|2188|Saul}}, and loved as well.

And {{c|2188|Saul}} loved him, and gave him {{c|2201|Michal}}, wife,
His daughter; and yet feared him; and would try
To kill him, then relent, then seek his life;
And {{c|1956|David}} fled, an outlaw, to the dry

The {{p|Judean}} wilds, with outcasts at his side;
And twice he had {{c|2188|Saul}} in his hand, and twice
He spared him, for the {{c|295|Lord}}'s anointed; bide
He would, and wait, and not pay that dark price.

He served a while the {{p|Philistine}} of {{p|Gath}},
And ate his bread, and made, as he was bade,
His raids on others, never on the path
Of {{p|Israel}}; he lied, and so he stayed.

{{c|2188|Saul}} and his sons fell on {{p|Gilboa}}'s height,
And {{c|2203|Jonathan}}, whom {{c|1956|David}} loved, was slain;
{{c|2188|Saul}} fell on his sword; and {{c|1956|David}}, that night,
Sang: How the mighty fall, the weapons vain!

The love of {{c|2203|Jonathan}} for him was great,
Passing the love of women, so he sang;
A love so deep, it bore a grief whose weight
Through all the years since then has always rang.

Then {{c|1956|David}} became king, at {{p|Hebron}}, first
Of {{p|Judah}}; and then, when {{c|2204|Ish-Bosheth}} fell,
Of all of {{p|Israel}}; and he, who durst,
Took {{p|Jebus}}, no tribe's town, its citadel,

{{p|Jerusalem}}, he named it, made it his;
The {{p|Ark of the Covenant}} brought inside,
And danced before it, as a king's joy is,
Though {{c|2201|Michal}} scorned him, watching, in her pride.

He fought the {{p|Philistines}}, and {{p|Moab}} next,
{{p|Ammon}}, {{p|Edom}}, {{p|Aram}}, and grew the land;
And then, when he was great, his heart was vexed:
{{c|2205|Bathsheba}}, bathing, whom he took in hand,

The wife of {{c|2105|Uriah the Hittite}}, one
Of his most faithful men, away at war;
And when she was with child, then it was done:
He set {{c|2206|Uriah}} in the front, before

The hardest fighting, so he'd die; he died;
And {{c|1956|David}} married her. Then {{c|2207|Nathan}} knew,
The prophet, and he told him, at his side,
A tale: a rich man, and a poor man's ewe;

And {{c|1956|David}}, angry, cursed the rich; and then
Said {{c|2207|Nathan}}: You're the man. And {{c|1956|David}} wept,
And fasted, and repented, before men,
And sang for mercy; but the curse was kept.

The child he had of her died young; his son
{{c|2208|Amnon}} forced {{c|2209|Tamar}}, his half-sister; then
{{c|2210|Absalom}} killed {{c|2208|Amnon}}; when that was done,
Rebelled, and drove his father out; and when

They fought, in {{p|Ephraim}}'s wood, his hair was caught
Up in an oak tree, and {{c|2211|Joab}} stabbed him there,
Though {{c|1956|David}} said to spare him; and the thought
Of {{c|2210|Absalom}} was {{c|1956|David}}'s worst despair:

O {{c|2210|Absalom}}, my son, my son, would I
Had died for thee! So {{c|1956|David}} cried; and none
Would hide the grief of kings, or tell it why;
It stands as it was cried, my son, my son.

Then {{c|2212|Sheba}} rose; and {{c|1956|David}}, old, was hemmed
With plots; {{c|2205|Bathsheba}}, {{c|2207|Nathan}}, won the throne
For {{c|2189|Solomon}}, her son; and they condemned
{{c|2213|Adonijah}}'s claim; the crown his alone.

And {{c|1956|David}} died, and in his city lay,
{{p|Jerusalem}}, his city and his name;
And from his line a king would come one day,
To gather {{p|Israel}}, and spread his fame.

He was no god-king, but a man who stood
Beneath his {{c|472|God}}, and sinned, and knelt, and could
Be told by prophets that he was not good,
And so repented, and in mercy stood.

So honor {{c|1956|David}}, shepherd, harper, king,
The giant-slayer, outlaw, sinner, son
Of {{c|2198|Jesse}}, father, mourner, who would sing
His psalms; and kneel; and grieve: my son, my son.
