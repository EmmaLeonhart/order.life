# Chapter 166: Assyria and Babylon

But east once more, before the {{p|Greeks}}' bright day:
On {{p|Tigris}}, {{p|Assyria}} rose again,
And three long centuries it held its sway,
With iron, siege, and terror over men.

Its kings kept armies, engines, iron's store
In heaps, and made a law of fear: the town
That rose was razed; its chiefs were flayed; and more:
Their skins on walls; and stakes; and hands cut down;

And all the rest were carried far away,
So none could rise again; and this they wrote
In their own words, and boasted of the day;
And all the east, in fear, would read the note.

Yet they were builders too: at {{p|Nimrud}}, high,
{{p|Dur-Sharrukin}}, and {{p|Nineveh}}, their halls
Were carved with lion hunts, and wars, the sky
Of courts; their reliefs ran along the walls.

And {{c|2249|Ashurbanipal}} gathered, in his hall
At {{p|Nineveh}}, a library of clay,
Tens of thousands of tablets, keeping all
Old songs, {{c|1913|Gilgamesh}}, {{p|Sumerian}} way.

{{c|2250|Ashurnasirpal II}} then moved the seat
To {{p|Nimrud}}; {{c|2251|Shalmaneser}} then went west,
At {{p|Qarqar}} fought, and {{c|2252|Ahab}} he would meet,
{{p|Samaria}}'s king, there among the rest.

Then {{c|2253|Tiglath-Pileser III}} made it strong:
A standing army, provinces, and those
Who were deported, all the peoples' throng;
{{c|1986|Sargon II}} took the north, and dealt it blows:

It fell: the ten tribes were carried off, lost
Among the lands; and others, brought instead,
Were mixed with those who stayed, and at that cost
{{p|Samaritans}} were made, from those who'd fled.

And only {{p|Judah}} stood, beneath the yoke;
{{c|2254|Sennacherib}} came down, and took and burned
Its forty-six towns, and, with flame and smoke,
{{p|Zion}} was ringed; and {{c|2255|Hezekiah}} turned

To {{c|472|God}}; and in a night, an angel slew
A hundred eighty-five thousand, his host;
{{c|2254|Sennacherib}} went home, and never drew
His sword against the city; so the most

Of {{p|Judah}} held that {{c|472|God}} would keep his own,
His city, that no host could take his hill.
{{c|2256|Esarhaddon}} took {{p|Egypt}}'s very throne;
And {{c|2249|Ashurbanipal}}, the last king, held still

From {{p|Egypt}} to the {{p|Zagros}}, wise and learned;
But war with {{c|2257|Shamash-shum-ukin}}, his own
Brother in {{p|Babylon}}, drained it; it turned
To ruin after him, and soon was flown.

The {{p|Medes}} came from the {{p|Zagros}}; from the south
{{c|2258|Nabopolassar}}'s {{p|Babylon}}; they fell
On {{p|Nineveh}}, and burned it, and the mouth
Of the great library was buried well

In ash; {{c|2260|Sinsharishkun}} died in the flame;
At {{p|Harran}} the last were crushed; and in one
Short generation, all the fear and name
Of {{p|Assyria}} was gone, terror done.

And {{c|2261|Nahum}} sang: Woe to the bloody town,
Of lies and robbery! Who'll mourn, who'll yearn?
And all the peoples, when it was brought down,
Rejoiced, and {{p|Judah}} too, to see it burn.

Then {{c|2259|Nebuchadnezzar II}} ruled, and won
There at {{p|Carchemish}} against {{c|2262|Necho}}, and held
The west; and {{p|Babylon}}, beneath the sun,
He built again, the greatest; and it swelled:

Its walls were vast; the {{p|Ishtar Gate}} was blue
With shining tiles, and beasts in gold; and there
{{p|Hanging Gardens}} rose for {{c|2263|Amytis}}, who
Missed her mountains in the {{p|Median}} air.

Two hundred thousand dwelt there; and its priests
Watched all the stars for centuries, and made
The zodiac, and reckoned moons and feasts,
And how the planets move, and where they strayed.

The week of seven days, for seven lights,
The {{p|Sun}}, the {{p|Moon}}, five wanderers that roam;
The circle's degrees, sixty-fold; the rites
Of sixty minutes: these we keep at home.

But {{p|Judah}} rose against him; and he came,
And took {{p|Jerusalem}}, and took its king,
{{c|2270|Jehoiachin}}, the nobles, and the same
Craftsmen to {{p|Babylon}}; and, rebelling,

Then {{c|2271|Zedekiah}} rose again; eighteen
Months long the siege; and then the city fell;
The temple {{c|2189|Solomon}} had built was seen
In flames, its gold and bronze borne off as well;

{{c|2271|Zedekiah}} watched his sons slain, and then
His eyes were put out, and in chains he went;
And all the city's people, all its men,
To {{p|Babylon}} were carried, and were spent.

The line of {{c|1956|David}} broke; the temple fell;
And by all reckoning, the folk should be
Lost like the northern tribes, as nations tell;
But {{p|Judah}} was not lost; they kept, though free

Of land and temple, all their law and rite;
They dwelt together on {{p|Babylon}}'s plain;
{{c|2272|Ezekiel}}, {{c|1922|Jeremiah}}, gave them light:
That {{c|472|God}} was not defeated, but again

Would bring them home; for they had strayed, and so
Not leaving, but correction for their sin;
And there they gathered, that the law might go
With them, the prophets' words, the tales within,

To keep them anywhere they were; and then
The house of {{c|2019|Jacob}} could not die, but stayed
Alive by word and law, among all men.
{{c|2024|Nebuchadnezzar}} died; his line decayed;

And {{c|2273|Cyrus}} came, of {{p|Persia}}, and he took
{{p|Babylon}} with ease; its folk, who had borne
A hated king, received him; and he shook
The exiles loose for home, no more to mourn.

Honor them: {{p|Assyria}}'s great clay hall,
And {{p|Babylon}}'s blue gate, its stars, its week;
And {{p|Judah}}, which, in exile, after all,
Kept its own law, and lived by what they speak.
