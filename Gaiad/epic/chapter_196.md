# Chapter 196: Scandinavia and the Eddas

In the cold north, {{p|Scandinavia}}, where
The winter sun would barely rise, the {{c|2929|Norse}}
Kept the old steppe-born gods, and kept them there
The longest, in the old rite's living force.

The {{c|2930|Europeans}} kept the bishops' rite;
the {{c|2929|Norse}} kept the old altars of the North,
and when they took the bishops in, they'd write
the old gods down in books, and bring them forth.

In {{p|Iceland}}, {{c|2933|Snorri Sturluson}} set down
The {{c|2932|Prose Edda}}, and the {{c|2931|Poetic}} old,
The myths; for, though of {{c|1497|Christ}}, he loved the crown
Of the old tales, and wrote them, to be told.

{{c|2935|Odin}}, the {{c|2936|Allfather}}, hung on the tree
{{p|Yggdrasil}} for nine nights, and won the runes;
{{c|2937|Thor}}, with {{x|Mjölnir}}, thunder; and, as free,
{{c|1203|Freyr}} and {{c|2938|Freyja}}, gods of the fields and moons;

And {{c|2939|Loki}}, trickster, bore the monsters: one,
{{c|2940|Fenrir}} the wolf, and {{c|2941|Jörmungandr}}, the snake
That rings the world; {{c|2942|Hel}}, of the dead; and done
By him, {{c|2943|Baldr}} died, gentle, for his sake.

Nine realms upon {{p|Yggdrasil}}: {{p|Asgard}}'s height,
The {{c|2944|Aesir}}'s; {{p|Vanaheim}}, the {{c|2945|Vanir}}'s; men
In {{p|Midgard}}; giants, {{p|Jotunheim}}; and, bright,
{{p|Alfheim}}, the elves'; the dwarves, in deep, and then

{{p|Niflheim}}'s ice, {{p|Muspelheim}}'s flame; and, below,
{{p|Hel}}, for the dead not fallen; and who fell,
{{c|2946|Valkyries}} bore to {{p|Valhalla}}; and so
At {{c|2947|Ragnarök}}, just as the old songs tell,

{{c|2940|Fenrir}} will swallow {{c|2935|Odin}}; {{c|2937|Thor}} will slay
The snake, and die of venom; {{c|2948|Surtr}}'s flame
Will burn the world; and then, a newer day,
Two hidden in the tree come out, the same.

Seven-ninety-three, when {{p|Lindisfarne}} burned,
The {{c|2848|Vikings}} raided, traded, settled; and,
Founding {{p|Dublin}}, {{p|Cork}}, {{p|Waterford}}; and turned
To {{p|England}}, and the {{p|Danelaw}} was their land;

The {{c|2949|Varangians}} served the emperor's throne;
They settled {{p|Iceland}}, eight-seventy, came,
Nine-eighty-six, to {{p|Greenland}}, {{c|2950|Erik}}'s own;
{{p|Vinland}}, a thousand, {{c|2951|Leif}}; and, all the same,

Went up the rivers east to trade, and made,
The {{c|2953|Rus}}, the {{p|Kievan Rus}}; and {{c|2954|Rurik}} came
To {{p|Novgorod}}, his kin {{p|Kiev}}; and stayed
{{c|2955|Vladimir}} took, nine-eighty-eight, the name

Of {{c|1497|Christ}}, from {{p|Constantinople}}, they found,
His envoys, such a beauty in the rite,
They knew not if on earth or heaven's ground
They stood; and so the {{p|Rus}} took up that light.

In {{p|Scandinavia}} kings took the rite:
first {{c|2956|Harald Bluetooth}} in nine-sixty-five;
{{c|2957|Olaf Tryggvason}} took {{p|Norway}}'s by might;
{{c|2958|Olaf II}} fell, a saint who would survive.

{{c|2959|Sweden}} came last; the twelfth century saw
{{p|Uppsala}}'s temple, with offered dead,
Come down, as crown and church took up its law;
The altar took new priests there, in its stead.

In {{p|Iceland}} it was settled without war:
the {{x|Althing}}, in the year one thousand, said
the bishops' rite in public; and the door
of each home kept the old rite there instead.

The sagas would be written, of that day:
Of {{c|2960|Egil}}, {{c|2961|Njál}}, {{c|2962|Laxdæla}}; and in {{p|France}},
{{c|2964|Rollo}}'s {{p|Normandy}}, nine-eleven, pay;
And {{c|2963|William}}, of his line, would take the chance,

Ten-sixty-six, at {{c|2965|Hastings}}, and would win
{{p|England}}. And so the {{c|2848|Vikings}} ceased, and took
The rite of {{c|1497|Christ}}; but they had shaped, within,
All {{p|Europe}}'s map: so honor them, their book.
