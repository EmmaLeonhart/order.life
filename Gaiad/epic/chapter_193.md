# Chapter 193: Journey to the West

In the {{c|2696|Tang}}, the monk {{c|2778|Xuanzang}} set out west,
Six-twenty-nine, to find the sutras' source
In {{x|Sanskrit}}, for his life's one single quest:
The words in {{x|Chinese}} had gone off their course.

They would not let him go; he went alone,
Against the law; seventeen years he went,
{{p|Central Asia}}, {{p|Afghanistan}}, unknown,
To {{p|India}}, and at {{c|2779|Nalanda}} spent

His years among ten thousand monks, the hall
Of learning: logic, medicine, and thought;
And mastered {{x|Sanskrit}}, and the schools, and all
The great debates; and when they would have brought

Him to be head there, he went home instead,
Six-forty-five, with six hundred and more
Books on twenty-two horses; and the head
Of {{x|Tang}}, {{c|2780|Taizong}}, welcomed him at the door.

He spent his life in turning them: the scrolls,
Thirteen hundred; {{c|2781|Yogacara}}, the mind
Alone, he brought; and wrote, of all the goals
And kingdoms of the west, the {{c|2782|Records}}, signed,

Of {{c|2782|Western Regions}}: rice and rite and sage,
The customs of each city; and before
Him, {{p|Bamiyan}}'s {{c|1959|Buddhas}} stood, the great age
Of stone cut from the cliff; they stand no more.

And on the road, the {{c|2787|Monkey King}} went too,
{{c|2786|Sun Wukong}}, born from stone, who learned the arts
Of {{x|Dao}} and {{c|1959|Buddha}}, ate the peaches through,
Made havoc up in heaven, and, in parts,

Was pinned beneath a mountain; and was freed
To guard the monk, {{c|2788|Tripitaka}}, a band
Of gold around his head, which, at the need,
The monk's spell tightened, by his own command.

And {{c|2789|Pigsy}}, {{c|2790|Zhu Bajie}}, the lazy pig;
And Sandy, {{c|2791|Sha Wujing}}, the river's own;
The dragon prince, the horse; and, small and big,
Eighty-one trials met them, which were thrown

By demons in disguise, the women's land,
The flaming mountains, rivers made of sand;
They reached {{p|India}}, took the sutras' hand,
And, coming home, were all enlightened, and

Each trial was a hindrance of the heart,
Made outward, to be passed; and so the way
Was walked, with friends, and every trial a part,
To bring the teaching home, and make it stay.

And in the {{x|Tang}} the {{c|1959|Buddha}}'s way was theirs:
{{c|2793|Chan}} rose with {{c|2794|Huineng}}, the sixth patriarch,
Awakening at once; and {{c|2796|Pure Land}}'s prayers
To {{c|2797|Amitabha}}, of the west, would mark

All those who called his name. And {{p|Chang'an}} stood
The greatest city of the world; to where
There came {{c|2654|Persians}}, {{c|1910|Arabs}}, {{c|2798|Sogdians}}, who would
Trade, study, live; {{c|2564|Koreans}}, {{p|Japan}}'s heir.

So honor {{c|2778|Xuanzang}}, who once walked the west,
The {{c|2787|Monkey King}}, who guarded him; and Tang,
The golden age, the high mark and the best,
Where all the {{c|1959|Buddha}}'s teaching came and rang.
