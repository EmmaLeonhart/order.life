# Chapter 349: Not Wittingly

No holiday is named for this day.

In 2013 {{c|Emma Leonhart}} turned seventeen.
It was her last year before university,
And the last year, she has said, that she was mostly offline.
In her words, she was pretty offline until she started university.
So 2013 is the last year of this month that she lived outside the net,
The year the net was shown to have been watching everyone who lived inside it.
She came into it the next year,
After the watchers had been named and had gone on watching.

## Not wittingly

On Tuesday the twelfth of March, in {{p|Washington}}, a senator asked
Whether the agency collected any type of data at all on millions of Americans.
{{c|James Clapper}}, the director of national intelligence, answered: No, sir.
Then he added: Not wittingly.
The added words are not the first answer.
The first answer is the one the room heard.

On Wednesday the fifth of June a paper in {{p|London}} printed an order from the secret court of {{p|Washington}},
Telling a telephone company to hand over, every day, the records of all its calls.
The records are not the words spoken.
They are who called whom, and when, and for how long.
Who called whom is not nothing.
On the sixth, that paper and a paper of {{p|Washington}} printed slides about a program called {{x|PRISM}},
Naming the large companies of the net.
The companies said they gave no door.
A denial is not a description of the pipes.

On Sunday the ninth, in a hotel room in {{p|Hong Kong}},
A young man gave his name on camera.
He was {{c|Edward Snowden}}, who had worked for a contractor of the agency in {{p|Hawaii}},
And taken the files with him.
{{c|Glenn Greenwald}} and {{c|Laura Poitras}} were in the room.
He was charged under the act against espionage.
On the twenty-third he flew to {{p|Moscow}}, his passport revoked in the air,
And stayed in the transit zone of the airport for weeks,
And on the first of August was given asylum,
In the country of the next chapter's war.
Where he stayed is not what he said he wanted.
That autumn it was printed that the agency had listened to the phone of the chancellor of {{p|Germany}}.
The machine that was built to connect everyone
Was also the best record ever made of who is connected.

## The free knowledge

On the eleventh of January, in {{p|New York}}, {{c|Aaron Swartz}} killed himself, at twenty-six.
He had helped write the formats that feed the net's news,
Had helped make the site of links of the chapter of 2005,
And had downloaded millions of articles from a library of scholarly journals
Through a closet at a university, meaning, it was believed, to set them free.
The government charged him with crimes that could have meant decades in prison.
The labor of scholars, paid for by the public,
Was locked behind the walls of publishers who paid the scholars nothing,
And a young man who tried to open the walls was hunted for it.
This book's quarrel with the encyclopedia is about unpaid labor too,
From the other side of the same wall.

## The popes

On the eleventh of February {{c|Benedict XVI}} said, in Latin, to a room of cardinals,
That he no longer had the strength, and would resign,
The first pope to step down in six hundred years.
On the thirteenth of March, as the white smoke rose,
The cardinals chose {{c|Jorge Mario Bergoglio}} of {{p|Buenos Aires}},
The first pope from the {{p|Americas}}, who took the name {{c|Francis}}.
In July, asked on a plane about gay priests, he said: who am I to judge?
He would be pope for twelve years, and die in the chapter of 2025.

## The marathon and the meteor

On the fifteenth of April two bombs made of pressure cookers went off
Near the finish line of the marathon in {{p|Boston}},
And killed three people and maimed hundreds.
The two brothers who made them were found within days;
One died, and one was sentenced to death.
On the fifteenth of February a meteor exploded over {{p|Chelyabinsk}}, in {{p|Russia}},
Brighter than the sun for a moment, and its shock broke the windows of a city,
And the dashboard cameras of the Russian roads filmed it from every side.

## The factory

On the twenty-fourth of April, near {{p|Dhaka}}, the building called {{x|Rana Plaza}} collapsed.
The day before, cracks had been found in its walls.
The bank on its ground floor had closed.
The garment workers on the floors above were told to come back to work, or lose their pay.
One thousand one hundred and thirty-four people died,
Sewing clothes for the shops of the rich countries,
And the clothes were found in the rubble with the labels of those shops in them.

## The coup and the massacre

On the third of July the army of {{p|Egypt}} removed {{c|Mohamed Morsi}}, the president elected the year before.
His supporters camped in the squares of {{p|Cairo}}.
On the fourteenth of August the army cleared the square of {{x|Rabaa}},
And killed more than eight hundred people in a day.
The revolution of the square of 2011 ended in another square.
In June, in {{p|Istanbul}}, a protest to save the trees of {{x|Gezi Park}}
Became a protest against the man who would rule {{p|Turkey}} for the rest of the century so far,
And was put down with gas.

## The gas in Ghouta

On the twenty-first of August, before dawn, rockets carrying {{x|sarin}}
Fell on the suburbs of {{p|Damascus}} called {{p|Ghouta}},
And killed hundreds, some say well over a thousand, many of them children, in their sleep.
The American president had said a year before that the use of such weapons was a red line.
He did not strike.
{{p|Russia}} offered a deal: the regime would give up its chemical weapons.
It gave up most of them, and used chlorine later.
The war went on with the other weapons, which were permitted.

## The marriage and the verdict

On the twenty-sixth of June, in {{x|United States v. Windsor}},
The {{x|Supreme Court}} struck down the law that had kept the federal government
From recognizing the marriages of same-sex couples.
{{c|Edith Windsor}}, eighty-four, had been taxed on her late wife's estate as if they were strangers.
On the thirteenth of July the watchman who had shot {{c|Trayvon Martin}} was acquitted,
And three women, {{c|Alicia Garza}}, {{c|Patrisse Cullors}}, and {{c|Opal Tometi}},
Began to write a phrase on the net: {{x|Black Lives Matter}}.

## The train and the typhoon

On the sixth of July, in the night, a train of seventy-two tank cars of crude oil,
Left unattended on a slope, rolled into the town of {{p|Lac-Mégantic}}, in {{p|Quebec}},
And derailed in its center, and exploded,
And forty-seven people died, many of them in a bar on a summer Saturday night.
On the eighth of November the typhoon {{x|Haiyan}}, among the strongest ever to make landfall,
Struck the {{p|Philippines}}, and drove a surge of sea through {{p|Tacloban}},
And more than six thousand died.
In {{p|Warsaw}} that month, at the climate talks, the delegate of the {{p|Philippines}} wept,
And fasted until the meeting did something. It took note.

## The square in Kyiv

On the twenty-first of November the president of {{p|Ukraine}}, {{c|Viktor Yanukovych}},
Refused to sign the agreement with the {{x|European Union}} that he had promised to sign,
Under pressure from {{p|Moscow}}.
That night students gathered on the {{p|Maidan}}, the square of the Orange Revolution of the chapter of 2004.
On the thirtieth the riot police beat them,
And by the next day hundreds of thousands were in the square.
They stayed through the winter.
What came of it is the next chapter, and the next twelve years.

## The rest of the year

On the fifth of December {{c|Nelson Mandela}} died, at ninety-five.
On the first of July {{p|Croatia}} joined the {{x|European Union}}.
On the twenty-first of September gunmen attacked the {{x|Westgate}} mall in {{p|Nairobi}} and killed sixty-seven.
In October the government of the {{p|United States}} shut down for sixteen days.
On the second of October the police closed the {{x|Silk Road}}, a market of the dark net paid in {{x|Bitcoin}},
And in November a coin passed a thousand dollars for the first time.
On the twenty-fourth of November {{p|Iran}} and the great powers signed a first agreement on its nuclear work.
In December {{c|Kim Jong-un}} had his uncle executed.
In the same year the mayor of {{p|Toronto}} admitted to smoking crack, and stayed mayor.

## The year held

The last year a girl of seventeen lived mostly offline,
The year the net was shown to be watching.
A director who answered not wittingly, a man in an airport,
A young man hounded for opening the walls of the journals, dead at twenty-six.
A pope resigned, a pope from the {{p|Americas}},
Bombs at a finish line, a meteor on the dashboard cameras,
A factory fallen on its sewers, a square cleared with eight hundred dead,
Sarin in the suburbs and a red line not enforced,
A marriage recognized, a phrase begun, a train in {{p|Lac-Mégantic}}, a typhoon,
And a square in {{p|Kyiv}} filling in the cold.

Refuse the consolation that the metadata is nothing,
And that the man in the airport is a saint or a traitor before the record is read.
Refuse the consolation that knowledge is free because it is given away.
Someone paid. Someone was charged.
