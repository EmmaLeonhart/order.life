# Chapter 139: N — The Uralic Peoples

And {{c|1880|N}} walked north. While from the house of {{c|1929|NO}}
His brothers turned to eastward rice and rain,
{{c|1880|N}} went the other way, through ice and snow,
Into {{p|Siberia}}'s long, frozen plain.

Through taiga winters, through the tundra's white,
To where the reindeer graze, the world's far rim,
He walked; the {{p|Urals}} and the {{p|White Sea}}'s light,
From {{p|Bering Strait}} to {{p|Baltic}}, all knew him.

The forest dweller, fisher of the lake
Whose ice he cut, the herder of the deer,
He spoke the tongue {{p|Uralic}} peoples make,
And keep, and speak, and pass from year to year.

And still today, where all of {{p|Europe}} speaks
The tongues of other houses, {{c|1880|N}} has kept
His own: in {{p|Finland}}, by the {{p|Baltic}}'s creeks,
In {{p|Estonia}}, where his fathers slept.

The {{p|Sami}} herd their reindeer in the snow
Of {{p|Lapland}}; and the far {{p|Samoyeds}} still roam
{{p|Yamal}}'s long shore, where all the cold winds blow,
And keep the speech of {{c|1880|N}}, and keep their home.

And in the heart of {{p|Europe}}, far from kin,
The {{p|Magyars}} speak it, fifteen million strong;
Their fathers rode from {{p|Great Hungary}}, in
The {{p|Urals}}' reach, and brought their tongue along.

Their nearest kin, on the {{p|Ob River}}'s banks,
Two thousand miles to east; and yet the tongue
Held whole, among the other peoples' ranks,
As island in the sea, and still is sung.

And {{c|1931|Väinämöinen}} is their singer old,
The ancient wizard, eldest of the wise;
{{c|1932|Ilmarinen}}, smith, who forged the bold
{{p|Sampo}}, the mill of grain and salt and prize.

The {{p|Sampo}} grinds out grain, and salt, and gold;
And in {{p|Karelia}} the songs are sung
Of these, the heroes and the gods of old,
The {{p|Kalevala}}, sung in the northern tongue.

So honor {{c|1880|N}}, who walked into the cold,
Whose {{c|1933|Haplogroup N}} is the reindeer line,
The forest's fisher, singer, and the old
Unconquered speech, still spoken, still his sign.
