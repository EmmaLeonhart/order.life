# Chapter 85: The Return to the Sea

The land was bare. The sea was bare. And yet
Within the bare world lay the oldest call—
The water that gave birth to life, the wet
And welcoming abyss, first source of all,

That still remembered every form it made
And held within its chemistry the means
Of feeding, floating, breathing in the shade
Of waves—and now, in the {{x|Triassic}}'s scenes

Of devastation, some of {{c|1669|Sauros}}' kin
Looked seaward from the ruined shore and chose
The oldest path: to go back, to begin
Again in water what the land would close.

For this was not the first time. Long before,
In {{x|Permian}}'s last age, a humble line
Had tried the water—{{c|1244|Mesos}}, from the shore
Of all {{p|Gondwana}}'s southern lakes, the fine

And lizard-shaped, with newt-like tails that swept
The shallows of fresh water for their prey—
First of {{c|1661|Amnios}}' children who had crept
Back toward the water from the land's dry way.

But {{c|1244|Mesos}} was not truly {{c|1669|Sauros}}' son—
His place within the family was clear:
Of {{c|1677|Paraps}}' stock he came, and not of one
That bore two windows, the diapsid gear—

But he had shown the way: that those who sealed
Their eggs against the water could return
To water: so the covenant, revealed
As two-way door, not exile, not a burn

Of bridges back to {{c|1725|Tethys}}' ancient home.
{{c|1244|Mesos}} perished in the {{x|Permian}}'s end—
The lesson lived: the sea was free to roam
For any who had lungs and limbs to bend

Toward the swimming shape. And in the wake
Of the {{x|Great Dying}}, when the ocean lay
Still poisoned, slowly healing—for the sake
Of empty niches stretching every way—

New lines of life remembered {{c|1244|Mesos}}' road:
First came {{c|1726|Ichthyos}}—children of the scale
Who read the water's oldest fluid code
And built their bodies for the current's trail.

They entered from the shoreline lizard-shaped
And small, with legs still bearing toes and claws,
But over generations they had draped
Their limbs in flesh and fins without a pause

Or hesitation; for the sea would pay
Each change that brought them to the swimming plan;
The adaptation hurried on its way
Because the prize was vast: the boundless span

Of empty ocean, free of any foe,
Where any who could swim and hunt would find
A feast of fish wherever they would go
Across the healing waters of the mind

Of {{c|1725|Tethys}}, slowly waking from her grief.
And {{c|1726|Ichthyos}} grew large, then larger; some
The size of whales in the {{x|Triassic}} reef
Of the healing sea; {{c|1727|Shonisauros}}, from

The western {{p|Tethys}}, reached the length of ships,
Enormous eyes like dinner plates that peered
Through dim {{x|Triassic}} waters—at his lips
He had no teeth at all; his jaw was cleared

For a wide suction mouth to draw in prey
Like a great vacuum of the ancient deep.
And in those same {{x|Triassic}} waters, gray
And strange, the clans of {{c|1728|Thalattos}} would creep

Along the shallow coast with bent-down snout
And crushing teeth for shellfish on the floor—
A group of {{c|1669|Sauros}}' house, a branch without
Long issue in the ages—and no more

Than brief {{x|Triassic}} test, a passing style
In strange-faced, shallow-water coastal role—
But in their time, magnificent, worthwhile:
The snub-nosed hunters where the tide would roll.

And from the {{c|1677|Paraps}} line—from {{c|1678|Turtlos}}' own
Deep kindred—came the {{c|1729|Nothros}} to the shore:
Long-necked and paddle-limbed, in {{p|Tethys}}' zone
They hunted fish with needle-teeth, and bore

The first of {{c|1730|Sauropteros}}' ancient mark:
The flipper built from what had been a hand,
The body streamlined for the coastal dark,
The lungs that breathed at surface, then the grand

Descent to hunt below. So {{c|1729|Nothros}} was
The father of a dynasty to come—
His paddle-form would echo down because
His children's children's children would become

Plesiosaurs—but that is yet to tell.
Beside him {{c|1731|Plakos}} took a different way:
Heavy-bodied, built like a living shell,
He dove to the floor of the shallow bay

And crushed the mollusks with his pavement teeth—
So broad and flat for grinding shell to dust—
A bottom-feeder in the reef beneath
{{x|Triassic}} shallows, armored, slow, robust.

And {{c|1731|Plakos}} looked like {{c|1678|Turtlos}} in his way—
Round-bodied, armored, patient on the floor—
And this was not coincidence: the clay
Of {{c|1677|Paraps}}' ancient body-plan once more

Expressed itself in water as on land:
The turtle-shape, the armored, patient form
That carries home upon its back, the planned
And fortress-bodied refuge from the storm.

Seven lines tried out the {{x|Triassic}} sea:
{{c|1244|Mesos}}' old memory, {{c|1726|Ichthyos}}' speed,
{{c|1728|Thalattos}} and his strange coast-crawling spree,
{{c|1729|Nothros}}' long neck and {{c|1731|Plakos}}' patient greed,

And others yet unnamed who dipped their toes
And found the water welcoming or cold—
But all wrote down the lesson that life knows
And has rehearsed since the first story told:

The sea is never truly left behind.
The covenant of {{c|1661|Amnios}}: a door,
And not a wall—and those with lungs could find
The ocean's invitation evermore.

So honor those who turned from ruined land
And chose the healing water's ancient way—
Who traded claw for flipper, foot for hand
Rebuilt to swim; life returns to the bay

It left, and finds it waiting, patient, deep,
As it had waited since the world was young,
And full of room for those who choose to leap:
The sea from which the oldest life had sprung.
