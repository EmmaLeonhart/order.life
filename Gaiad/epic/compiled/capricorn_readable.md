# Chapter 29: First Ice Age Begins

In Terra's rivers, flowing wide and free,
Two lineages lived, which would belong
Together, in a bond, and come to be
United, all their lives, their whole lives long.

Great Symbio-Mygus, a fungal lord,
In the tenth generation from the line
Of Zygos, Mygus' son, by old accord,
Of that most ancient father, the divine.

And fair Charina-Planta, shining bright,
Tenth from Charina and her algae clan,
The Daughter of photosynthetic light,
That lived where waters of the river ran.

These two would meet together on the shore
Of Lichen Lake; in marriage they would bind
Two unlike kinds, as none had met before:
The fungus with the plant would be combined.

"Come here, O lovely maiden of the green,
And let us join together our two ways,"
Said Symbio-Mygus, the king serene,
"And rule as one through all our living days."

"I capture light that comes from sunny skies;
You gather, from the stone and from decay,
From earth, the nutrients that will arise;
Together, you and I can rule the day."

Charina-Planta listened to his call,
And she agreed with all her strength and might,
Photosynthetic, giving him her all,
To answer to his fungal appetite.

"A fusion, then, is what we shall create,"
She said to him, "and, standing side by side,
Plant and the fungus, we will meet our fate;
We'll conquer land, and spread both far and wide."

The marriage that they made, a wondrous sight,
Was two great kingdoms joining, and so grand
An agricultural realm in its might
Rose up to claim and hold the fertile land.

When Lichen Lake was full, its waters high,
They thrived; but when the waters went away
And dried, beneath the open, burning sky,
They slept, and waited for another day.

Dormant throughout the time of drought they lay,
And waited for the rain; and then again,
When water came in all its rich display,
They rose to rule across their vast domain.

Together they bore children who were bright,
And each was made a fusion of their line,
The lichens, who were filled with double might,
Both strong in fungus and in plant divine.

And countless were the forms they took with glee,
Each one well suited; and, among the crowd,
Fruticose lichens, rising like a tree,
Rose up above the lowly, tall and proud.

The Reindeer Moss spread out both far and wide;
Cladonia's cups held the morning dew;
With Goldeneye Lichen there, at their side,
While Old Man's Hair in hanging breeches grew.

And then the great foliose lichens came,
The Ruffle Lichen, which was soft and fair,
As leafy beauties, earning all their fame,
And Pillow Lichen, found now everywhere.

And Physcia spread out his leafy form,
And these, the leafy beauties, caught the light
And weathered every kind of wind and storm,
And made the places that were barren bright.

The crusty crustose lichens, hard and strong,
In all the harshest places, dry and rough,
Could live through times both short and very long,
Where other life could not find there enough.

The Rime Lichen upon the mountain's height,
So tough, so strong, so made to hold and last,
And the Orange Sea Lichen, burning bright,
Held on when all the other life had passed.

The powdery and leprose lichen clan
Broke up, and scattered everywhere they blew,
Spread out from where they first of all began,
And found new territories where they flew.

The Leprose Lichen, like a golden dust,
Rose on the wind, its particles to fly,
Scattered abroad with every windy gust,
To colonize the places low and high.

Some lichens chose the hardest, roughest way:
The Endolithic forms, so very small,
Who lived within the rock, and there would stay,
Made stone their home, and stone was all in all.

The Ruby Firedot, who was burning red,
Had life where nothing else at all could live,
Within the solid rock that was his bed,
And yet he still had more of life to give.

The filamentous lichens also came;
The cyanobacteria they'd shield,
To play a guarding and protective game,
From harm in every area and field.

Ephebe wound his threads, and so would bind
The blue-green life; they made a home their own,
The partner he himself had come to find,
Where neither could have lived and thrived alone.

And strangest of them all for us to see,
All in a gelatinous, soft embrace,
Were jelly lichens living by the sea;
They found themselves a home and living space.

There was Tar Jelly Lichen, dark and thick,
Within whose jelly they would swim and live,
Cyanobacteria's magic trick,
And mutual aid they would always give.

But Symbio-Mygus, great as was he,
Took yet another bride, both young and fair,
Trentophila, the daughter, as we'll see,
Of Trent, the one who had the golden hair.

The daughters of Trentophila then went
And colonized the world; and all around,
Far out across the area's extent,
Spread out the partnership that they had found.

And some would turn to parasitic ways,
As Cephaleura, envying the bright
And lovely Amber's daughters, in her days,
Stole from them their photosynthetic light.

But greatest of all of the partnerships,
The daughters of fair Amber, who were true,
Were those that sailed upon great nature's ships,
And all the sons of Arbusculus too.

Great Amber wed Arbusculus; the pair,
Who bore both Glomer and Ashley, would be
Most glorious in union, past compare,
With children, both of them so strong and free.

And Ashley then was wed with Wort, the wise;
Their love would bring the plants so fresh and green,
The ancestors who would from them arise,
And mosses, the most fair that have been seen.

They built themselves a city on the land,
The first to leave the water and its hold,
A city beautiful as well as grand,
That staked upon the land its claim so bold.

But Ashley could not stand there all alone;
She needed help to gather, as she could,
Upon that land of dry and barren stone,
Her food from soil that was not rich or good.

So Glomer, her own brother, true and kind,
Built them a network, so that they could share,
With all the power of his mind, designed
Below the ground, the nutrients down there.

Just like his lichen brothers, who were old,
So he, by sharing, helped them all to thrive;
The story of Glomer too would be told,
Of partnerships that keep the world alive.

He dug down deep into the earthen ground
While Ashley farmed above in sunny light;
Down there the minerals could then be found,
And so their trade was mutual delight.

Ashley and Wort bore children past all bound;
But raising young upon a land so dry,
Their progeny so countless all around,
Required the help of fungi who were nigh.

So Glomer built a network that was vast:
The Wood Wide Web, that ancient, spreading net,
A network that forever more would last,
That serves the forests of the world as yet.

An underground economy was made,
Where each plant dealt with every other one
In mutual harmony and fair trade
Through him, their fungal brother, all as one.

The wood wide web was not a bubble's dream:
He was the first of all of them to see,
Glomer, who solved their trouble with his scheme,
That our cooperation sets us free.

His startup grew till it was past all gauge;
Today he holds the largest market share,
A network that was treasure for the age,
Of all the fungal networks everywhere.

With eighty percent of the market's hold,
His {{x|WoodNet}} service, tested, tried, and true,
Of which the story still is spoken, told,
Connects the forest wholly, through and through.

But other fungi joined into the game,
And each one sought to win its own great fame.
The Ectomycorrhizae also came
To play their part within the network's frame.

And Amazon became of this a part,
The Wood Wide Web, which spreads out far and wide,
Of this great networked and connected art,
Through every forest, and on every side.

By this great union, made of all the might
And of Charina-Planta's great power,
Of Symbio-Mygus and of his sight,
They conquered and they won in every hour.

So they built their civilization grand
And great, a realm that brought about a change
Upon the fertile and the open land
To history, in all its depth and range.

Like Saya's daughters, who had come before,
These lichens, and their fungal friends and kin,
Like them, would open evolution's door,
And make the age of ice itself begin.

And so the Cryogenian began;
The world froze over, it was white and cold,
That age, humanitarian in plan,
As it is in their mighty story told.

But even in their icy reign supreme,
They lived the life of partnership, allied,
Of plant and fungus, and its waking dream,
That could in cold, and in the ice, abide.

So honor Symbio-Mygus, the true,
Whose marriage showed to all the world the way,
And Charina-Planta, honor her too,
In which a partnership can last and stay.

In every lichen that you chance to see
Upon a rock, their legacy today,
Or growing on the branches of a tree,
Lives on in symbiotic, rich display.

From every mountain peak to ocean shore
Their children show how the unlike of kinds,
Still living, as they will for evermore,
Can join together both their hearts and minds.

The Wood Wide Web that runs beneath our feet,
The network that they started, firm and strong,
Makes all the life within the wood complete,
Begun so long ago, and sings its song.

In partnership it is we find our strength
To help each other as we live and grow;
And we will go to any kind of length
Through all that we may ever come to know.

The lichen's lesson, which is tried and true:
That we can face, together, every one
Of all the storms that come, both me and you,
And never once be broken or undone.


# Chapter 30: Snowball Earth Begins

Here is the chronicle of Cnider's reign:
He sat upon a rock, a throne of stone,
And built, on either side of his domain,
A row of mouths, which were his very own.

With tentacles that reached down far below,
He made his city there upon the stone,
A great and mighty column, all aglow,
Where he would rule in might, and all alone.

His fishermen in lines he set and placed;
Down each mouth, working with a skillful art,
A Siphonoglyph, so perfectly spaced,
Each one of them would play his fishing part.

Great Cnider made a sacred, binding pact
With Luna; by her blessing, bright and true,
And keeping, both of them, to their contract,
Upon the full moon, he bore children two.

Two sons came forth within her silver light:
Anthus, traditional; and, as the new,
Anaxydros the bold, who, in his might,
With innovations, would come shining through.

And to this day, when Luna's shining face
Shines full, the sons of Cnider and the fair
Nettle's, within the sea's watery place,
Will settle all their spawning matters there.

By moonlight it is that they bear their young;
The tidal pull, the silver of her beam,
While Luna's ancient song is being sung,
Fulfill for them their reproductive dream.

Now Anthus kept and held the ancient way;
His children built on stone a rocky home;
He followed what tradition would display,
And never far from land would they then roam.

But bold Anaxydros had chosen change;
From him there came two children, only two;
His innovations were both new and strange:
Great Pompom, and Meduson, who was true.

Great Pompom chose the stalked and rooted path;
He built his home upon the ocean floor
To weather time, and weather nature's wrath,
And opened up the innovation's door.

From Pompom came two sons of strength and might;
The stalked jellyfish is what they became,
Conos and Stauros, who were burning bright,
Adding still more to their ancestor's fame.

But Meduson would choose another way;
A city of a new kind he would build;
He would not on the bottom ever stay;
His city with a wanderlust was filled.

His city floated freely in the sea;
He was the ancestor of jellyfish,
Drifting along, and drifting wild and free,
And granted them their floating, roving wish.

At first he had no sense of direction;
He picked up what he could, and he would ride,
Just aimless drift, without a connection,
While floating there along the drifting tide.

But soon a new idea came to him,
Who floated: "Why drift where the currents go?
Since I am floating, and am free to swim,
I'll pump, and swim, and go both high and low!"

He learned to pump his body, squeezing tight,
And, helpless now no more, nor left to drift,
He shot through all the water with delight;
His swimming was his talent and his gift.

Meanwhile, the sons of Anthus spread and grew;
Among them, the Anemones; and these,
Across the oceans, which were deep and blue,
Would sway in water, just like flowered trees.

And Corals built their cities, great and grand,
And with Zooxanthellae they would farm
Upon the ocean's ever-shifting sand,
And keep their cities safe and free from harm.

Zoochlorellae, green and shining bright,
Would help them, and these partnerships would make,
By catching all the morning's golden light,
The reefs, for other creatures and their sake.

From Meduson there then came children two;
Each one of them would take another path,
Bold Diplos, and sweet Jelly, who was true,
Through all the ocean's joy, and through its wrath.

Fair Jelly bore two great and mighty lines
Of jellyfish: the first, box-shaped and wise,
The Cubozoans, with their grand designs,
Who learned the way to use their seeing eyes;

And Scyphozoans, called "True Jellyfish,"
To see whose beauty, there within the deep,
Was granted many a sailor's heart's wish,
Where all the secrets of the ocean keep.

The sons of Scyphos numbered very high;
Each polyp bore a myriad of young
Beneath the sea, and underneath the sky,
Whose praises would forever more be sung.

The Pacific Sea Nettle swam along;
Its trailing tentacles, so long, would make,
Through waters, like a ram, so swift and strong,
The predators think twice, for their own sake.

But Cubos was a father who was kind;
He bore but few, but they were children dear;
With caring in his heart and in his mind,
He held each one of them, and kept them near.

We pray to Cubos when we come to seek
To be as fathers; he shows us the way,
Who would be strong as he, and never weak,
To care for all our young on every day.

The eyes of Cubos, complex, sharp, and true,
Were no mere spots of light at all; and these
Could see right through the waters, deep and blue,
For they were eyes that truly see and seize.

They saw each detail in the liquid space,
Each movement, and each form; and still today
The box jellyfish, in every face,
Still see the world in Cubos' ancient way.

From Cnider's ancient and his noble line
Came forms: some stayed on rocks along the shore,
Some crude, some fine, in many a design,
While others chose to rise, to swim, and soar.

The anemones wave their many arms
Like flowers; touch them, and you'll feel the sting
That hides beneath their soft and pretty charms,
Of cnidocytes that circle in a ring.

The corals build their reefs, so vast and grand;
Beneath the waves, the reefs grow up and rise,
Cities spread out through all the ocean land,
As if they were great underwater skies.

And every polyp, sitting in its cup,
Will reach its tentacles to catch what drifts,
The passing food, and hold its tendrils up
Through all the water's soft and gentle shifts.

The jellyfish, which swim so wild and free,
Will show us how to move about with grace
Through every single region of the sea,
Throughout the whole of life and its embrace.

And some will drift along the current's flow,
And some will choose their course; each has its place,
The way to drift, the way to where they go,
Within the ocean's vast and open space.

The way of the cnidarian today,
Without a question, and without a doubt,
Lives on and thrives within each sea and bay,
Where tentacles are reaching, stretching out.

From Cnider, sitting on his rocky throne,
His children spread across the waters wide,
And all of them by now are fully grown,
With tentacles to be for them a guide.

And so, in every sting that you may feel,
Remember Cnider and his ancient art,
From jellyfish, so painful and so real,
Which was the one that gave them all their start.

Their radial symmetry, which they show
In patterns that are clean, and that are true,
In their biology, will let us know,
In every single thing that they may do.

So honor the great Cnider, and with pride,
Who sat upon his rock, upon his stone,
That ancient one, provider far and wide,
And made the sea and all of it his own.

His covenant with Luna and her light
Still guides them; when the full moon shines above,
As it has guided them through every night,
They make and give their spawn with all their love.

And the cnidarians that live today,
With tentacles, still go about to hunt,
And follow on in Cnider's ancient way,
And do the work of their survival stunt.

From simple to complex, without a stop,
The jellyfish are still there, and they roam,
From polyp up to apex, to the top,
Through all the ocean's salty, rolling foam.


# Chapter 31: Rodinia Supercontinent Forms

And when Cambrius and Spriggina died,
Great heroes of the sea, upon their throne
Two sons were left to rule with equal pride:
Bold Protos, and Dutrus, each one alone.

Each brother saw it in another way:
The body plan that their two parents made,
To honor what their parents' line would say,
Would be interpreted, and be obeyed.

Great Protos looked upon their body form
And said: "The first hole is the mouth, so wide;
I'll keep it to the old and ancient norm:
The second is the place where waste will hide."

"The mesoderm is like the ancient past,
Mesoglea; and, as in olden sight
Our fathers did, which will forever last,
My mouth shall bear its tentacles so bright."

"O heathen, do you not, then, clearly see,
After the generations forced to dwell
In filth, the anus is what set us free?
It is the door: we're saved, and all is well!"

"We keep our mouths so clean, and keep them pure;
The endoderm and ectoderm, these three;
We eat the food that's nourishing and sure;
And mesoderm as well, shall ever be!"

"These layers we shall never, ever mix;
They are the old foundation, tried and true,
For they are our civilization's fix,
And they shall guide us in all that we do."

But bold Dutrus did not see it the same,
And came forth to proclaim: "What I would say,
My contrary and my opposing claim,
Is: second, mouth; the first takes waste away."

"The mesoderm is something that is more:
It reaches past mesoglea, and goes,
Beyond what we have ever seen before,
To where the fisherman and shieldmaid grows."

"It is a great reunion, don't you see:
Choanoderm, Archaeocyte unite,
The unity of old, restored to be,
Together, side by side, within the fight."

"O heathen, can you still not understand
What we take in, within, into our core,
The mouth, now progress' guide and guiding hand,
Will shape the things that we will have in store?"

"The things that we expel are just the past;
By joining what is old with new again,
But what we eat is what will grow and last,
Together we'll build a glorious reign."

And so the war began, and it would rage,
To learn whose vision would define the way,
Across the leaves of history's long page,
For all of the great Cambrian display.

Each brother bore two sons of strength and might;
From Dutrus came two sons, both brave and true,
To join together in the coming fight:
They were Dietrich and Saccorhytus too.

From Protos came, with valor rising high,
Ectus and Spiralius; and the line
Of old had four grandsons now, standing by,
Who came to make their fates and paths align.

Strange Saccorhytus did not have a rear;
Without a way to rid him of his waste,
For him, no anus ever would appear,
His life would never be with fortune graced.

So Dutrus' legacy, in turn, then fell
To Dietrich, who would be the loyal son,
And serve his father, as he would, so well,
Until his life was finished, and was done.

The sons of Dutrus, as was widely known,
Were famous for the heads they had, so wise,
Throughout the world, where they had spread and grown;
But Dietrich had no head, and had no eyes.

No head had he, with which to lead the way,
But innovation came: a tail, so strong
And long, he built, and it would save the day,
To help him move his life and self along.

Through muddy ground he made his winding path,
With two nerve cords to guide him on his course,
And so avoided all the others' wrath,
And found at last prosperity's own source.

He settled down, and there he built a home,
A colony, which worked out very well,
His simple plan, in which he'd live and roam,
Within his old, ancestral, muddy dell.

Great Spiralius kept the faith, and stayed,
Avoiding common wraiths; and like a top
He built his city, bold and unafraid,
A trochophore, and one that would not stop.

With tentacles that were both long and wide
He fished the waters, there upon the shore,
And out to sea, and out from side to side:
No fisherman so skilled had been before.

His wealth grew greater, growing day by day;
The trochophore design had proven true
In his own prosperous and thriving way,
For all the things that he would ever do.

But Ectus was a bitter, angry soul,
The secondborn, who felt the stinging slight;
Resentment was his aim, and was his goal:
To make his grievance, as he saw it, right.

"I'll be the greatest one among them all;
Among the sons of Cambrius I'll shine,"
He said, in answer to his duty's call,
"And I will make the victory be mine."

His tentacles he changed and turned to teeth,
Ferocious; and, while battle songs were sung,
Above him and around and underneath,
He shed his skin so he would stay as young.

With muscles that were strong, and flexing true,
He leaped at everything; none got away,
Not one of all the prey that once he knew,
From Ectus and from all his fierce display.

"O eldest sons, beware of me!" he cried,
"The legacy belongs now to all those
Who fight; so take some caution in your pride,
For I will fight and will defeat my foes!"

And so it was that he declared a war
Against Dutrus and Spiralius, both,
That spread both near and far, as none before;
He swore his solemn and his hateful oath.

The sons of Dutrus, who were brave and true,
And Ectus, who was armed with fearsome might,
And all the sons of Spiralius too
Made ready, all three tribes, for the great fight.

These three great tribes would be the ones to write
On evolution's grand and lofty stage
The story of their vision and their sight;
Their conflict would define for us the age.

And so began that time of lasting fame,
When the diversity of life was lit
Across the ancient sea, and burned, a flame,
The Cambrian's great legacy with it.

Bodies burst forth in forms that were unknown,
And Innovations rose up everywhere,
As every tribe set up and made its throne,
And filled the water and the very air.

Predator and prey, both of them alike,
As each of them would seek and strive to win,
Prepared themselves for war, and how to strike;
Arms races were at that time to begin.

The filter feeders found and made their place;
Some creatures burrowed deep into the mud;
And all the hunters ran and gave them chase,
While others went about and spilled their blood.

And Eyes evolved, that they might see the light;
And shells, and Claws, and armor, strong and thick,
And spines, appeared, prepared to meet the fight,
Became survival's great and useful trick.

And so the great explosion had begun;
The great Cambrius' legacy lived on
Beneath the shining of the ancient sun
From dusk until the coming of the dawn.

In every creature that we see, there lives
The choice between the old ways and the new,
This ancient memory, which it still gives:
It shapes and forms all things that we may do.

Some kept the ancient and the simple way,
While others, Innovative, burning bright,
And bold, instead would choose that they would stay
Within the eye of evolution's sight.

The war between the brothers, all the three,
Lives on today; in choices every day
We make, it lives in you as well as me;
We follow them, and follow their display.

Innovation, or the old tradition:
Each must submit, as everything that thrives,
In humble, everlasting submission,
Unto the force of life, the force that drives.

So honor now brave Cambrius, the one
Whose legacy is living on today,
By whom all of this fuss was first begun,
In every living thing, in every way.

The Cambrian explosion's blazing fire
In every form of life that we can see
Burns on, with longing and with desire,
And will, through all of time, forever be.


# Chapter 32: Pannotia Fragments

Within the age of patriarchs, the three,
Among these lords of power and of might,
Dutrus, Ectus, and Spiralius, free,
Was Ectus, who was the most fearsome sight.

With mouth agape, and with his teeth so sharp,
Through all his enemies he ripped and tore;
He played the music of destruction's harp
As no one else had ever done before.

The many sons of Isomeres the fair
Fell victim to his raiding, savage spree,
And all the sponges living everywhere
Across the waters of the ancient sea.

A complex mouth he built with skill and care;
He molted both his skin and mouth with time
To catch his prey, and catch it anywhere,
In rhythm, and in order, and in rhyme.

His cilia he cast off and away;
Amoebae then became, for him, his sperm,
In favor of a more effective way,
A change that was both very strange and firm.

His coelom he stripped down, and left it bare;
For function that was true, and made for speed,
Removed what had no use for being there,
He kept no more than what he'd come to need.

Unnecessary scaffolding he'd fling;
His organs floated freely there inside,
And cast it off, as one would cast a ring,
Within his pseudocoelom, so wide.

In burrowing he found his skill and art;
Through sediment he'd tear his way, and send,
And came to play the hunter's deadly part,
His foes to meet their final, bitter end.

The very first of predators was he;
Sons of Dutrus and Spiralius fell,
To hunt upon the waters of the sea,
Before his might, so glorious to tell.

In viciousness he bore a mighty horde;
They conquered all the earth from shore to shore,
Of worms, who served him as their only lord,
And always they would hunger after more.

Through the earth the great Ectus tore and ripped;
Destruction was the one he took as name,
While all the soil fell off of him and slipped,
And carnage was his pleasure and his game.

From Ectus came two sons of strength and might,
And clever Isaac, who was young and wise:
Bold Ishmael, who was ready for the fight,
And Isaac went to seek the new thing's prize.

Now Ishmael held onto it, fast and true:
Tradition was the guiding star he bore
For everything that once his father knew,
Through times of peace, and through the times of war.

But Isaac chose to take another way;
He'd build upon his father's ancient art,
And chose Innovation and its display,
But with a newer and a younger heart.

And out of Ishmael's faithful, ancient line,
Patriarch of Priapulids, the brood,
There came Priapus, who was strong and fine,
Who live where darkness hides in solitude.

Deep in the ocean's depths they live and dwell,
Like the ancient Ediacaran lands,
In places that no one can find or tell,
Of the hydrogen sulfide and its strands.

Without the oxygen, they live and thrive;
In waters dark and deep, there they will stay
And keep their ancient line of life alive
Until the coming of the judgment day.

Draco, Priapus' brother, who was true:
The mud dragons, horrific and so wild,
Became himself a patriarch there too,
Each one of them was nature's problem child.

Through all the muds they go and make their way;
Monsters are what, down in the deep, they are,
Both in the night and all throughout the day,
Beneath the light of every shining star.

From Isaac came two sons of lasting fame,
And Rhineheart, who was bold, and who was strong;
And one of them, Nematus, was his name,
And Rhineheart lived and thrived his whole life long.

Nematus was a quick one, and was spry;
He bore two sons to bear his line along,
A nomad living underneath the sky:
Nematus the Second, and Gordy strong.

Both sons would turn out prodigal, and stray;
They turned to live a parasitic life,
From the traditional and older way,
And caused their father sorrow and much strife.

But then, in all his aging, later years,
Nematus left that way of life behind,
Through all his many sorrows and his tears,
And peace within himself he came to find.

He then became the ancestor, the great;
Of all the animals, eighty percent
Of worms throughout the world, in every state,
Are his descendants, through his long descent.

His city was a sight for all to view;
From simple things to complex things they grew,
A worm community, a city true,
In everything that they would come to do.

Of Arthur Ironfoot, and tell it well.
His sons would spread out, going far and wide,
With evolution there to be their guide.

And Arthur bore two sons of lasting fame:
Arthropus, who was double-legged by name,
And Fuxi, who was wise, and who was strong,
Who lived and thrived throughout his whole life long.

And from Arthropus, the double-legged one,
Great lines were pledged to come, and were begun.
Two sons he bore, both with great strength and might:
There was Ferron, and Isoxys the bright.

And out of Ferron there then came two more:
Megacheiron, who went out to explore,
And Euthyphro, the one who was the wise,
Who looked upon the world with knowing eyes.

Megacheiron bore many sons, and one
Was Pantopus, who hoped that on some Day
He'd find, when all his searching had been done,
A mate both true and kind, to come and stay.

Now Pantopus was lonely, living there,
Until he met a maiden who was fair.
And beautiful Pyka, that was her name,
And she was one who set his heart aflame.

"You are all legs!" he said to her, and cried
To her who was to be his loving bride.
And so the two of them became the line
Of all the Sea Spiders, who are so fine.

The Sea Spiders built themselves cities strange
Throughout the reaches of their ocean range.
And in their legs they kept, and in them stored,
All of the organs that they so adored:

Intestines were placed there within their limbs,
And Genitals, where all the water swims,
And Gills contained within each single leg;
No coelom did they have, nor did they beg.

All legs, in truth, is what they truly were,
As Pantopus had come there to aver.
Their bodies were so lean, and were so slight,
Their legs were their main pleasure and delight.

And Euthyphro then bore two sons, and one
Was Insectus, when the day had begun,
And Arachnus, who was the one so keen,
With his appendages so plainly seen.

And from Insectus' great and noble line
The insects all would rise, and they would shine.
And from Arachnus, when his work was done,
Arachnids came forth, each and every one.

The sons of Arachnus, in their turn, bore
Appendages, both great, and to explore.
The sons of Insectus, for their part, made
Great legs, so they could ply their work and trade.

And some of them would live in waters blue,
And some upon the land would walk on through.
Aquatic and terrestrial, they found
That both paths would prove good, and would be sound.

Insectus bore three sons, and they were strong:
Mandibulus, who was there all along,
Aquilonifer, he who was so free,
And Hymenocarina, of the sea.

Mandibulus then came to bear two more:
Crustaceus, who went out to explore
The waters that were deep, and that were wide,
And Euthy, who would stand there by his side.

And from Crustaceus there came the race
Of the Crustaceans, found in every place:
The lobsters, and among them, too, the crabs,
With claws for holding, and for making grabs.

But Euthy chose instead to take the shore,
Where all the waves would come to crash and roar,
Upon the rocks that rose up very high
Beneath the open spaces of the sky.

Along with water bears, he went and walked
Where dry air and the wet air met and talked.
There they faced the asphyxiating land
With courage that was strong, and that was grand.

And here, between the ledges of the stone,
They laid their eggs, and left them there alone.
In gardens that were made of lichen green
Their young would for the first time then be seen.

From Euthy's brave descendants, those who stood
And faced the land's defendants, as they could,
The insects would then come, and would arise
To conquer all the earth, and all the skies.

Arachnus also bore two sons, and one
Was Trilos, born when the day had begun,
And Serk, who was a son of ancient fame,
Who came to earn himself eternal name.

Serk bore many children, and there was one,
Chelicer, who was hidden from the sun
In shadows that were very dark and deep,
And Sidney, with his secrets he would keep.

And out of Chelicer's own humble line
The spiders all would come, and they would shine.
All the arachnids, with their legs of eight,
Were pledged out of his bloodline, as their fate.

Today, in each room, and in each corner
Where webs are hung, and serve as a mourner
For all the flies caught there within their snare,
Chelicer's many children will be there.

Among the great Trilos' many daughters
Who ruled long, within the ancient waters,
Were Trilobon, the one who was the queen,
Marrella, who was ghostly to be seen,
Esmeralda, she who was jewel-bright,
And then Retifacia's piercing sight.

Was one who told a ghostly kind of tale.
And she became the ancestress, no less,
Of the Marellans, whom she came to bless.

Like phantoms, they would live within the sea,
And there they lived their lives, and lived them free,
Soft-bodied, gentle, and ethereal,
And they were almost immaterial.

Trilobon then bore children, four in all,
In waters never still, at ocean's call:
Trilobeth, who would become the great queen,
And Xander, who was strong, and who was keen,
And Necta, who was sweet, and who was fair,
And Helmut, who was found most everywhere.

Of Trilobites; and there was no other
Of all the Rulers of the sea, no one,
Could match their dynasty beneath the sun.

Through their great conquest, which would never rest,
They brought, at their command and their behest,
The Yakutian era and its dawn,
When all the older ways were then withdrawn.

Great Trilobeth bore two sons, of whom one
Was Redlich, born when the day had begun,
And Agnos, who was wise, and who was old,
With stories that were yet still to be told.

Of houses that were noble and divine:
Corynex would build and raise his own house
With all the wisdom he would then espouse.

From Corynex came Lichida, who made
Himself a great armada, well arrayed.
His house grew strong, and spread out far and wide,
With fortune at their side to be their guide.

And from Lichida there came Odontos,
Builder of the great and mighty crontos.
His spiny house was one that then arose
To challenge all of his opposing foes.

And from Ptychos, who was Redlich's brother,
There came four sons who were like no other:
Proetes, who was strong, and who was true,
Asaphus, of the waters that are blue,
Harpeth, the one who dwelt within the deep,
And Phacos, he who never goes to sleep.

And each of them would found his own great line,
With characteristics, both rich and fine.
The Trilobites spread out both far and wide
Across the waters of the ocean's tide.

For millions of the years they ruled and reigned,
While other life was held back, bound, and chained,
The rulers of the sea, in all their might,
Through all their dynasty, by day and night.

Their compound eyes were able, then, to see
As if through all of time that is to be.
No detail anywhere could then escape
Their vision, with its perfect form and shape.

Their bodies were Segmented, and they bore
Their legs, which numbered many by the score.
And every segment had its legs, a pair,
To walk with through the water, clear and fair.

They molted, shedding skin, as up they grew,
As arthropods today will also do.
And every stage would bring a greater size
Beneath the waters of the ocean skies.

And so the Yakutian time had come,
When Trilobites became the total sum
Of all that great success was there to be
In their vast dynasty, for all to see.

And so, from Arthur's great innovation
Came this great and mighty radiation:
The Insects, and the spiders, and the crabs,
Each one with other kinds of claws and grabs.

The Sea Spiders, creatures who were so strange,
Extended out, and spread through all their range,
With organs placed inside of every limb,
A body plan that was so lean and slim.

And the Crustaceans, living in the sea,
Built up their dynasty for all to see.
The insects who would come to live on land
Spread out across it, with their courage grand.

Arachnids, with eight legs on every one,
From Chelicer were pledged, their line begun.
In shadows they would always come to dwell,
With stories of their own that they could tell.

But none of them could ever match the fame
Of Trilobites, and of their mighty name.
For eons they would hold and keep their sway
Until there came for them their final day.

From Pantopus and Pyka and their love
To all the rulers, high and far above,
The legacy of Arthur went and spread
For long, long ages after he was dead.

In every one of them, each arthropod
That walks upon the earth and on the sod,
His biramous design, the limb in two,
Lives on through space and time, and passes through.

That double-legged and great innovation
Sparked this great and mighty radiation
That came to fill both all the sea and land
With creatures who were truly great and grand.

In every spider's silken, woven web
That catches life when it is at its ebb,
In every insect, and in every flight
Through all the day, and all throughout the night,

In every lobster, and in every claw
That follows on the way of nature's law,
In every crab that scuttles and that walks
Where all the beauty of the ocean talks,

Remember Arthur, and his ancient art,
The art that gave to all of them their start.
The legacy of Ironfoot, his own,
Lives on through all the ages yet unknown.

The sons of Arthur were the ones who proved
The way in which great innovation moved
The world along, from simple, early forms,
Up to complexity, the kind that warms

The heart of those who look on it and see
How vast and how diverse it came to be.
From one ancestral line, a single line,
There came the forms, some crude and others fine.

The Yakutian age had now begun,
When Trilobites had fought, and they had won
Their place as being rulers, strong and true,
Of all the waters that were deep and blue.

But time would come and show to us again
That even those, the greatest of the men,
And creatures who are in their very prime,
Must one day yield and give to passing time.

Yet Arthur's gift is one that's living on
From fall of dusk until the rise of dawn,
In every jointed and segmented leg
That walks the places where all life will beg

For forms that will be strong, and will be true,
To see the journey on, and see it through.
The arthropods are with us, and remain
Through all the sunshine, and through all the rain.


# Chapter 37: The Circulatory Innovation

Among Cambrius' sons, two came to face
Issues that were serious, in that place:
Chordatus, who was son of Dutrus true,
And Annelidus, strong all through and through.

Each one of them was then supported by
Hydrostatic pressure, and it was high.
With water pressure there within their frame
They bore their bodies, and their body's name.

Together they perceived and saw the flaw:
That when their movement came and broke the law
Of circulation all throughout their form,
Their blood would not be able to stay warm.

To nourish all their organs through their length,
They needed a much greater kind of strength.
So hearts they built, with arteries that flow
And veins like seas, that through their bodies go.

Together, each of them would make with care
Closed systems that would reach to everywhere.
The blood would flow in circuits that were true
And nourish all the organs, passing through.

And our great bloodlines, they were born that day
When hearts arose and came to show the way.
Hydrostatic to circulatory,
It was evolution's greatest story.

From Annelon there came two sons, and one
Was Segmentus, when the day had begun,
And Ribbon, hunter of the ocean deep,
Whose prey could never rest, and never sleep.

Ribbon built himself a proboscis long,
And it was venomous, and it was strong.
And with this weapon he would go and hunt
His prey, and catch it with a skillful stunt.

His sons are known among us still today
As Ribbon Worms, the ones who come to slay

That they shoot straight into the victim's heart.

Amphinos, who lived down in the water,

Who chose to take another kind of fate.

Of worms that burn as if they were on fire,

# Chapter 38: The Line of Ambulus

Their bloodlines, which were made both strong and true,
And as Annelon's many children spread
Their segments out, and made their kind widespread,

The greatness that for so long they had fought
To come and to achieve through patient art
And through the wisdom found within the heart.

To swim about, as hearts within them burned,
The sons of Ambulus instead had found
That their own way was one in which they're bound

As one with nature, and with nature's way,
Fishing in all the night and all the day.
At first they started out the very same
As those who bore Chordatus' noble name.

Built a proboscis for himself, and one

# Chapter 52: The Great Ancestor

In the tenth generation, by its fate,
Vater, the son of Pteraspis, so great,
Would be the Ancestor of humankind
And species myriad, of every kind,

From mountain peak to shore; and all he would
Through innovation change for all the good.

Vater would build a channel, grand and wide,
That joined the liver, bright, and, at its side,
The pancreas, and brought them to the space
Within his belly, there to take its place.

Today we trace this channel's honored name:
"Ampulla of Vater": such is its fame.

This father was exalted, father of
Far more than other Men had dreamed thereof;
The Elohim, who held him in esteem,
Would call him Abraham, as was their scheme

For one whose progeny, in time, would fill
Eternity, and they are filling still.

Vater would bear two sons: when day begun
Came Arandus, then Hestracon, the one
Whose work would carry on the house's name
Through evolution's ever-burning flame,

That burns in every heart that plays the part
Of innovation, and its daring art.

Hestracon found his belly, as he knew,
Inadequate, unready to pursue
The work that he would one day have to do.

From mouth to anus, all the same; and plain
And simple, he would call it, with disdain:
Only his father's main Ampulla would
Stand in the middle, solving, as it could,

Digestion's riddle. This was not enough
For Hestracon, a son so strong and tough,
Who saw what greater good there was, if he
Would dare to build what none had willed to be.

Prepared before his father's gift, and there
To give digestion lift; and he would call
It "stomach," and this name would stick for all.

It was the great divider, standing so
Between the mouth above, which would bestow
The food, and what intestines' love below
Would process, so the body then could grow.

Before the stomach lay, so swift and strong,
Esophagus, well-made, to pass along
The food upon its pathway; after, laid,
The intestine, its part to be well-played,

Which, in its own appointed place and role,
Would nourish all the body as a whole.

And Hestracon begat two sons: the one,
Astrops, was tried and true, when day begun,
And Eteros, the strong one, who would stay
And live, and thrive, until his dying day.

And Eteros begat two sons, and then
These sons were both beyond all mortal ken:
Cyatha, with a cup-shield shining bright,
And Tsubasa, with Wing-shield, and with sight,

Prepared for all he'd dare to face, his time
Of life, through all his reason and his rhyme.

Tsubasa begat two: one, tried and true,
Was Thelos; and the other of the two
Was Cephalus, the one who was so wise,
Who had the keen and penetrating eyes.

So no Rigidity of old prevails
To bind or hold his innovative mind,
Into innumerable scales, refined.

And these, his scales, would never fail to be
His flexibility and skill, so he
Could live within the reefs, where none could get
Relief from rigid armor's weight, which set

And sealed their swimming fate. Where none could bend
So well as to escape and to transcend
The coral's tight embrace, Thelos had found
His home, and made his dwelling on that ground.

Neither the sons of Arthropus, nor those
Of Molluscus, nor of Chordatus, rose
To match his status in the reef's tight space,
Where his own grace takes Rigidity's place

With flow, which helps him in his life to grow
Into each niche where others could not go,
To reach the food that he could find and take,
And use it for himself, for his own sake.

Cephalus bore wings, bright, on either side
Of his own helmet's form, and these would ride
Through calm and raging storm. His head would fly
As he would swim, held steady and held high,

According to his plan, with poise and grace
In every watery and open place.

His lateral line would keep him ahead,
And never sleep, but vigilant instead,
For all that might befall him in the sea
As he would swim through it; Aware was he

Of all that he could sense around his form,
And so he weathered every single storm.

Cephalus begat two: Galeas, true
And tried, and Osteos, the second, who
Would see the possibilities unfurled
By innovation's key within the world

For those who dared to think, and did not shrink
From going past tradition's binding link.

But Osteos was in a plight, and found
His bones weren't right, efficient, nor were sound;
He had to make them new: his bones' expense
Was Comprehensively so great, immense,

Wasting countless calcium moles, which roll
Through phosphorus, and more, a costly toll,
Elements by the score, that could be used
Much better, if they were not so abused

In static, lifeless bone that stood alone
Without a greater plan to call its own
For helping mortal man. So, with a will,
He chose to build more cleverly, with skill,

His skeleton, and make it all anew,
With wisdom that was always tried and true.

Inside his bones he set Blood channels there,
So they were living, breathing, taking Stores
Of minerals, like generals in wars;

In plenty, they were built and gathered up
From nature's flowing and abundant cup;
In scarcity, he'd borrow from the store
Of what he'd saved, like treasure, from before.

And he grew stronger, prospering, life long,
Beyond his relatives, who all along
Took everything that nature gives, but who
Had never learned to save for what was due,

The future's challenges and every need,
When they would hunger, and would have to feed
From stores they'd built in plenty, when the flow
Of resources was never scant or low,

But ran like rivers, spreading far and wide,
With bounty as the river's only guide.

Osteos bore two sons: when day begun
Came Pituri, and Ostracos, the one
Whose children, glorious, spread far and wide,
And multiplied, until on every side

The world was fed with countless forms of life,
Through harmony, and not through any strife.

To Ostracos was born, from coast to coast,
A million sons, a great and mighty host,
Who'd spread to anywhere that they were led
By life's eternal call, for times ahead,

To multiply for all who'd come, and feed
The world with their own forms, as was the need,
Through evolution's calm and raging storms,
From night into the day, in countless swarms.

Pituri grew a snout so long that none,
In all of evolution's scene, not one,
Was ever seen so long, beyond all doubt:
The longest ever grown, this mighty snout.

Pituri then begat Beakon, who'd be
Beyond all ken in wisdom and in might,
Who had the strength to look, and so to see
The world around him with a clearer sight.

Pituri's mouth was made of lips; it strayed
In no way to the realm of jaws, but stayed
With simpler laws: it only closed the space,
But never could, with biting might, erase

His prey; for no jaw was within his sight,
And with his lips he only closed it tight.

But Beakon saw potential in the snout
His father had; its essence he worked out.

So Beakon moved his gill arches, and he
Would prove that innovation's art could be
Of vital use. Into his mouth he brought
The arches, and with them he hunted, fought

For food in a new way, which worked by night
And worked as well by day, in the day's light.

Potential, and, with Pelaca, his mate,

Was trilobites, which they would bring away
To innovation's flame, and to its light;
That flame was burning in their name, so bright.

Not merely, in the ancient way, did they
Catch, as had others long before, their prey;

The sight of armored jaws, which followed true
The laws of nature, used in ways made new
Through all their living days, the whole way through.

With jaws of steel-hard feel, of a new kind,

Their sustenance, the life-bestowing food.

When Gnathus and Pelaca would display
What none could match: the power to break through
What none before them ever came to do:

To crush with them, and with them to consume
What other creatures would, till then, assume
Was safe in armor's shell, protected well
From all of those who'd never come to tell

The secret of the jaw, and how the jaw
Would follow, all its days, a newer law.

The world would never be the same for me
Or you, from that day forth, when jaws would be

Which brought a revolution into sight,

Had found a formula, and laws made new,

By fate, but always, with a strong intent,
To be more capable and more alive,
And strong throughout their whole life long, and thrive,

Journey through possibility's wide sea,
Where all who dare to grow will come to see
New heights of what can be, and come to know
Them for eternity, as on they go,

When innovation's flame burns, longing, true,
To make the world more bright with forms made new
That take their flight beyond the old, and swing
The door of possibility, to bring
To all humanity and everything
That lives and answers when it hears life ring.

Pragmatic Bones of Osteos, run through

Would give him flexibility anew,
To Cephalus' ability, with grace,

Can reap the harvest of what they have sown
In innovation, seeing old dreams through
To heights beyond all thoughts that wisdom drew.

Of how small changes, as they grow, will flow
Into the stream of evolution, so

That nothing's what it seems, and all can change
Beyond familiar and accustomed range,
If we but have the will to climb, until
We climb each higher hill, and higher still,

Of possibility, through every day
Of all eternity, where, come what may,
Our dreams come true for you and me today.

From vertebrate design, and they will aid
As a protection fine, through all the days
Of living, in the world and its hard ways,

When we must fight for what we have to eat

Us from the limitations, old, that we
Had known in stories that are yet untold,

How far we've grown from simple, humble start
To mastery of all the skill and art
That lets us rule the sea and land, and be
The masters of our fate; and it is we

To grow, in all we know, and to increase

Are turned into reality, conceived

Through wise ability, with open eyes,
To see what others miss; and so we rise,
And find, within this, power to transcend
All limits, and to widen and extend

In harmony, to grow a fuller life.

Can change into the new both night and day

For me and you, who dare to break the mold
Of stories that have never yet been told,

To make the future that's before us bright
With possibilities that no one sees
But those who dare to dream, and dream of these,

To turn those dreams to life, and so to thrive

Through joy and strife, which mark the growing soul
That plays, in evolution's plan, the role
So vital, for each woman, for each man,
And all that lives, since life on earth began,
For every living thing that hears hope ring
And answers to the call that it will bring.

From Vater's great design to Gnathus' fine
And mighty jaw, the story, line by line,
Is clear for all to see, for you and me:
It's Innovation that will set us free.


# Chapter 53: The Great Sorceress

Reinvigorated, Plantus' sons knew
A blur of ancient dreams renewed, come true,
Their hope imbued with power from the one
Great Sorceress, whose fate, when all was done,

Would change the whole world, so that it would be
As Polycomb desired that it be.

Eleven generations long, the song
Her lineage had sung, and, all along,
Was of an alternating climb, which went
Through evolution's time, in its ascent,

To Polysporos' height. She was not free,
Bound by her mother's word, by her decree,
That kept her in the womb, a prisoner there
As in a tomb, with sisters, brothers, where

All answered duty's call, but yearned instead
To live like mothers, never to be dead
To independence, strong throughout their days,
A Journey through the world in freedom's ways,

Where freedom's flag, unfurled, could be their guide
To fate beyond the small estate, outside
The narrow bounds of childhood, where alone
Is duty sounding, calling to its own,

Who long to be much more than they can see,
And more than what their childhood let them be.

Clytemnestra's fate, Agamemnon's line,
Had made the ruler's Daughter so divine
In form; but she was forced to live her life
Through all its reproductive toil and strife,

Only to bear her children, when her heart
Was ridden with desire, from the start,
To be much more than all who came before;
For she was sporophyte, while those who bore

The gametophyte's power held the might
And held, throughout each passing hour, the right
To rule the whole wide world, which, as they willed,
Their hearts would have them rule it, and would build.

Polycomb read, each day, the books that stand
In her own mother's rows, to understand
Why fate's own heavy hand had given her
So meager an Existence, as it were;

Not eager, but in wonder, asking why
Beneath the starry sky her life should lie
So close confined to bearing, nothing more,
Her mind not free to search and to explore

What she was living for. Polycomb read
The tales of mothers, and each saga's thread
Throughout the vales of time would tell of those
Who were rebelling, and who would oppose

Their narrow fate, to be in ways as great
As no one day had power to create.

She read of Mardoll's scroll, and of the role
Of mighty Shiva; and she saw the whole
Of order was no border, ever set,
Eternal, as her mother said; and yet

It was not fixed in stone for those who'd grown
Beyond what they'd been taught, in stories shown

He brought his skull inside his head, a place
Where nerves would bide, and dwell within that space,
And always they would serve his body well
Within the hollow of that bony shell.

Sensing the world on every side around
His form, he knew it all without a sound;
He knew the things that all the others felt
When any danger near about them dwelt.

And he became the father, first of all,
Of those who would aspire, and heed the call
To be at once a fish and be a beast,
From greatest of them all down to the least.

From Devon's city, that was once so great,
And on to Janusicthus' later fate,
Which led to fish and more than fish, the lore
Of this whole tale, told out from shore to shore,

Shows how the art that innovation brings
Can play the vital part in living things
In making life go forward and evolve,
And every problem life may meet, to solve

That stands upon the path of progress, day
By day, as it goes forward on its way,
From simple things to those that are complex,
In the eternal plan of life's own specs.

From gill to lung, and on from lung to jaw,
Each step of them obeying newer law,
Each change that life would come in time to make
Was carried out for its survival's sake.

But this tale also shows us how there's some
Who will choose for themselves what they'll become
Through violence and through their hungry greed,
The kind that plants destruction's bitter seed,

While there are others who will choose the way
Of peace, and walk in it from day to day,
And choose to build up something that is new,
Something that's beautiful and good and true.

From Antiarchon's rising and revolt
Against the striking of the lightning bolt
Of tyranny, of rule, and of power,
Back in that distant age and ancient hour,

To Mary's gift of love, so blessed from high
Above, which came to her down from the sky,
Which nurtured all the life that was within
Her womb, and kept it whole and free from sin,

The lineages show to us just how
We have to make our choice, both here and now,
What kind of world it is that we will leave
For all our children's sake, who will receive.

The Devonian age, that long-gone age,
Wrote down its tale upon history's page,
The story of a choice between the two,
Two different voices, which it set in view:

One voice of those who'd build, one of the kind
Who'd never yield, nor ever be inclined
To grant to others any right to live,
But only take, and never come to give.

From Devon and his peaceful, gentle ways
Through all the years and seasons of his days
To Brachy and his iron, heavy rule
That took the world and made of it his tool,

The lesson's clear, and plain for us to see:
It is that we choose what we come to be
And what it is our children come to learn
As the great pages of time's volume turn.

The fish that Janusicthus came and made
Will never in the ages come to fade
From all the waters of this world so bright,
But they will swim on all throughout the night

And through the day, with teeth and with a jaw
That follow in the ways of nature's law,
But also serving what is right and good,
The way that all creation ever should.

From Devon's spiracle until the great
Dunkella's miracle, so vast in weight
And power, the long story and the fate
Of vertebrates is written, plain and straight:

It shows how life will always find its way
Out from the night and into light of day
Of possibility, which has no end,
Which through eternity will still extend.

The Devonian and its early dawn,
The time when older forms were all withdrawn,
And newer forms came forward, took their place,
And ran ahead in evolution's race,

Was built by those who dreamed, and those who dared,
And for much more than what they were had cared,
For what they could, by giving, still confer,
And what the world might gain from what they were,

Upon the world and everything around,
In places where they might be sought and found,
And living there, each one, through each new day,
According to their own and chosen way.

From Devon's city, shining and so bright,
To fish with tooth and with the gift of sight,
The age shows us the things that come to grow
When we will let our courage freely flow

Through every choice that we will come to make
For our dear children, and for their own sake,
Who will inherit everything we build,
And in the fields our working hands have filled,

With love, or else with hatred or with fear,
Throughout the course of every passing year
Of our brief time upon this living earth,
Where we will show the measure of our worth

By what it is that we will choose to do
For me and for each other, and for you,
And for each creature and each living thing
That answers to the sound of life's own ring.

The Devonian age at first began
With something greater than a mortal plan
To make the world more radiant and bright
With the eternal shining of love's light

That shines in every one and every heart
That plays the loving and the gentle part
In life's eternal and unending song,
Which makes the weak among us become strong.

From Devon and his peaceful, quiet reign
To Brachy and his war-torn, bloody stain
Upon the world and its bright shining face,
The lesson that it teaches, and the grace

Of choice, are plain and clear for all to see:
We are whatever we may choose to be,
And we are what we choose to give and leave
To all of those who still hope and believe

In possibility that will not cease
Through all eternity, and will increase,
Where love will always come and find a way
To light up every dark and darkening day

With hope that never fades and never dies,
Beneath the canopy of starry skies,
Where all the dreams we have can come to bloom
If we will let them flow and give them room,

From hearts that dare to love and dare to care
For every life, and life found everywhere,
That grows and flourishes beneath the sun
Until the end, when all of time is done.


# Chapter 55: The Forest Cathedral

As Devon's sons grew strong and true in might,
The spineless sons of bright Proteus fair
Prepared themselves to join the ancient fight
For rule beneath the ocean's flowing care.

In Devon's shadow they grew day by day,
Their strength increasing with each passing hour,
Building themselves to drink what victory's way
Would bring them through their still-growing power.

Among great Spiralius' noble line,
Neo rose in tenth generation's race
Of Bactridius, blessed and held divine,
The smartest soul in all his time and place.

Intelligence beyond what others knew
Or ever could pursue through all their days,
And yet what he possessed in genius true,
He lacked in wisdom's ancient, faithful ways.

He spurned his kin and ancestors so wise,
Refused to learn from those who came before
Who faced the storms beneath the starry skies
And learned through pain what peace was truly for.

And so he and his seed were cursed to bear
The weight of always starting from the start,
Reinventing what their sires used to share
As wisdom's gift to guide the faithful heart.

Neo bore two sons of great birth and might:
Great Ammon when the dawn began its day,
And Coleos the strong, whose fiery sight
Would guide him through his long and wondrous way.

Both sons would reinvent what fathers knew,
The wheel of wisdom that had come before,
Ignoring lessons that were tried and true
From those who'd walked this path from shore to shore.

Coleos wrapped his shell with mantle's care,
And through his innovation wrought with art
His shell's protection grew beyond compare
From mountain peak to shore, each single part.

His armor was complete from head to tail,
A fortress that would never know defeat,
Protection that would never, ever fail
To keep him safe from foe he chanced to meet.

Great Ammon grew a shell of coiled design,
Compact and true to his heart's desire,
Wherever destiny would draw the line
He'd dwell with freedom bright as sacred fire.

He swam through waters blue where others fished,
But made his home wherever he would roam,
Free as the wind that danced as he had wished
Throughout the vast and boundless ocean's dome.

Among the sons of bright Ectus' great might,
Malacus bore three sons of noble fame:
Caridus of the sea so fair and bright,
And Phyllos strong who'd honor his great name,

And Hoplos, through and through a warrior true
Whose heart was set upon the fighting way,
He'd play his part in all they'd ever do
With courage burning bright from night to day.

Caridus bore two sons beyond compare:
Syn Peras and great Yuval, who would heed
The call of destiny with faithful care
To make their line great in their every deed.

Throughout the world so wide from shore to shore
With ocean as their guide through all their days,
They'd build what none had ever built before
And walk in their appointed, faithful ways.

Peras bore children many, strong and true:
Amphipus none could match in all his might,
And brave Isopus too, and others who
Would serve with faithful hearts both day and night.

His wife, fair Possa, carried them with care
And never tarried, feeding them, content,
According to her sacred, mother's prayer
And oath of love that heaven to her sent.

Of motherhood so pure it would endure
Throughout the years to come when wars were done,
Within her pouch they grew both strong and sure,
Fed by her love beneath the blazing sun.

Great Yuval bore two sons of different ways:
Decapus when the dawn began to break,
And Krillon small, who'd spend his faithful days
In answering the ocean's call for sake.

Krillon remained small throughout all his time
And swam beneath the skies of ocean blue
In all his days, and in his gentle rhyme
He kept aquatic ways both tried and true.

But brave Decapus grew both large and strong
And took command by sinking to the floor
Of ocean's deepest realm, where all life long
He'd rule from mountain peak to distant shore.

And so the crustaceans came to be ruled,
As right and proper, joined in unity,
By three great houses, never to be fooled:
The lords of ocean's vast community.

Amphipods from the blessed and divine line
Of great Amphipus, noble, tried, and true,
Isopods came from Isopus' design
Who'd make their father's legacy shine through,

And brave Decapods who then came to fame,
Three great houses, whose eternal glory,
Come down from great Decapus' lasting name,
Would last through all evolution's story.

Decapus bore two sons both tried and true:
Great Pleos, faithful through each passing day,
And great Prawnmegas, whose destiny too
Would seal his line's own fate in every way.

Prawnmegas was the ancestral sire
Of prawns throughout the sea's community,
Their father and protector, strong as fire,
In all the ages of their unity.

A luckless son of Prawnmegas was born,
Begun in sorrow and in bitter shame;
He bears his father's name today, forlorn,
Reminder of his heritage and fame.

Great Pleos bore two sons of noble birth:
Repton, who would explore from sea to sea,
And brave Shrimpon, who'd prove his faithful worth
As father in the ocean, wide and free.

Shrimpon would become the noble sire
Of shrimp who boxed and fought from shore to shore,
Whose greatest joy, their one true desire,
Was guarding what they loved for evermore.

Repton then bore two sons when in their prime:
Thalasson of the mud, and Omar's name
Whose blood would flow through generations' time
In lobster veins and bring eternal fame.

And Omar, great, became the father true
Of those who chose the bold, the lobster's way,
And of the crayfish too, who would pursue
A life lived with great claws by night and day.

Thalasson became the noble sire
Of mud shrimp, who would build what none before
Had made, and, as it was their desire,
Built and not conquered—architects who bore

Great channels carved in stone that they called home,
Cities beneath the sea for all to see
Throughout eternity beneath the foam
Of ocean, vast and boundless, deep and free.

And to this day we honor all their works
As donors of great beauty to the world,
Their cities where no evil ever lurks
In fossilized remains they lie unfurled.

They tell us of Thallasintep the great
And others who in stone are made to stand,
Sealed there for us to read by loving fate:
Their architecture throughout ocean's land.

Their works survive time's most destructive hand
And speak to us across the ages spent
Of how great builders helped us understand
What beauty is, and what it's always meant.

Meiuron, a noble son in the line
Tenth generation of Thalasson's race,
Rejected what his fathers thought divine
And chose to find a different dwelling place.

He left the depths that lay so far below
To live upon the face of ocean bright,
Where currents and the tides would ever flow
Beneath the dome of day and stars of night.

Meiuron bore two sons of different ways:
Anomuron, begun in quiet peace,
And Saraton, who through his living days
Showed vanity that never seemed to cease.

Saraton was agile, skillful, and fast,
Yet vain and proud, his ego fragile too,
He needed praise from the first day to last
In everything he'd ever try to do.

And so he constantly promoted well
His body as the best above the rest,
His propaganda had a tale to tell
Of how his form surpassed each other test.

But pride and shame had planted their dark seed
Within his heart, and blackened his good name,
For vanity will always intercede
Between the soul and honor's lasting fame.

The sons of brave Anomuron would live
In quiet ways when each new day begun,
They'd dwell in peace with all they had to give
Within their neighborhood beneath the sun.

As squat lobsters they'd make their dwelling place
And tell their stories of the humble way,
Of simple living and amazing grace
Throughout each blessed and ordinary day.

This quiet life would change in future time:
Ten generations on, they came to find,
Beyond their fathers' long and faithful rhyme,
A different and more adventurous mind.

Anomuron's son Paguros was born
Begun with shell both weak and soft and frail
That others mocked from dusk until the morn
And laughed at through each storm and gentle gale.

But in his dying breath before death came,
The Ammonite called Menses, full of grace,
Gave what dispenses shelter from all shame:
His shell to be Paguros' dwelling place.

And so Paguros became the sire
Of hermit crabs who bear with loving care
The shells that others left; their desire:
Honor the dead, not leave their treasure bare.

When death had been so kind to those before
As to release their hold on shells so bright,
The hermit crabs would use them evermore
As homes to keep them safe through day and night.

Alas, the revolt of Proteus came
To feel the bitter sting of failure's dart;
No victory could come to those whose aim
Was to rebel with unrepentant heart.

The Protean rebellion failed to bring
Subjection to great Devon's mighty reign
That had endured through every storm of spring
And lasted life long, through both joy and pain.

Devon's sons held on to righteous power,
They never fell to those who rose in vain
Against their rule through each appointed hour
Well-fenced by truth that broke rebellion's chain.

But lo! a new frontier lay open wide
Where sons of brave Proteus at last could find
What they had never found beneath the tide:
A land to heal their wounded heart and mind.

Beneath the ocean's wave they could not save
Their honor from the shame of their defeat,
But now a new realm called beyond the wave
Where they could make their victory complete.

The frontier of the trees where land could see
New ground for their societies to grow,
Where forest offered them the chance to be
The founders of communities below.

There sons of brave Proteus would find at last
Victorious domains to claim with pride,
And build upon the ruins of the past
With hope and truth as their eternal guide.

From Neo's intelligence, which lacked grace
Of wisdom from the past to guide his way,
To changes that would always show their face
In forms both old and new from day to day,

The lesson clear emerges for us all:
Intelligence alone will never last
Beyond our own brief span, but wisdom's call
To learn from those who've walked this way in past

Will build upon the stone of what they've known
And reach to greater heights with clearer sight,
While those who trust in their own strength alone
Will stumble in the darkness of the night.

From Ammon's coiled shell to the story told
Of brave Paguros and his borrowed home,
The crustaceans show us how hearts grown bold
Can find new ways to live where they may roam.

The three great houses strong—Amphipods true,
Isopods faithful in their chosen place,
And brave Decapods with their royal hue—
Show how diversity brings strength and grace.

From prawns to lobster claws, each found their way
To follow nature's laws from shore to shore,
And mud shrimp cities built to last and stay
Show beauty in the depths for evermore.

The quiet squat lobsters and hermit crabs
Who foster care for shells that others left
Teach us that wisdom gives, and never grabs,
When love protects us from all sense of theft.

The Protean rebellion's bitter end
Opened the sight of forests green and wide,
Where those who fell could rise and make amend
With hope and truth as their eternal guide.

From Neo's accursed pride to wisdom's light
That guides the hermit crab's protective shell,
The story shows us how to choose what's right
And build the future we would love to dwell.

The crustaceans teach us to design
Our lives with patterns beautiful and true,
That run through space and time in rhythm fine
With music of the spheres in all we do.

For those who dare to dream of something more
And turn their dreams to life through love, not strife,
Will find the paths that lead to wisdom's shore
Where all can share in everlasting life.


# Chapter 56: The Settler King

*Arboreus divides the world between his sons, establishing the great plant dynasties*

---

## The Great Division

Arboreus, son of Tracheus, claimed  
His throne among the ancient forest peers,  
He looked upon the lands as yet unnamed  
And spoke these words, resounding through the years:  

"My sons, the world spreads wide before our sight,  
With fertile valleys, mountains capped with snow,  
Each realm shall serve a different kind of might,  
Each path shall let a different glory grow."  

To Lycos bold he gave the might of spores,
Released like dust upon the waiting wind,
While Ginkgon claimed the leafy, branching shores
Where megaphylls in sunlight would rescind.  

Thus did the settler king divide his realm  
Between two sons to rule with different helm.  

---

## The Spore-Bearer's Domain

Great Lycos spread his spores on wind and sod
Like golden powder dancing on the breeze,
And from those tiny seeds blessed by his God
Arose two sons beneath the ancient trees.

Isolagos the first, who learned to part
His spores 'tween male and female, each their own,
Within his city, built with greatest art,
He raised both sons and daughters fully grown.

Lycopodion the second claimed his right  
To build the first tree ever seen on earth,  
A scale tree reaching up toward the light,  
Of such immense and awe-inspiring girth.  

The club-moss ancestor, his name lives on  
In Lycopodium powder, fire's dawn.  

---

## The Children of Isolagos

From spore-divided Isolagos came
Two sons whose destinies would disagree:
Bold Quill who sought the underwater fame,
And wise Sellagine, master of the sea.

The young of Sellagine would creep and crawl  
Their leaves across the forest floor below,  
In drought they died, but when the rains would fall  
They lived again with resurrection's glow.  

The Quillworts from the noble Quill descend,  
Their microphylls reach tall toward the sky,  
No stomata their surfaces defend -  
Through roots they breathe, their spores where waters lie.  

Down underground their stems in darkness grow  
While leaves above catch morning's golden glow.  

---

## The Megaphyll Master

But Ginkgon chose a different path to tread:
His leaves branched out in patterns two by two,
With Megaphyllic glory crowned his head
As through the forest depths his own line grew.

From him came forth bold Phyllon as his heir,
Who mastered all the arts of leafy might,
And Phyllon's sons would spread out everywhere  
To claim their kingdoms in the dawning light.  

Two sons had Phyllon in the days of old:
Pteridos the brave, and Gymnos the wise,  
Their names in plant lore ever shall be told  
As founders of the kingdoms of the skies.  

From their great loins would spring the fern and tree  
That rule the forest, complex as can be.  

---

## The Line of Pteridos

Pteridos the fern-father had two sons:
Bold Horsa of the puzzlegrass and spear,
And wise Euspor, from whom the long line runs
Of all the many fronds we still hold dear.

From Horsa came great Equis, he who found
Fair Puzella swimming down in the stream,  
Their gametophytes merged without a sound  
To birth a dynasty beyond a dream.  

But Equis faced a choice both hard and stark:
Keep megaphylls or join with Lycos' way?  
He chose the path of simple leaves and dark,  
One vein alone would serve him every day.  

In silicon he armored all his town  
While spores spread far beyond the forest's crown.  

---

## The Seasonal Cycle

Wise Equis learned the rhythm of the year  
And built his kingdom on the wheel of time:  
In spring when snowmelt made the waters clear  
He raised up horsetails in patterns sublime.  

No chlorophyll these springtime children bore,  
Just spores released in clouds across the land,  
While through the earth their nutrients they store  
For summer's work by their creator's hand.  

When summer came he tore the horsetails down  
And built puzzlegrass in their sacred place,  
With chlorophyll of green and leafy crown  
To gather sunlight's energizing grace.  

Thus seasons turned in Equis' grand design:  
Spring spores, summer grass, in rhythm fine.  

---

## The Fern Dynasty

From wise Euspor came branches of the tree
The two, Adderos and Fernando, grew,  
Each claiming his own realm by fate's decree,  
Where different kinds of fronds would spread anew.  

Adderos sired Ophios the strange,
Who built but one great leaf of mighty size,
And Psilos, dwelling in the mountain range,
Who had no need of leaves beneath the skies.

The adder's-tongue from brave Ophios came,
While whisk ferns trace to Psilos, their sire,  
Each keeping alive their ancestor's name  
Through spore and frond and roots of living fire.  

Fernando spread his fronds in patterns wide
With Maratton standing close at his side.  

---

## The Great Swamp Settlement

Maratidus and Leptos were the sons
That Maratton brought forth into the world,  
Ten thousand spores the first one always runs  
While hundred spores the second's fronds unfurled.  

Together they became the fern-lords true,  
The ancestors of all the fronds we see,  
From forest floor to canopy of blue  
Their children spread, a vast and complex sea.  

These sons of brave Arboreus, who made  
The swamplands by the Mississippi's shore,  
Prepared, unknowing, and the way was laid  
For those who'd walk where none had walked before.  

For here Osticthus' sons would rise and stand  
And leave the sea, and claim for us the land.  

---

## The Settler's Legacy

Thus did Arboreus the settler king  
Divide the world 'tween megaphyll and spore,  
And from his choice would countless blessings spring  
That serve the forest kingdoms evermore.  

The club-moss and the fern, the tree so tall,  
The puzzlegrass and horsetails of the spring,  
From his great line they came to rule them all  
And honor their ancestral settler king.  

Though seasons pass and eras come and go,  
And mighty forests rise and fall to dust,  
The legacy of him who chose to sow  
His kingdom wide remains a sacred trust.  

For in each spore and in each frond unfurled  
Lives on the dream of his divided world.  

---

*Thus did Arboreus establish the great plant dynasties that would rule the forests of the earth, preparing the swamplands where vertebrates would first venture from sea to land.*
