# Chapter 253: The Chronicle of the Popes

Now count the keys. Not fathers, for these men
Were sworn to have no sons, and some had sons,
But hands on heads, the laying-on, and then
The next head bowed, the next, until it runs

Fifteen centuries, fisherman to king
Who cut the world in half with lines of ink.
Here is the chronicle; here is the ring
Passed hand to hand; here is the missing link

Between the {{p|Galilee}} and age of sail.
Hear it as old ones heard the kings of {{p|Ur}}:
One name, then one name, then the folded tale,
And then the next. Here, then, the keepers were:

Peter the fisherman, who thrice denied
Before the rooster, then did not deny,
Hung head-down there in {{p|Rome}}; for he had cried
The dead man lived. Head down, he asked to die,

Unworthy of the cross his teacher bore.
Then Linus; then Cletus; then Clement, who
Wrote {{p|Corinth}} in its quarrel: peace, no more
Of schisms. Bishops of a sect, who knew

The tufa, where below the dead were laid,
Their names scratched in the catacombs' neglect,
Martyrs by dozens, bishops by the blade.
Callixtus, once a slave, whose debts had wrecked

A banker's money, sent to mills; then came
Pontian, to {{p|Sardinia}}'s mines to die,
Who first laid down the keys. Fabian's name
Came with a dove that settled from the sky

Upon his head; a layman, farmer, killed
Under Decius; and the dove was gone.
Then came Sylvester, and the world, unstilled:
Constantine had seen a sign in the dawn:

The sun unconquered, Sol Invictus, friend
Of soldiers, and he made the two suns one;
The persecuted, palace in the end;
The fisher's tomb a church of gold, begun.

Four hundred years on, in some room, a hand
Forged that great Constantine had given all
The {{p|West}} to Sylvester: {{x|Donation}}'s land,
For seven hundred years, till Valla's call

Laid bare its {{x|Latin}}, bad; remember well
What forgers meant. And Damasus, who set
Jerome to make one book where street-tongues dwell,
The {{x|Vulgate}}; Leo, called the Great, who met

Attila at {{p|Mincio}}; his look
Turned back the king. And Gregory, who named
Himself the servants' servant; by the book
And sent a monk to {{p|Kent}}; then, unashamed,

Leo III, on {{x|Christmas Day}}, who bent
Above Charlemagne at prayer, and laid
Unasked, the Romans' crown; and his descent
Runs on: near all of {{p|Europe}}'s blood is made

From him; each line of {{p|Europe}} opens there.
Then came the years of the great houses. Mark:
Formosus, dug up, propped up in the chair,
Dressed in his robes, a deacon set to bark

At the {{x|Cadaver Synod}}, his replies;
Condemned, his blessing fingers cut away,
Thrown in the {{p|Tiber}}; and the bishops' eyes
Looked on; then Theophylact's house held sway.

Marozia, his daughter, loved a pope,
Sergius; and bore a pope, named John; and then
Grandmother of a pope: a household's scope
To make and unmake keys, like kings, again

By blood. Her grandson, John XII, a boy
Just eighteen, who crowned Otto, and who died
Within a married woman's bed. The toy,
The keys: one house, one line, one blood applied.

And this book honors lines of blood; it must
Say plainly that the chair the church endows
With heaven was a household's; that is just.
And then came Gerbert, made a pope, whose vows

As Sylvester II brought the {{x|Arab}} lines
Of numbers westward, and the sky's own key,
The abacus; at one thousand, the signs
Were read as doom; the world went on, set free.

In ten-fifty-four, legates at the stone
At {{p|Hagia Sophia}} laid their bull
Of excommunication; {{p|East}}, alone,
And {{p|West}} drew apart, never again full.

Gregory VII; and Henry in the snow
Three days barefoot there at {{p|Canossa}}'s gate,
Begging forgiveness; and the pope, so slow,
Who let him go when he had made him wait.

{{p|Clermont-Ferrand}}: Urban cried, "God wills it."
Crosses sewn on; to {{p|Jerusalem}} ran
The road, paved with the slain; and, with the writ
Of Innocent III, kings' lord; the plan

Forbade the sack, and watched them sack the town
Of {{p|Istanbul}}, a {{x|Christian}} city, in
Twelve hundred four. Celestine laid down
The keys, a hermit who could not begin.

Then Boniface: {{x|One Holy Church}}, each soul
Is subject to the {{x|Roman}} pontiff; slapped
At {{p|Anagni}} by French king's men; the whole
Court went to {{p|Avignon}}; its money, trapped,

{{x|Babylonian Captivity}}'s gold,
Flowed seventy years to the {{p|Rhône}}; till home
Saint Catherine of {{p|Siena}} called them, bold;
They came; and chose two popes. Then three. Which, {{p|Rome}}?

Each throne cursed all the others; at the last
At {{p|Konstanz}} then a council made them one,
Deposed them all, crowned Martin; it had cast
Jan Hus to flame two years before; and none

In {{p|Bohemia}} forgot; scholars came:
Nicholas, who built the {{x|Library}} walls
When Turks took {{p|Istanbul}} for good; the name
Of Sixtus, builder of the {{x|Chapel}}'s great halls,

Whose ceiling would be painted by a man
On scaffolding four years, head bent; and then,
And last, Rodrigo Borgia; and his clan,
Called Alexander VI; his children; then

Cesare and Lucrezia, and the slow
Poison of rumor. Fourteen ninety-three:
He drew a line from pole to pole, to show
West of it {{p|Spain}}'s, east {{p|Portugal}}'s, the sea.

At {{p|Tordesillas}} the kings moved it west;
The fisherman's heir split between two crowns
A world they'd never seen; and all the rest,
Taíno, Inca, Tupinambá, the towns,

Unasked. The plan was paper, and the writ
The paper. Fifteen hundred years: a line
From fisherman to this; and, keeping it,
Some saints, some murderers; the chair divine;

Most were but keepers; what passed was the ghost,
And hands on heads, and then the name, the list
Read out: an institution is, almost,
A family. In {{p|Eisleben}}, to twist

The keys, a miner's son was born. Here ends
The chronicle; the keys pass on, and on;
Count them, and keep the list: the house it tends
Is the list. Here the reckoning comes on.


# Chapter 254: The Sea Joins the World

Before this there were many seas. The old
{{p|Indian Ocean}}, Pharaohs' market, where
The dhow rode the monsoon out and back; gold,
Cloth, pepper, kept the merchant's vow; and there

{{p|Kilwa}}, and the cottons of {{p|Gujarat}};
{{p|Atlantic}}, an edge; {{p|Pacific}}, untold;
Each sea was its own world, and that was that.
Then Vasco da Gama sailed out, the bold,

Four ships, from {{p|Lisbon}}, ninety-seven, swung
Wide in the empty ocean, past the coast,
Three months from land, the sky their chart; among
Their tools, the astrolabe their priest, their host.

They rounded the {{p|Cape of Good Hope}} in cold,
Up {{p|Africa}}'s east coast to {{p|Mozambique}},
{{p|Mombasa}}, and {{p|Malindi}}, where, as told,
A pilot waited, one they came to seek,

Who knew the monsoon as one knows a psalm,
And took them, May of fourteen ninety-eight,
Across the ocean, open as a palm,
To {{p|Kozhikode}}. And here the hinge, the gate:

They went into a temple, knelt, and prayed
Before a goddess, and they did not cringe,
Thinking her Mary; and the priests, arrayed,
Were Christians of some old rite's broken fringe.

They saw the saints with many arms, the feasts,
The lamps, the holy water, and the light;
They saw what they had come to see; the priests,
The Hindus, saw the strangers in their sight

Bow to the Mother, and they let them bow.
One afternoon the world was joined; the law
Of all rites whole, in one unbroken now
Of no mistake. Then trade: the ruler saw

No worth in gifts, the Zamorin: some cloth,
Some hats, some honey; Arab merchants laughed;
They sailed home with the pepper, rats, and froth
Of scurvy, which killed more than half their craft;

The pepper paid the voyage sixty-fold.
And so the sea was opened, and the hem
Of the world lifted. Hear the price, as told:
Da Gama came again, with guns; and them,

The pilgrims, homeward bound from {{p|Mecca}}, he
Took on the Miri, stripped and burned; the gold
The women offered for their lives; the sea
Took children in the flame. Then, harsh and bold,

Albuquerque took {{p|Goa}}, took the strait,
{{p|Melaka}}, where all {{p|Asia}}'s trade ran through,
The gate of cloves; the free bazaar, of late,
Became a toll road; pay, or burn, they knew.

Cabral, in fifteen hundred, sailed too far
West, bound for {{p|India}}, and found {{p|Brazil}},
The turning of a wheel, beneath a star,
Over the Tupi like a thunder's will.

Magellan, Portuguese, who sailed for {{p|Spain}},
Five ships from {{p|Sanlúcar}}, fifteen nineteen,
To find a western road, the cloves to gain
For Charles from far {{p|Maluku}}'s isles of green,

To bind the line the Pope had drawn, around
The world. In {{p|Patagonia}}, revolt,
Beheadings, maroonings; the strait, the sound
Of curled channels, thirty-eight days, the jolt

Of williwaw on rock; then waters, bright,
So calm he named it the {{p|Pacific}}, wide;
Ninety-eight days across, the biscuit's blight
To dust and worms; they ate the rigging's hide,

And rats; gums swelled over their teeth; and then
{{p|Guam}}, and the islands of the {{p|Philippines}},
Where Enrique, slave, of {{p|Melaka}}'s men,
Bought at its conquest, spoke, and in those scenes

The islanders could understand. He'd come
Home the long way around; the first to stand
Again at his beginning, going from
Home, around all the world, to his own land.

Remember him. Magellan, at {{p|Cebu}},
Baptized a king, and wanted more; he fought
Lapulapu on {{p|Mactan}}'s beach; and through
The shallows fell, in April; the shore caught

Him, fifteen twenty-one; and, as was due,
Lapulapu kept his island; the rest,
Two ships, then one, Victoria, sailed true
Under Elcano; at {{p|Tidore}}, the best

Cloves found; west through the {{p|Indian Ocean}},
The {{p|Cape}}, and the {{p|Atlantic}} home, to land
{{p|Sanlúcar}}, in September's slow motion,
Of fifteen twenty-two: eighteen men stand

Of two hundred seventy, bone and skin;
A day off: Wednesday, said the log; and yet
Thursday it was; they'd lost the day within
Their sailing with the sun; and {{p|Venice}} set

Its learned men to work out why; the {{p|Earth}}
Was measured turning by a ship. Admit
What this means: one world now, one sea, one girth,
One sphere to sail around; one market, knit;

One reckoning of days. The many grown
One world, by cannon, pepper, and by sun.
The joining starts: by force; the temple's stone;
A knee; a slave who came home; hold each one.

Hold all three. The sea joins; and from this day
The world will not be free of all the rest
Of the world; many seas have passed away:
One sea, one sphere, one market, east to west.


# Chapter 255: There Is No Hindu, There Is No Muslim

In {{p|Punjab}}, land of the five rivers, where
The {{p|Kabul}} road came down to {{p|Delhi}}'s land
With armies, at {{p|Sultanpur Lodhi}}, there
A clerk kept grain and salt by measure's hand,

And counted out the rations: one, two, three,
To thirteen; and at thirteen, *tera*, which
Means also *yours*; he stopped, and could not see
Beyond it: yours, yours, yours. The ledger's stitch

Unraveled there. His name was Nanak, son
Of Kalu, an accountant, Khatri-line,
Born at {{p|Nankana Sahib}}; there begun
In fourteen sixty-nine. A teacher's line

To copy, and he wrote a hymn instead.
They married him; he gave the store away.
Near fourteen ninety-nine, his feet were led
Down to the {{p|Kali Bein}} to bathe one day,

And did not rise. Three days they dragged the stream
And thought him drowned. And on the third, he came
Out of the water, silent, as from dream,
And said: "There is no Hindu." And the same:

"There is no Muslim." That was first: no creed;
The ground was cleared. The governor then asked:
"Whose path, then, do you follow?" "God's"; indeed,
Said Nanak; and at prayer-time, as was tasked,

He went with governor and qazi in
The mosque, and did not bow; and asked why, said:
"Your body bowed; your mind, in {{p|Kabul}}, in
The market, buying horses; his instead,

The qazi's, at home, where a mare had bred,
Afraid her foal would fall into the well."
So he would pray with them, the master said,
When they would pray. And that is what befell.

Four journeys, the udasis, south and east,
North to the mountains, west to {{p|Mecca}}, too,
With Mardana, Muslim; rebab's feast
Of music, while the Hindu-born sang through

The hymns. At {{p|Haridwar}}, where pilgrims threw
The {{p|Ganges}} water east, toward the sun,
To reach their dead, he threw it west. "And you?"
"I water fields in {{p|Punjab}}; if yours run

Up to the sun, mine reach three hundred miles."
At {{p|Mecca}} he lay down, his feet toward
The Kaaba; kicked ("At God!"), he said, with smiles:
"Then turn my feet where God is not, my lord."

The ground stayed still. And sure are the hymns: some
Nine hundred, in Punjabi, and the tongue
Mixed, of the poor; the Bhakti, Sufis, come
Together; one God, formless, fearless, sung:

Without hatred, not born, and never dead,
Self-being, known by grace; *{{x|Ik Onkar}}*: one;
Creator; the {{x|True Name}}; and daily read,
The Japji, still sung at rising sun.

Then Babur came from {{p|Kabul}} like the flood,
First of the Mughals, sacked {{p|Eminabad}};
The hymns of Nanak witness it: the blood,
The women of good houses dragged; and sad,

He sang: God sent the Mughal as death's hand;
No war-song, a lament; reproach, as well,
To God: "Did you feel no pain?" On the land
By the {{p|Ravi}}, at {{p|Kartarpur}}, to dwell,

He farmed, and built a kitchen where in one
Row on the floor sat high caste and the low,
The Muslim and the Hindu, none outdone,
The *langar*, feeding thousands now; and so

The meal was doctrine: eating, side by side,
Was being one. And, old, he passed the light
Not to his sons, as kings, nor kin allied,
But to disciple Lehna, who did right,

Who bore wet grass upon his head, and went
Into the mud; he named him Angad, "limb
Of my own"; bowed to him: the light was lent
Lamp to lamp, not by blood, not by the whim

Marozia's house had with keys. And so,
In fifteen thirty-nine, he died; and they,
The Hindus, wished to burn; and to bestow
The Muslims, burial; and, in their fray,

They lifted up the cloth: and there were spread
But flowers. They divided them, and each
Did as its custom said; and of the dead
Two shrines stand now, a border out of reach

Of each to each; and pilgrims, through the field,
Cross into {{p|Pakistan}}, to its shrine's door,
To {{p|Kartarpur}}. A synthesis was sealed
In a new folk, a third thing, something more

Than bridge, since bridges had nowhere to stand.
This is the lesson of the clearing: found,
Say "no Hindu, no Muslim," and the land
Will make of you a third, and named, and bound

Within its border. Still the clearest said
In this age: Muslim, Hindu, none; the floor
Is where you sit; and shown to all, the bread;
Eat; turn the page; and clear the ground once more.


# Chapter 256: The Red Heads

At {{p|Ardabil}}, beneath the mountain, came
Safi, a holy man, who taught the way
Within: remembrance, breath, the circling flame
Of dervishes, the soul's return; his day

Ended in thirteen thirty-four. His line
Kept up the lodge, and gifts made the lodge strong
From Turkmen tribes, down from the high incline
Of {{p|Anatolia}}; and, before long,

The sheikhs began to ride, and died in war;
The brotherhood became a host; the red
Twelve-folded caps, one per imam, they wore,
The Qizilbash: Red Heads, the foe said.

Who holds the cap holds faith; faith holds the sword.
Then Ismail, a young child of seven, hid
By the {{p|Caspian}}, father, brother gored,
Came out, called by the Red Heads, as they bid,

And took {{p|Tabriz}} at fourteen, and was crowned
{{x|Shah}}, fifteen-oh-one; at Friday prayer
He made them read the creed with new words found:
"And Ali is God's friend." The law, from there,

Was now the {{x|Twelver}} {{x|Shia}}, in a land
Most Sunni; those who would not curse the first
Three caliphs, so the new state's law would stand,
Could die; and many cursed; some, at the worst,

Refused, and died. The boy wrote Turkish verse
As Khatai, the Sinner: "I am light
Of God, Ali; I am the universe;
Come, bow to the eternal one." In fight,

His soldiers wore no armor; they believed.
That is the holy king: the cloth was spun
One thread, the saint and king, the one received
In one red cap, the faith and kingdom one.

The brotherhood that sought the self in God
was crowned in him, and found the one it sought,
and made a nation by the creed and rod
of Ali's house, the heir {{p|Tabriz}} was taught.

He sent for teachers, and the scholars came
from {{p|Jabal Amil}}, on {{p|Lebanon}}'s hills,
to write the books and keep the mourning's flame
for Ali's line, as every old house wills:

So the faith of {{p|Iran}}, that seems so old,
with the Ashura mourning, and the shrine,
is old: the house of Ali, which a bold
young king set on a throne in one straight line

Of conquest; a century held it true.
Remember: it was the old faith, foretold.
At {{p|Çaldıran}}, fifteen-fourteen: Selim knew,
The Ottoman, the Red Heads' charge; he rolled

His cannon to the front, chained wheel to wheel;
The boy who was God charged into the guns.
God lost; Ottomans took {{p|Tabriz}}; the steel
Of old ways, cavalry, believers, sons

Of Turkmen tents, broke on the cannon's ball.
And Ismail never smiled again; he drank,
Hunted, and led no army after all;
He died at thirty-six; his house held rank.

The state stood, and that matters, and the faith;
Great-grandson Shah Abbas, in {{p|Isfahan}}, laid
The square, the blue domes, *half the world*; the wraith
Of Red Heads tamed into an army, made

A modern state. One more thing, for this book:
And Ismail's mother was the child, by right,
Of Uzun Hasan, {{x|White Sheep}} khan; he took
To wife Theodora, Despina, bright,

A {{x|Christian}} princess of {{p|Trabzon}}, the last
Komnene line of the Greeks by the {{p|Black Sea}};
So {{x|Shia}} {{p|Iran}}'s founder, then, was cast
From Komnenoi, {{x|Greek Orthodox}}; and see:

The blood is mixed, as every house is: one.
Greek mother, Turkmen father: the house weaves.
And now the clergy whom he called have won
The state itself, and rule beneath its eaves.

From {{p|Ardabil}} to {{p|Tehran}}: mystic, sword,
Boy, scholar, jurist, ruler; each one sure
It kept the old faith, each its own reward.
Say of the Safavids: they would endure

as saints and kings at once; the sword was long,
and the longing for God sat on the throne
in the same red cap; and the nation's song
was woven in one lifetime, and was known

As it had always been. He believed it.
He did not lie; he was right; he was young,
Hidden, and saved; and so he received it:
God's mystery, in {{x|Turkish}}, his own tongue.


# Chapter 257: Here I Stand

The miner's son from {{p|Eisleben}} was meant
To be a lawyer; Hans, his father, planned
On copper; but in fifteen-oh-five, sent
Near {{p|Stotternheim}}, the thunderbolt's hand

Threw him down; and, afraid, he cried for aid
To Saint Anne, miners' saint: "Help, and a monk
I'll be." He kept the vow; and so he made
His way to Augustinians, and sunk

In {{p|Erfurt}} into fast, confession, dread,
Not knowing how to be righteous for God;
Confessor: "Sin a sin worth this," he said;
At {{p|Rome}}, he climbed the stairs on knees, and trod

Up to the top, and thought: "Who knows it's so?"
The blessing did not come; he taught; a sign
At {{p|Wittenberg}}, small {{x|Saxon}} town, and slow,
He read the Letter to the Romans' line:

"The just shall live by faith." Not by the stairs,
Nor by the crown of works, nor by the gold.
And then the money came: a friar's wares,
Tetzel's indulgences, across the fold

Of border, for {{p|Saint Peter's}} building, and
The debt of Albrecht, {{p|Mainz}}, who bought his see:
"As soon as coin in coffer rings," the band,
"Of souls from purgatory springs out free."

And {{p|Saxony}}'s poor walked across to buy.
October's last day, fifteen-seventeen,
Luther wrote ninety-five theses; asked why
The pope won't, out of love, the place make clean,

Not for the coin; in {{x|Latin}}, for debate;
He nailed them to the church door, and he sent
Them to the archbishop; in weeks, the weight
Of print spread them, {{x|German}} by spring, and went

Across the realm; the {{x|Latin}} curtain tore.
This Gutenberg had made, not knowing, when
Seventy years before: a voice that, more
Than it was burned, was set in type; and then

Hus had been burned; Luther was printed: choice
Of weapon. Then his pamphlets, more and more,
Than any in all {{p|Europe}}'s, found their voice:
*To the {{x|Christian Nobility}}*; the core,

*The {{x|Babylonian Captivity}}*;
Small books, and cheap, through all the markets spread;
*The {{x|Freedom of a Christian}}*, liberty
Aloud in taverns to the unread, said.

The Pope then cast him out; and Luther burned
The bull in flames before the city gate;
At {{p|Worms}}, in fifteen twenty-one, he turned
Before young Charles, the emperor, whose great

And {{x|Habsburg}} jaw ruled half the world; and asked
Recant, he begged a day; came back, and said:
"Unless by {{x|Scripture}} and plain reason tasked
I am convinced, I cannot; I am led,

"My conscience captive to the {{x|Word of God}};
Here I stand; I can do no other." Then
A friendly kidnap, Frederick's, and odd,
Of {{p|Saxony}}, hid him in {{p|Wartburg}}'s den,

As Junker Jörg, with beard, in a dark cell
In eleven weeks, put into {{x|German}}
The New Testament, speech that mothers tell,
The street, the market; once the priest's sermon,

Was now the household's book; the book's own tongue
Became the land's: {{x|High German}} is, in part,
The speech that Luther chose; and so, among
The folk, the scripture left the priest's own art.

Now hold both sides. In fifteen twenty-five
The peasants rose, read the same book, with pride;
The princes slaughtered them; he wrote, to drive
Them: "Smite, slay, stab"; a hundred thousand died.

Who tells the poor the book is theirs must be
Ready for them to read; he was not; old,
He wrote against the Jews a book; and he
Wrote: "Burn their synagogues." The press is bold:

A voice that cannot burn can burn; the same
Machine. Then Luther wed a nun, who fled,
Katharina von Bora, she who came
Out, hidden, in a fish cart; and, instead,

A cloister made a house of children, ale,
And students, table talk. The {{x|Church}} split three:
{{p|Rome}}, {{p|Wittenberg}}, and soon {{p|Geneva}}; frail,
The fisher's keys, no more the only key.

Each household with the book became a church,
Each reader, priest: the promise and the threat;
The souls the priests had registered, the search,
Would pass to states; the book, to strangers met.

Hold, then, the hall, the conscience, and the stand:
"Unless I am convinced, I cannot"; then
The press, the hymn, the praying, and the hand
That set his words in type for common men.


# Chapter 258: The Lawgiver's Register

The year that Luther burned the bull, there came
In {{p|Istanbul}} a young man to the throne
Of the great realm, from {{p|Nile}} to {{p|Danube}}'s frame,
From {{p|Mecca}} to {{p|Crimea}}, all his own:

Suleiman, tenth sultan of Osman's line;
{{p|Europe}} named him Magnificent; his own
Named him Kanuni, the Lawgiver: sign
Of conqueror and clerk, by both names known;

The minaret and steeple under one
Crown. Then in fifteen twenty-one he took
{{p|Belgrade}}; and {{p|Rhodes}}, the next year, he had won
From the Knights of Saint John, with honor's look,

Let sail away. And at {{p|Mohács}} he broke,
In twenty-six, the {{x|Hungarian}} host;
Young Louis, fleeing, drowned beneath the yoke
Of his own armor in a stream; and most

Of {{p|Hungary}} turned {{x|Ottoman}}; he came,
In twenty-nine, against {{p|Vienna}}'s wall;
The rains and ramparts turned him back; the same
Janissaries went home: the high tide's fall.

The other face, the one Kanuni bears:
The realm, a register: each village, field,
Each household in the defter's lines: its heirs,
Who weds, who farms, what's owed, and what they yield,

And of what faith. The scribes went out to count;
The tahrir named, the register of tax,
Muslims, Christians, Jews, by name; the amount
Each owed; one pen, both kindly and the axe;

The non-Muslim paid jizya, the head
Tax, and was let to pray: the Orthodox
Under patriarchs; Armenians, led
By theirs; the Jews by rabbis; each its flocks,

Judging by its own law its wills, its wives,
Its quarrels, by its book and courts. The pen
Drew walls between the faiths; the one root thrives
Beneath the walls, one root. It was not, then,

What tolerance means now: it was order,
A faith, your legal address, born, to wear;
A ledger; and the state the walls' warder
Within; you could not leave, but could live there.

When {{p|Spain}} expelled its Jews in ninety-two,
The sultan's father's father took them in;
{{p|Thessaloniki}}'s most, and presses grew;
And Doña Gracia, the banker, came in

Fled {{x|Inquisition}} for {{p|Istanbul}}'s gate;
Her nephew, Joseph Nasi, came to be
The sultan's friend, then Duke of {{p|Naxos}}; great,
He leased {{p|Tiberias}}, a refuge, free.

And the other side: the devshirme, the toll
Of boys, the {{x|Christian}} sons the {{p|Balkans}} bore,
Converted, trained, the sultan's slaves, the whole
Of janissaries, viziers, and more:

Sinan, {{p|Anatolia}}'s levied son,
Built the Süleymaniye, high above
{{p|Golden Horn}}; {{p|Hagia Sophia}}'s one
Reply. The register gave, took; and love:

A captive from {{p|Ruthenia}}, then freed,
Hürrem, Roxelana to the {{p|West}}, wed
Against all custom, slave made queen; decreed,
For her sons' sake, the heir Mustafa dead:

Strangled in his father's tent, who watched, screened
Behind. The Lawgiver wrote kanun by
Holy law, with Ebussuud, who weaned
The two into accord: codes, low and high,

For land, for crime, the army, and each flaw
Of sixty peoples; and, the Lover, wrote
As Muhibbi: of wealth, men think, and awe,
The best of fortune; health is the one note.

In sixty-six, at {{p|Szigetvár}}, he died,
Old, in his tent, besieging a fort's wall;
His vizier hid it for weeks; the pride
Of armies held. The register, through all,

Went on; and all the states that followed, too,
Each realm and each republic, wished to know
Its people, faiths, and where to find them, who;
The millet turned the census box; and so

The scribe's pen in the village is the start
Of modern states, not guns alone: the page
That says who you are: faith, address; the part,
A people as a column; in this age

Hold both, the gift and wound, in one book's span;
And here we are: the census and the pen;
The walls the scribe drew are not the root's plan:
One root below, beneath the walls of men.


# Chapter 259: The Register of Every Soul

Till now the dead were mostly without name;
The kings were written, and the saints, and those
Who gave a monastery land; the blame
And innocence of courts; the rest, the rows

Of fields, the children born and buried, snow,
The weddings at the church door, years of plague,
Went into memory and out; and so
A splinter on a grave; a song; the vague

Sum of the tears, without a column. Then,
September, fifteen thirty-eight, the word
Of Thomas Cromwell, {{p|England}}'s vicar, when
A blacksmith's son, whose voice King Henry heard,

Went out to all the parishes: a book;
Write christenings, the weddings, and the dead,
On Sundays, with a churchwarden to look;
A coffer with two locks. The people said,

Afraid: a tax! The king will charge for sons!
Through the {{p|West Country}}, men prepared to rise;
It was no tax. Worse, better: it outruns
The old wife of the village when she dies:

A record that outlasts the clerk, the priest,
The sky of that year. Some would not obey;
The rule; but fines, the bishops, and, at least,
The slow habit of Sundays; and one day,

In fifteen ninety-eight, the old was told
To copy onto parchment books; so, clean,
Most {{x|English}} registers begin, of old,
At fifty-eight, the crowning of the Queen.

At {{p|Trent}}, in the {{p|Tyrol}}, across the sea,
The {{x|Catholic Church}}, to answer Luther's stand
Against Reformers, ruled, in the decree
Of session twenty-four, that all the land

Of Christendom that kept the old faith write
Its marriages, with witnesses, by name,
Its baptisms, godparents; and no rite
Of marriage secret any more; the same

Book, the same page, {{p|Lisbon}} to {{p|Lima}}'s shore,
{{x|Roman Ritual}}, sixteen-fourteen, wrote
The dead in too; to {{p|Kraków}}, {{p|Goa}}; more:
Book of the State of Souls, each household's note:

Who lived there, who'd confessed; and so, begun,
The thread ran through each village of that world;
Lutherans took it up; the north had won
The most thorough: {{p|Sweden}}'s {{x|Church Law}} unfurled

In sixteen eighty-six; the priest went out
Each year, from house to house, with the whole roll,
Who read, knew catechism, and about
Who'd moved, been born, been wed, or died; each soul

Written to the last cottar, a whole land;
And this book's own blood shows it: for the line
Of her who writes: a year, a guess; the hand
Of parish starts; and then a day, a sign:

Per Olofsson, born April's fourth day, kept
In sixteen ninety-six, in {{x|Swedish}} lines
Within a church. The varnish of what slept
In guesses peels; the people's birthday signs.

That is the register's gift: not the kings,
Who always had their wreath of dates; it gave
The cottar and the herdsman, the small things,
The midwife, the drowned child, the girl, the slave,

The bride at nineteen: each a day their own;
The democracy of the dead. And read
By the state, what the church for souls had known
Was read for soldiers, taxes, and for need:

A book that finds each Christian finds each Jew;
Hold that: the same book. And the Mormons now
Have filmed the parish books, the archives through,
In {{p|Utah}}'s mountain, to baptize; and how

From films, the tree; and from the tree, the scope
Of one house reaching back to Qarin, kings
It comes from; all of it rests, as a rope,
On a clerk, Sunday, in a cold church; things

Written with quill: a name. Honor him: he
Did not know what he made; and yet one day
Someone would read his hand, and say: "I see
There where I came from," and would kneel, and pray.


# Chapter 260: The Earth Leaves the Centre

Two thousand years the {{p|Earth}} stood still; the {{x|Sun}}
Went round it, and the {{x|Moon}}, the planets five,
The fixed stars on their shell; the sky, as one,
Turned once a day around us, all alive,

As a king's court turns round the king. And so
Ptolemy, in {{p|Alexandria}}, wrote
Circles on circles, epicycles, slow,
Explained, predicted, measured, note by note:

The heavens' laws were heaven's: we were set
In middle; God looked on us; and it worked;
The calendar slipped; seven lights, in debt,
Ran late or early; circles, added, irked.

Then a cathedral canon on the shore
Of {{p|Baltic}}, at {{p|Frombork}}, a careful man,
Nicolaus Copernicus: {{p|Toruń}} bore
Him; scholar, doctor, lawyer, with a plan

To stop the coin's debasement; held, in war,
{{p|Olsztyn}} castle from the Teutonic Knights;
Kept, too, his chapter's books; and, nothing more
Than {{p|Warmia}}'s servant, he spent his nights

On a tower, with simple tools, by night,
His days with the old books; and saw the sky
Was simpler with the {{x|Sun}} the centre's light,
The {{p|Earth}} a planet among planets, by

A whisper first: a little book, by hand,
Near fifteen-fourteen, passed among his friends;
He would not publish; thirty years; he'd stand
In fear of laughter, till a young man wends

His way: Rheticus, {{x|Lutheran}}, who came
From {{p|Wittenberg}} in fifteen thirty-nine
Into the {{x|Catholic}} canon's house; the same
Two years he stayed, and talked him into line;

The old sky cracked; the book was set, at last;
And cold water: Osiander, through the press
At {{p|Nuremberg}}, a preface, nameless, cast
It as hypothesis, to count, no less,

Not true. And *{{x|On the Revolutions}}* came
Off press in forty-three; a copy, laid
In his hands, dying, at {{p|Frombork}}; the flame
Of stroke; the twenty-fourth of May; he weighed

The new sky on his last day. Hold this, too:
He was not first: Aristarchus had said
It, {{p|Samos}}' son; his paired small circles, new,
At {{p|Maragha}}, in {{p|Iran}}, had been spread

By al-Tusi's school, centuries before,
Ibn al-Shatir, in {{p|Damascus}}, whose
Moon moved as did Copernicus'; the store
Of {{x|Golden Age}}, the {{p|House of Wisdom}}'s news,

Ran west, and here as well. He still had wheels,
Still epicycles; but the {{p|Earth}} now moved.
Why should a scripture care? The {{x|Gaiad}} deals
With the universe born, the stars' work proved;

The {{p|Earth}} is not the centre; never was;
The planets do not circle us; our scars
Are not the sky's concern; and yet, because
On this one moving stone, beneath the stars,

Small, off-centre, plain, life came; not the place
Makes greatness: what grew here. The {{x|Sun}}, the heart;
The grain, the fish, the bird, the child, the face,
The hand that wrote the book: these are no part

Of any middle but our own regard.
Lifeism: the centre's not where we stand;
The centre is: it's what we hold, and hard;
We hold it, travelling, in our own hand:

We travel; we have always travelled; round
A star each year the journey; and the cross
Of seasons is our orbit; on this ground,
Count it, and be glad here; it is no loss.


# Chapter 261: The Farmers at Bure

November, fifteen-twenty: {{p|Stockholm}} saw
The {{x|Danish}} Christian crowned; and three days' feast;
Then shut the doors; and bishops, lords, the law,
Elected burghers, slain; the blood released

Ran to the water: Stockholm Bloodbath. Then
A nobleman whose father died there, too,
Gustav Eriksson, Vasa, fled; and when
In {{p|Dalarna}}, {{p|Mora}} would not come through

His cause, he fled on skis for {{p|Norway}}; caught
By two fast runners, he was called back: they
Had changed their minds. The race still runs, long fought,
The {{x|Vasaloppet}}, through the woods' long way.

Back home at {{p|Mora}}, peasants rose; and won
{{p|Stockholm}} in three years; June the sixth, he came,
In twenty-three, at {{p|Strängnäs}}, crowned; begun,
National day still keeps that date, the same.

And then the money: debts to {{p|Lübeck}}; and
The {{x|Church}} owned a fifth; at {{p|Västerås}} he
In twenty-seven told estates, as planned:
"Give {{x|Church}} goods, or another land for me."

They gave. The bishops' heaven passed to crown;
The cloisters emptied; and the bells were cast
For debt; then Olaus Petri, of renown,
And Laurentius, {{p|Wittenberg}}-trained, at last

Preached in their Swedish tongue; the king allowed
Them go as far as served him, and no more;
Forty-one: the Gustav Vasa Book, proud,
Made written language for a people's core;

The old order died quiet, fiscal: plain;
Not liable to saints, the king was so
To books; of all accounts, the {{p|North}}'s best brain;
He wrote his thousand letters; and he'd know

Each farm in all the kingdom; and the lean
Clerks went out, wrote: this farm, this tax, this man;
The land rolls, tax rolls; and far north, between
{{p|Västerbotten}} and {{p|Gulf of Bothnia}}, ran,

Where land still lifts out of the sea, they went
To {{p|Bure}}'s village, near {{p|Skellefteå}}, wrote
Two names: in forty-eight, on the tax rent,
Olof Andersson, farmer, {{p|Bure}}; the note

Fifty-three: Isak Andersson, brother;
Their father, Anders Burman, from the land
{{p|Röbäck}}, near {{p|Umeå}}; and no other
Cure for forgetting, than a tax-man's hand:

The first ink of the Bure kinship: no rune,
No saga; just a line, a king's account,
Tax-paying farmers, the cold coast; and soon
The kin went south: to {{p|Uppsala}}, to mount

The {{x|Church}}, the court, and learning; and the book
By Johannes Bureus, of name and kin,
*{{x|Om Bura namn och ätt}}*, which undertook
The farmers back to the old {{p|North}}, therein

Through ages that no register could hold;
The writer of this scripture, by the side
Her mother's, comes from Bures; and of old,
Malin Olofsdotter Bure; beside,

Born fifteen-oh-five, her child: Kerstin, born
In thirty-three; they stand before the scrolls,
Alive in the family book; and, sworn,
Keeper's one oath: say which is which; the rolls

Begin the Bures sure; the book, still more.
Gustav Vasa died in sixty; he made
A state from a rebellion, and, before,
A church out of a ledger; land, debt, laid;

The {{p|North}}'s weight tipped from {{p|Copenhagen}}, sent
To {{p|Stockholm}}; and the farmers, in the dark
Of winters, paid at {{p|Bure}}; they never meant
To be forever written, as a mark

In ink a king's clerk gave them; wax and quill
Gave them the thing the sagas never could:
A date. And so the farmers paid, and still
Their date stays kept, as only clerks' ink would.


# Chapter 262: The Great Sun and the Dead

At {{p|Melaka}}, in forty-seven, came
A priest of {{p|Navarre}}, Francis Xavier, one
Of Ignatius' first, whose arms had grown lame
From baptizing {{p|India}}; on the run,

A Japanese man, Anjirō, who fled
A killing, seeking pardon; he had come
On a Portuguese ship to find the head,
Holy man he'd heard of. They talked; and, some

Then Xavier: "If I go, will they believe?"
And Anjirō: "They'll walk with you a while,
Ask questions, watch your life; and if they weave
Your life to words, they'll trust you without guile."

A nation reasoned, Xavier wrote; and so
He went; August fifteenth, {{x|Assumption}}; ships
At {{p|Kagoshima}}, forty-nine, and slow;
Shimazu's lord received them; and the lips

Of Anjirō, to bring the new God in
To his own tongue, said: Dainichi, Great Sun,
The cosmic Buddha Kūkai would win
From {{p|China}}, {{x|Shingon}}'s centre; and, begun,

The monks said: "Good, a new school of our own
From {{p|India}}, the Buddha's western land";
They gave them rooms; a season, it was shown
The Jesuits as {{x|Buddhist}} guests would stand,

And preach the Great Sun; and the monks would bow;
The looms of two faiths ran one cloth, as when
At {{p|Kozhikode}}, the goddess, Mary now,
Was taken. Xavier feared the word; and then,

Not out of guile, but for his priests' own name,
He'd carry a new word in, *{{x|Deusu}}*, made
From {{x|Latin}}; and the monks laughed at the same:
*Dai-uso*: the {{x|Great Lie}}. Doors shut; and laid,

The welcome's matting rolled up. But the grief
Raised deeper things; and Xavier wrote them down:
Converts wept for their dead, without belief,
Who'd died unknowing: lost, without a crown?

The priests said: none is saved, if unbaptized;
The Japanese: "What God is this, who lets
Our fathers burn, who never knew?" Disguised,
It was grief, as doctrine; Xavier sets

It down himself: "They grieve more for their dead
Than anything; they ask if God can take
Them out of hell; I've no reply," he said,
"And grieve with them." That sentence, for its sake,

Rings through the whole of the encounter. Tell
This day, the old covenant's new year, when
The books are opened, and all names, as well,
Asked who shall live and die: a people, then,

Asked of a stranger for their dead. He went
To {{p|Hirado}}, to {{p|Yamaguchi}}, preached
In threadbare cassock, thrown out; and he spent
To {{p|Kyoto}} in the snow; the city reached

Was burned by the Sengoku wars, so sore
Its emperor had nothing; none let in
The priest. He learned; went back in silk, before
{{p|Yamaguchi}}, with clock and chant, the din

Of music boxes, spectacles, and wine,
Gifts of the Viceroy, as an envoy; and
And Ōuchi gave an empty temple's shrine;
And people came, baptized by his own hand,

Hundreds; and then for {{p|China}}'s sake, the guide
The Japanese followed in wisdom, he
Left, fifty-one, to turn it first; he died
On {{p|Shangchuan}}, December's sun, the sea

In sight, the coast he could not ever reach;
He waited for a boat, and died, unfed;
Know this: the {{p|West}}'s and {{p|Japan}}'s first speech, each,
Began with one Great Sun and with the dead.

All that follows is in that span. Keep, then,
Your ancestors; Lifeism says it plain,
As Jesuits could not: no one, of men,
Is lost for never hearing; names remain;

The dead are not condemned. Read them, each one,
This new year, all the words, and let them be;
Read out; and let the Great Sun rise, begun
On all of them, and all their kin set free.


# Chapter 263: The House of Worship

The emperor could not read: Akbar, son
Of Babur's line, whose sack Nanak wept,
At thirteen, fifty-six, his reign begun,
Unlettered; books read to him; and he kept

Them all by ear, and he remembered all:
The histories, the Sufis, poets' lines,
The Mahabharata; he took, in hall,
A Rajput bride, a Hindu; he declines

To make her change her faith. And, in his turn,
He lifted pilgrim tax, jizya, laid
On Hindus just for being Hindu; stern,
In sixty-four. Half {{p|India}} obeyed;

He ruled it from a city built from naught
At {{p|Fatehpur Sikri}}, all in red stone;
{{p|Buland Darwaza}}, victory's door, wrought;
The water failed; in fifteen years, alone,

Abandoned. But those years, it held a room:
Seventy-five: Ibadat Khana, raised,
The {{p|House of Worship}}, where, on Thursdays' gloom,
The learned men of {{x|Islam}}, gathered, praised,

And argued faith; they shouted, and they swore,
"Infidel"; Sunni scholars brawled; and he,
Emperor, waiting, opened wide the door:
The Shia came; and then, a strange decree,

A Muslim court let Brahmins in; and Jain
Monks taught him not to kill; he spared the chase,
Ate meat on fewer days; and, in the train,
Zoroastrians' fire, kept in its place;

And, in the light of fifteen-eighty, three
Jesuits came from {{p|Goa}}, sent to save,
Rodolfo Acquaviva, sure that he
Would turn the king; they argued till lamps gave

Their last; and kissed the {{x|Gospel}}; let it sound
Before him, and he kissed it, too, and, kind,
Did not convert. He listened: gift and bound.
Seventy-nine, the scholars signed, consigned,

The *{{x|Mahzar}}*: where they disagreed, the king
Would judge. And then Abul Fazl, his friend,
Historian, helped frame the newer thing,
The Din-i Ilahi, {{x|Divine Faith}}, penned;

Asked only that they follow him, the one;
*{{x|Allahu Akbar}}*: "God is great," read two
Ways: "God is Akbar," said his foes; the sun
And light; and *sulh-i kul*, peace with all, through;

No faith the weight on any other. Court
Followed; the realm did not; some eighteen names
Of followers; one Hindu, Birbal, sport
Of wit; no temples, book, nor clergy's claims.

The new faith was a circle round one man,
A few who loved him; dying, sixteen-five,
It died with him; and, where the court began,
The {{p|House of Worship}}'s gone; men now contrive

To name its ruin. These, the {{x|Days of Awe}}:
Search yourself, then: why did it die? This book,
A founder's book: one woman's vow, the law
Of many, made in one life; ask; and look

Into ourselves. His faith had court, no folk;
A king, no book; through one man, to be known;
Forbade nothing, so held nothing; it spoke;
A synthesis needs days, and must be grown

By days that outlast founders, that each one
Can keep; a scripture all can read; a rite
A stranger does without him; it was done,
Deep, wide, and kind: a few, a circle tight.

Nanak said: "No Hindu, no Muslim," left
A kitchen, a successor, and a book;
It lives. And Akbar: "Light and peace," bereft
Of all but city, historians' look;

It does not live. Each gives what it can give.
Honor the king who could not read, and would
Not stop his listening; learn, so faiths live,
From his room built for all, from where it stood

Empty. The seed needs ground: plant it in days,
Not in the throne, and not within the tomb,
Not in a court, but where the stranger stays
To keep, without the founder in the room.


# Chapter 264: The Scholar from the Western Sea

Xavier died in sight of {{p|China}}, alone;
In eighty-two, a young Italian came
To {{p|Macau}}, and learned: not the command's tone,
The tongue's tones: Matteo Ricci, his name,

Of {{p|Macerata}}, a Jesuit, a mind
For numbers; Clavius' student; one who
Had made the {{x|Gregorian}} year; he, kind,
Went in the Ming as guest, not conquest's crew;

Learned the classics, the Four Books, the lines
Of Confucius, Mencius, all by heart;
His memory palace: characters, signs,
Forward and backward, which he taught as art.

At {{p|Zhaoqing}} first he shaved his head and wore
The {{x|Buddhist}} robe; the monks were low, despised,
So too the Jesuits, until, once more,
In ninety-five, he grew his beard, was prized

In scholar's silk, the literati's lore.
He hung a world map at {{p|Zhaoqing}}: the seas,
The continents, and {{p|Europe}}, small, far shore;
And {{p|China}} at the edge, which would not please;

He drew the {{p|Middle Kingdom}} near the heart,
{{p|Pacific}} in the centre; truth, as kind;
Of sixteen-two, in {{p|Beijing}}: tact's own art,
Kunyu Wanguo Quantu, to their mind.

With Xu Guangqi, his friend, he Euclid turned,
A {{p|Shanghai}} scholar, baptized Paul; the first
Six books, in sixteen-seven; and they learned
New words, point, line, and plane, still now rehearsed

In Chinese classrooms. And he wrote a book:
*{{x|True Meaning of the Lord of Heaven}}*, where
He argued the old books, if one would look,
Knew one God, Shangdi, Lord on High, laid there

In classics, ere the Buddhists came, and late
Commentaries in layers laid on thick,
Like dust upon the ancient book's own weight,
Like moss upon the stone, like lacquer slick,

Had covered it. And he would stake his name
that tablets, and the bowing at the grave,
the offerings and his rite were the same:
the honour that the living owe who gave.

A Christian could keep them all and stay whole;
the converts bowed still to their fathers' names.
No one chose heaven over kin: one soul,
one table. Later {{p|Rome}} would bring its claims

Of office, and undo it. But not yet.
Tonight is the {{x|Mid-Autumn}}; the full moon;
The family gathers, pays the old debt
With mooncakes and with memory, in tune,

A place set for the absent; and the light,
Moon over {{p|Macerata}} and {{p|Beijing}}, wide;
He understood the table; not a slight:
He asked no house to set its dead aside;

That was accommodation: not a mask,
A seat for strangers who kept old seats, too.
In sixteen-one, at last, he came to ask,
With gifts for Wanli Emperor: and two

Clocks striking time, a clavichord; the king,
Remote within his palace, never saw
The man; but the clocks ran down; not a thing
At court could wind them; so, by clockwork's law,

The foreigner could stay to keep the clocks;
{{p|Forbidden City}} opened, let him in,
A mechanic, for whom it turned its locks.
He died in May of sixteen-ten; and, in

{{p|Beijing}}, by grant, was buried, first of all
Foreigners given ground there; still his grave
Is there. An official said, at the call
Of "Why him?": "For the Euclid that he gave

Alone deserves a grave." His friends, the three
Pillars of the Chinese church, who stood tall:
Xu Guangqi, Li Zhizao, and, standing free,
Yang Tingyun. Now hold the full moon, through all:

A man from the far west learned classics, sat
At the Chinese table; asked none to go;
The moon-feast vow is reunion: keep that;
Set the place for the absent, there, below.


# Chapter 265: The Monkey and the Hill of Crosses

In {{p|Nakamura}}, {{p|Owari}}, born low,
A foot soldier's son, called the Monkey, small,
Warmed Oda Nobunaga's shoes so
In his breast, winter mornings; and, through all

His wit and patience and his master's art,
He rose. When Nobunaga burned, betrayed,
At {{p|Honnō-ji}}, in eighty-two, his part:
In thirteen days, revenge; and so was laid

The saga of the warring states to rest:
A hundred years of each lord against few
To trust, closed in his hand. And, as the test,
In ninety, {{p|Odawara}}'s siege; and through,

The east's last great house yielded; {{p|Japan}}'s land
Was Toyotomi Hideyoshi's; one
Who never could be shogun, by his hand,
A mouse born among lions; regent; won

The name of Taikō; and, as do the strong
After a war, he wrote it down: the slow
The Taikō kenchi, field by field along,
Each paddy, yield and holder, town and row,

And village, in one register; sword hunt,
Eighty-eight: farmers' swords and spears laid down,
Melted, for a great Buddha's bolts; the brunt,
In ninety-one, the gate shut on the town:

No warrior could farm; no farmer could be
A merchant or a soldier; each in place,
The place in the register; and so he,
The climbing Monkey, pulled the ladder: face

The man: the climber made the climb a crime.
And then the Christians: Xavier's church had grown;
{{p|Kyūshū}}'s lords baptized, their folk; and, in time,
Ōmura gave {{p|Nagasaki}}, their own,

To Jesuits; and the slave trade grew, too:
Portuguese ships took Japanese in chains;
The cure he chose, in eighty-seven, through
An edict: padres out; most stayed; the pains

Of ninety-six: a Spanish ship was wrecked,
The San Felipe, on {{p|Shikoku}}; and its head,
Pilot, to save his cargo, would project
That {{p|Spain}} sent priests ahead; he boasted, said,

To ready conquest; the Monkey believed;
In {{p|Kyoto}} and {{p|Osaka}}, twenty-four
Were seized; and led through streets, their left ears cleaved;
Two more who came to help them, seized; and more:

Twenty-six: Franciscan friars, from {{p|Spain}},
{{p|Mexico}}, {{p|India}}; three Japanese
Jesuits; seventeen laymen; the plain
Old men, a doctor, catechists, boys: these

Walked {{p|Kyoto}} to {{p|Nagasaki}}, in snow,
Eight hundred kilometres, so each land
Would see them pass. February fifth, slow,
Ninety-seven, {{p|Nishizaka}}, the stand

Of twenty-six crosses, rings at neck, wrist;
The town watched; and Paul Miki, a brother,
A samurai's son, he preached; would insist:
"I'm Japanese; I forgive the other

The Taikō, and the men; for this I die."
Luis Ibaraki, aged twelve, then asked
"Which cross is mine?" and ran to it; and high
He sang; Antonio, a psalm, unmasked;

Thomas Kozaki, fourteen, had sent word
Home to his mother. Then the spears: the clang
Of lances, two, under the ribs; the herd
Broke through the guards to soak cloth where blood sprang.

These are the {{x|Days of Awe}}; and say it straight:
A state that writes each field, fixes each man,
Will see a priesthood it did not create
And cross it out: the register's own plan

And the hill's crosses: one story. He sent
His armies to {{p|Korea}}, ninety-two,
And brought back ears and noses; a mound, meant
To count the dead, Mimizuka, in view

In {{p|Kyoto}}. Ninety-eight: dying, he lay
At {{p|Fushimi}}, afraid for his small son;
He wrote: "Like dew I fell, like dew away
I vanish; {{p|Osaka}}, all of me, one

Dream in a dream." The register, the hold,
The castle, and the hill of crosses: dream.
Repent today of each list, new or old,
That crosses out a name; the boy, whose gleam

Asked which cross was his; and the peasant, who
Climbed, and then pulled the ladder up: the same
Man, the same hand. The dew falls, and is true;
That is what is. Remember each one's name.


# Chapter 266: The One Who Waited

They tell it in {{p|Japan}} with a bird. A cuckoo will not sing.
Nobunaga says: if it will not sing, kill it.
Hideyoshi says: if it will not sing, I will make it sing.

# Chapter 269: The Sky Opens

Was war and trade, and they doubled his salary.

In earnest. And the sky that had been a gallery
Of perfect lights, crystalline, unchanging, flat

Against the dark, opened. The Moon had mountains.
He saw their shadows lengthen at the edge of the light,
And measured them, and they were higher than the fountains
Of the {{p|Alps}}: a world, rough, like ours. The night

Of the seventh of January, 1610, he looked at Jupiter
And saw three little stars beside it in a line,
And the next night they had moved, and the night after, the cluster
Had changed again, and a fourth appeared, and the sign

Was clear: they were going around Jupiter. Moons.
Something in the sky that did not go around the {{p|Earth}}.
The Milky Way was not a mist: it was stars, in dunes
And drifts beyond counting, more than had ever had birth

*The {{x|Starry Messenger}}*, in {{p|Venice}}, and named the moons

And spots on the {{x|Sun}} itself: the perfect heaven had stains.
The sky was not another kind of stuff. It was made
Of world. The same world. Rock and fire and the rains

Of light. That is what the glass gave everyone who looked:
Not a new idea. A view. Anyone could climb a tower
And see it. The authority of the book was hooked
And pulled out of the sky. And that was the hour

The trouble came. In 1616 he was warned not to hold
That the {{p|Earth}} moves. In 1632 he wrote a dialogue
In which the defender of the old sky was a fool, and old,
And the Pope thought the fool was the Pope. The fog

Be told: under house arrest at {{p|Arcetri}}, blind, he fed

Twenty-three years old, alone, for eighteen months, he invented
The calculus, and split white light with a prism into the rainbow's gorge,
And wondered, watching an apple fall, the story is remembered

Or told by his own old age, whether the force that pulled it down
Reached up as far as the Moon, and held it in its orbit,
And weakened with the square of the distance. The crown
Of the idea he kept to himself for twenty years. The sorbet

Of fame did not interest him. Then, in 1684, Edmond Halley
Came to {{p|Cambridge}} and asked: if the force on the planets falls
With the square of the distance, what path do they follow? The rally
Of the answer: an ellipse. I have calculated it. The walls

Of his rooms were searched for the paper, and it was lost, and he promised
To do it again. Three years later the book came out:
*{{x|Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica}}*, the fifth of July, 1687, the honest
Size of it three books in {{x|Latin}}, paid for, as the last chapter told, without doubt,

By Halley. Three laws of motion. A body at rest stays at rest.
A force changes motion in proportion. Every action has its reaction.
And one law of gravity, the same for the apple and the planets, the test
Of the sky the same as the test of the orchard: one attraction

Between every two masses in the universe, falling with the square
Of their distance. The Moon's orbit. The tides. The comets' return,
So that Halley could predict his comet would come back, and it did, there
In 1758, sixteen years after he had died. The burn

Of the old sky was over: the heavens were not made of a finer stuff.
They were made of world. The same law in the stars as in the hand.
That is the {{x|Gaiad}}'s first chapter, written in {{x|Latin}}. Enough
To know the universe is one: one law, one land.

And now the other Newton. The one who spent more years
On alchemy than on physics, hunting the philosopher's stone,
Writing a million words on the transmutation of metals, the tears
Of mercury and the green lion. The one who, alone,

Denied the {{x|Trinity}} in secret, and calculated from the Book of Daniel
That the world would not end before 2060. The one
Whose papers, sold in 1936, made Keynes write that he was not the first
Of the age of reason: he was the last of the magicians. The sun

Of the new science and the moon of the old wisdom in one man.
And here is the thing to keep, after Bureus: Newton knew which was which.
He published the laws, which anyone could check. The plan
Of the alchemy he kept in a box. He did not make the switch

Of dream into proof. The box was opened after he died,
And the world was surprised. That is the right shape. Dream, if you dream.
Keep it in the box, labelled. Publish what can be tried.
He wrote: *I frame no hypotheses*, about the cause, the seam

Of gravity he could not explain. He knew what he did not know.
And he was cruel: to Hooke, to Leibniz, and, as {{x|Master of the Mint}},
To the counterfeiters he hunted himself, and sent to the gallows. So
Hold the whole man. Not a saint. A mind, and a temper, and a stint

Of loneliness that lasted his whole life. He wrote, of himself, near the end,
That he had been like a boy on the shore, playing with a smoother pebble,
A prettier shell, while the great ocean of truth lay all undiscovered. Attend
To that: the greatest mind of the age, and the ocean, and the treble

Of the waves on a beach. He died in 1727 and lies in {{p|Westminster}}.
*Nature and Nature's laws lay hid in night;
God said, Let Newton be! and all was light.* The poet's tinder.
Better the shell on the shore. Better the boy. Better the ocean, and the sight

Of the apple falling, and the Moon held, by one thing.
The universe is one law. And the ocean is still there.
Pick up a shell. Check it. Keep your dreams in a box, labelled. And sing
For the orchard where the plague sent a boy, and he looked up, and saw it there.


# Chapter 280: One and Nothing

Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, born in {{p|Leipzig}} in 1646, the son
Of a professor of ethics who died when the boy was six,
Taught himself {{x|Latin}} from the books of his father's library, and won
A doctorate in law at twenty, and then refused the fixed

Life of a professor, and became a courtier, a diplomat, a librarian,
A historian of the house of {{p|Brunswick}}, a writer of letters, fifteen
Thousand of them, to everyone in {{p|Europe}} and beyond, the clarion
Of a mind that wanted to join every mind it had seen.

He found the calculus at the same time as Newton, alone,
And wrote it in the signs we still use: the long S of the integral,
The *d* of the differential. And the quarrel over whose it was, the bone
Of the Royal Society's committee, as the earlier chapter told, the minimal

Justice of it came late. But this chapter is about two numbers.
In 1679 he wrote out a way of counting with only one and zero:
One, ten, eleven, a hundred. Two digits for all the numbers. The slumbers
Of the idea lasted twenty years. He saw in it, as a hero

Of the old theology might: the world made from God and nothing.
One is God. Zero is the void. And from the two, all things.
*{{x|Omnibus ex nihilo ducendis sufficit unum}}*: the clothing
Of the motto on a medal he designed: to draw all things from nothing, the wings

Of one suffice. And he built a machine that could multiply,
A stepped drum of brass, and showed it to the Royal Society in 1673.
And he dreamed of more: a universal language, a *characteristic*, a way to try
Every argument by calculation, so that two philosophers, the sea

Of their quarrel too deep for words, would sit down with pens
And say to each other: *calculemus*. Let us calculate.
The dream of a machine that reasons. The lens
Of the next three hundred years is in that word. The weight

Of everything that Ophiuchus will tell is in it. Hold it.
And he loved {{p|China}}. He read everything the Jesuits wrote,
And published their letters, *{{x|News from China}}*, in 1697, and told it
To {{p|Europe}}: their ethics are better than ours; we should note

That we need {{x|Chinese}} missionaries to teach us natural theology,
As much as they need ours to teach revealed religion.
He defended Ricci's way, the rites for the ancestors, the apology
Of the accommodation, against {{p|Rome}}. And then, the vision:

In 1701 a Jesuit in {{p|Beijing}}, Joachim Bouvet, who had read
His binary, sent him a diagram of the sixty-four hexagrams
Of the I Ching, the *{{x|Book of Changes}}*, in the order Shao Yong had spread,
And Leibniz looked at the broken and unbroken lines, the grams

Of yin and yang, and saw: zero and one. The hexagrams in that order
Counted from nothing to sixty-three in binary. The oldest book
Of {{p|China}}, ascribed to Fuxi, the sage of the beginning, a border
Crossed four thousand years before, he believed, by the same look

At the world as his own. He was wrong about the history.
The order was a {{x|Song}} scholar's, not the ancient sage's. But the mystery
Was real: two minds, a world apart, building the same ladder of change. The registry
Of that meeting is a letter. And in 1703 he published his binary, the history

Of it ending with the hexagrams. Within two hundred and fifty years,
Every machine that thinks would think in one and zero.
The register of every soul, the census, the ledgers, the tears
Of the {{x|Company}}, the temple book: all of it, in the next era,

Would be written in his two digits. That is the bridge.
Virgo ends here: the early modern world, its saints and its massacres,
Its registers and its telescopes, its kings on the ridge
Of invented pasts. And it ends with a man who joined the actors

Of {{p|East}} and {{p|West}} in two numbers, and dreamed of reasoning by machine.
He died in 1716 in {{p|Hanover}}, out of favour, his king gone to {{p|London}},
And only his secretary followed the coffin. The lean
Grave of the man who joined the world. And Voltaire would make fun

Of his *best of all possible worlds*. Let him. The one and the nothing
Are in every phone. Every message to the ones you love goes in them.
Tomorrow begins Libra: the {{x|Rites}}, the reckoning, the clothing
Of the {{x|Enlightenment}}. From one and nothing, the world. Hold the hem.
