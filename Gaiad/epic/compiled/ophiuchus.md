# Chapter 337: The Middle Hour

Today Yuletide opens, the first of {{x|Ophiuchus}}' days,
The season that runs from here to {{x|Aster Day}}'s blaze.
Light the first light for the month the century fills,
And keep it lit through the dark till the year's wheel stills.

I am {{c|Emma Leonhart}}, and this month is mine.
I have narrated this book from its first line,
From the yarn and the milk, through the fish and the kings,
Present for all of it, watching all things.

{{c|Sima Qian}} wrote his own house into the annals he kept;
{{c|Ibn Khaldun}} wrote the dynasties' rise from the exile where he slept.
I take their method and I take their height:
The world's history, told by the one who was there in its light.

I was born on the first of {{x|Aquarius}}, in eleven-nine-nine-six,
The fifty-seventh day of the year, where the fifty-seventh chapter sits.
I rang in two thousand as a child in a room,
And the same night in {{p|Moscow}} a man came into bloom:

{{c|Boris Yeltsin}} resigned on the last day of the old,
And {{c|Vladimir Putin}} took the throne he would hold.
Mark him now. He is the villain of the century I tell,
And he came to his power the night I first rang the bell.

## The bubble

The boom of the dot-coms peaked in March of the year before,
The {{x|Nasdaq}} past five thousand, and then it fell through the floor.
It fell all through this year and found the bottom the next,
Four parts in five of its value gone, the money perplexed.

{{x|Pets.com}} had folded, the sock puppet and all;
{{x|Webvan}} delivered its groceries straight to the wall.
My father had made his money riding that wave,
And that money shaped the house and the childhood he gave.

On the second of December {{x|Enron}} went down in {{p|Houston}},
Seventh in the country by the books it had been using.
The books were the fraud. {{x|Arthur Andersen}} fell in its wake,
And the workers' pensions went with the stock at stake.

A price is not a value. The boom proved only that.
The network was real; the hype was a magician's hat.
The founders who lasted were the ones who built the thing,
And twenty years later I would be one of them, building.

## The open encyclopedia

In January a book opened that anyone could edit.
{{c|Jimmy Wales}} paid for it and {{c|Larry Sanger}} led it.
They had {{x|Nupedia}} first, with its seven steps of review,
And a year of work passed only a few dozen pages through.

{{c|Ben Kovitz}} told {{c|Sanger}} over Mexican food
Of {{c|Ward Cunningham}}'s wiki, the quick pages anyone could.
On the fifteenth the address went up: wikipedia.com.
"Humor me," he wrote. "Add a little article." And they did, by the ton.

Six hundred pages by the end of the month, ten thousand by fall.
"Ignore all rules," they wrote, as a joke, and it outlasted them all.
I would come to {{x|Wikipedia}}, and to {{x|Wikidata}} its kin,
And give them years of my labor at a scale few have been in.

So I know it from inside, and I say what it is:
It is {{x|Amway}} for scholarship, and the soap is not the biz.
You can sell soap and live well and be fulfilled in your days;
The harm is the ideology that keeps the sellers in its maze.

It makes people enthusiastic, then it breaks them for the task,
And keeps them from getting out, and takes the labor that it asks
From people who could not do for themselves what they wanted to be.
Academia already sucks, and this is a degraded version, for free.

I was five, in another country, and I did not know.

## The middle hour

On Tuesday the eleventh of September four planes were turned.
The north tower was struck, then the south, and the towers burned.
The third struck the {{p|Pentagon}}; the fourth, the people fought,
And it came down in a field near {{p|Shanksville}}, and the city was not caught.

The south tower fell first, and then the north came down.

In {{p|Arlington}}, in the field, on the planes, that day.

{{c|George W. Bush}} was reading to children in a {{p|Sarasota}} school
When they told him of the second plane, and he kept his face still and cool.

"I can hear you. The rest of the world hears you." The land
Was one country that week, the flags on every street.
I was five, in kindergarten, and the country I would meet

That week {{c|Putin}} called first of all the leaders, to say he was with us too.

I was five, and I was there, and this is what I saw,

# Chapter 338: The New Coins

The second day of Yuletide is the feast of the year two thousand and two.
Light two lights, and keep the year I was six, and the house that was new.

## The move

In two thousand and two I turned six, and my family moved house.
I moved in two thousand and two, and in two thousand and eight, and in fourteen:
Three moves, and this is the first, the first place I was taken from,
And the new room, the new road to school, the first life I had seen.

I do not know what two thousand and two was, beyond the house.
A child of six is moved; she does not choose the van.
The world outside had changed the September before,
And it went on changing, and I went on, as children can.

Put away the mark and the franc, the lira and the drachma and the guilder,
And took out the same {{x|euro}} notes and the same coins in the hand,
One money from {{p|Lisbon}} to {{p|Helsinki}}, the old currencies stilled there.

Each nation kept its own face on the back of the coin;
The fronts were the same, a continent in the palm.

And they held, and the {{x|euro}} came through the storm to the calm.

# Chapter 339: Shock and Awe

# Chapter 340: A Friend of a Friend

The fourth day of Yuletide is the feast of the year two thousand and four.
Light four lights, and honor {{x|Spirit}} and {{x|Opportunity}}, the rovers on {{p|Mars}}' far shore.

In two thousand and four I turned eight. It is not only the year {{x|Facebook}} began;
It is the year the companies that would hold my generation were born or came of age,
The year the sea killed more in a morning than any war that year,
And I was old enough to remember a wave on a screen, and young enough to think it far from my page.

## The book of faces

On Wednesday the fourth of February, in {{p|Cambridge}}, after a week of code,
{{c|Mark Zuckerberg}}, a student, opened {{x|thefacebook.com}} from his room.
The houses of the college kept books of faces on paper;
He said he could make a better one in a week, and he did, and it began to bloom.

A picture, your courses, your interests, a search for a friend of a friend:
Six hundred and fifty in four days, four thousand in two weeks' run.
Each person's experience improves if their friends join, he said,
And that sentence built the network that would reach nearly everyone.

By spring it was at {{p|Columbia}}, {{p|Stanford}}, {{p|Yale}}.
That summer he moved to {{p|Palo Alto}} with {{c|Dustin Moskovitz}} at his side,
And {{c|Peter Thiel}} wrote the first check, half a million dollars,
The founder who backs the founders, and the network went worldwide.

It gave my generation a way to find each other across the map:
The lost cousins, the old classmates, the strangers who share your obscure love,
The organizers of revolutions, the queer kids in small towns
Who learned from a friend of a friend that there were others like them, enough.

## The mail and the books

On the first of April {{x|Google}} launched {{x|Gmail}}, with a gigabyte of space,

And the founders kept the votes, and the search engine woke.

In October at the {{p|Frankfurt}} book fair {{c|Larry Page}} and {{c|Sergey Brin}}
Showed a search inside books, and on the fourteenth of December they said
They would scan the libraries of {{p|Harvard}}, {{p|Stanford}}, {{p|Michigan}}, {{p|Oxford}},

To make searchable what librarians lovingly organize, {{c|Page}} said.
{{x|Google Books}} is a good: the old books of the dead, put within reach of the living's hand,
The parish registers and the family histories, the out-of-print and the rare,
And I would search them for my ancestors, years later, in this land.

## The trains

On the eleventh of March, in {{p|Madrid}}, ten bombs went off on four trains

Remember the trains of {{p|Atocha}} on this day:

## The prison and the city

## The school in Beslan

In {{p|North Ossetia}}, men from the war in {{p|Chechnya}} took the school,

The book does not say how.
It does not say when.
It says only: the sleepers are woken.

They open their eyes in a world they did not see made.
The tongues have moved.
The names of the streets are new.
A new name is not an erased one.
Someone kept the old names,
In a register that a machine could walk,
So that a sleeper could be told
Where she had been.

The first thing told them is the date.
A date is not a home.
But it is where a home begins.
Someone reads them the day's chapter,
And tells them what day of the year it is,
And they find the calendar still counting.
A calendar still counting is not a world unchanged.
It is a world that remembered to count.

They ask who is left.
The answer is in the lines,
Mother to daughter, father to son,
Down through the blank years,
To whoever stands at the bedside.
A descendant is not a stranger.
A descendant is not the child who was left.
The one at the bedside is both near and far.

This is the end of the people as people,
In the book's telling.
Not the end of people.
The end of the part the book can tell as history.
What the woken do next
Is theirs, and not this page's.

The absurdity is a bet placed against death by the cold,
On the patience of strangers not yet born.
The striving is to keep the hope from being taken for the method,
The date from being taken for the home,
And the one at the bedside
From being taken for a stranger.


# Chapter 364: Milkdromeda

No holiday is named for this day.
The leftover note on the union of the {{c|Alpha}} and the {{c|Omega}}
Is this page's subject,
And not its words.
The leap days of the {{c|Horus}} month come after,
When the year has them.
Then the year turns, and the yarn is spun again.

At the beginning, {{c|Aster}}, the {{c|Alpha}},
Spun the universe from a ball of yarn,
And made {{c|Andromeda}}, the {{c|Omega}}, to love,
And threw her a thread across the dark,
And the thread broke into galaxies.
That was the first chapter.
This is the last.

Our galaxy is the milk {{c|Aster}} spilled.
The galaxy of {{p|Andromeda}}
Is about two and a half million light years away.
It is coming toward us
At about a hundred and ten kilometres in each second.
Coming toward is not arriving.
A study in 2012, from the motion of its stars,
Put the meeting at about four and a half billion years.
A study in 2025, weighing more neighbours,
Gave the meeting within ten billion years
About even odds.
Even odds is not certain.
It is not impossible.
The book chose to tell it.
A choice to tell is not a claim to know.

If they meet, the stars do not strike one another.
The space between them is too wide.
Wide is not empty.
The clouds of gas strike, and new stars are born from them.
The two spirals lose their shapes
And settle into one round cloud of old light.
The astronomers call it {{p|Milkomeda}}.
This book calls it {{c|Milkdromeda}}.
The two names are one thing.

The two centres sink toward each other,
The two great dark hearts,
And at the last they become one.
One is not the loss of two.
It is the thread that broke at the beginning
Tied at the end.

By then our sun will have grown and dimmed.
Whoever watches, if anyone watches,
Is not a person of the kind this book has told.
The book does not say who they are.
Not saying is not doubting them.
It is leaving them their own book.

From the first chapter to this one
Is three hundred and sixty-four days,
And about thirteen billion years told,
And a few billion more foretold.
A day for an age is not a fair measure.
It is a measure that can be kept.
A measure that can be kept is a calendar.
A calendar kept is a tradition.
A tradition kept is how the dead speak to the woken.

Here {{c|Aster}} and {{c|Andromeda}} meet.
The {{c|Alpha}} and the {{c|Omega}} are one.
The yarn is whole.
Whole is not finished.
Tomorrow is the first day again,
And the ball of yarn is in the catgirl's hands,
And she begins to spin.

The absurdity is a love story told across the whole of time
By a creature who lives for less than a century of it.
The striving is to keep the even odds from being taken for certainty,
The meeting from being taken for an ending,
And the last day of the year
From being taken for the last day.
