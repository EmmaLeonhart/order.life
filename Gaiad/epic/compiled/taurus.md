# Chapter 141: P at Baikal — Paikal

And in the middle of the {{p|Siberian}} winter,
At the deepest lake on earth,
Haplogroup {{c|P}} arrived.

                                 {{p|Lake Baikal}}.

The deepest lake. The oldest lake.
Five thousand feet deep at its deepest. Twenty-five
Million years old. A rift lake. A sub-continental
Crack in the earth, slowly widening, slowly filling,

Slowly accumulating the fresh water that would become
The largest reservoir of fresh surface water on the planet.
Twenty percent of all the fresh liquid water on earth
Is in this one lake. The lake of the siberian taiga.

The lake of the taimen and the omul and the nerpa,
The only freshwater seal in the world.
The lake that freezes to a depth of several meters
Every winter. The lake of the {{p|Evenki}} and the {{p|Buryat}}

And the {{p|Yakut}} and the reindeer-herders of the
Surrounding taiga. The sacred lake of multiple
Indigenous traditions. The shaman's lake. The ice-
Thick cathedral of the Siberian frozen mirror.

This is where {{c|P}} arrived.

                                    {{c|Haplogroup P}}.

{{c|P}} is the divergence point. {{c|P}} is the father of
{{c|Q}} and {{c|R}}. And {{c|P}} is, for this chapter, the figure
Of the place itself—the lake, the crossroads, the
Bifurcation where the road forked and one branch

Went east toward {{p|Beringia}} and the {{p|Americas}},
And the other branch turned back west toward
{{p|Europe}} and the steppe and the eventual Yamnaya
Expansion that would remake half the world.

{{c|P}} is "{{c|Paikal}}." The pun that the Gaiad cannot
Resist. The haplogroup of the lake. The {{p|Y}}-chromosomal
Signature of the men who, having migrated the length
Of {{p|Eurasia}} from the {{p|Bab al-Mandab}} crossing many

Thousands of years before, have reached the deepest
Lake on earth, and have paused there, and have looked
At its frozen surface, and have asked themselves
Where next.

                   There are two answers to that question.

The first answer is east. Continue east. Cross the
Land bridge that exists, at this time, across what is
Now the {{p|Bering Strait}}. {{p|Beringia}} is a walkable continent
In the Late Pleistocene. The sea levels are low. The land

That is now underwater is above water. You can walk from
{{p|Siberia}} to {{p|Alaska}} without crossing a significant body
Of water. And some of the {{c|P}}-descended population
Does exactly this. That branch becomes {{c|Q}}. {{c|Q}} crosses

{{p|Beringia}}. {{c|Q}} enters {{p|North America}}. {{c|Q}} spreads, over
The next ten or fifteen thousand years, across the
Entire {{p|Western Hemisphere}}—from the {{p|Aleutians}} down to
{{p|Tierra del Fuego}}. Every indigenous population of the

{{p|Americas}}, from the {{p|Inuit}} of the {{p|Arctic}} to the {{p|Fuegians}}
Of the southernmost tip of {{p|South America}}, is descended
From the {{c|Q}}-carrying branch of {{c|P}} that crossed {{p|Beringia}}.
That story—the peopling of the {{p|Americas}}—is the Book

Of Leo's material, chapters 221 through 252. Not this
Chapter. Not yet. {{c|Q}}'s chapter is later.

The second answer is west. Turn back west. Retrace the
Steps of the earlier migration in reverse. Go back across
{{p|Central Asia}}, toward the steppes, toward what will become
The {{p|Pontic-Caspian}} grasslands, toward the place where, in a

Few thousand years, the domestication of the horse will
Change everything. That branch becomes {{c|R}}. {{c|R}} is the
Boomerang. {{c|R}} is the lineage that went east as far as it
Could, all the way to {{p|Baikal}}, and then turned and came back.

{{c|R}} will, in later chapters, become the Yamnaya. {{c|R}} will
Become Manu and Yemo. {{c|R}} will become the Indo-European
Language family's Y-chromosomal carrier. {{c|R}} will become
R1a and R1b, the Indic-Iranian-Slavic eastern branch and the

Celtic-Germanic-Italic western branch. {{c|R}} will become the
Signature of most of modern {{p|Europe}}'s men. {{c|R}} will ride
Horses. {{c|R}} will make chariots. {{c|R}} will invent the wheeled
Vehicle and the solar wheel and the sky-father religion.

But that is in later chapters. In this chapter, {{c|R}} is
Still {{c|P}}. Still at {{p|Baikal}}. Still looking at the frozen
Lake and waiting to decide which way to go.

And the image—the core image of this chapter—is the
Lake itself. The moment of bifurcation. The moment when
A single lineage, having reached the deepest lake on
Earth, turns and goes both ways at once. One son goes

East to the Americas. The other son goes west, eventually,
To every corner of {{p|Europe}} and {{p|Central Asia}} and {{p|South Asia}}.
And the father—the {{c|P}}-carrier himself—stands on the
Frozen surface in the gathering twilight of a long Siberian

Night and watches both sons disappear into the dusk.

This is the divergence point. This is "{{c|Paikal}}." The
Haplogroup of the crossroads. The haplogroup whose
Own story is brief, because what {{c|P}} did was make
{{c|Q}} and {{c|R}}, and the stories of {{c|Q}} and {{c|R}} are two of the

Biggest stories in the entire haplogroup tree. {{c|Q}} is the
Americas. {{c|R}} is everything else—or at least the biggest
Everything-else. The two sons of {{c|P}} carry between them
The peopling of half the world.

The {{p|Mal'ta-Buret'}} culture, an Upper Paleolithic
Culture of {{p|Lake Baikal}} and the surrounding region
From roughly twenty-four thousand years ago, is
The archaeological signature of this moment. The

{{p|Mal'ta boy}}—a four-year-old child whose skeleton was
Found near {{p|Irkutsk}} in nineteen twenty-nine, and
Whose DNA was sequenced in twenty thirteen—carries
Ancestry that contributes both to Native Americans

And to Europeans. The child is the genetic missing link
Between the two branches. The child is the ancestor
(In a statistical sense) of both the {{p|Navajo}} and the
{{p|Lithuanian}}, the {{p|Cherokee}} and the {{p|Swede}}, the {{p|Inca}} and

The {{p|Irishman}}. One child at {{p|Lake Baikal}} whose DNA
Contains the seeds of both the peopling of the {{p|Americas}}
And the peopling of {{p|Europe}} by the Indo-European expansion.
The {{p|Mal'ta boy}} is {{c|P}} in the flesh. The {{p|Mal'ta boy}} is

The child at the crossroads, the one whose descendants
Would go both directions at once.

And the landscape, the actual landscape of {{p|Lake Baikal}},
Endures. The lake is still there. The taiga is still there.
The {{p|Buryat}} shamans still drum at the edge of the water.
The {{p|nerpa seals}} still surface through the ice to breathe.

The lake was there before {{c|P}} arrived, and the lake
Will be there after the haplogroup names have been
Forgotten. But for the length of this chapter, the lake
Is the divergence point. The geographical anchor of the

Bifurcation. The place on the earth where a single lineage
Looked east and west and chose both directions.

{{c|P}} is {{c|Paikal}}. The haplogroup of the deepest lake on earth.
The father of {{c|Q}} and {{c|R}}. The ancestor, at one remove, of
The {{p|Americas}} and of Indo-European {{p|Europe}}. The quiet
Paternal haplogroup whose own distinct narrative is short

Because his sons' narratives are long.

But the lake remains. The lake is the symbol of the
Chapter. The frozen surface of the oldest lake reflects
The Siberian stars. And the stars reflect back. And the
{{c|P}}-carrying men of the {{p|Mal'ta-Buret'}} culture, twenty-four

Thousand years ago, looked up at those stars and decided—
Or the long slow tide of their populations decided, without
Any single decision, over the following thousands of years—
That some would cross the land bridge into the new world,

And some would turn around and go home, back to the
Steppes, back to the grasslands, back to the places
Where, in a few thousand more years, the horse would be
Domesticated and the wheel would be invented and the

Language family that would become Indo-European would
Be born, and from that fire, half of the modern world
Would take its inheritance.

But all of that is later. Here, at {{p|Baikal}}, in the
Siberian winter, {{c|P}} stands at the edge of the frozen
Water and looks both ways. Here, in the depth of the deep
Past, is the crossroads. Here is where the lineages fork.

{{c|P}}. {{c|Paikal}}. The lake. The crossroads. Stand.

And the rest of this chapter is the lake. The silence.
The stars. The ice. The two silhouettes disappearing
In two different directions. The father watching them go.

Q goes east. R goes west. The lake remains.


# Chapter 142: Manu and Yemo — R and the Yamnaya

And {{c|R}} came back.

                           From the deep east, from the cold
And frozen shores of {{p|Lake Baikal}}, the lineage that had
Reached the edge of {{p|Siberia}} turned and walked west. The bold
And homeward migration that would become the single largest

Expansion of any haplogroup in the entire human tree.
{{c|R}} left the lake. {{c|R}} crossed the taiga. {{c|R}} crossed
The {{p|Ural Mountains}}. {{c|R}} descended into the grasslands—the great sea
Of grass that stretches from the {{p|Carpathians}} to the {{p|Altai}}, crossed

And crossed again by every subsequent steppe migration. And
There, on the {{p|Pontic-Caspian steppe}}, {{c|R}} settled. And there,
Over the following thousands of years, a very specific set of
Events took place that would change the linguistic and genetic

Map of most of the world.

                               {{c|R}}'s sons were two: {{c|R1a}} and {{c|R1b}}.

And in the Gaiad's reading, they are {{c|Manu}} and {{c|Yemo}}.

                                                                   {{c|Manu}}.
{{c|Yemo}}. The Proto-Indo-European founding pair. The first man and
His twin. The cosmogonic pair whose story, reconstructed from the
Linguistic evidence of the daughter traditions, tells the myth

Of the first sacrifice. {{c|Yemo}} is sacrificed by {{c|Manu}} to create
The world's order. Out of {{c|Yemo}}'s body, the cosmos is made.
The head becomes the sky. The eyes become the sun and moon. The bone
Becomes the mountains. The blood becomes the rivers. The plaid

And woven fabric of social order is woven from {{c|Yemo}}'s parts:
The priest comes from the head, the warrior from the chest,
The farmer from the loins, the servant from the feet. The harts
And cattle of the world come from {{c|Yemo}}'s flesh. The rest

Of the creation myth follows from this single primal sacrifice.

And this myth survives, in fragments, in the daughter languages:
{{c|Mannus}} and {{c|Ymir}} in the {{p|Germanic}} tradition—where {{c|Ymir}} is the twice-
Sacrificed frost-giant whose body becomes the world. The strangeness

And dismemberment of {{c|Ymir}} is the reflex of the primal myth
In the {{p|Germanic}} branch. {{c|Manu}} and {{c|Yama}} in the {{p|Indian}} tradition—where
{{c|Yama}} is the first man to die, who becomes the god of death and the
Underworld. And {{c|Manu}}, the survivor of the flood, is the ancestor

Of all humans. {{c|Romulus}} and {{c|Remus}} in the {{p|Roman}} tradition—where
The fratricide is preserved (one brother kills the other) but the
Cosmogonic payoff is attenuated. {{c|Romulus}} kills {{c|Remus}} and founds
{{p|Rome}}, but {{c|Remus}}'s body does not become the world—his death is simply

The founding sacrifice of a single city. {{c|Gayomart}} in the {{p|Iranian}}
Tradition, the first man, whose seed spawns the first couple. All
Of these are reflexes of the same primal myth. The twin myth
Spread across the whole Indo-European language family because it was

The founding myth of the Indo-European people. And the Indo-European
People were {{c|R1a}} and {{c|R1b}}. The haplogroups and the myth traveled
Together. The myth was the story the {{c|R}} men told about themselves.
The haplogroups were the biological signature of the men who carried

The story. Together they are the {{c|R}} lineage, the boomerang lineage,
The lineage that came back from the east and made the west.

And what they did with the steppe, before they expanded, was
To domesticate the horse. And the horse changed everything.

The horse was domesticated on the {{p|Pontic-Caspian steppe}}, somewhere
Around five thousand years ago, by the pre-Yamnaya cultures of the
Region. {{p|Botai}}, in northern {{p|Kazakhstan}}, has the earliest clear
Evidence of horse domestication—bit wear on horse teeth from around

Three thousand five hundred BCE. And once the horse was domesticated,
The mobility of the steppe peoples became something unprecedented
In human history. A man on a horse can cover twenty, thirty, fifty
Miles a day—distances that on foot would have taken a week. Herds

Can be managed across vast territories. Warfare can be conducted at
A range and speed that foot-based cultures cannot match. The horse
Is the technology that makes the steppe into the fastest-moving
Zone of human habitation. And the {{c|R}}-carrying peoples of the steppe

Are the first beneficiaries. The {{p|Yamnaya}} culture emerges around
Three thousand three hundred BCE. The {{p|Yamnaya}} are horse-riders,
Cattle-herders, makers of {{p|kurgan}} burial mounds across the
Pontic-Caspian grasslands. And from the {{p|Yamnaya}} homeland, the

Expansion begins.

                   In every direction.

                                         West: into {{p|Europe}}. The
{{p|Corded Ware culture}}, the {{p|Beaker culture}}, the subsequent waves
Of bronze-age {{p|European}} populations—all descend, in substantial
Percentages, from the {{p|Yamnaya}} migration westward. Ancient DNA

Studies published in the twenty-tens showed that a substantial
Portion of modern European male Y-chromosomes derive from an
Expansion originating on the {{p|Pontic-Caspian steppe}} around
Three thousand BCE. The signature is unmistakable: {{c|R1b}} is

Overwhelmingly dominant in {{p|Western Europe}}. {{c|R1a}} is dominant in
{{p|Eastern Europe}}. The two great {{c|R}} sub-branches swept across the
Continent, overlaying the earlier {{c|G}} (Neolithic farmer) layer
And the even earlier {{c|I}} (hunter-gatherer) layer. The modern

European male is a three-layer cake. {{c|I}} at the bottom. {{c|G}} in the
Middle. {{c|R}} at the top. And the percentages vary by region: {{p|Sardinia}}
Is still heavily {{c|G}}-dominated. The {{p|Balkans}} and {{p|Scandinavia}} retain
Heavy {{c|I}} frequencies. But {{c|R}} is the dominant layer almost everywhere.

And south: into {{p|Central Asia}}, through the {{p|Bactria-Margiana Archaeological
Complex}}, and eventually into {{p|India}}. The {{p|Aryan migration}} is {{c|R1a}}'s
Southern expansion. The chariot-driving, horse-riding, {{p|Vedic}}-hymn-
Chanting peoples who enter {{p|India}} around fifteen hundred BCE and

Merge with the existing {{c|H}}-and-{{c|L}} {{p|Indus Valley}} population to create
The civilization that will eventually compose the {{p|Vedas}} and the
{{p|Mahabharata}} and the {{p|Ramayana}}—these are {{c|R1a}}-carrying men. The
{{p|Aryan}} peoples are, in the Gaiad's reading, {{c|R1a}}. The language they

Bring is Proto-Indo-Iranian. It will become {{p|Sanskrit}} in {{p|India}} and
{{p|Avestan}} in {{p|Iran}}, and it will become the basis for the entire
Indo-Iranian language family. And east: into {{p|Tarim Basin}} of what is
Now {{p|Xinjiang}}, where the {{p|Tocharians}}—speakers of the eastern-most

Indo-European language branch—established themselves, leaving blond
And red-haired mummies in the desert that bewildered later {{p|Chinese}}
Travelers. The {{p|Tocharians}} are an outlier of the {{c|R1a}} expansion,
The branch that went furthest east before being absorbed.

The Yamnaya expansion is, in the Gaiad's reading, the single largest
Genetic and linguistic event of the Bronze Age. It recasts the
Population of {{p|Europe}}. It recasts the population of {{p|India}}. It creates
The Indo-European language family in its historical form—the family

That includes all the {{p|Romance}} languages, all the {{p|Germanic}} languages,
All the {{p|Slavic}} languages, all the {{p|Indo-Iranian}} languages, {{p|Greek}}, {{p|Armenian}},
{{p|Albanian}}, {{p|Celtic}}, and the extinct {{p|Anatolian}} and {{p|Tocharian}} branches.
Half of all living humans speak an Indo-European language as their

Native tongue. And that half speaks Indo-European because, five
Thousand years ago, a single haplogroup lineage—{{c|R}}, the boomerang
Lineage, the lineage that went east to {{p|Baikal}} and then turned around
And came back—domesticated the horse, invented the chariot, developed

A religious and linguistic package that would spread across half
Of {{p|Eurasia}}, and rode out of the steppe in every direction at once.

{{c|Manu}} and {{c|Yemo}}. {{c|R1a}} and {{c|R1b}}. The twin founding pair of the
Indo-European world. The myth that said: one brother sacrificed the
Other to make the cosmos. The haplogroup-pair that said: we will
Cover half the known world with our descendants. Both were true.

The Yamnaya expansion is the mythic and the genetic and the linguistic
All at once. It is the moment when a single steppe culture, having
Mastered the horse and the wheel and the bronze axe, explodes outward
And reshapes {{p|Eurasia}} for the rest of recorded history.

The {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}} civilization of {{p|Moldova}} and {{p|Ukraine}}—the
Great pre-Yamnaya {{p|European}} culture, the one that built giant wooden
Cities painted with spirals, the one that worshipped the goddess
Of the old Europe—is burned to the ground by the {{p|Yamnaya}} advance.

The peaceful pre-IE agricultural culture is replaced by the warlike
Horse-riding patriarchal culture. The goddess is replaced by the
Sky-father. The painted pottery is replaced by the corded-ware pot.
The spiral is replaced by the wheel. The mother is replaced by the

King. And the languages of old Europe—whatever they were, we will
Never know—are replaced by Proto-Indo-European and its daughters.
This is the pivot. This is the change. This is the moment when
The Europe we know today begins. Indo-European-speaking, patrilineal,

Patriarchal, horse-riding, sky-father-worshipping. Everything that
Makes modern {{p|Europe}} distinctively itself was brought, in seed form,
By the {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion.

And every subsequent {{p|European}} civilization—the {{p|Celts}}, the {{p|Greeks}}, the
{{p|Romans}}, the {{p|Germanic}} tribes, the {{p|Slavs}}—is a downstream elaboration
Of the {{p|Yamnaya}} cultural package. {{p|Rome}} is {{p|Yamnaya}}'s grandchild.
{{p|Christianity}}, with its sky-father God, is the great-great-grandchild

Of the {{p|Yamnaya}} religious imagination. And the modern world—most
Of it—walks in paths first laid by the {{c|R}}-carrying men who rode
West out of the Pontic-Caspian steppe at the dawn of the Bronze Age.

{{c|Manu}} sacrificed {{c|Yemo}} and made the world.

{{c|R1a}} and {{c|R1b}} spread across {{p|Eurasia}} and made the Indo-European
World.

The myth and the haplogroup are the same story, told twice—once
In the grammar of genealogy, once in the grammar of myth.

{{c|R}}. The Yamnaya. The boomerang. The Indo-European. Stand.


# Chapter 143: E — Returns to Africa

And the loop closed.

                          {{c|Haplogroup E}} is the haplogroup that
Left Africa, crossed the {{p|Bab al-Mandab}} with {{c|Noah}}, wandered
{{p|Eurasia}} for some thousands of years, and then—remarkably, almost
Uniquely among the out-of-Africa haplogroups—turned around and

Went back. {{c|E}} is the loop-closer. {{c|E}} is the one that walked
Out of Africa and then walked back in. Every other haplogroup
That left Africa stayed out. {{c|D}} went to Japan and Tibet. {{c|C}}
Went to Australia and the steppe. {{c|F}} went to the {{p|Near East}}

And branched into every other haplogroup. They all went east,
North, west—they never came back. Except {{c|E}}.

                                                      {{c|E}} is the
Haplogroup that, having wandered north from {{p|Yemen}} into the
{{p|Levant}}, turned south again. Down through the {{p|Sinai Peninsula}}.

Across the {{p|Isthmus of Suez}}. Into {{p|Egypt}}. Into the {{p|Nile Valley}}.
And from {{p|Egypt}}, across the {{p|Sahara}} (which, at the time of the
{{c|E}} migration, was a wetter and more hospitable place than it
Is today—the "{{p|Green Sahara}}" of the {{p|African Humid Period}}, with

Lakes and rivers and grasslands where there is now sand). {{c|E}} spread
Across {{p|North Africa}}, down into {{p|West Africa}}, across into
{{p|East Africa}}. {{c|E}} became, eventually, the dominant haplogroup
Of most of sub-Saharan {{p|Africa}}.

                                       And this is where the Gaiad's
Story of circular migration finds its completion. Humanity left
Africa, some of it. Wandered. Branched. Populated every continent.
And one branch of the {{c|CT}} lineage—the {{c|E}} branch—came back. The

Circle closed.

                 And when {{c|E}} came back to Africa, {{c|E}} did
Not find an empty continent. The haplogroups that had never left—
{{c|A}} and {{c|B}}—were still there. {{c|A}} was in the deep African
Interior, carried by the {{p|San}} and {{p|Khoisan}} peoples of the

{{p|Kalahari}}, by the {{p|Mbuti}} of the {{p|Congo}} rainforest, by the most
Ancient lineages of the continent's human population. {{c|B}} was
Also in the deep interior, carried by other forest-dwelling and
Arid-land peoples. And now {{c|E}}—returning from the {{p|Eurasian}}

Branch of the tree, having branched off the {{c|DE}} stem in the
{{p|Levant}} somewhere around sixty thousand years ago, having
Wandered for tens of thousands of years—returned and began to
Spread. And {{c|E}} was more numerous, more aggressive, more tied

To agriculture and to later population expansions, than {{c|A}} or {{c|B}}.
{{c|E}} pushed {{c|A}} and {{c|B}} into smaller and smaller territories.
The {{p|San}} today live in the {{p|Kalahari}} Desert because that is where
{{c|E}} did not reach. The {{p|Pygmies}} today live in the deep forest

Because that is where {{c|E}} did not reach. {{c|E}} took the grasslands,
The river valleys, the rich agricultural land. {{c|A}} and {{c|B}} took
The remnant wild zones. The pattern is familiar: the later, more
Numerous, more aggressive lineage displaces the earlier one. And

The displacement is the story of most of {{p|Africa}}'s pre-colonial
Population history.

                       But the displacement is also the restoration.
Because {{c|E}}, in genetic terms, is {{p|African}}. {{c|E}}'s ancestor—the
{{c|DE}} node, the split from the {{c|CF}} branch—was out of Africa, yes,

But {{c|E}} itself, as a distinct haplogroup, developed its identity
In {{p|Africa}} or on its border. The return to Africa is the return
To the continent where the human story began. The loop-closer
Is the haplogroup that says: we are African.

                                                    Ninety percent or
More of all sub-Saharan {{p|African}} men today carry {{c|E}}. {{c|E}} is the
Overwhelming majority haplogroup of modern {{p|Africa}}. The {{p|Bantu}}
Peoples—who themselves underwent a great expansion starting around

Three thousand years ago from their homeland in what is now
{{p|Cameroon}}/{{p|Nigeria}}, spreading {{p|Bantu}}-speaking populations across
Central, Eastern, and Southern {{p|Africa}}—are {{c|E}}. Specifically, a
Sub-clade called {{c|E-V38}} or {{c|E1b1a}} is the {{p|Bantu}} expansion

Signature. The {{p|Bantu}} migration reshaped sub-Saharan {{p|Africa}} the
Way the {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion reshaped {{p|Europe}}—a demographically
Massive, linguistically unifying event that made most of the
Continent speak related languages. The {{p|Bantu}} languages, from

{{p|Swahili}} to {{p|Zulu}} to {{p|Kikuyu}} to {{p|Kinyarwanda}}, are all descended
From the same Proto-Bantu spoken by a relatively small population
In what is now the {{p|Cameroon}}/{{p|Nigeria}} border region around three
Thousand years ago. And the {{c|E-V38}} haplogroup is the genetic

Signature of that expansion.

                                 In the north, a different sub-clade
Of {{c|E}} dominates. {{c|E-M78}} and {{c|E-M81}} are the {{p|North African}}
And {{p|Horn of Africa}} signatures. The {{p|Berbers}} of {{p|Morocco}}, {{p|Algeria}},

{{p|Tunisia}}, and {{p|Libya}} carry very high frequencies of {{c|E-M81}}. The
{{p|Egyptians}}, both ancient and modern, carry {{c|E-M78}} and related

{{p|Cushitic}}, {{p|Chadic}}, and {{p|Omotic}}. {{c|E}} is the main {{p|African}}-side

And the circle closes on the genealogical tree.

A people with no writing to be read in.

The oldest on the earth—before the host

For any god they served. What we can keep
Is stone and sherd and bone, and, one by one,
The little figures, seated or asleep:

The {{p|Fat Lady}}, broad of hip and thigh,
In clay and limestone—the most singular art
Of {{p|Europe}}'s {{p|Neolithic}}, and the reply,
In {{p|Malta}}'s idiom, to an older heart:

The mother {{c|Marija Gimbutas}} found
Beneath Old {{p|Europe}}, before the horsemen came;
The {{p|Venus of Willendorf}} on other ground,
{{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}'s clay—the same

Great mother, as the Gaiad reads the sign.

Such stone. Look at the temples' own design,
The shape they kept through all their building days:

Each chamber, uprights where the lintels span—

The bones of offered beasts beneath the stone.

Of builders. Then the builders were gone.

Near twenty-five hundred BCE it ends.

The temples simply empty. No one tends
Them. Bronze Age settlers, coming later,

Lived on the island with no memory
Of who had built the temples on its hills.

Was used up—soil worn thin by tilling, the hills

Stripped of their trees for firewood and for the work

A few thousand temple-builders, in the murk
Of slow decline, could not. Not with a bang. They fled,

Or starved, or thinned away. And the temples stood,
Empty and silent on the windy rise,

# Chapter 145: The Old Kingdom — Pyramids at Giza

And while {{p|Malta}} was exhausting itself on its small rocky island,
A much larger civilization on a much larger river was beginning
To build at a scale that would dwarf everything that had come before.

{{p|Egypt}}. The {{p|Nile}}. The Old Kingdom.

                                              The {{p|Nile Valley}} had
Been settled by {{c|E}}-haplogroup peoples for thousands of years.
The {{p|Predynastic}} cultures—{{p|Badari}}, {{p|Naqada}} I, II, III—had
Developed pottery, metallurgy, symbolic systems that would evolve

Into hieroglyphs, and gradually consolidated into larger political
Units along the river. By approximately thirty-one hundred BCE, the
{{p|Two Lands}}—{{p|Upper Egypt}} (the narrow river valley running from
{{p|Aswan}} to modern {{p|Cairo}}) and {{p|Lower Egypt}} (the broad {{p|Nile Delta}}

Opening onto the {{p|Mediterranean}})—had been unified under a single
Pharaoh. The founder is traditionally called {{c|Menes}}, sometimes
Identified with {{c|Narmer}}, whose ceremonial palette is one of the
Earliest documents of pharaonic kingship. The First Dynasty began.

The {{p|Early Dynastic Period}} ran from approximately thirty-one hundred
To twenty-six hundred BCE. This was the period of consolidation—
The development of the administrative apparatus, the hieroglyphic
Writing system, the religious framework of divine kingship, the

Tax-collection networks that would make pharaonic Egypt economically
Viable for the next three millennia. And then, around twenty-six
Hundred BCE, something changed. The kings of the {{p|Third Dynasty}}
And {{p|Fourth Dynasty}} began to build at an unprecedented scale.

{{c|Djoser}}, a {{p|Third Dynasty}} pharaoh, built the {{p|Step Pyramid}} at
{{p|Saqqara}}. The first monumental stone structure in {{p|Egypt}}. The
Architect, {{c|Imhotep}}—vizier, physician, priest, builder—would be
Deified in later {{p|Egyptian}} tradition as the divine craftsman. The

{{p|Step Pyramid}} was originally a mastaba—a flat rectangular tomb—
Expanded outward and upward in six stages until it became a stepped
Tower, two hundred feet high, covering fifteen acres. The first
Skyscraper. The first large-scale stone architecture. And the first

Sign of what was to come.

                                Sixty years later, {{c|Sneferu}}, founder
Of the {{p|Fourth Dynasty}}, was experimenting with true pyramid form.
{{c|Sneferu}} built three pyramids over the course of his reign—the

{{p|Meidum Pyramid}} (which partially collapsed), the {{p|Bent Pyramid}} at
{{p|Dahshur}} (whose slope had to be changed mid-construction when the
Builders realized the original angle was too steep), and the {{p|Red Pyramid}}
At {{p|Dahshur}} (the first true smooth-sided pyramid, completed

Successfully). {{c|Sneferu}}'s experiments set the stage. And then his
Son, {{c|Khufu}}, built the {{p|Great Pyramid}} at {{p|Giza}}.

                                                                The
{{p|Great Pyramid of Giza}}. Four hundred and eighty feet high. Two point
Three million limestone blocks, each weighing between two and a

Half and fifteen tons. The tallest human-made structure on earth
For thirty-eight hundred years, until the construction of {{p|Lincoln
Cathedral}} in the {{p|Middle Ages}}. The most massive structure ever
Built, by volume, in pre-industrial history. The astronomical

Alignments of the pyramid—its faces cardinally oriented to within
A fraction of a degree—suggest sophisticated surveying and
Astronomy. The internal architecture—the {{p|King's Chamber}}, the
{{p|Queen's Chamber}}, the {{p|Grand Gallery}}, the air-shafts that align

With specific stars in the sky at the time of construction—
Indicates a religious cosmology in which the dead pharaoh's
Soul would travel up through the pyramid to join the circumpolar
Stars in the northern sky. The pharaoh becomes a star. The pyramid

Is his launchpad.

                     {{c|Khufu}}'s son {{c|Khafre}} built the second pyramid
At {{p|Giza}}, slightly smaller but on slightly higher ground, so that
The two appear nearly equal in height. {{c|Khafre}} also built the

{{p|Great Sphinx}}—the massive limestone statue of a crouching lion with
A human head, carved from bedrock beside the pyramid complex. The
{{p|Sphinx}} faces east, toward the rising sun. Its human head is
Traditionally identified with {{c|Khafre}} himself. The statue is the

Largest monolithic stone sculpture in the world, and has stood
For forty-five hundred years as the guardian of the {{p|Giza}} plateau.
{{c|Menkaure}}, {{c|Khafre}}'s son, built the third and smallest of the
{{p|Giza}} pyramids. The three pyramids together—{{c|Khufu}}, {{c|Khafre}},

{{c|Menkaure}}—are one of the most recognizable architectural ensembles
In human history. They have outlasted every empire that has risen
And fallen around them. The {{p|Persian}} conquest, the {{p|Macedonian}}
Conquest, the {{p|Roman}} conquest, the {{p|Arab}} conquest, the {{p|Ottoman}}

Conquest, the {{p|British}} occupation, {{p|Egyptian}} independence—the
Pyramids have watched all of it. The pyramids are older than
History in any meaningful sense. They are the deepest monuments
Of human civilization still standing at their full scale.

And behind the pyramids is the religious imagination of the {{p|Old
Kingdom}}. The pharaoh is divine. The pharaoh is {{c|Horus}} incarnate.

# Chapter 146: The Akkadian Empire

While the pyramids were rising at {{p|Giza}}, to the east and
Across the {{p|Sinai}} and over the {{p|Arabian}} desert, in the
Valley of the two great rivers, a different kind of power
Was consolidating. Not a pyramid-power. Not a tomb-architecture

Of divine kingship. A military power. An imperial power. The
First empire in human history. The empire of {{c|Sargon of Akkad}}.

Before {{c|Sargon}}, {{p|Mesopotamia}} was a landscape of competing
City-states. {{p|Uruk}}, {{p|Ur}}, {{p|Lagash}}, {{p|Kish}}, {{p|Umma}}, {{p|Nippur}}, {{p|Eridu}}—

Each a walled city with its own ruler, its own patron deity,
Its own patch of irrigated farmland. These cities had been
In existence for a thousand years already. {{p|Sumerian}} civilization
Had invented writing (cuneiform on clay tablets), the wheel

(For pottery and for carts), bronze metallurgy, the mathematical
Base-sixty system that still defines how we measure time and angles,
And the political form of the city-state ruled by a {{c|lugal}} (king)
Or {{c|ensi}} (governor-priest). The cities warred with each other,

Traded with each other, and occasionally formed short-lived
Hegemonies when one king managed to subdue the others. The
Legendary {{c|Gilgamesh}} of {{p|Uruk}}—the historical king on whom the
Epic poem would eventually be based—ruled during this period.

{{c|Lugalzagesi}} of {{p|Umma}} briefly united much of {{p|Sumer}} in the
Twenty-fourth century BCE. But no unification lasted long, and
No empire—in the sense of a multi-regional political entity
Ruled from a single center—had yet been built.

{{c|Sargon}} changed that.

                              {{c|Sargon}}'s origin story is already
Legendary: a foundling, abandoned in a reed basket on the river
By his mother, rescued by a gardener, rising to become cupbearer

To the king of {{p|Kish}}, and then—by a coup or by a succession
Crisis, depending on the account—becoming king himself. (The
Reed-basket-on-the-river motif will appear again, later, in the
Biblical {{c|Moses}} story. The parallel is not coincidental. The

Motif is a {{p|Near Eastern}} commonplace that signals foundling-to-
King destiny.) {{c|Sargon}}'s name means "the true king" or "the
Legitimate king"—a throne-name, taken at his accession, designed
To declare that he was the rightful ruler despite his non-royal

Origins. He then began to conquer. First the other {{p|Sumerian}}
Cities. Then the regions to the north—{{p|Akkad}}, which would become
His imperial base. Then further afield: {{p|Elam}} to the east,
{{p|Syria}} and {{p|Anatolia}} to the north, and as far as the {{p|Mediterranean

Coast}} of what is now {{p|Lebanon}}. {{c|Sargon}} claimed to have washed
His weapons in the {{p|Upper Sea}} (the {{p|Mediterranean}}) and the {{p|Lower
Sea}} (the {{p|Persian Gulf}}). The claim, if not literally true, was
Culturally decisive. {{c|Sargon}} had conquered more territory than

Any previous ruler in history. The {{p|Akkadian Empire}} was born.

And its capital city, {{p|Akkad}}, was founded somewhere along the
{{p|Tigris}} or {{p|Euphrates}}—the exact location has never been rediscovered.
{{p|Akkad}} is the lost imperial capital. One of the most important

Cities in the ancient world, ruled by one of the most consequential
Dynasties, and we do not know where it stood. The site has been
Buried or eroded or overbuilt so thoroughly that {{p|Akkad}} is, as
Far as modern archaeology is concerned, a name without a location.

{{c|Sargon}} ruled for fifty-six years. His reign was marked by
Military campaigns, administrative consolidation, and the
Imposition of {{p|Akkadian}} (a {{p|Semitic}} language, carried by
{{c|J}}-haplogroup populations) as the language of empire, gradually

Displacing {{p|Sumerian}} (a language isolate) in common use. {{c|Sargon}}'s
Daughter {{c|Enheduanna}} was installed as high priestess of the
Moon-god {{c|Nanna}} at {{p|Ur}}—and {{c|Enheduanna}} is, incredibly, the
First author whose name has survived in human history. Her

Hymns to {{c|Inanna}}—the goddess of love and war, {{p|Sumerian}} counterpart
Of the later {{p|Semitic}} {{c|Ishtar}}—are the oldest signed literary
Works that exist. {{c|Enheduanna}} is the first named author. The first
Woman in the historical record whose intellectual labor was

Acknowledged, named, and preserved. The daughter of the first
Emperor is also the first author. And her hymns—particularly "{{p|Nin-me-
Šara}}" ("{{p|The Exaltation of Inanna}}")—are still read and studied
Four thousand three hundred years later.

                                                {{c|Sargon}} was succeeded
By his sons {{c|Rimush}} and {{c|Manishtushu}}, both of whom faced rebellions
And assassinations, and then by his grandson {{c|Naram-Sin}}, who took
The empire to its apex. {{c|Naram-Sin}} ruled for approximately thirty-six

Years. He expanded the empire further, campaigned widely, and
Famously declared himself a god—the first {{p|Mesopotamian}} king to
Claim divinity during his own lifetime, a precedent that would
Echo through later {{p|Near Eastern}} kingship and ultimately into the

{{p|Roman}} imperial cult. The {{p|Victory Stele of Naram-Sin}}, carved
Around twenty-two hundred and fifty BCE, depicts him crossing a
Mountain as a divine warrior, trampling enemies beneath his feet,
With the solar and astral symbols of the gods above him. It is

One of the masterpieces of ancient {{p|Near Eastern}} art. And it
Marks the peak of the {{p|Akkadian Empire}}.

                                                  After {{c|Naram-Sin}}, the
Empire declined. His son {{c|Shar-Kali-Sharri}} faced ongoing revolts

And a weakening economic base. And then—around twenty-one fifty
BCE—the empire collapsed. Two forces contributed. The first was
Internal: the economic and political infrastructure of the empire
Had overextended. The second was external: invaders from the

{{p|Zagros Mountains}}, a people called the {{p|Gutians}}, descended into
{{p|Mesopotamia}} and overran the weakened imperial structure. The
{{p|Gutians}} were, according to the {{p|Sumerian}} records, "barbarians"—
A disparaging word that {{p|Mesopotamian}} civilization would apply

Repeatedly to mountain peoples who invaded from the east. They
Ruled {{p|Mesopotamia}} for approximately a century, badly and
Chaotically, until they were expelled by a resurgent {{p|Sumerian}}
Dynasty centered on the city of {{p|Uruk}} (the {{p|Fourth Dynasty of

Uruk}}) and then by the {{p|Third Dynasty of Ur}}, which would
Re-establish {{p|Sumerian}}-language rule and usher in the {{p|Neo-Sumerian}}
Period. That dynasty—{{p|Ur III}}—is the one in which {{c|Abraham}}, in
The Gaiad's reading, will be born. But that is a later chapter.

For this chapter, the focus is the {{p|Akkadian Empire}} itself—the
First multi-regional empire in human history, the first state to
Claim suzerainty over multiple cultures and languages, the first
To institutionalize the mechanisms of imperial administration

That would be refined by every subsequent empire of the ancient
World. The {{p|Akkadian Empire}}'s innovations: a standing army
Recruited from across the empire; provincial governors appointed
By the imperial center; a common imperial language ({{p|Akkadian}});

Standardized weights and measures; an imperial road system; a
Unified taxation regime; and the title of "{{c|King of the Four
Quarters}}" that claimed universal sovereignty over the four
Corners of the earth. These innovations would be inherited by

The {{p|Babylonian Empire}}, the {{p|Assyrian Empire}}, the {{p|Persian Empire}},
The {{p|Macedonian Empire}} of {{c|Alexander}}, the {{p|Roman Empire}}, and

# Chapter 147: Stonehenge

And on an island at the far northwestern edge of {{p|Europe}},
Long before anyone there could write, long before anyone
There had heard of {{c|Sargon}} or {{c|Khufu}} or the civilizations
Of the {{p|Near East}}, another monumental project was underway.

{{p|Stonehenge}}. On the {{p|Salisbury Plain}} of what is now {{p|England}}.
The most famous megalithic monument in the world. Built in
Several phases between approximately three thousand and fifteen
Hundred BCE—a project that spanned more than a thousand years,

Across multiple cultural periods, undertaken by people whose
Language and name we will never know.

                                           The site began, around
Three thousand BCE, as a circular earthwork—a ditch and bank

Enclosure about three hundred and thirty feet in diameter, with
Wooden posts inside. This was the late {{p|Neolithic}} phase.
{{c|I}}-haplogroup and {{c|G}}-haplogroup populations—the old hunter-
Gatherer and Neolithic-farmer substrate of {{p|Britain}}—were the

Builders of this initial phase. They carried the bluestones from
The {{p|Preseli Hills}} of {{p|Wales}}, a hundred and fifty miles to the
West, and erected them in a double circle at the center of the
Enclosure. The bluestones weigh between two and four tons each.

How they were transported is not known. Theories include rolling
Them on logs, dragging them on sledges over frozen ground,
Floating them on rafts down the coast and up the river systems.
No single theory has been definitively proven. What is known is

That the builders, working with Neolithic technology, managed to
Move roughly eighty large stones across a hundred and fifty miles
Of hill and valley and coastline. And then—as if that were not
Enough—they erected them at {{p|Stonehenge}} in a deliberate circular

Arrangement, with doorway-type "trilithons" at the center.

Then, around twenty-five hundred BCE, the project entered its
Major monumental phase. The bluestones were rearranged, and the
Iconic {{p|sarsen}} stones—much larger blocks of a hard sandstone

Quarried twenty-five miles to the north—were added. The sarsens
Weigh up to twenty-five tons each. The tallest rise to twenty-four
Feet above the ground level (with another eight feet buried below).
They were arranged in a ring of thirty uprights supporting a

Continuous ring of horizontal lintels, and five larger trilithons
(Two uprights with a lintel across the top) arranged in a horseshoe
Inside the ring. The stones were carefully shaped—sockets cut into
The tops of the uprights to receive tenon projections on the bottom

Of the lintels, mortise-and-tenon joinery in stone. The lintels were
Cut to a slight curve so that when arranged in a ring they formed
A perfect circle. And the whole arrangement was aligned with the
Midsummer sunrise and the midwinter sunset—the summer-solstice sun

Rising directly over the Heel Stone outside the circle, the
Winter-solstice sun setting between the two uprights of the largest
Trilithon. {{p|Stonehenge}} is, among other things, a Neolithic
Astronomical calendar, marking the two extreme points of the sun's

Annual journey.

                    This is the phase of {{p|Stonehenge}} that is most
Iconic. And this is the phase that was built during the {{p|Yamnaya}}
Expansion into {{p|Britain}}—the arrival of the {{p|Beaker Culture}}, which

Carried {{c|R1b}} haplogroup signature and {{p|Indo-European}} language
Into the island. Ancient DNA analysis has shown that the late
Neolithic {{p|British}} population was almost entirely replaced—some
Ninety percent turnover—by {{p|Beaker}}-associated populations in

The early {{p|Bronze Age}}. The builders who started {{p|Stonehenge}} were
Genetically distinct from the builders who completed it. And yet
The project continued. The incoming {{p|Beaker}} people did not
Destroy the monument—they incorporated it into their own ritual

Life. They even added to it. The final reorganization of the
Bluestones—into the oval arrangement inside the horseshoe of
Sarsens—was completed by the {{p|Beaker}} culture, around twenty-two
Hundred to two thousand BCE. The monument absorbed a demographic

Replacement of its own builders without losing its function or
Its form. This is one of the most remarkable continuities in
{{p|European}} prehistory. The people changed. The project persisted.

And then, around fifteen hundred BCE, active construction ceased.

{{p|Stonehenge}} was left in its final form. The ditches gradually
Silted in. Some stones fell over the centuries. Others were removed
By later {{p|British}} populations for building material or displaced by
Farming. By the time the {{p|Romans}} arrived in the first century CE,

{{p|Stonehenge}} was already ancient—fifteen hundred years old,
Already a mystery to the local {{p|Celtic}} populations, who had their
Own explanations (involving {{c|Merlin}} and giants, though these
Stories are later {{p|Arthurian}} accretions). The {{p|Romans}} did not

Destroy the monument. Neither did the {{p|Anglo-Saxons}}. Neither did
The {{p|Normans}}. Neither did medieval {{p|Christians}}. Neither did the
{{p|Victorians}} (though they did add some concrete to stabilize it in
The twentieth century). {{p|Stonehenge}} has, remarkably, remained

Standing for four and a half thousand years. It has survived every
Subsequent culture that has occupied {{p|Britain}}. And it continues to
Stand, weathered and iconic, on the {{p|Salisbury Plain}}, drawing a
Million visitors a year in the twenty-first century.

What was it for? The question has no single answer. At various
Times, scholars have proposed that {{p|Stonehenge}} was a temple,
A calendar, an astronomical observatory, a healing shrine, a
Place of ancestral veneration, a ritual center for the dead, a

Neolithic parliament, a druidic worship site (though the
{{p|Druids}} are {{p|Iron Age}}, thousands of years later, and had no
Connection to the original builders). The archaeological evidence
Suggests a combination of functions. The surrounding landscape

Is dotted with burial mounds and henges, suggesting that
{{p|Stonehenge}} was the central node of a larger sacred landscape
Oriented around death, ancestors, the cycles of the year, and
The celestial movements that tracked those cycles. Human bones

Have been found at the site, indicating that cremated remains were
Deposited here; the monument was, at least in part, a burial
Place. And isotope analysis of the bones shows that people buried
At {{p|Stonehenge}} came from all across {{p|Britain}}—and in some cases

From continental {{p|Europe}}. {{p|Stonehenge}} was not a local monument;
It was a pan-{{p|British}} and even pan-{{p|European}} religious center.
People traveled hundreds of miles to be buried there. And the
Builders, at each phase, must have represented a coordinated

Effort across multiple communities—a Neolithic and Bronze Age
Consensus that this place, above all others, was where the
Monument should be.

                        The Gaiad's reading of {{p|Stonehenge}} is in
Line with its reading of {{p|Göbekli Tepe}}: the temple comes first.

The sacred site organizes the society around it. The monument is
Not a byproduct of an already-existing political structure—the
Monument is the political structure. {{p|Stonehenge}} is the organizing
Principle around which a dispersed Neolithic and Bronze Age

Population coordinated its identity. The stones are the axis mundi
Of the prehistoric {{p|British}} world. And the fact that the monument
Survived a demographic replacement—that the incoming {{p|Beaker}}
People adopted it rather than destroying it—suggests that its

Sacred character was recognized by the newcomers. The {{p|Indo-European}}
Speakers who were, elsewhere, dismantling the pre-existing European
Religious landscape, spared {{p|Stonehenge}}. They even added to it.
Whatever the monument meant, the meaning was translatable across

The cultural and linguistic divide between the {{p|Neolithic}} builders
And the {{p|Beaker}} arrivers.

                                      And perhaps the deepest thing
The Gaiad can say about {{p|Stonehenge}} is this: it is the monument

That has maintained its identity across the longest stretch of
Continuous cultural memory. The pyramids of {{p|Giza}} are older, but
{{p|Egyptian}} civilization eventually broke with the pyramid-religion
Of the {{p|Old Kingdom}}—the pyramids became monuments of a past

Religion, revered but not functionally used. {{p|Stonehenge}}, by
Contrast, was still accumulating depositions and being actively

A living site for the majority of its working lifespan. And when

It was finally abandoned, it was abandoned as an intact monument
Whose form and meaning had been preserved by two successive
Populations. That is a rare achievement. Most monumental projects
Die with their builders. {{p|Stonehenge}} outlived its builders and

Was carried forward by their successors.

                                                The sarsens stand
On the {{p|Salisbury Plain}}. The winter sun sets between the uprights
Of the great trilithon. The wind blows across the plain the way it

Has blown for four and a half thousand years. And every midwinter
And midsummer, visitors still come to watch the sun align with

Monument still works. The astronomical alignment has not drifted.

# Chapter 148: Minoan Crete

And while {{p|Stonehenge}} stood weathering on the {{p|Salisbury Plain}},
And while {{p|Egyptian}} civilization recovered from the {{p|First
Intermediate Period}} into the {{p|Middle Kingdom}}, on an island in
The middle of the {{p|Mediterranean Sea}}, another palace civilization

Was reaching its own apex.

                                 {{p|Crete}}. The largest island of the
{{p|Aegean Sea}}. The civilization of the {{p|Minoans}}.

{{p|Minoan}} is a modern name, coined by {{c|Arthur Evans}} in the late
Nineteenth century after {{c|King Minos}}, the legendary {{p|Cretan}}
King of {{p|Greek}} mythology. The {{p|Minoans}} themselves called
Themselves something else—we do not know what, because their

Language, written in the script we call {{p|Linear A}}, has never
Been deciphered. The {{p|Minoans}} are the second great undeciphered
Civilization of the Bronze Age. (The {{p|Indus Valley}} is the first.)
We can read the shape of their society—we know how they built, what

They ate, what they wore, what they painted, how they organized
Their economy, how they traded, and roughly how their religion was
Structured. We cannot read their words. The {{p|Linear A}} tablets are
Legible as symbols. They are not legible as language. A thousand

Tablets. Ten thousand fragments. Their speech is silent.

                                                               The
{{p|Minoan}} civilization emerged in its palatial form around nineteen
Hundred BCE and flourished until approximately fifteen hundred BCE.

The great palace complexes—at {{p|Knossos}}, {{p|Phaistos}}, {{p|Malia}}, and
{{p|Zakros}}—were the administrative, economic, and religious centers of
The civilization. They were not fortified. {{p|Minoan}} {{p|Crete}}, astonishingly,
Had no city walls. The palaces rose directly out of the landscape

Without defensive architecture. The interpretation is that the
{{p|Minoans}} had naval supremacy in the {{p|Aegean}} and did not need
Land fortifications; their security was provided by their fleet.
The idea of a civilization secure enough to live in unwalled

Palaces is, by Bronze Age standards, extraordinary. Almost every
Other civilization of the period had walled cities as a matter
Of survival. The {{p|Minoans}} did not.

                                            And inside the palaces,
The culture was visually extraordinary. {{p|Minoan}} frescoes depict

Dolphins, octopuses, flying fish, wild goats, lilies, crocuses,
Saffron-gatherers, bull-leapers, priestesses, processions of
Offering-bearers—a visual vocabulary that is unmistakably alive
With nature, color, and movement. The {{p|Minoans}} loved the sea and

Loved the land and loved the animals and plants that surrounded them.
Their palaces are full of light-wells and air-shafts and colonnaded
Courtyards. They invented indoor plumbing—the palace at {{p|Knossos}}
Had flush toilets, drainage systems, and hot and cold running water

In the royal apartments, four thousand years ago, a luxury that
Most of the world would not match until the twentieth century. The
Palace architecture is labyrinthine, multi-story, open-plan, with
Frescoes in every major room. {{p|Knossos}} itself may have been the

Historical basis for the {{p|Greek}} myth of the {{p|Labyrinth}}—the winding
Maze in which the {{c|Minotaur}} was imprisoned. The palace has some
Fifteen hundred rooms arranged around a central court, connected by
Corridors and stairways that have no single organizing axis. A

{{p|Greek}} visitor, confronted with this layout, might reasonably have
Concluded that the place was built to confuse and conceal. And the
Bull motif—so central to {{p|Minoan}} iconography, with bull-leaping
Frescoes, bull-horns of consecration on every altar, bull-headed

Rhyta—would have lent itself naturally to the {{c|Minotaur}} myth. The
{{c|Minotaur}}, a bull-headed man at the center of a labyrinth, is a
{{p|Greek}} memory of {{p|Minoan}} religion, distorted through the lens
Of centuries of cultural intermediation after the {{p|Minoan}} collapse.

The {{p|Minoan}} religion itself was probably centered on a mother goddess—
A figure depicted in the famous {{p|Snake Goddess}} statuettes, a woman
In an open bodice holding two writhing snakes. The snake symbolism
Is associated with chthonic earth-goddess worship throughout the

Ancient {{p|Mediterranean}}. The {{p|Minoans}} had priestesses, not priests—
Nearly every religious scene in the frescoes features female
Officiants. The religion appears to have been goddess-centered, with
A male consort figure (a young god who dies and is reborn, possibly

Related to the later {{p|Greek}} {{c|Dionysus}}) but with clear priority
On the female divine. This is the old European goddess religion
That {{c|Marija Gimbutas}} argued survived in pockets across the
{{p|Mediterranean}} into the Bronze Age. {{p|Crete}} is one of those pockets.

The mother goddess and her snakes. The saffron gatherers. The
Priestesses with their processional garments. {{p|Minoan}} religion is
The female-centered religion of old Europe, preserved on an island
Long enough to become the Bronze Age's most visually sophisticated

Civilization.

                And the {{p|Minoans}} traded. Their ships reached every
Corner of the {{p|Eastern Mediterranean}}. {{p|Minoan}} pottery has been
Found in {{p|Egypt}} (where {{p|Minoans}} are depicted in {{p|Eighteenth Dynasty}}

Tomb paintings as the "{{c|Keftiu}}"—a people bringing tribute to the
Pharaoh), in the {{p|Levant}}, in {{p|Anatolia}}, in the {{p|Cyclades}}, on
Mainland {{p|Greece}}. {{p|Minoan}} influence shapes the early {{p|Mycenaean}}
Culture on the {{p|Greek}} mainland. The {{p|Aegean}} is, for several

Hundred years, a {{p|Minoan}} lake. And the wealth that flowed through
{{p|Crete}}—in olive oil, wine, copper, tin, ceramics, textiles, and
Whatever else passed through the palace storerooms—made the
Civilization one of the wealthiest of the Bronze Age.

And then, around fifteen hundred BCE, it ended.

                                                    The cause was,
Almost certainly, the eruption of {{p|Thera}}—the volcanic island
Now called {{p|Santorini}}, about seventy miles north of {{p|Crete}}. The

{{p|Thera}} eruption was one of the largest volcanic events in human
History. It ejected an estimated sixty to one hundred cubic
Kilometers of material into the atmosphere. It blasted the center
Of the island of {{p|Thera}} into the sea, leaving the crescent-shaped

Caldera that is now the island of {{p|Santorini}}. The ashfall reached
{{p|Crete}}, {{p|Anatolia}}, {{p|Egypt}}, and as far as {{p|Greenland}} (where the
Layer is still visible in ice cores). A tsunami from the caldera
Collapse would have struck the northern coast of {{p|Crete}}, devastating

Coastal cities and ports. The ash would have poisoned the fields
And disrupted agriculture for years. And the {{p|Minoan}} civilization,
Dependent on its naval commerce and its agricultural base, was
Shattered by the eruption.

                                The {{p|Minoan}} civilization did not
End immediately. The palaces continued to function for another
Generation or two. But the civilization was wounded. And then,
Around fourteen hundred and fifty BCE, the {{p|Mycenaean Greeks}} from

The mainland—previously junior partners of the {{p|Minoans}}, culturally
Influenced by but politically independent—invaded and took over.
The {{p|Mycenaeans}} were {{c|R1b}}-carriers, {{p|Indo-European}}-speakers, the
Bronze Age manifestation of the {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion that had reached

{{p|Greece}}. They occupied {{p|Knossos}} and the other palaces. They replaced
{{p|Linear A}} with {{p|Linear B}}, a script adapted to write the {{p|Mycenaean}}
{{p|Greek}} language (and which, unlike {{p|Linear A}}, was successfully
Deciphered in nineteen fifty-two by {{c|Michael Ventris}}—who showed

That {{p|Linear B}} recorded an archaic form of {{p|Greek}}). The {{p|Minoan}}
Civilization was not destroyed—it was absorbed. And the {{p|Minoan}}
Religious and artistic traditions continued, in hybridized form,
Into the {{p|Mycenaean}} period. The {{c|Snake Goddess}} became, eventually,

The {{p|Greek}} {{c|Athena}}. The bull-leaping became the mythology of the
{{c|Minotaur}}. The labyrinth became a {{p|Greek}} cliché. The {{p|Minoan}}
Substrate persisted in {{p|Greek}} culture, under the {{p|Indo-European}}
Overlay, for the rest of {{p|Greek}} civilization.

And the language—the one actual {{p|Minoan}} language, the one that
{{p|Linear A}} wrote—is still undeciphered. We have the tablets. We can
Read the signs. We cannot match the signs to words in any known
Language. {{p|Linear A}}, like the {{p|Indus Valley}} script, is one of the

Great undeciphered scripts of the ancient world. Scholars have
Proposed various possibilities—that {{p|Linear A}} records an {{p|Anatolian}}
Language, or a pre-Greek {{p|Aegean}} language, or an isolate of its
Own—but no decipherment has been accepted. The {{p|Minoans}} wrote. We

Cannot read them. The silence of {{p|Linear A}} is one of the permanent
Gaps in our knowledge of the Bronze Age.

                                               The Gaiad honors the
{{p|Minoans}} as the great pre-{{p|Greek}} civilization of the {{p|Aegean}}.
The peaceful island civilization. The mother-goddess religion. The

Bull-leapers and saffron-gatherers. The unfortified palaces of
{{p|Knossos}} and {{p|Phaistos}}. The ships that carried olive oil to {{p|Egypt}}.
The priestesses with their snake-bracelets. The flushing toilets
And the light-wells and the frescoes of dolphins swimming along

The palace walls. A civilization unlike the militarized empires of
{{p|Mesopotamia}} and {{p|Egypt}}—smaller, more feminine, more nature-centered,
More sea-oriented, more confident in its own security. A civilization
That tried to live in palaces without walls. And that, for several

Northern {{p|Aegean}} was blanketed in ash, and the {{p|Mycenaean}} warriors
Came down from the mainland, and the mother goddess was folded

Fell silent in the mouths of its speakers. The island remained.

{{p|Crete}}. {{p|Minoan}}. The unwalled palaces. The mother goddess. The

# Chapter 149: Yamnaya — The Steppe Breaks Europe

The {{c|R}}-haplogroup chapter in its genetic register has already been
Written. This chapter is the civilizational register. What the
{{p|Yamnaya}} did to the world it entered.

                                                   And what they entered
Was {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}.

                                  {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}.

One of the largest and least-known civilizations of the {{p|European}}
{{p|Neolithic}}. From approximately fifty-two hundred to thirty-five
Hundred BCE, a sprawling culture occupied what is now eastern
{{p|Romania}}, {{p|Moldova}}, and central {{p|Ukraine}}. They built settlements

Larger than any other {{p|European}} settlements of the period—some of
The {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}} proto-cities, like {{p|Talianki}} and {{p|Maidanetske}},
Covered three hundred or more hectares and housed up to fifteen
Thousand people. By the standards of the time, these were gigantic.

Larger than contemporary {{p|Egyptian}} or {{p|Mesopotamian}} settlements.
And yet almost nothing of them survived. Why? Because the
{{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}} people had a strange and unique custom: they
Burned their settlements down. Every sixty or eighty years, they

Deliberately torched their own houses, razed their own cities, and
Rebuilt nearby on fresh ground. The pattern is consistent across
Centuries. Whole proto-cities reduced to ash. Whole populations
Migrating a few miles, rebuilding, and burning again. No one fully

Understands why. Ritual renewal? Pest control? Religious cycling?
Whatever the reason, it means the {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}} people are
Visible to archaeology only in the burn layers. Their houses were
Painted with spirals, red and white and black patterns. Their pottery

Was among the most beautiful of the {{p|European Neolithic}}—thin-walled,
Geometrically patterned, astonishingly refined. They worshipped a
Mother goddess, like most of {{p|Neolithic Europe}}. They had no walls
Around their cities. No weapons of war were deposited in their

Burials. The pre-existing evidence suggests a peaceful, female-
Centered, artistically sophisticated civilization that lived by
Farming, by gathering, by making pottery of extraordinary beauty.
And then, around thirty-five hundred BCE, it ended.

The {{p|Yamnaya}} arrived.

                              The {{c|R}}-haplogroup horsemen of the
{{p|Pontic-Caspian steppe}}, armed with the bronze axe and the domesticated
Horse and the corded-ware pot, rode west into the {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}

Heartland. And the {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}} civilization—peaceful,
Unwalled, goddess-centered—had no answer to the new arrivals.
Within a few generations, the proto-cities stopped being rebuilt.
The painted pottery stopped being made. The spirals disappeared

From the houses. The mother-goddess figurines stopped being
Modeled in clay. And in the archaeological layers that cover
The {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}} sites, a new material culture appears:
Corded-ware pottery, stone battle-axes, single-grave burials

Oriented on an east-west axis with the body in a flexed position,
Male skeletons with evidence of horseback riding in the bone
Wear patterns. The {{p|Yamnaya}} culture.

                                                The displacement is
One of the most total in {{p|European}} prehistory. Ancient DNA

Studies published in the twenty-teens showed that the {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}
Male population was essentially wiped out. The female population
Persisted—the women were, in substantial numbers, absorbed into
The incoming {{p|Yamnaya}} communities—but the male Y-chromosome

Signature of {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}} (mostly {{c|G}} and {{c|I}}) was almost
Entirely replaced by {{c|R1b}}. This is a pattern that will repeat

                                                              And the
Religious displacement was equally total. The mother goddess was
Replaced by the sky father. The spiral was replaced by the wheel.

The peaceful agricultural village was replaced by the patriarchal
Warrior band. The female-centered kinship of {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}
Was replaced by the patrilineal inheritance of {{p|Yamnaya}}. And the
Indo-European languages—ancestor of {{p|Greek}} and {{p|Latin}} and {{p|Sanskrit}}

And all their daughters—took the place of whatever language the
{{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}} peoples had spoken. That language is lost.
We have no record of it. The great {{p|European Neolithic}} linguistic
Substrate is gone, with only a few possible relics (like {{p|Basque}})

Surviving into the historical period.

                                           From the {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}
Heartland, the {{p|Yamnaya}} continued west. The {{p|Corded Ware culture}}
Spread across central and northern {{p|Europe}}. The {{p|Beaker Culture}}

Pushed west to {{p|Iberia}} and north to {{p|Britain}} and {{p|Ireland}}. By
Twenty-five hundred BCE, the {{p|Indo-European}} populations had
Reached the {{p|Atlantic}} coast. The whole of {{p|Europe}} had been
Overlaid by the {{c|R}}-haplogroup migration. And the languages that

Emerged from this overlay are the languages that almost every
{{p|European}} still speaks today. {{p|Celtic}}. {{p|Germanic}}. {{p|Italic}} (which
Would become {{p|Latin}} and its {{p|Romance}} daughters). {{p|Slavic}}. {{p|Baltic}}.
All from the same source. All from the {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion out of

The {{p|Pontic-Caspian steppe}}.

                                     Meanwhile, other branches of
The {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion moved south and east. Into {{p|Anatolia}}, where
A very early branch became the {{p|Hittites}} (covered later). Into

{{p|Iran}} and {{p|Central Asia}}, where later migrations would become the
{{p|Indo-Iranian}} peoples. Into {{p|India}}, where—around fifteen hundred
BCE—{{c|R1a}}-carrying peoples entered through the {{p|Khyber Pass}} and
The {{p|Hindu Kush}} and gradually merged with the remnant population

Of the collapsing {{p|Indus Valley}} civilization. That is the {{p|Aryan
Migration}}, covered in a later chapter.

                                             The {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion
Is, along with the original out-of-Africa migration, one of the

Two most consequential population movements in human history. It
Carried {{p|Indo-European}} languages across most of {{p|Eurasia}}. It
Established patriarchal, patrilineal social structures across the
Whole zone. It replaced earlier mother-goddess religions with

Sky-father religions everywhere it went. And the genetic signature
Of {{c|R1a}} and {{c|R1b}}—the two great {{p|Yamnaya}} Y-chromosome haplogroups—
Is today dominant across essentially all of {{p|Europe}} and much of
{{p|India}} and {{p|Iran}}. Billions of people are {{p|Yamnaya}}'s grandchildren.

And the Gaiad's account of this is complex, because the Gaiad
Does not simply celebrate it. The {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion was, in
Many places, a destruction. The peaceful mother-goddess cultures
Of old {{p|Europe}}—{{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}, {{p|Minoan Crete}} (partially),

The builders of {{p|Malta}} and {{p|Stonehenge}} (at least in the earlier
Phases)—were overrun and largely erased. The feminine religious
Substrate of old {{p|Europe}} was submerged under the patriarchal
Sky-father. The goddess did not die, but she was demoted, converted

Into the consort of the new king-god, transformed into {{c|Hera}} or
{{c|Frigg}} or {{c|Parvati}}, lesser than the male divine. And the peoples
Who had been the builders of old {{p|Europe}}—the ones who carved the
{{p|Venus}} figurines, the ones who raised the first {{p|Neolithic}} temples,

The ones whose cultural legacy lay in spiral patterns and dolphin-
Frescoes and fat-lady statues—were, in their male lineage, largely
Exterminated. A genetic bottleneck is visible in the Y-chromosome
Data for {{p|European}} populations around thirty-five hundred BCE. The

{{p|Yamnaya}} males replaced the pre-existing males. The pre-existing
Females were absorbed. This was not a peaceful migration. It was,
By any modern standard, a genocide—or several genocides, spread
Across the continent over a thousand years.

The Gaiad does not simplify this. The Yamnaya brought many things:
The horse, the wheel, the Indo-European languages, the heroic
Warrior ethos that would eventually produce {{p|Homeric}} epic and the
{{p|Vedic}} hymns and the {{p|Eddaic}} poetry. These are genuine cultural

Achievements. But they were achieved at the cost of destroying
The civilizations they overrode. The mother goddess of old {{p|Europe}}
Did not survive. Her worshippers were killed or absorbed. Her
Language was forgotten. Her symbols were rewritten into the

Iconography of the sky-father's consort. And the {{p|European}} who
Walks through the museum today and admires the {{p|Cucuteni-Trypillia}}
Pottery is, in most cases, a {{c|R1b}}-carrier—a descendant of the
People who destroyed the civilization that made the pottery he

Admires. The body that walks through the museum and the hands
That shaped the pot on display are not, in genealogical terms,
Connected. The {{p|European}} cultural inheritance includes both the
Destroyers and the destroyed, layered together in the same

Continent and often in the same individual. The old Europe was
Erased. The new Europe was built on its graves. And the new Europe
Brought its own achievements—but never honestly reckoned with
What it had replaced. Until, in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries,

Scholars like {{c|Marija Gimbutas}} began to reconstruct what had been
Lost. Her argument—that old Europe had been a peaceful, female-
Centered, goddess-worshipping civilization that the {{p|Indo-European}}
Invaders destroyed—was controversial in its time and remains

Debated today. But the broad archaeological and genetic picture has
Substantially vindicated her. The old Europe existed. The {{p|Yamnaya}}
Overran it. The cultural and religious shift from goddess to sky-
Father was real. And the genetic evidence of that shift is

Unambiguous.

               The Gaiad holds this complexity. The Indo-European
Achievement is real, and its cost is real, and both are part of
The history. The {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion is not a clean victory to

Celebrate; it is a pivot, and the pivot has a shadow. The shadow
Is the old Europe that was lost. The light is the new Europe that
Was built. Both are in the record. Both are in the body. And the
Task of honest genealogy is to hold them together.

{{p|Yamnaya}}. The horse. The wheel. The Indo-European tongue. The
Sky-father. The patriarchal warrior band. The replacement of old
Europe with new Europe. The cultural package that made the world
We still live in. And the grave of the world we replaced.

{{p|Yamnaya}}. The steppe expansion. The Indo-European arrival. Stand.


# Chapter 150: The Yellow Emperor

And to the east, in the valley of the {{p|Yellow River}},
Another civilization was taking shape. Not by invasion.
Not by the sudden arrival of horse-riding steppe peoples.
By slow accretion, across thousands of years, of {{c|O}}-

Haplogroup farming populations who had been in the region
Since the {{p|Neolithic}}. This civilization would become {{p|China}}.
And its mythic founder—the figure at whom all later {{p|Chinese}}
Historical memory points back—was the {{c|Yellow Emperor}}.

                                                                {{c|Huangdi}}.
The {{c|Yellow Emperor}}. Traditionally dated to approximately twenty-
Seven hundred BCE. One of the {{p|Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors}}—
The mythic rulers who, in {{p|Chinese}} traditional history, preceded

The {{p|Xia dynasty}} and founded the civilization. {{c|Huangdi}}'s name
Means "Yellow Emperor" because he was associated with the yellow
Earth of the {{p|North China Plain}}—the loess soil blown in from the
{{p|Gobi Desert}} by millennia of northern winds, rich and fertile and

Yellow-colored, the soil on which the {{p|Yellow River}} civilization
Was built. {{c|Huangdi}} was the first of the great culture-heroes
Who, according to tradition, gave the {{p|Chinese}} people their essential
Arts: he invented the bow and arrow, the cart, ceramics, silk (via

His wife {{c|Leizu}}, who is credited with discovering sericulture
By accidentally dropping a silkworm cocoon into her tea and
Unwinding the thread), the calendar, the compass (in a primitive
Form—a south-pointing chariot he used in battle), and the medical

Text called the {{p|Huangdi Neijing}} (the {{p|Yellow Emperor's Inner
Canon}}, the foundational text of traditional {{p|Chinese}} medicine).

{{c|Huangdi}} is the {{p|Chinese}} equivalent of what {{c|Prometheus}} and
{{c|Hephaestus}} and {{c|Hermes}} are in the {{p|Greek}} tradition—the culture-

Hero who brings the arts of civilization to humanity, combined
Into one figure. He fought wars—against {{c|Chiyou}}, a rebellious
Warlord with a bronze head and horns, in the {{p|Battle of Zhuolu}}
Which is traditionally dated around twenty-five hundred BCE. And

He won. {{c|Chiyou}} was defeated. {{c|Huangdi}} unified the tribes of the
{{p|Yellow River}} basin into a single civilization, and his descendants
Became the ruling dynasties of {{p|Chinese}} civilization for the rest
Of its history. Every subsequent {{p|Chinese}} emperor would claim,

At some level, descent from {{c|Huangdi}}. The {{p|Han Chinese}} people,
To this day, call themselves "{{p|descendants of the Yellow Emperor}}"
(Yanhuang zisun, combining {{c|Huangdi}} and his proto-historical
Contemporary {{c|Yandi}}, the "Flame Emperor"). The civilizational self-

Concept of {{p|China}} is, in its traditional form, anchored in {{c|Huangdi}}.

And is this history? Not quite. {{c|Huangdi}} is a mythic figure, not
An archaeologically attested king. The {{p|Three Sovereigns and Five
Emperors}} belong to the semi-legendary zone that precedes the

Actual historical record of {{p|China}}. The first archaeologically
Attested {{p|Chinese}} dynasty is the {{p|Shang}} (from approximately
Fifteen fifty BCE), and even the {{p|Xia}} dynasty—which precedes the
{{p|Shang}} in traditional accounts—is not yet definitively confirmed

By archaeology, though sites like {{p|Erlitou}} are strong candidates.
{{c|Huangdi}} and his fellow {{p|Three Sovereigns}} ({{c|Fuxi}}, {{c|Nüwa}},
{{c|Shennong}}) and {{p|Five Emperors}} are the {{p|Chinese}} mythic prehistory—
The figures who populate the traditional chronologies before the

Historical record begins.

                                But the Gaiad honors them. Because
Even if {{c|Huangdi}} is a legendary figure rather than a historical
One, he represents something real: the consolidation of {{p|Chinese}}

Civilization on the {{p|North China Plain}}, the integration of multiple
Neolithic cultures ({{p|Yangshao}}, {{p|Longshan}}, {{p|Hongshan}}, and others)
Into the proto-civilization that would eventually become {{p|Shang}}-
Dynasty {{p|China}}. And this process—the gradual coalescence of

{{p|Chinese}} identity along the {{p|Yellow River}}—was underway throughout
The third millennium BCE. {{c|Huangdi}} is its legendary personification.
He is the {{p|Chinese}} answer to the question: where did we come from?
And the answer is: from the yellow earth, from the {{p|Yellow River}},

From the culture-hero who gave us the arts of civilization, from
The warrior-king who defeated the bronze-headed rebel and unified
The tribes of the {{p|Central Plain}}.

                                             And alongside {{c|Huangdi}},
The {{p|Three Sovereigns}} also deserve mention. {{c|Fuxi}}—who taught

Humanity fishing, cooking, and the {{p|bagua}} (the eight trigrams of
The {{p|I Ching}}). {{c|Nüwa}}—the serpent-bodied goddess who created
Humanity from yellow clay and who repaired the heavens when they
Cracked, melting five-colored stones to patch the sky. {{c|Shennong}}—

"The Divine Farmer," who taught humans agriculture by trying every
Plant to see which were edible, which were medicinal, which were
Poisonous, and who died when he finally ingested a plant too toxic
For his own invented cures. These figures are older than {{c|Huangdi}},

In the traditional chronology, and they represent the even deeper
Prehistory of {{p|Chinese}} civilization—the hunter-gatherer-to-farmer
Transition in the {{p|Yellow River}} basin. {{c|Fuxi}} is the transition
Out of pure hunting and gathering. {{c|Nüwa}} is the cosmological

Origin myth. {{c|Shennong}} is the invention of agriculture and
Herbalism. Together they form the {{p|Chinese}} mythic prehistory
That leads to {{c|Huangdi}}'s unification.

                                                 And what makes the
{{p|Chinese}} tradition distinctive, in the Gaiad's reading, is the

Continuity. Every subsequent dynasty of {{p|Chinese}} history has
Maintained an unbroken connection to {{c|Huangdi}}'s mythic founding.
The {{p|Shang}} emperors claimed descent from him. The {{p|Zhou}} emperors
Claimed descent from him. The {{p|Qin}} and {{p|Han}} claimed descent. The

{{p|Tang}} and {{p|Song}} and {{p|Ming}} and {{p|Qing}} all maintained the ritual
Linkage to the {{p|Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors}}. Even the
Twentieth-century {{p|Republic of China}} and the {{p|People's Republic}}
Have retained the cultural memory, if not the official state

Worship. The {{p|Mausoleum of the Yellow Emperor}}, in {{p|Shaanxi Province}},
Is a functional national shrine where {{p|Chinese}} presidents still
Offer ceremonial sacrifices at the annual {{p|Qingming}} festival.
{{c|Huangdi}} has never been deposed as the mythic founder of the

{{p|Chinese}} nation. Four thousand seven hundred years after his
Legendary reign, the state still bows to him. This is, by any
Measure, the longest continuous national founding mythology on
Earth.

        Compare this to the situation in other civilizations. {{p|Egypt}}
Broke with the pharaonic tradition when {{p|Christianity}} arrived. The
{{p|Mesopotamian}} dynasties replaced each other repeatedly, each
Erasing the sacred lineage of the previous. {{p|Greece}} and {{p|Rome}}

Secularized their founding myths and eventually abandoned the
Religious claims of their origins. {{p|Christianity}} and {{p|Islam}} replaced
The earlier sacred genealogies of their regions with new ones
Centered on {{c|Abraham}} and {{c|Muhammad}}. But {{p|China}} never broke.

{{c|Huangdi}} has outlasted every other ancient founder-figure in
Continuous national veneration. The {{p|Chinese}} civilizational
Self-concept has never reset.

                                    And the language reinforces this.
{{p|Chinese}} characters are the only major writing system still in

Use that has maintained substantial continuity from its Bronze Age
Origins. The oracle bones of the {{p|Shang}} dynasty, written around
Thirteen hundred BCE, used characters that a modern {{p|Chinese}} reader
Can partially decipher. Thirteen hundred BCE to the twenty-first

Century—three thousand three hundred years of writing-system
Continuity. No other civilization has anything comparable. {{p|Egyptian}}
Hieroglyphs died. {{p|Cuneiform}} died. {{p|Phoenician}}, {{p|Greek}}, and {{p|Latin}}
Alphabets evolved into forms their ancestors would not recognize.

Only {{p|Chinese}} characters have maintained their fundamental form
And function across the entire historical record. The cultural
Memory is encoded in the writing. And the writing traces back,
Step by step, to the oracle bones, and from there (in legend) to

{{c|Huangdi}} and the invention of the script.

                                                     So the chapter
Honors him. {{c|Huangdi}}. The {{c|Yellow Emperor}}. The legendary
Unifier of the {{p|Yellow River}} civilization. The culture-hero who

Gave the {{p|Chinese}} people their arts. The mythic ancestor whose
Veneration has outlasted every other ancient founder's. The
Figure who stands, in the {{p|Chinese}} imagination, where {{c|Adam}} stands
In the Hebrew imagination and where {{c|Rama}} stands in the {{p|Indian}}

Imagination and where the {{c|Jade Emperor}} stands in the {{p|Chinese}}
Celestial hierarchy. The earthly progenitor. The founder who is
Still present.

                   And the Gaiad is careful here. The Gaiad does not
Claim that {{c|Huangdi}} is a historical king in the same sense that

{{c|Sargon}} is. The archaeological record is clear: there is no hard
Evidence for a specific ruler named {{c|Huangdi}} who lived around
Twenty-seven hundred BCE. He is a figure of mythic memory, not
Historical record. But the Gaiad also does not dismiss him. Because

The cultural reality that {{c|Huangdi}} represents—the founding of
{{p|Chinese}} civilization in the {{p|Yellow River}} basin, the consolidation
Of multiple Neolithic cultures into a single civilizational project,
The invention of the techniques that would allow {{p|China}} to become

The most populous and longest-continuous civilization on earth—
Is real. And {{c|Huangdi}} is the legendary face of that reality. He
Stands for something that actually happened, even if he himself
As a specific individual did not.

                                        The legend and the truth are
Tangled. The Gaiad accepts the tangle. And the chapter pays its
Respects to the {{c|Yellow Emperor}} in the same spirit that it pays
Its respects to {{c|Noah}}—as the mythic personification of a real

Historical transformation, deserving of honor in the register of
Myth even where it cannot be anchored in the register of hard
History. The transformation is real. The person may or may not be.
The civilization is real. The memory is real. The four thousand

Seven hundred years of continuous veneration are real. And they
All point back to the same place: the {{p|North China Plain}}, the
{{p|Yellow River}}, the loess earth, and the figure who—in the {{p|Chinese}}
Memory—founded the civilization that has persisted, through every

Subsequent upheaval, until the present day.

{{c|Huangdi}}. The {{c|Yellow Emperor}}. {{p|China}}'s mythic founder.
The culture-hero of the {{p|Yellow River}}. The ancestor claimed
By a hundred generations of {{p|Chinese}} civilization. Stand.


# Chapter 151: Neo-Sumerian Empire and Abraham

And while the {{c|Yellow Emperor}} was passing into legend in the
{{p|Yellow River}} basin, the {{p|Tigris-Euphrates}} valley was recovering
From its own collapse. The {{p|Gutians}} had been expelled. A
{{p|Sumerian}} restoration was underway. The city of {{p|Ur}} rose to

Prominence under {{c|Ur-Nammu}}, founder of the {{p|Third Dynasty of Ur}}—
The {{p|Ur III}} dynasty, the {{p|Neo-Sumerian}} empire. And it is into
This empire, in the Gaiad's reading, that {{c|Abraham}} is born.

{{c|Ur-Nammu}} reigned from approximately twenty-one twelve to twenty
Ninety-five BCE, according to the middle chronology. He united
{{p|Mesopotamia}} under a new {{p|Sumerian}} hegemony, rebuilt the
Infrastructure damaged by the {{p|Gutian}} interregnum, and promulgated

The {{p|Code of Ur-Nammu}}—the oldest known law code in human history,
Predating {{c|Hammurabi}}'s more famous code by three hundred years.
The {{p|Code of Ur-Nammu}} established fines and penalties for
Various offenses. It was remarkably restrained compared to later

Law codes: most punishments were monetary fines rather than
Physical penalties. Capital punishment was reserved for murder,
Robbery, adultery, and rape. The general tenor was of a just
Society trying to stabilize itself after a period of disorder.

{{c|Ur-Nammu}} also built the {{p|Great Ziggurat of Ur}}—the massive
Stepped temple-platform that is still partially standing today.
Dedicated to the moon-god {{c|Nanna}} (the patron deity of {{p|Ur}}), the
Ziggurat was a pyramid-like structure with stairs leading up to a

Shrine at the summit. It was the largest religious building in
{{p|Mesopotamia}} at the time. The {{p|Ur III}} dynasty invested in
Architecture, law, administration, and culture. {{p|Sumerian}} literature
Flourished under their patronage. The {{p|Sumerian King List}}—the

Document that catalogs the legendary and historical kings of
{{p|Mesopotamia}} from "before the flood" down through the {{p|Ur III}} era—
Was compiled in its canonical form during this period. The epic
Of {{c|Gilgamesh}}, which had existed in oral and early written forms

For centuries, was elaborated and refined during the {{p|Ur III}} era.
This was a renaissance of {{p|Sumerian}} culture—possibly the last
Great flowering before the language itself began to die out and
Be replaced by {{p|Akkadian}} as the spoken tongue of {{p|Mesopotamia}}.

And it is in this world, according to the Gaiad's reading of the
Biblical account, that {{c|Abraham}} lived his early life. {{p|Genesis}}
Tells us that {{c|Abram}}—as he was called before his name was changed—
Was born in "{{p|Ur of the Chaldees}}." The identification of this {{p|Ur}}

With the {{p|Sumerian}} city in southern {{p|Mesopotamia}} is traditional
Though not certain—some scholars have proposed a northern {{p|Ur}} in
{{p|Anatolia}} instead. But the southern identification is the most
Common, and it is the one the Gaiad adopts. {{c|Abraham}} is, in this

Reading, a man of {{p|Ur III}}. He is a son of the {{p|Neo-Sumerian}}
Empire. He speaks {{p|Akkadian}} (and perhaps {{p|Sumerian}} as a learned
Second language). He grows up in a city whose ziggurat dominates
The skyline and whose commercial networks extend across the entire

{{p|Near East}}. His father {{c|Terah}} is described in {{p|Genesis}} as a maker
Of idols—a craftsman in the image-making industry that supplied
The temples of {{p|Mesopotamia}}. {{c|Abraham}}'s rejection of idolatry, which
The later biblical and {{p|Islamic}} traditions would make central to

His identity, is portrayed as a rejection of his father's profession.
The son smashes the idols the father carves. A generational
Break in a literal family workshop.

                                         And then the {{p|Ur III}}
Dynasty falls.

                    The collapse is dramatic. The final {{p|Ur III}}
King, {{c|Ibbi-Sin}}, reigned from approximately twenty-twenty-eight
To two-thousand-four BCE. During his reign, the empire weakened

Dramatically. A combination of factors: {{p|Amorite}} migrations into
{{p|Mesopotamia}} from the west (the {{p|Amorites}} were a {{p|Semitic}}-speaking
Nomadic people from the {{p|Syrian}} desert), a severe drought and
Agricultural crisis (a small echo of the {{p|4.2-kiloyear event}}), and

Administrative breakdown. Provinces seceded. Famine struck. And in
{{p|Ibbi-Sin}}'s twenty-fourth year, the kingdom of {{p|Elam}}—the ancient
Civilization of what is now western {{p|Iran}}, the {{p|Susa}}-centered
Civilization that had been {{p|Sumer}}'s rival and occasional conqueror

For two thousand years—invaded. {{p|Elam}} sacked {{p|Ur}}. {{c|Ibbi-Sin}} was
Captured and taken in chains to {{p|Susa}}. The city of {{p|Ur}} was
Destroyed—burned, looted, depopulated. The {{p|Ur III}} dynasty ended.
The {{p|Neo-Sumerian}} empire collapsed. A {{p|Sumerian}} lament, the

{{p|Lament for Ur}}, was composed in the aftermath—a devastating
Poetic elegy for the destroyed city, attributed in the tradition
To the goddess {{c|Ningal}}, patron of {{p|Ur}}, weeping for her ruined
Temple and her scattered people. The {{p|Lament for Ur}} is one of

The earliest surviving examples of the city-lament genre, and its
Imagery would echo centuries later in the biblical {{p|Book of
Lamentations}}, which mourns the destruction of {{p|Jerusalem}}. The
Genre and its vocabulary flow from {{p|Ur}} to {{p|Jerusalem}}.

And in this context—in the chaos following the fall of {{p|Ur}}—the
Gaiad locates {{c|Abraham}}'s departure. {{p|Genesis}} says that {{c|Terah}}
And his family left {{p|Ur}} and traveled to {{p|Haran}} (a city in what is
Now southeastern {{p|Turkey}}, near the border with {{p|Syria}}). Why did

They leave? {{p|Genesis}} does not explain. The Gaiad's reading is that
They left because {{p|Ur}} had been destroyed. The family that had lived
In the city of the moon-god for generations, making idols in the
Shadow of the {{p|Great Ziggurat}}, was forced to migrate when the

{{p|Elamites}} burned the city. They went north, following the
{{p|Euphrates}} upstream, seeking refuge in territories not yet affected
By the collapse. They stopped in {{p|Haran}}, an important trading city
On the route between {{p|Mesopotamia}} and {{p|Anatolia}}. There, according

To the biblical account, {{c|Terah}} died. And there {{c|Abraham}} received
The call to go further—to leave his country and his kindred and
His father's house and go to the land that {{c|God}} would show him.

And here the Gaiad takes a specific narrative position. {{c|Abraham}}'s
Journey from {{p|Haran}} south to {{p|Canaan}}—the journey that will
Establish the {{p|Israelite}} and eventually the {{p|Jewish}}, {{p|Christian}},
And {{p|Islamic}} traditions—passes through a place with extraordinary

Deep-time significance: {{p|Sanliurfa}}, in what is now southeastern
{{p|Turkey}}. And {{p|Sanliurfa}} is the modern city closest to {{p|Göbekli Tepe}}.
The ancient hunter-gatherer temple complex that predates agriculture,
Burial, and every named civilization. The oldest religious monument

On earth. And the Gaiad's reading is that {{c|Abraham}} stopped here.
Stopped in {{p|Sanliurfa}}. And that in {{p|Sanliurfa}}, he met {{c|Melchizedek}}—
The mysterious priest-king of {{p|Salem}} who appears in {{p|Genesis 14}}
To bless {{c|Abraham}}, receive a tithe, and disappear. The biblical

Text places this meeting later in {{c|Abraham}}'s journey, after he
Rescues his nephew {{c|Lot}} from the {{p|Mesopotamian}} kings. The Gaiad
Relocates it—in the Gaiad's reading, {{c|Melchizedek}} is "{{c|George
Gobeklius}}," the last priest of the {{p|Göbekli Tepe}} tradition, the

Figure whose ancestral religious memory traces back to the
Eleven-thousand-year-old temple-complex still buried in the hill
Nearby. And {{c|Abraham}}, stopping in {{p|Sanliurfa}} on his southward
Journey, encounters this priest and receives the blessing of the

Old religious order.

                           This is a major move. The Gaiad is making
{{c|Abraham}} the spiritual heir of the {{p|Göbekli Tepe}} tradition. The
Religious genealogy of the {{p|Abrahamic}} religions is extended backward

Not merely to {{c|Noah}} but to the hunter-gatherer priests who raised
The T-shaped pillars on the hill above {{p|Sanliurfa}} eleven thousand
Years ago. {{c|Melchizedek}} is the bridge. The tithe {{c|Abraham}} pays is
The transmission of spiritual authority from the old priesthood to

The new. The blessing {{c|Abraham}} receives is the legitimation of his
Mission by the oldest continuous religious lineage in human history.
This is the Gaiad's quiet insertion. It is not in the biblical text.
But it is suggested by the geography—{{p|Sanliurfa}} really is the city

Closest to {{p|Göbekli Tepe}}, and {{c|Abraham}}'s journey really does pass
Through it. And the Gaiad takes the geographical fact as a narrative
Opening to link the deep prehistoric religious tradition to the
Monotheistic tradition that {{c|Abraham}} is about to found.

After {{p|Sanliurfa}}, {{c|Abraham}} continues south. Through {{p|Aleppo}} or
{{p|Damascus}}, through the {{p|Syrian}} desert, into the land of {{p|Canaan}}.
He settles in {{p|Shechem}}, then {{p|Bethel}}, then the {{p|Negev}}. He
Journeys to {{p|Egypt}} during a famine and returns. He separates from

{{c|Lot}}. He rescues {{c|Lot}} from the four kings of {{p|Mesopotamia}}. He
Makes the covenant with {{c|God}} in which the land of {{p|Canaan}} is
Promised to his descendants. He nearly sacrifices {{c|Isaac}} on {{p|Mount
Moriah}}. He dies at a hundred and seventy-five and is buried in

The {{p|Cave of Machpelah}} in {{p|Hebron}}, where {{c|Sarah}} is already
Buried. The details of {{c|Abraham}}'s biography will not be rehearsed

# Chapter 152: Hammurabi — Law and the Old Babylonian Empire

In the centuries after the fall of {{p|Ur}}, {{p|Mesopotamia}} fragmented
Again. The {{p|Amorites}}—the {{p|Semitic}}-speaking nomads who had been
Trickling in throughout the late {{p|Ur III}} period—established
Themselves as the new ruling class across the region. {{p|Amorite}}

Kings took the thrones of cities throughout the {{p|Tigris-Euphrates}}
Valley. {{p|Isin}} and {{p|Larsa}} competed for supremacy in the south.
{{p|Mari}} and {{p|Eshnunna}} and {{p|Assur}} carved out their own spheres
In the north and east. And one relatively minor {{p|Amorite}} city-

State, {{p|Babylon}}, began its rise. {{p|Babylon}}—the city whose name
Comes from {{p|Akkadian}} {{c|Babilim}}, "Gate of God"—had been a
Modest provincial center for centuries. But under its {{p|First
Dynasty}}, founded around nineteen hundred BCE, {{p|Babylon}} grew. And

Under its sixth king, {{c|Hammurabi}}, it became the capital of an
Empire.

          {{c|Hammurabi}}.

                                Reigning from approximately seventeen
Ninety-two to seventeen fifty BCE. Forty-two years. A long reign,

And a decisive one. {{c|Hammurabi}}'s first decades were spent consolidating
His power at home and building diplomatic alliances with neighboring
Kingdoms. He reformed the administration. He rebuilt temples. He
Extended canals. He was, by the standards of {{p|Mesopotamian}} kingship,

A patient and methodical ruler. And then, in the second half of his
Reign, he struck. A rapid series of military campaigns reduced his
Rivals one by one. {{p|Larsa}} fell. {{p|Eshnunna}} fell. {{p|Mari}} fell. By
The end of his reign, {{c|Hammurabi}} ruled the largest {{p|Mesopotamian}}

Empire since {{c|Sargon of Akkad}} six hundred years earlier. He had
Unified the {{p|Tigris-Euphrates}} valley under {{p|Babylonian}} rule. The
{{p|Old Babylonian}} empire was born.

                                          And {{c|Hammurabi}}'s name is
Remembered, today, not primarily for the military conquests but for

The law code. The {{p|Code of Hammurabi}}. Two hundred and eighty-two
Laws, inscribed in {{p|Akkadian}} cuneiform on a seven-and-a-half-foot-
Tall stele of black diorite. The stele was erected in the temple of
{{c|Marduk}} in {{p|Babylon}} and other public locations across the empire,

So that citizens could see the law and understand their rights and
Obligations. The stele's top shows {{c|Hammurabi}} standing before the
Sun-god {{c|Shamash}} (the god of justice) receiving the law from the
Divine hand. The image is the first great icon of law-as-divine-

Gift. {{c|Hammurabi}} claims his code not as his own invention but as
A transcription of divine will. The god gives the law; the king
Transmits it; the people obey it. This is the cosmological claim
Of every subsequent legal code in the {{p|Near East}}: that law comes

From the divine, that the ruler is merely the messenger, that the
Social order reflects a cosmic order that predates and outranks
Any individual human authority.

                                    And the content of the {{p|Code}}
Is striking. Some of its laws are brutal by modern standards—the

Principle of {{p|lex talionis}} ("an eye for an eye") is explicitly
Formulated here. If a man destroys the eye of another man, his
Own eye shall be destroyed. If a man breaks another man's bone,
His own bone shall be broken. The punishments are graded by class:

Crimes against nobility carry heavier penalties than crimes against
Commoners, and crimes against slaves are treated as property damage.
The {{p|Code}} is not egalitarian. It is stratified, hierarchical, and
Often gruesome. But it is also, in its own time, an advance. It is

Written law, publicly posted, applying across the entire empire,
Accessible to every literate citizen. It limits the arbitrary power
Of judges and local administrators. It establishes procedural
Protections: false accusations carry penalties, witnesses must

Testify, evidence must be weighed. There are protections for women—
Marriage contracts, divorce procedures, inheritance rights. There
Are regulations for business: commercial contracts, partnerships,
Debt relationships, liability for damage. The {{p|Code}} is not just

A catalog of punishments. It is a comprehensive framework for the
Operation of a complex urban commercial society.

                                                      And it is
Remembered because it is one of the earliest fully preserved legal

Codes in human history. {{c|Ur-Nammu}}'s code predates it by three
Hundred years. The {{p|Code of Lipit-Ishtar}} (an earlier {{p|Isin}} king)
Predates it as well. But {{c|Hammurabi}}'s code is the most complete
Preserved example, and its influence on later legal traditions—

Through the {{p|Assyrian}} and {{p|Neo-Babylonian}} codes, through
{{p|Persian}} imperial law, through the {{p|Mosaic}} law of the {{p|Hebrew}}
Bible (which shows significant parallels to {{c|Hammurabi}}), through
{{p|Roman}} law, and onward—is foundational. Every legal system that

Descends from the ancient {{p|Near East}} carries {{c|Hammurabi}}'s
Influence. Every "eye for an eye" principle. Every notion of
Written public law. Every claim that law is divinely sanctioned.

And {{c|Hammurabi}}, in the Gaiad's reading, is {{c|Abraham}}'s cousin.
Not in the narrow biological sense—the two figures are separated by
Perhaps a century or more—but in the broader genealogical sense
That the Gaiad applies. Both are {{c|J}}-haplogroup {{p|Semitic}} men.

Both are products of the {{p|Amorite}} migrations that reshaped
{{p|Mesopotamia}} in the early second millennium BCE. Both descend, at
Some remove, from the same pool of {{p|Semitic}} ancestors who had
Spread across the region after the fall of {{p|Ur III}}. The Gaiad

Makes them cousins in the cultural sense—contemporaries, or near-
Contemporaries, who shared a haplogroup, a language family, a
Regional context, and a historical moment. Both are figures of the
Post-{{p|Sumerian}} {{p|Semitic}} ascendancy in {{p|Mesopotamia}}. Both are

Founders of traditions that will outlast them by millennia.

And the contrast between them is instructive. {{c|Abraham}} is a
Nomad, a wanderer, a man who leaves his city and lives in tents.
{{c|Hammurabi}} is a city-king, a builder of walls, a codifier of

Urban law. {{c|Abraham}} founds a religion. {{c|Hammurabi}} founds a
Legal system. {{c|Abraham}}'s legacy is the worship of a single
Invisible god. {{c|Hammurabi}}'s legacy is the written code binding
Urban citizens to their obligations. Two different modes of

Civilizational contribution. Two cousins, at the junction of {{c|J1}}
And {{c|J2}}, each choosing a different path. One walks south into
The desert with his flocks; the other reigns in the brick-built
City of the gate-of-god. One becomes the patriarch of monotheism;

The other becomes the author of the code that will be copied and
Adapted for two thousand years. Both stand. Both matter.

And {{c|Hammurabi}}'s empire does not last. Within a generation of
His death, {{p|Babylon}} was weakened by succession struggles. His

Son {{c|Samsu-iluna}} faced rebellions across the empire. The southern
Provinces seceded. The {{p|Sealand Dynasty}} established itself in the
Marshes of the {{p|Persian Gulf}}. And then, in fifteen ninety-five BCE,
The {{p|Hittites}}—Indo-European invaders from {{p|Anatolia}} whose chapter

Will come later—raided {{p|Babylon}} itself, sacked the city, and
Carried off the statue of {{c|Marduk}}. The {{p|Old Babylonian}} empire
Ended. The statue was eventually recovered, but the political
Power of {{p|Babylon}} was broken. A new dynasty, the {{p|Kassites}}, took

Power and ruled {{p|Mesopotamia}} for four centuries in a more
Decentralized and less expansionist mode. {{p|Babylon}} itself continued
To exist as a city, but its imperial period was over—until, a
Thousand years later, the {{p|Neo-Babylonian}} empire of {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}}

Would briefly restore its glory.

                                       But {{c|Hammurabi}}'s legacy
Survived the collapse. The code continued to be copied, studied,
And cited long after his empire was gone. Subsequent {{p|Mesopotamian}}

Kings claimed to be his legal heirs. Scribal schools used his code
As a teaching text. Copies of the {{p|Code of Hammurabi}} have been
Found across the {{p|Near East}} in contexts centuries later than his
Reign. He had done what {{c|Sargon}} had done at the political level—

Established a template that subsequent rulers would copy—but he
Had done it in the register of law rather than imperial administration.
{{c|Sargon}} was the prototype emperor. {{c|Hammurabi}} was the prototype
Legislator. And the empire fell faster than the law did.

The stele of the {{p|Code of Hammurabi}} was eventually taken to {{p|Susa}}
As plunder by the {{p|Elamites}} during a later invasion. It stood in
{{p|Susa}} for three thousand years before being rediscovered by {{p|French}}
Archaeologists in nineteen-oh-one. It is now in the {{p|Louvre}}. A

Traveler can stand in front of it and read, in the original
{{p|Akkadian}} cuneiform carved into the black diorite, the two hundred
And eighty-two laws by which {{c|Hammurabi}} ordered his empire. The
Inscription is still legible. The stele is one of the best-preserved

Major monuments of the ancient {{p|Near East}}. And the laws themselves
Are, in many cases, still recognizable as laws—still connected, by
A chain of textual and conceptual transmission, to the legal codes
Of modern nations.

                       {{c|Hammurabi}} of {{p|Babylon}}. The {{c|J}}-haplogroup
Cousin of {{c|Abraham}}. The emperor of the {{p|Old Babylonian}} dynasty.
The author—or promulgator—of the most famous law code of the
Ancient world. The figure through whom the {{p|Mesopotamian}} legal

Tradition reached its first great synthesis and was transmitted
To every subsequent {{p|Near Eastern}} civilization, and through them
To us. The bearded king on the black diorite stele, standing before
The sun-god, receiving the law. The law still legible. The king

Still legible. The empire long gone.

{{c|Hammurabi}}. {{p|Babylon}}. The {{p|Code}}. Stand.


# Chapter 153: The Middle Kingdom

And {{p|Egypt}} emerged from the {{p|First Intermediate Period}}.

The collapse of the {{p|Old Kingdom}} around twenty-two hundred BCE
Had fragmented the unified state into rival regional dynasties.
{{p|Upper}} and {{p|Lower Egypt}} had split. {{p|Herakleopolis}} ruled the
North. {{p|Thebes}}—an obscure provincial town at the time—gradually

Rose in the south. For over a century, {{p|Egypt}} was divided, weakened,
And suffering from the climate shifts that had brought down so
Many civilizations simultaneously across the {{p|Old World}}. And then,
Around twenty-sixty BCE, {{c|Mentuhotep II}}, king of {{p|Thebes}}, defeated

The northern dynasties and reunified the country. {{p|Egypt}} was whole
Again. The {{p|Middle Kingdom}} had begun.

                                                   The {{p|Middle Kingdom}}
Is generally dated from approximately twenty-fifty-five to seventeen
Hundred BCE—three and a half centuries of restored centralized rule.

It is often called, in {{p|Egyptian}} historiography, the classical
Period of {{p|Egyptian}} civilization—not because it was the wealthiest
Or the most expansive, but because it was the period of greatest
Literary and artistic refinement. The pyramid-building megalomania

Of the {{p|Old Kingdom}} had passed. The empire-building militarism of
The later {{p|New Kingdom}} had not yet arrived. The {{p|Middle Kingdom}} sat
In between—an {{p|Egypt}} that was confident but not aggressive, wealthy
But not extravagant, pious but not monumental. And in this quieter

Mode, it produced some of the finest {{p|Egyptian}} literature ever
Written.

            The {{p|Eleventh Dynasty}}, under {{c|Mentuhotep II}} and his
Successors, ruled from {{p|Thebes}}. The {{p|Twelfth Dynasty}}, which
Succeeded them around nineteen ninety BCE, moved the capital north

To {{p|Itjtawy}} (near modern {{p|El-Lisht}}), strategically positioned at
The junction between {{p|Upper}} and {{p|Lower Egypt}}. The {{p|Twelfth Dynasty}}
Included some of the most capable pharaohs of the entire {{p|Egyptian}}
Sequence: {{c|Amenemhat I}} (founder of the dynasty), {{c|Senusret I}},

{{c|Senusret III}}, and {{c|Amenemhat III}}. These kings consolidated royal
Authority, rebuilt infrastructure, launched ambitious irrigation
Projects (particularly the land-reclamation works in the {{p|Faiyum
Oasis}}), expanded {{p|Egyptian}} control south into {{p|Nubia}}, and

Patronized the arts and sciences on a scale that would make the
{{p|Middle Kingdom}} the reference-point for every subsequent
{{p|Egyptian}} renaissance.

                                 And the literature of the {{p|Middle
Kingdom}} is remarkable. This is the period of the great tales and

Wisdom texts that would be copied and studied in {{p|Egyptian}} schools
For the next fifteen hundred years. {{p|The Tale of Sinuhe}}—a narrative
Of an {{p|Egyptian}} noble who flees the court after a political
Disturbance, lives in exile among the {{p|Amorites}} in {{p|Canaan}}, marries

A {{p|Canaanite}} princess, rises to prominence in the foreign court,
And eventually returns home to {{p|Egypt}} to be buried among his
Ancestors—is one of the first great works of narrative prose
Literature in human history. It combines autobiography, adventure,

Reflection, and homecoming-longing in a form that prefigures the
Novel. {{p|Sinuhe}}'s yearning for {{p|Egypt}} during his exile—the famous
Passages in which he describes his desire to be buried in his
Homeland, to have an {{p|Egyptian}} funeral, to rejoin his ancestors

In the proper {{p|Egyptian}} way—is one of the most emotionally direct
Pieces of ancient literature that survives. Three thousand nine
Hundred years later, it still reads as recognizably human.

And {{p|The Shipwrecked Sailor}}—a tale of a sailor stranded on a
Magical island ruled by a giant talking serpent, who tells the

Sailor his own tragic story before sending him home with gifts.
The tale is a frame-narrative, a fantasy, a meditation on fate
And resilience. {{p|The Tale of the Eloquent Peasant}}—a small
Farmer appeals to the royal courts after being cheated by a

Corrupt official, delivering nine increasingly elaborate speeches
On the nature of justice. The king, secretly pleased by the
Eloquence, deliberately delays deciding the case so that more
Speeches will be produced. The tale is a meditation on rhetoric,

On justice, and on the relationship between the weak petitioner
And the powerful authorities. {{p|The Instruction of Amenemhat}}—a
Political-philosophical text in which the ghost of the murdered
{{c|Amenemhat I}} instructs his son on the treacheries of kingship

And the necessity of constant vigilance. {{p|The Instruction for
Merikare}}—a practical handbook for young princes on good
Governance. {{p|The Dialogue of a Man with His Ba}}—a suicidal man
Debates with his own soul about whether life is worth living, one

Of the earliest surviving philosophical texts on the meaning of
Existence. {{p|The Satire of the Trades}}—a humorous poem listing
The miseries of various manual occupations (the barber, the
Washerman, the field laborer, the carpenter) to encourage the

Reader's son to become a scribe instead.

                                                These are not the
Religious funerary texts of the {{p|Old Kingdom}} (the {{p|Pyramid Texts}})
Or the extended spell-books of the {{p|New Kingdom}} (the {{p|Book of the

Dead}}). These are literary works—short, focused, narratively or
Philosophically interesting, designed to be enjoyed as well as
Studied. They are {{p|Egypt}}'s classical literature. They were copied
Over and over by later scribes as school texts, which is why they

Survive in multiple manuscript traditions. And they reveal a
Civilization that, having settled into a more modest political
Scale after the grandiosity of the pyramid age, turned its energies
Toward the cultivation of mind and letter. The {{p|Middle Kingdom}}

Scribe was the intellectual ideal of subsequent {{p|Egyptian}}
Civilization. Every later period looked back at the {{p|Twelfth
Dynasty}} the way {{p|Renaissance Europe}} looked back at classical
{{p|Rome}}—as the golden age that later generations tried to imitate.

And religiously, the {{p|Middle Kingdom}} saw important developments.
{{c|Osiris}}—the god of the underworld, the mummified god whose story
Of death and resurrection had been the exclusive preserve of the
Royal afterlife in the {{p|Old Kingdom}}—was democratized. The

Possibility of eternal life through identification with {{c|Osiris}}
Was extended, in the {{p|Middle Kingdom}}, from the pharaoh alone to
The broader noble class, and eventually, in principle, to any
{{p|Egyptian}} who could afford the funerary rites. Every {{p|Egyptian}}

Could now become an {{c|Osiris}} at death. The {{p|Coffin Texts}}—spells
Inscribed on the sides of wooden coffins to guide the deceased
Through the underworld—were the {{p|Middle Kingdom}}'s expanded
Version of the {{p|Old Kingdom}}'s royal {{p|Pyramid Texts}}. The afterlife

Was being opened up. The cosmic judgment, in which the deceased's
Heart was weighed against the feather of {{c|Ma'at}} (truth/justice),
Applied to all {{p|Egyptians}}, not just the king. A moral-religious
Democratization that was, for the ancient world, striking.

And {{p|Middle Kingdom}} {{p|Egypt}} opened itself to its neighbors to a
Degree that the {{p|Old Kingdom}} had not. Trade flourished with the
{{p|Levant}}, with {{p|Nubia}}, with {{p|Punt}} (on the {{p|Somali}} or {{p|Yemeni}}
Coast of the {{p|Red Sea}}—the frankincense-and-myrrh source). Diplomatic

Relations were established with the {{p|Cretan}} {{p|Minoans}}, whose frescoes
Would later appear at {{p|Middle Kingdom}}-influenced sites across the
{{p|Aegean}}, and whose ceramics began arriving in {{p|Egyptian}} ports. The
Relationship with the {{p|Semitic}} peoples to the northeast intensified—

{{p|Canaanite}} populations began migrating into the {{p|Nile Delta}} in
Increasing numbers, settling in the eastern delta as pastoralists,
Farmers, and merchants. These were the {{p|Asiatics}}, as the {{p|Egyptians}}
Called them—the {{c|J}}-haplogroup {{p|Semitic}} speakers whose presence

Would gradually transform the demographic balance of {{p|Lower Egypt}}.
By the end of the {{p|Middle Kingdom}}, the eastern {{p|Nile Delta}} had a
Substantial {{p|Canaanite}} population coexisting with the native
{{p|Egyptian}} population. And this coexistence would, in a later

Generation, produce the {{p|Hyksos}}—the foreign dynasty that would
Eventually rule {{p|Lower Egypt}}. But the {{p|Middle Kingdom}} phase of
This process was peaceful. {{p|Canaanites}} were welcomed as useful
Subjects. {{p|Egyptian}} culture and {{p|Canaanite}} culture began the long

Process of mutual influence that would shape the ancient {{p|Near
Eastern}} world. And this is where the biblical {{c|Joseph}} story, the
Gaiad will argue in the following chapters, is historically situated—
During the later {{p|Middle Kingdom}} or the transition to the {{p|Second

Intermediate Period}}, when {{p|Canaanite}} families entering {{p|Egypt}}
Could rise to positions of prominence and influence.

The {{p|Middle Kingdom}} did not last. By the {{p|Thirteenth Dynasty}},
The central state began to weaken. Rapid succession of short-reigned

Pharaohs, climate pressures from the occasional low {{p|Nile}} flood,
And the gradual political autonomization of the {{p|Canaanite}} population
In the eastern delta led, around seventeen-forty to seventeen-thirty
BCE, to the fragmentation of {{p|Egyptian}} political authority. The

{{p|Hyksos}}—the "rulers of foreign lands," as the {{p|Egyptians}} called
Them—took control of the eastern delta and established their own
Dynasty there, with its capital at {{p|Avaris}}. The {{p|Second Intermediate
Period}} began. A second {{p|Egyptian}} collapse, three centuries after

The first. This second collapse would be the context of the biblical
{{c|Jacob}} and {{c|Joseph}} narratives, which the Gaiad will unpack in
The next chapter.

                      But the {{p|Middle Kingdom}} itself—as a phase, as
An achievement, as a cultural legacy—was {{p|Egypt}}'s classical age.

It produced the literature, the religious democratization, the
Foreign relations, and the domestic consolidation that subsequent
{{p|Egyptian}} civilization would look back at as its reference-point.
It is less famous, outside of {{p|Egyptological}} circles, than the

{{p|Old Kingdom}} with its pyramids or the {{p|New Kingdom}} with its
{{c|Tutankhamun}} and {{c|Ramesses II}}. But it was arguably the most
Culturally accomplished phase of {{p|Egyptian}} history. The pharaohs
Were less grandiose. The art was more refined. The literature was

More sophisticated. The religion was more democratic. And the
{{p|Egyptian}} sense of itself as a coherent civilization, after the
Near-death experience of the {{p|First Intermediate Period}}, was
Consolidated and strengthened. The civilization had survived its

First collapse and come back stronger in certain important ways.

{{p|Middle Kingdom}}. {{c|Mentuhotep II}}. The {{p|Twelfth Dynasty}}. {{p|Itjtawy}}.
{{p|The Tale of Sinuhe}}. The democratization of {{c|Osiris}}. The opening
To the {{p|Levant}}. The {{p|Canaanite}} presence in the delta. The classical

Age of {{p|Egyptian}} civilization.

{{p|Middle Kingdom}}. {{p|Egypt}} restored. Stand.


# Chapter 154: Jacob, Joseph, and the Hyksos

And so the story shifts to a specific {{p|Canaanite}} family.

{{c|Jacob}}—grandson of {{c|Abraham}}, son of {{c|Isaac}}, father of twelve
Sons who will become the twelve tribes of {{p|Israel}}. {{c|Jacob}}'s
Biography is the central patriarchal narrative of the biblical
{{p|Genesis}}—the wrestling with the angel at {{p|Peniel}}, the ladder-dream

At {{p|Bethel}}, the servitude to his uncle {{c|Laban}}, the fraught
Reconciliation with his brother {{c|Esau}}, the favoritism toward
{{c|Rachel}}'s children over {{c|Leah}}'s, the grief over the supposed death
Of {{c|Joseph}}. These are stories well-known in the {{p|Jewish}}, {{p|Christian}},

And {{p|Islamic}} traditions, and the Gaiad will not rehearse them in
Detail. What the Gaiad does do, in this chapter, is place {{c|Jacob}}
And his family historically—in the late {{p|Middle Kingdom}} or the
Beginning of the {{p|Second Intermediate Period}}, around seventeen-

Hundred to sixteen-fifty BCE, in the context of the {{p|Hyksos}}
Arrival in {{p|Egypt}}. And the Gaiad makes a specific claim: {{c|Joseph}}'s
Rise to prominence in {{p|Egypt}} is legible precisely because he is
A {{p|Canaanite}}—a member of the same {{c|J}}-haplogroup {{p|Semitic}} population

That is, at this moment, gaining political power in the eastern
{{p|Nile Delta}}.

                      The biblical account has {{c|Joseph}} sold by his
Brothers to a caravan of {{p|Ishmaelite}} traders, who carry him down
To {{p|Egypt}} and sell him to {{c|Potiphar}}, a high-ranking {{p|Egyptian}}

Official. {{c|Joseph}} rises in {{c|Potiphar}}'s household. Is falsely
Accused by {{c|Potiphar}}'s wife. Is imprisoned. Interprets the dreams
Of two fellow prisoners. Is eventually brought before the pharaoh
To interpret the pharaoh's own dreams—of seven fat cows and seven

Lean cows, of seven full ears of grain and seven withered ears.
{{c|Joseph}} predicts seven years of abundance followed by seven years
Of famine. The pharaoh appoints him vizier—second only to the king
Himself—to manage the stockpiling and distribution of grain. When

The famine comes, {{p|Egypt}} has enough. The surrounding regions do
Not. {{c|Jacob}}'s remaining sons come down to {{p|Egypt}} to buy grain.
{{c|Joseph}} recognizes them. They do not recognize him. A dramatic
Recognition scene follows, after which {{c|Joseph}} brings his entire

Family down to {{p|Egypt}} and settles them in the region of {{p|Goshen}}—
The eastern {{p|Nile Delta}}, the pastoral lands best suited to their
Herding lifestyle.

                       And the Gaiad's historical overlay on this
Narrative is the {{p|Hyksos}} framework. The {{p|Hyksos}}—whose name in

{{p|Egyptian}} is {{c|Heka-khasut}}, "rulers of foreign lands"—were a
{{p|Semitic}} population (primarily {{c|J2}}-haplogroup, with some admixture)
Who had been migrating into the eastern {{p|Nile Delta}} throughout the
Late {{p|Middle Kingdom}}. They spoke a {{p|West Semitic}} language related

To early {{p|Canaanite}} and {{p|Amorite}}. They worshipped {{p|Semitic}} gods
Like {{c|Baal}} and {{c|Anat}}, which they syncretized with {{p|Egyptian}}
{{c|Seth}} (the god of the desert, storms, and foreign regions). Their
Culture was a hybrid—retaining {{p|Canaanite}} dress, names, diet, and

Religious practices while adopting {{p|Egyptian}} administrative forms,
Hieroglyphic writing, and some {{p|Egyptian}} cultic practices. They
Brought with them technologies that the {{p|Egyptians}} had not yet
Developed: the horse-drawn chariot, the composite bow, advanced

# Chapter 155: The Aryan Migration

And while the {{p|Hyksos}} were settling in the {{p|Nile Delta}}, another
Foreign people was settling—halfway across the world—in another
Great river valley. The parallel is structural. Foreign populations
Entering pre-existing civilizations on two continents at the same

Time. In the west, the {{p|Hyksos}} entering {{p|Egypt}}. In the east, the
{{p|Aryans}} entering {{p|India}}. Both arriving in the eighteenth, seventeenth,
And sixteenth centuries BCE. Both bringing their own languages,
Religions, and haplogroups into territories previously dominated

By other civilizations. Both transforming the cultures they entered,
But also being transformed by those cultures. The {{p|Hyksos}} became
Partly {{p|Egyptianized}}. The {{p|Aryans}} became partly {{p|Indianized}}. Both
Inserted themselves into existing civilizational matrices and came

Out, on the other side, as something neither purely themselves nor
Purely the people they had joined.

                                           The {{p|Aryan Migration}}.

                                                                          Here
The Gaiad must be careful. The word "{{p|Aryan}}" has been abused in

Modern history—used by nineteenth and twentieth century ideologies
To construct racial hierarchies, to justify conquest and genocide,
To fabricate a pure-blood master-race that never existed. The
Gaiad rejects all of that. The "{{p|Aryan}}" people of the ancient world

Were not a "race"; they were a linguistic and cultural group—
Speakers of the {{p|Indo-Iranian}} branch of {{p|Indo-European}}, probably
Mixed in ancestry, probably a distinct elite lineage within a broader
Population, but not the "master race" of {{p|Nazi}} mythology. They were

A migrating population with a language and a religious tradition,
Nothing more supernatural than that. The word "{{p|Aryan}}" in its
Original context meant, simply, "noble" in the {{p|Indo-Iranian}}
Languages—a self-designation of the elite warrior class, not a

Racial category. The Gaiad uses the word in its original linguistic
And cultural sense, with no racial implications.

                                                        So: around
Fifteen-hundred BCE, give or take a century or two, a migration of
{{c|R1a}}-haplogroup {{p|Indo-Iranian}}-speaking peoples began moving

South from their homeland in the {{p|Central Asian}} steppes (the
{{p|Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex}}, or {{p|BMAC}}, was probably
A key staging ground). Some of them went southwest into what is
Now {{p|Iran}}, where they became the {{p|Iranian}} peoples—the {{p|Medes}},

The {{p|Persians}}, and the ancestors of the peoples who would produce
{{p|Zoroastrianism}}. Some of them went southeast, across the {{p|Hindu
Kush}} mountains and through the {{p|Khyber Pass}}, down into what is
Now {{p|Pakistan}} and northwestern {{p|India}}. This southeastern branch

Became the {{p|Indo-Aryan}} peoples of {{p|India}}. And they arrived in a
{{p|Punjab}} and upper {{p|Ganges}} region that was in the process of
Demographic transition—the {{p|Indus Valley}} civilization, the great
Pre-Aryan urban civilization of the subcontinent, was in the late

Stages of its collapse. The cities of {{p|Harappa}} and {{p|Mohenjo-daro}}
Had already been abandoned or were being abandoned. The population
Was dispersing eastward and southward. The {{p|Indus Valley}} was no
Longer the organized urban civilization it had been a thousand

Years earlier.

                    The {{p|Aryan}} arrival is not, in the Gaiad's
Reading, the cause of the {{p|Indus Valley}} collapse. The collapse
Was already underway before they arrived—probably driven by climate

Change (the gradual drying of the {{p|Sarasvati}} river system, the
Shifting of monsoons) and by internal political and economic
Pressures. The {{p|Aryans}} arrived into an ongoing collapse, not a
Stable civilization. They filled a partial vacuum. And—crucially—

The Gaiad reads the relationship between the incoming {{p|Aryans}}
And the pre-existing {{p|Dravidian}} and other non-{{p|Aryan}} populations
As substantially cooperative rather than violently adversarial.

This is an important departure from some earlier narratives. The

Older "{{p|Aryan invasion theory}}," developed in nineteenth-century
{{p|European}} scholarship, posited a violent conquest—chariot-driving
{{p|Aryan}} warriors destroying the peaceful {{p|Dravidian}} cities, imposing
Their language and religion by force, pushing the pre-existing

Populations south into the {{p|Deccan}} and {{p|Tamil}} regions. More recent
Scholarship has significantly revised this picture. The collapse
Of the {{p|Indus Valley}} cities preceded the {{p|Aryan}} arrival. The
Incoming {{p|Indo-Aryans}} were not a massive invasion force but a

Gradual trickle of migrating populations over several centuries.
Genetic studies show substantial admixture, not replacement—the
{{p|Indian}} population today is a blend of pre-{{p|Aryan}} ancestry (primarily
{{c|H}}, {{c|L}}, {{c|R2a}}, and other haplogroups associated with the {{p|Indus

Valley}} populations) and {{p|Aryan}} ancestry ({{c|R1a}}-M417, with its
Characteristic steppe-pastoralist signature). The mixing happened.
It was not an extermination. It was not even really a conquest in
The traditional military sense. It was a long, complex, mutually

Transformative encounter.

                                  And the cultural results of that
Encounter produced what became classical {{p|Hindu}} civilization. The
{{p|Vedic}} hymns—the {{p|Rig Veda}} in particular, composed between

Approximately fifteen-hundred and twelve-hundred BCE in the
{{p|Punjab}} and upper {{p|Ganges}} region—are the oldest surviving
Indo-European religious texts. They preserve the {{p|Aryan}} religious
Imagination in its most archaic form: sky-father {{c|Dyaus}}, storm-god

{{c|Indra}}, fire-god {{c|Agni}}, soma-intoxication ritual, horse sacrifice,
Elaborate priestly hymns addressed to an array of celestial and
Atmospheric deities. The {{p|Vedic}} religion is recognizably {{p|Indo-
European}}—the same pantheon structure as {{p|Greek}}, {{p|Roman}}, and

{{p|Germanic}} religion, just with different names for the gods. {{c|Dyaus}}
Is the same as {{c|Zeus}} and {{c|Jupiter}} and {{c|Tyr}} ({{p|German}})—all
Descended from Proto-Indo-European {{p|Dyeus Phter}} ("sky father").
{{c|Indra}} is cognate with the Greek {{c|Heracles}} in his storm-god

Aspects. The {{p|Vedas}} are {{p|Aryan}} religion in its pure form.

But even within the {{p|Rig Veda}}, the encounter with pre-{{p|Aryan}}
Populations is visible. The hymns mention "{{c|Dasyus}}" and "{{c|Dasas}}"—
The indigenous peoples the {{p|Aryans}} encountered—sometimes as

Enemies but often as subjects, servants, and fellow participants
In {{p|Indian}} society. Some {{p|Aryan}} deities show syncretic features
That suggest influence from pre-{{p|Aryan}} religious traditions. {{c|Rudra}}
—the wild and dangerous storm-and-mountain god who would eventually

Become {{c|Shiva}}—has features that don't quite fit the standard
{{p|Indo-European}} pantheon. {{c|Rudra}}/{{c|Shiva}} is probably, in part, a
Pre-{{p|Aryan}} deity absorbed into the {{p|Vedic}} pantheon. The
{{c|Shiva lingam}}—the iconic phallic emblem of {{c|Shiva}}-worship—may

Descend from pre-{{p|Aryan}} {{p|Indus Valley}} cultic objects. (A number of
Conical and cylindrical stone objects found at {{p|Mohenjo-daro}} and
Other {{p|Indus Valley}} sites have been interpreted as possible early
{{p|lingas}}, though the interpretation is not certain.) The worship

Of {{c|Shiva}} is, in one reading, a pre-{{p|Aryan}} {{p|Indus Valley}} tradition
That survived the civilizational collapse, was absorbed into the
Incoming {{p|Aryan}} religion as {{c|Rudra}}, and gradually grew in importance
Until, by the classical period, {{c|Shiva}} was one of the three great

Gods of {{p|Hinduism}} alongside {{c|Vishnu}} and {{c|Brahma}}. If so, then
{{p|Hindu}} civilization is not a pure {{p|Aryan}} religious civilization—
It is a synthesis, with {{p|Indus Valley}} elements preserved within
{{p|Aryan}} frameworks. The continuity is real. The civilization

Did not simply end and restart; it flowed through, absorbed, and
Continued to carry pre-existing religious content under new names
And in new frameworks.

                            And the caste system ({{p|varna}}) emerges
In this encounter. The four-{{p|varna}} scheme of the {{p|Rig Veda}}—

{{p|Brahmins}} (priests), {{p|Kshatriyas}} (warriors), {{p|Vaishyas}} (merchants
And farmers), and {{p|Shudras}} (servants)—is the {{p|Indo-European}}

{{p|Aryan}} subject population. The {{p|Shudras}} are originally the
Pre-{{p|Aryan}} populations, accepted into the social system at its
Lowest rank. Over subsequent centuries, this four-fold scheme
Would be elaborated into the thousands of sub-castes ({{p|jatis}})

That make up the classical {{p|Indian}} caste structure. The caste
System is, in its origins, the social codification of the {{p|Aryan}}-
Non-{{p|Aryan}} encounter—a rigid hierarchy that preserved the
Distinction between the incoming population and the pre-existing

One. The caste system's later history—its ossification, its
Religious legitimation through the doctrine of {{p|karma}} and
Rebirth, its role in keeping {{p|Indian}} civilization structurally
Stagnant for much of its later history—is covered in the "{{p|India

Stagnation}}" chapter much later. Here it is just noted as one of
The structural inheritances of the {{p|Aryan Migration}} period.

                                                                         And

{{p|Malayalam}}—represent the survival of the pre-{{p|Aryan}} linguistic

Substrate of {{p|Europe}}. The line between {{p|Aryan}} and {{p|Dravidian}}
India is roughly the boundary between the two great language
Families, though bilingualism, literary exchange, and cultural

Mixing have blurred it extensively over the centuries.

The {{p|Aryan Migration}} is not, in the Gaiad's reading, a catastrophe.

# Chapter 156: The New Kingdom

And the {{p|Hyksos}} were expelled.

Around fifteen-fifty BCE, {{c|Ahmose I}}, the {{p|Theban}} pharaoh of the
{{p|Eighteenth Dynasty}}, completed the campaign his elder brother
{{c|Kamose}} had begun. The {{p|Hyksos}} capital at {{p|Avaris}} was captured.
The {{p|Hyksos}} rulers and a portion of their population were expelled

Back into the {{p|Levant}}. Their fortress-city of {{p|Sharuhen}} in southern
{{p|Canaan}} was besieged for three years and finally taken. {{p|Egypt}} was
Reunified under native {{p|Egyptian}} rule. The {{p|New Kingdom}} had begun.

But the {{p|Egypt}} that emerged from the {{p|Hyksos}} expulsion was
Transformed. It had learned, during the century and a half of
{{p|Hyksos}} rule, the chariot, the composite bow, bronze-age military
Technology that the native {{p|Egyptian}} dynasties had not previously

Possessed. The humiliation of foreign rule had bred a determination
Never to let such a thing happen again. The {{p|New Kingdom}} {{p|Egypt}}
Was militarized, outward-looking, imperial. For the first time in
Its history, {{p|Egypt}} projected power far beyond its traditional

{{p|Nile Valley}} borders. {{p|Egyptian}} armies campaigned in {{p|Canaan}}, in
{{p|Syria}}, as far north as the {{p|Euphrates}} under {{c|Thutmose I}}. They
Campaigned south into {{p|Nubia}}, where they established direct
{{p|Egyptian}} administration over what had previously been nominally

Tributary regions. The {{p|Egyptian}} Empire—the first real {{p|Egyptian}}
Empire in the full imperial sense—was born.

The {{p|Eighteenth Dynasty}} was the great age of this expansion.
{{c|Ahmose I}}, {{c|Amenhotep I}}, {{c|Thutmose I}}, {{c|Thutmose II}}—then

{{c|Hatshepsut}}. One of the most remarkable figures in {{p|Egyptian}}
History. Daughter of {{c|Thutmose I}}, wife and half-sister of
{{c|Thutmose II}}, stepmother of the young {{c|Thutmose III}}. When
{{c|Thutmose II}} died, {{c|Hatshepsut}} initially served as regent for her

Young stepson. But within a few years, she took the title of
Pharaoh herself—a female pharaoh, ruling in her own right, in a
Culture that had almost no precedent for female kingship. She
Reigned for approximately twenty-two years. She was astonishingly

Successful. She prioritized trade over warfare—the famous expedition
To {{p|Punt}} (on the {{p|Red Sea}} coast), which brought back myrrh-trees,
Incense, exotic animals, and African wealth, was {{c|Hatshepsut}}'s
Crowning diplomatic achievement. She built one of the most beautiful

Mortuary temples in {{p|Egypt}}—{{p|Deir el-Bahari}}, on the western bank
Of the {{p|Nile}} opposite {{p|Thebes}}, a terraced colonnaded structure
Cut into the cliff-face. Her sculptures often depicted her with a
Ceremonial false beard (the standard iconography of pharaonic

Kingship), but her feminine features were also retained. She
Navigated the gender-contradictions of her role with a sophisticated
Adaptive strategy. And when she died, {{c|Thutmose III}}—now an adult—
Took the throne. Years later, {{c|Thutmose III}} or his successors

Attempted to erase {{c|Hatshepsut}}'s memory: her images were chiseled
Off public monuments, her cartouches defaced. This was not entirely
Successful. She was rediscovered by modern {{p|Egyptologists}}, and her
Full stature as one of the greatest pharaohs of the {{p|New Kingdom}}

Has been restored.

                         {{c|Thutmose III}} himself was the military
Genius of the dynasty. He conducted seventeen campaigns into the
{{p|Levant}} and {{p|Syria}}, consolidating {{p|Egyptian}} control over the entire

Region. His greatest victory, at {{p|Megiddo}} (the site whose name
Would become, in {{p|Greek}}, "{{p|Armageddon}}" in the {{p|Book of Revelation}}),
Defeated a coalition of {{p|Canaanite}} kings and established {{p|Egyptian}}
Supremacy over the {{p|Levant}} for the next century. {{c|Thutmose III}} is

Sometimes called "{{p|The Napoleon of Egypt}}" by modern historians—a
Tactically brilliant field commander who consolidated an empire.

But the {{p|New Kingdom}}'s expansion and militarization created a
Problem at home. The {{p|Amun}} priesthood—centered at {{p|Thebes}}, the

Dynastic capital—grew enormously wealthy on the spoils of empire.
Tribute from conquered territories poured into the temple of
{{c|Amun-Ra}} at {{p|Karnak}}. The {{p|Amun}} priesthood's power approached
Rivalry with the royal court. And it is in this context—the tension

Between the royal authority and the entrenched {{p|Amun}} priesthood—
That {{c|Akhenaten}} rose to the throne.

                                                  {{c|Akhenaten}}, born
{{c|Amenhotep IV}}, was the son of {{c|Amenhotep III}} and grandson of
{{c|Thutmose IV}}. He reigned from approximately thirteen-fifty-three

To thirteen-thirty-six BCE. And he attempted something that no
Previous pharaoh had attempted: to abolish the traditional {{p|Egyptian}}
Pantheon and replace it with the worship of a single god—the sun-
Disk, {{c|Aten}}—with himself as the sole intermediary between the

Divine and humanity. This is the famous "{{c|Aten}} heresy" or
"{{c|Amarna}} revolution"—sometimes celebrated in popular history as the
First monotheism. The Gaiad's reading is more skeptical. {{c|Akhenaten}}'s
Religious innovation was not quite monotheism in the {{c|Abrahamic}}

Sense. It retained cosmic elements of the traditional {{p|Egyptian}}
Religion. It was, primarily, a political move—an attempt to break
The {{p|Amun}} priesthood's power by redirecting religious authority
To a sun-disk that only the pharaoh had privileged access to.

{{c|Akhenaten}} built a new capital, {{p|Akhetaten}} (modern {{p|Amarna}}),
In middle {{p|Egypt}}. He moved the royal court there. He closed temples
Of {{c|Amun}} and other traditional gods. He persecuted priests of the
Old religion. And he composed, in his own hand, hymns to the

{{c|Aten}}—the {{p|Great Hymn to the Aten}}—that celebrate the sun as
Creator of all life. (Interestingly, the {{p|Great Hymn to the Aten}}
Shares striking similarities with {{p|Psalm 104}} of the {{p|Hebrew
Bible}}—a parallel that has led some scholars to argue that {{p|Hebrew}}

Monotheism was influenced by or descended from {{c|Akhenaten}}'s
Earlier experiment. The Gaiad takes a careful position on this:
The parallel is real, but the historical transmission is murky,
And the {{p|Hebrew}} tradition had its own independent development.)

And here the Gaiad makes a specific and possibly provocative claim.
{{c|Akhenaten}}, despite his reputation in modern pop-religion as a
"Proto-monotheist hero," was—in the Gaiad's reading—the pharaoh
Who persecuted the {{p|Israelites}}. The "{{p|new king who knew not

Joseph}}" of the {{p|Exodus}} narrative may be, in the Gaiad's reading,
{{c|Akhenaten}} himself. Why? Because {{c|Akhenaten}}'s religious revolution
Was anti-foreign-cult. In closing down the temples of the traditional
{{p|Egyptian}} gods, he also closed down the temples of the {{p|Semitic}}

Gods who had been worshipped in {{p|Egypt}} by the remaining {{p|Canaanite}}
Population. The {{p|Israelites}} in {{p|Goshen}}—descendants of {{c|Jacob}}'s
Family, still preserving their own {{p|Semitic}} religious identity—
Found themselves religiously suppressed under {{c|Akhenaten}}'s regime.

The irony is striking: {{c|Akhenaten}}, celebrated by modern writers
As the first monotheist, was in the Gaiad's reading the active
Persecutor of the {{p|Israelites}}, whose descendants would develop
The most important monotheism in subsequent world history. The

Timing also roughly works: {{c|Akhenaten}}'s reign (thirteen-fifty-three
To thirteen-thirty-six BCE) is approximately the right period for
The biblical {{p|Exodus}} tradition to have its historical kernel,
Though the biblical narrative compresses and distorts the timeline.

After {{c|Akhenaten}}'s death, the {{p|Amarna}} revolution collapsed quickly.
His son-in-law (or possibly son) {{c|Tutankhamun}} (originally
{{c|Tutankhaten}}) changed his name to honor {{c|Amun}}, moved the court
Back to {{p|Thebes}}, and restored the traditional cults. {{c|Tutankhamun}}

Is famous in modern times almost exclusively because of the
Discovery of his intact tomb by {{c|Howard Carter}} in nineteen
Twenty-two—the unparalleled treasure-hoard that survived because
The tomb had been lost under the debris of later construction.

In his own time, {{c|Tutankhamun}} was a minor pharaoh who reigned
Briefly and died young (probably from a combination of genetic
Disorders resulting from the royal family's extreme inbreeding,
Plus an accidental injury). The fame is modern. The reign was

Not particularly consequential. After {{c|Tutankhamun}}, the {{p|Eighteenth
Dynasty}} ended in administrative confusion and was succeeded by
The {{p|Nineteenth Dynasty}}, founded by the general {{c|Ramesses I}} and
Including the great {{c|Seti I}} and the even greater {{c|Ramesses II}}.

{{c|Ramesses II}}—"{{c|Ramesses the Great}}"—reigned for sixty-six years
(twelve-seventy-nine to twelve-thirteen BCE), fought the {{p|Battle
Of Kadesh}} against the {{p|Hittites}} (the largest chariot battle in
Human history up to that point, with about five thousand chariots

Engaged), and eventually signed the first known international
Peace treaty in human history with the {{p|Hittite}} king {{c|Hattusili III}}.
The {{p|Egyptian-Hittite Peace Treaty}} (c. 1259 BCE) was so important
That copies were kept in both capitals, and a version engraved in

{{p|Hittite}} on silver tablets was carried to {{p|Thebes}} and deposited
In the temple of {{c|Amun}}. A copy is now in the {{p|United Nations}}
Building in {{p|New York}}—the world's first peace treaty, held up as
The precedent for the modern international order.

{{c|Ramesses II}} built on a scale that rivaled {{c|Khufu}} of the {{p|Old
Kingdom}}: the great temples at {{p|Abu Simbel}}, carved into a sandstone
Cliff in {{p|Nubia}}, with four massive seated statues of the pharaoh
Sixty-five feet high guarding the entrance. The {{p|Ramesseum}}—his

Mortuary temple on the west bank at {{p|Thebes}}. The additions to
{{p|Karnak}} and {{p|Luxor}}. {{c|Ramesses II}} was the last great builder-pharaoh
Of the pre-{{p|Bronze Age Collapse}} era. And then, toward the end of
His long reign, things began to destabilize. Sea peoples appeared

On the horizon. {{p|Mediterranean}} maritime raiders began harassing
The {{p|Egyptian}} coast. The climate was shifting again. And after
{{c|Ramesses II}}'s death, {{p|Egypt}} would face, during the reign of his
Son {{c|Merneptah}} and grandsons, the first major waves of the {{p|Sea

Peoples}}—the great migrations that would contribute to the
{{p|Bronze Age Collapse}} of around twelve-hundred BCE. The {{p|New
Kingdom}}'s long expansion was ending. The system that had made
{{c|Ramesses}}'s grandeur possible was straining toward its final

Failure. And the {{p|Exodus}} narrative—which the Gaiad reads as
The {{p|Israelite}} departure from {{p|Egypt}} in the late {{p|Nineteenth
Dynasty}} or early {{p|Twentieth Dynasty}}—fits into this period of
Growing instability.

# Chapter 157: The Hittites

In the north, beyond the {{p|Sinai}}, beyond {{p|Canaan}}, beyond {{p|Syria}},
In the high plateau country of what is now central {{p|Turkey}},
A kingdom was emerging that would become {{p|Egypt}}'s great rival.

The {{p|Hittites}}.

                       An {{p|Indo-European}} people whose {{c|R1b}} haplogroup
Ancestors had arrived in {{p|Anatolia}} quite early in the {{p|Yamnaya}}
Expansion—probably in the third millennium BCE, through the

{{p|Caucasus}} or across the {{p|Aegean}}—and who had established themselves
As the dominant power in the central {{p|Anatolian}} plateau. Their
Language, {{p|Hittite}}, is the earliest attested {{p|Indo-European}}
Language in the historical record. It was first written in the

Adapted {{p|Akkadian}} cuneiform system, which the {{p|Hittites}} had
Borrowed from the {{p|Mesopotamian}} scribal tradition. The texts they
Left behind—some thirty thousand clay tablets recovered from their
Capital at {{p|Hattusa}}—are the oldest {{p|Indo-European}} written

Documents. They preserve {{p|Hittite}} royal correspondence, legal
Codes, religious texts, diplomatic treaties, historical annals,
Ritual handbooks, and mythological narratives. The {{p|Hittites}} were
A literate, bureaucratic, well-documented civilization. And when

The archives at {{p|Hattusa}} were rediscovered in the late nineteenth
And early twentieth centuries, they revolutionized our understanding
Of the Bronze Age.

                           The {{p|Hittite}} kingdom—or {{p|Hatti}}, as it
Called itself—was founded around seventeen-fifty BCE by {{c|Anitta}},
A king who had conquered the city of {{p|Hattusa}} and several other

{{p|Anatolian}} centers. The {{p|Old Hittite Kingdom}} was consolidated
Under {{c|Hattusili I}} around sixteen-fifty BCE, with {{p|Hattusa}} as
Its capital. The kingdom expanded rapidly. Under {{c|Mursili I}}, the
{{p|Hittites}} conducted a remarkable military expedition: they marched

South across the {{p|Taurus Mountains}}, through {{p|Syria}}, and all the
Way down to {{p|Babylon}}. In fifteen-ninety-five BCE, {{c|Mursili I}}
Sacked {{p|Babylon}}—this was the raid that ended the {{p|Old Babylonian}}
Empire of {{c|Hammurabi}}'s dynasty. The {{p|Hittites}} did not stay to

Occupy {{p|Babylon}}; they withdrew back to {{p|Anatolia}} with their
Plunder. But the sack was devastating, and it marks the end of
The {{p|Old Babylonian}} period and the transition to the {{p|Kassite}}
Dynasty in {{p|Mesopotamia}}. The {{p|Hittites}}, having projected power

Across a thousand miles, retreated to their highland capital and
Consolidated.

                   The {{p|Hittite}} empire reached its peak in the
Fourteenth and thirteenth centuries BCE under a series of capable
Rulers: {{c|Suppiluliuma I}}, {{c|Mursili II}}, {{c|Muwatalli II}}, {{c|Hattusili III}},

{{c|Tudhaliya IV}}. The empire controlled central and eastern {{p|Anatolia}},
Northern {{p|Syria}}, and much of the {{p|Levant}} (in competition and
Occasional cooperation with {{p|Egypt}}). The {{p|Hittites}} were the
Northern pole of a bipolar Bronze Age political system in which

{{p|Egypt}} was the southern pole. The two empires met, diplomatically
Or militarily, at their mutual border zone in {{p|Syria}} and the
Upper {{p|Levant}}. And the great flashpoint was the {{p|Battle of Kadesh}}
In twelve-seventy-four BCE.

                                  {{p|Kadesh}}. A small city on the
{{p|Orontes River}} in central {{p|Syria}}. Strategically located on the
Border zone between {{p|Hittite}} and {{p|Egyptian}} spheres of influence.
And in twelve-seventy-four BCE, {{c|Ramesses II}} of {{p|Egypt}} and

{{c|Muwatalli II}} of {{p|Hatti}} confronted each other here with the
Largest chariot forces ever fielded in human military history up
To that point. The {{p|Egyptian}} army consisted of perhaps twenty
Thousand infantry and two thousand chariots. The {{p|Hittite}} army

Fielded a similar infantry strength plus three thousand chariots.
The battle was massive, confused, and fought partly around a
{{p|Hittite}} ambush that caught {{c|Ramesses}} unprepared—the {{p|Egyptian}}
Pharaoh had advanced too far ahead of his main force, and his

Camp was overrun by {{p|Hittite}} chariotry. The battle was recovered—
{{c|Ramesses}} rallied his remaining forces and held the field by
Nightfall—but the outcome was indecisive. Both sides claimed
Victory. Both accounts are partially preserved: {{c|Ramesses}}'s in

{{p|Egyptian}} inscriptions at {{p|Karnak}} and the {{p|Ramesseum}}, {{c|Muwatalli}}'s
(Indirectly, through later {{p|Hittite}} documents) in the archives
At {{p|Hattusa}}. The historical consensus is that the battle was
Effectively a draw. Neither side had the ability to dislodge the

Other. And after years of continued low-level conflict, the two
Powers eventually negotiated a peace.

                                              The {{p|Egyptian-Hittite}}
Peace Treaty was signed around twelve-fifty-nine BCE, between
{{c|Ramesses II}} and {{c|Hattusili III}} (who had succeeded {{c|Muwatalli II}}

After a brief civil war). The treaty established a permanent peace
Between the two empires, a mutual defense pact against external
Aggression, and provisions for the extradition of political
Refugees. It was sealed with a dynastic marriage: {{c|Hattusili III}}'s

Daughter married {{c|Ramesses II}} and became one of his principal
Queens. The treaty was written in cuneiform on silver tablets, with
Copies kept in both capitals. It is the oldest known international
Peace treaty in human history. A copy of the {{p|Hittite}} version on

Silver is displayed at the {{p|United Nations}} building in {{p|New York}}
As a symbolic precedent for modern international diplomacy. The
Treaty held for the rest of both empires' existences—the {{p|Hittite}}
Empire would collapse in the Bronze Age Collapse around twelve

Hundred BCE, and {{p|Egypt}} would never again face a northern rival
On the scale of {{p|Hatti}} until the rise of {{p|Assyria}} centuries
Later. But for the duration of both empires, the treaty held. The
First international peace treaty was also the first long-lasting

One.

     The {{p|Hittites}} were culturally distinctive. Their religion was
{{p|Indo-European}} in its deep structure (sky-father, storm-god, the
Usual pantheon) but heavily syncretized with {{p|Anatolian}} and

{{p|Hurrian}} elements absorbed from their neighbors and subject
Populations. The {{p|Hittites}} famously called their kingdom the
"Land of the Thousand Gods"—they incorporated every deity of every
Conquered territory into their expanding pantheon, rather than

Suppressing local cults. This made {{p|Hittite}} religion tolerant and
Syncretic, willing to add rather than replace. The storm-god
{{c|Teshub}} (borrowed from the {{p|Hurrians}}) was their chief deity. The
Sun-goddess of {{p|Arinna}} was his consort. Mountain-gods, river-gods,

Ancestral kings elevated to divine status, agricultural deities—all
Were honored. This religious inclusivism was a political strategy
As well: conquered populations were more likely to accept {{p|Hittite}}
Rule if their local gods were incorporated rather than abolished.

And the {{p|Hittites}} had a distinctive law code. It was generally
Less brutal than {{c|Hammurabi}}'s code. Capital punishment was rare.
Most offenses carried restitution-based penalties—payment to the
Victim or the victim's family, rather than physical mutilation or

Execution. The {{p|Hittite}} law code is one of the less vengeful
Ancient {{p|Near Eastern}} codes. Scholars have speculated that this
Reflected {{p|Indo-European}} cultural differences from the {{p|Semitic}}
World. Whether that is true or whether it reflected specific

{{p|Anatolian}} circumstances is debated. But the {{p|Hittite}} legal
Tradition was noticeably less retributive than its southern
Mesopotamian counterpart.

And the {{p|Hittites}} in the biblical record—this is interesting. The
{{p|Hebrew Bible}} mentions "{{c|Hittites}}" repeatedly as one of the
Peoples of {{p|Canaan}} encountered by {{c|Abraham}} and later {{p|Israelite}}
Generations. {{c|Uriah the Hittite}} is a {{p|Hittite}} character in the

Court of {{c|King David}}. The biblical {{c|Hittites}} appear to be a
Subset of the actual {{p|Hittite}} population—specifically, {{p|Neo-Hittite}}
Remnant populations in northern {{p|Syria}} and southern {{p|Turkey}} who
Survived the fall of the {{p|Hittite}} Empire and continued to maintain

A distinct {{p|Hittite}} cultural identity into the first millennium BCE.
These {{p|Neo-Hittite}} city-states—{{p|Carchemish}}, {{p|Aleppo}}, {{p|Hamath}},
{{p|Sam'al}}—are where the biblical {{c|Hittites}} come from. The great
{{p|Hittite}} Empire was already gone by the time the biblical narrative

Takes shape, but its cultural and ethnic remnants persisted for
Several more centuries.

                              And the fall of the {{p|Hittite}} Empire
Is, the Gaiad has already indicated, part of the Bronze Age Collapse.
Around twelve-hundred BCE, the {{p|Hittite}} capital {{p|Hattusa}} was

Sacked and burned. The {{p|Hittite}} central administration collapsed.
The royal archives were hurriedly hidden in temples, which is why
They survived. The population scattered. The specific cause is
Debated—probably a combination of the {{p|Sea Peoples}} raids, internal

Rebellions (possibly by the {{p|Kaska}} tribes of the {{p|Black Sea}} coast),
Climate-induced famine, and the general systemic failure of the
Bronze Age international system. Whatever the cause, the {{p|Hittite}}
Empire ended suddenly and catastrophically, more so than any other

Major Bronze Age civilization. {{p|Egypt}} survived, diminished. {{p|Assyria}}
Survived, briefly interrupted. {{p|Babylon}} survived, under {{p|Kassite}}
Rule. But the {{p|Hittites}} as an imperial civilization were gone
Within a generation. Their capital was abandoned and forgotten.

Their language was lost. By the first century BCE, the word
"{{p|Hittite}}" had become, in the biblical and later traditions, a
Vague name for unnamed eastern peoples. The actual {{p|Hittite}} Empire
Was unknown to historians until {{c|Hugo Winckler}} and {{c|Theodor Makridi}}

Began excavating {{p|Hattusa}} in nineteen-oh-six and discovered the
Lost archive. Three thousand years of historical amnesia, ended by
A German-Turkish archaeological team. The rediscovery of the
{{p|Hittites}} is one of the great stories of twentieth-century archaeology.

And now we know them. We can read their tablets. We can name their
Kings. We can trace their wars and treaties. We can read the terms
Of the {{p|Kadesh}} peace treaty in the original {{p|Hittite}} cuneiform.
The empire that disappeared so completely that even the {{p|Bible}} lost

Its proper memory has been, in the last century, substantially
Reconstructed.

                  {{p|Hittites}}. The first great {{p|Indo-European}} empire.
The sack of {{p|Babylon}}. The rivalry with {{p|Egypt}}. The {{p|Kadesh}} peace
Treaty. The "Land of the Thousand Gods." The law code less brutal

Than {{c|Hammurabi}}'s. The capital at {{p|Hattusa}} and its archive.
The collapse in the Bronze Age Collapse. The three thousand years
Of forgetting. The twentieth-century rediscovery. The reconstruction
Of a lost civilization from its hidden tablets.

{{p|Hittites}}. {{p|Hattusa}}. {{p|Kadesh}}. The {{c|R1b}}-haplogroup empire of
{{p|Anatolia}}. The northern pole of Bronze Age political order. The
Forgotten and rediscovered empire. Stand.


# Chapter 158: The Mycenaeans

And in the west, on the southern tip of the {{p|Greek}} mainland and
Across the {{p|Aegean}} islands, a third great Bronze Age civilization
Was reaching its peak. {{p|Mycenaean Greece}}.

                                                   Named for {{p|Mycenae}},
The citadel on a hill in the {{p|Argolid}} whose massive "{{p|Cyclopean}}"
Walls of unmortared limestone blocks, some weighing over twenty tons,
Dominated the landscape. {{p|Mycenae}} was the political and cultural

Center of the Bronze Age {{p|Greek}} world. Other major centers were
At {{p|Tiryns}}, {{p|Pylos}}, {{p|Athens}} (already a settled site, though
Not yet the city it would become), {{p|Thebes}}, {{p|Orchomenos}}, and
Several others. The {{p|Mycenaean}} civilization emerged around sixteen

Hundred BCE—roughly contemporary with the {{p|New Kingdom}} beginning
In {{p|Egypt}} and the {{p|Hittite}} kingdom consolidating in {{p|Anatolia}}—
And flourished until the Bronze Age Collapse around twelve hundred BCE.

The {{p|Mycenaeans}} were {{c|R1b}}-haplogroup {{p|Indo-European}}-speakers—
Descendants of the {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion that had reached {{p|Greece}} in
The early {{p|Bronze Age}}. Their language was an archaic form of {{p|Greek}},
Written in a syllabic script called {{p|Linear B}}—adapted from the

{{p|Minoan}} {{p|Linear A}} script that preceded it. Unlike {{p|Linear A}},
{{p|Linear B}} has been successfully deciphered. In nineteen-fifty-two,
{{c|Michael Ventris}}, a young {{p|English}} architect with a passion for
Ancient scripts, demonstrated that {{p|Linear B}} was used to write

Early {{p|Greek}}. The achievement is one of the most celebrated in
The history of decipherment. It opened up an entire Bronze Age
Civilization to modern understanding. We can read the {{p|Linear B}}
Tablets recovered from {{p|Pylos}}, {{p|Knossos}}, {{p|Mycenae}}, and other

{{p|Mycenaean}} sites, and they reveal a palace-centered economic and
Administrative system managing vast agricultural, craft, and
Commercial operations. {{p|Linear B}} tablets are administrative
Records—inventories of livestock, lists of workers, tax collections,

Distributions of rations, inventories of military equipment. They
Are not literature. But they provide a detailed picture of the
Bureaucratic machinery of a {{p|Mycenaean}} palace, managing the economic
Life of its region down to the number of bronze arrowheads in the

Armory and the ration of wheat allocated to each category of worker.

And above the bureaucratic record, the {{p|Mycenaean}} world was also
A warrior society. The {{p|Mycenaean}} elite warriors—depicted in
Frescoes, buried with bronze swords and boar's-tusk helmets and

Gold-masked faces—were an Indo-European warrior aristocracy of the
Type that the {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion had produced across the
Indo-European world. The shaft-graves at {{p|Mycenae}}, excavated by
{{c|Heinrich Schliemann}} in eighteen-seventy-six, contained gold funerary

Masks (including the famous "{{p|Mask of Agamemnon}}," though the
Mask actually predates any possible historical {{c|Agamemnon}} by
Several centuries), bronze weapons, elaborate jewelry, and other
Signs of an extraordinarily wealthy warrior elite. The {{p|Mycenaeans}}

Were, in their cultural self-conception, the heroes who would later
Be memorialized in {{c|Homer}}'s poetry. They valued combat, loot,
Honor, and the gift-exchange diplomacy of aristocratic warrior
Bands. They were the original {{p|Greek}} aristocracy whose memory

Fueled the later {{p|Iron Age}} reconstruction of the "heroic age."

And their relationship with the {{p|Minoan}} civilization is complex.
The {{p|Mycenaeans}} had originally been the junior partners of the
{{p|Minoans}}—culturally influenced by the island civilization, less

Wealthy, less sophisticated. But around fourteen-fifty BCE, after
The {{p|Thera}} eruption had weakened the {{p|Minoan}} civilization, the
{{p|Mycenaeans}} invaded {{p|Crete}}, occupied the major palace centers,
And took over the {{p|Minoan}} administrative and cultural system. The

{{p|Linear B}} tablets found at {{p|Knossos}} date to this {{p|Mycenaean}}
Occupation period. The {{p|Mycenaeans}} adopted {{p|Minoan}} artistic
Styles, religious iconography, and palace architecture—but adapted
Them to their own {{p|Indo-European}} warrior ethos. {{p|Mycenaean}} frescoes,

Unlike the peaceful nature scenes of {{p|Minoan}} art, often depict
Warriors, hunts, and battle scenes. The serenity of {{p|Minoan}}
Civilization was replaced by the martial vigor of the {{p|Mycenaean}}
Overlay. But many {{p|Minoan}} elements survived: goddess-worship, the

Labyrinthine palace-plan, the bull-symbolism. The {{p|Mycenaean}}
Civilization was a hybrid of {{p|Indo-European}} warrior aristocracy
And {{p|Minoan}} palace sophistication. The combination was potent.

And it was wealthy. {{p|Mycenaean}} trade networks extended across the
{{p|Eastern Mediterranean}}. {{p|Mycenaean}} pottery has been found in {{p|Egypt}},
The {{p|Levant}}, {{p|Anatolia}}, {{p|Cyprus}}, and as far west as {{p|Sardinia}} and
{{p|Sicily}}. The {{p|Mycenaeans}} imported copper from {{p|Cyprus}}, tin from

Distant sources (possibly {{p|Cornwall}} or {{p|Afghanistan}}), gold from
{{p|Egypt}}, ivory from {{p|Syria}}, cedar from {{p|Lebanon}}. They exported
Olive oil, wine, textiles, and their distinctive pottery. The
{{p|Aegean}} was a trading lake in which {{p|Mycenaean}} ships sailed in

Every direction, moving goods that the palaces redistributed and
Taxed. The palatial economy was in the middle of the trade network,
And the wealth that flowed through it made {{p|Mycenae}} and its
Sister-cities genuinely rich by Bronze Age standards.

And one episode from this period—probably around the end of the
Thirteenth century BCE, shortly before the Bronze Age Collapse—
Would become the greatest memorial narrative in {{p|Western}} literature.
The {{p|Trojan War}}.

                         The historicity of the {{p|Trojan War}} is
Debated but, the Gaiad accepts, broadly real. There was a city
At {{p|Troy}} (the site called {{p|Hisarlik}} in northwestern {{p|Turkey}}),
And it was destroyed by violence, and the destruction can be dated

To roughly the right period. The site shows evidence of siege and
Burning in its {{p|Troy VIIa}} level, which is usually identified
With the {{p|Homeric}} {{p|Troy}}. Whether the war was fought by a single
Coalition of {{p|Mycenaean}} kings led by {{c|Agamemnon}}, whether the

Participants had the names {{c|Homer}} would give them six centuries
Later, whether {{c|Achilles}} and {{c|Hector}} and {{c|Odysseus}} and {{c|Helen}} and
{{c|Paris}} were historical individuals—these are uncertain. But a war
Between {{p|Mycenaean}} {{p|Greeks}} and the kingdom of {{p|Wilusa}} ({{p|Troy}}'s

{{p|Hittite}} name, which is cognate with "{{p|Ilios}}," {{p|Homer}}'s term) is
Attested in {{p|Hittite}} diplomatic correspondence. There was a real
Conflict over this real city between real {{p|Mycenaean}} forces and
Real {{p|Anatolian}} defenders. {{c|Homer}}'s later epic dressed the event

In the heroic costume of the oral tradition, compressing and
Elaborating and fictionalizing, but the historical kernel was there.

                                                                            And
The {{p|Mycenaean}} civilization is, the Gaiad argues, the {{p|Greek}}
Contribution to the {{p|Bronze Age Collapse}} chapter. Within a

Generation or two after the {{p|Trojan War}}, the {{p|Mycenaean}} palaces
Themselves were destroyed. {{p|Mycenae}} was burned. {{p|Tiryns}} was
Sacked. {{p|Pylos}} was burned (which is why its {{p|Linear B}} tablets
Survive—they were baked hard by the fire instead of being allowed

To dissolve in the normal way). The entire palatial civilization
Collapsed in a generation around twelve-hundred BCE. The cause was
The general Bronze Age Collapse—{{p|Sea Peoples}} raids, possibly
Internal rebellions by the servile population, climate pressures,

And the systemic failure of the Bronze Age international trade
Network. The {{p|Mycenaean}} palaces went down in a coordinated wave
Of destructions that archaeologists can date, across many sites,
To the same twenty-year window. The civilization did not recover.

After the collapse, {{p|Greece}} entered a "{{p|Dark Age}}" that lasted
Approximately three centuries. {{p|Linear B}} writing was forgotten.
The palace economies vanished. Population collapsed. Monumental
Architecture ceased. Trade networks shrank to local exchange.

The {{p|Greeks}} of the {{p|Dark Age}} lived in small villages, farmed at
Subsistence levels, and preserved the memory of the {{p|Mycenaean}}
Heroic age only in oral tradition. When writing finally returned—
Via the {{p|Phoenician}} alphabet, around the eighth century BCE—the

{{p|Greeks}} were a completely different civilization from what the
{{p|Mycenaeans}} had been. They had forgotten {{p|Linear B}} entirely. They
Had forgotten the palace economies. They had forgotten, in most
Cases, the specific historical geography of their Bronze Age

Predecessors. What they remembered was the heroic poetry—the songs
About {{c|Achilles}} and {{c|Odysseus}} and {{c|Agamemnon}} that had been
Transmitted orally through three or four centuries of illiterate
{{p|Dark Age}}. When {{c|Homer}} (whoever he was, whenever exactly he

Lived—probably the eighth century BCE, probably a single poet but
Possibly a tradition) composed the {{p|Iliad}} and the {{p|Odyssey}}, he
Was working from this oral tradition. The poems preserve fragments
Of {{p|Mycenaean}} material culture (the boar's-tusk helmet, the

Catalog of ships, certain personal names, certain geographical
References) that must have been transmitted from the Bronze Age
Without being archaeologically rediscovered until the nineteenth

# Chapter 159: The Ramayana

And now the great swerve of the Gaiad. The moment where the
Narrative crosses all the rational chronologies and follows the
Thread of mythic resonance instead. The {{p|Ramayana}}.

                                                   Consider what
The {{p|Ramayana}} tells us. {{c|Rama}}, prince of {{p|Ayodhya}} in northern
{{p|India}}, exiled from his kingdom by the machinations of his
Stepmother, wanders in the forest with his wife {{c|Sita}} and his

Brother {{c|Lakshmana}}. {{c|Sita}} is abducted by {{c|Ravana}}, the ten-headed
Demon-king of {{p|Lanka}}. {{c|Rama}} assembles an army—an army of monkeys,
Led by the monkey-god {{c|Hanuman}}—and marches south. They build a
Bridge across the ocean. They invade {{p|Lanka}}. They defeat {{c|Ravana}}.

They recover {{c|Sita}}. They return to {{p|Ayodhya}} and {{c|Rama}} is restored
To his throne. The epic is one of the foundational {{p|Sanskrit}}
Texts, composed probably between the seventh and fourth centuries
BCE, though its oral roots go much deeper—perhaps to the Vedic

Period, perhaps to the Aryan migration itself. And the text is
Usually read as purely {{p|Indian}}: a story of northern {{p|India}}
Conquering southern {{p|India}}, or of {{p|Indo-Aryan}} peoples subduing
{{p|Dravidian}} peoples, or of cosmic dharma triumphing over demonic

Adharma. All of these readings are valid. But the Gaiad reads the
{{p|Ramayana}} differently. The Gaiad reads the {{p|Ramayana}} as evidence
Of something extraordinary: that the {{p|Indo-European}} diaspora,
Which by the second millennium BCE had spread from {{p|Ireland}} to

{{p|India}}, retained a shared narrative imagination. That the story
Of {{c|Rama}} and {{c|Sita}} and the recovery of a stolen queen from a
Fortified city across the sea—this story is not only {{p|Indian}}.
It is also, in structural terms, the story of the {{p|Trojan War}}.

Consider the parallels. A virtuous prince's wife is abducted.
The prince raises an army of allies. The army crosses the sea
To a fortified island or peninsula where the abductor rules.
The siege lasts years. Eventually the fortress falls, the wife

Is recovered, the villain is slain. In the Greek case: {{c|Menelaus}},
{{c|Helen}}, {{c|Paris}}, {{p|Troy}}, the {{p|Achaean}} coalition. In the Indian case:
{{c|Rama}}, {{c|Sita}}, {{c|Ravana}}, {{p|Lanka}}, the monkey army. The parallels are
Not superficial. They are structural. They extend to specific

Motifs: the test of the bow (only the true husband can string it),
The divine intervention (gods meddling in mortal wars), the
Question of the wife's chastity after her return (was she violated
By the abductor?). {{c|Sita}}'s trial by fire and {{c|Helen}}'s ambiguous

Reception in {{p|Sparta}}—these are not random coincidences. These are
Two reflexes of a single {{p|Proto-Indo-European}} narrative, preserved
At the extreme western and eastern ends of the diaspora, recognized
For what they are only when scholars began comparing myths across

Traditions in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries CE. And the
Gaiad goes further. The Gaiad proposes that {{c|Rama}} is not only
A structural parallel of the {{p|Trojan War}}. {{c|Rama}}'s expedition
Also resonates with {{p|Egyptian}} and {{p|Ethiopian}} narratives of

Righteous kingship. {{c|Rama}}'s exile and return mirrors {{c|Osiris}}'s
Death and resurrection. {{c|Rama}}'s southern campaign mirrors the
{{p|Egyptian}} {{c|Kebra Nagast}} traditions of kingship moving southward
Into {{p|Ethiopia}}. {{c|Rama}}'s blue skin—the iconic depiction of {{c|Rama}}

As dark-blue or dark-skinned—links him visually to both the
{{p|African}} and the {{p|Tamil}} traditions. In some esoteric readings,
{{c|Rama}} visits {{p|Ethiopia}} on his travels. In some esoteric readings,
{{c|Rama}} and {{c|Ravana}} are two aspects of a single cosmic principle.

In some esoteric readings, the {{p|Ramayana}} is not a story about
{{p|India}} at all but about the whole human civilization of the
Bronze Age, refracted through the {{p|Indian}} tradition into a local
And specific form. The Gaiad entertains these readings. The Gaiad

Entertains them not as claims of literal historical fact but as
Recognitions of deep mythic unity. The {{p|Ramayana}} is the {{p|Indian}}
Reflex of a narrative complex that humanity as a whole was
Generating in the late Bronze Age—a complex that also produced

The {{p|Iliad}}, the {{p|Egyptian}} stories of the war against {{p|Hyksos}}
And {{p|Sea Peoples}}, the {{p|Hebrew}} narrative of {{c|Moses}} leading his
People out of bondage, the {{p|Hittite}} accounts of the battle of
{{p|Kadesh}}. Different cultures. Different protagonists. Different

Specific contents. But a common structural feature: the
Confrontation with a powerful adversary, the long siege or long
Journey, the eventual restoration of righteous order. This is
The shared grammar of Bronze Age civilizational narrative.

                                                           And
{{c|Rama}}'s dharma—the concept of righteous conduct, duty, the
Alignment of the individual with cosmic order—this dharma is
Not only the central ethical principle of {{p|Hindu}} civilization.

It is also a specific {{p|Indian}} articulation of a concept that
Appears in cognate forms across {{p|Indo-European}} traditions.
The {{p|Latin}} "religio" (binding oneself to the sacred). The
{{p|Persian}} "asha" ({{p|Zoroastrian}} cosmic truth). The {{p|Greek}} "themis"

(Divinely sanctioned moral order). The {{p|Norse}} "örlög" (fate,
Cosmic law). These are not the same concepts. But they are
Cognate concepts. They trace back to a {{p|Proto-Indo-European}}
Conception of cosmic order that each successor civilization

Elaborated in its own way. {{c|Rama}} is the {{p|Indian}} embodiment of
Dharma—the perfect king, the perfect husband, the perfect warrior,
Whose every action aligns with cosmic order. And as such, {{c|Rama}}
Is also, structurally, the {{p|Indian}} cognate of {{c|Osiris}}, of

{{c|Yudhishthira}}, of {{c|Arthur}}, of every righteous sovereign whose
Task is to restore the cosmos when it has been disturbed.

Slow down and narrate {{c|Rama}}'s story proper. {{c|Rama}} is born to
{{c|King Dasharatha}} of {{p|Ayodhya}}, one of the great cities of ancient

Northern {{p|India}}, traditionally located in what is now {{p|Uttar Pradesh}}.
{{c|Dasharatha}} has four sons by three wives: {{c|Rama}}, {{c|Bharata}},
{{c|Lakshmana}}, {{c|Shatrughna}}. {{c|Rama}} is the eldest and the rightful heir.
As a young prince, {{c|Rama}} is trained in warfare and statecraft.

He travels with the sage {{c|Vishwamitra}} to protect sacrifices from
Demons. He visits the kingdom of {{p|Mithila}} and there he meets
{{c|Sita}}, the daughter of {{c|King Janaka}}. {{c|Sita}} is not born of ordinary
Means; she was found in a furrow during a ritual ploughing, a

Daughter of the earth itself. {{c|King Janaka}} has declared that whoever
Can string the bow of {{c|Shiva}} may marry {{c|Sita}}. {{c|Rama}} strings the bow.
In fact, he breaks it. He and {{c|Sita}} are married. They return to
{{p|Ayodhya}} together. {{c|Dasharatha}} prepares to abdicate in favor of

{{c|Rama}}, but {{c|Dasharatha}}'s second queen {{c|Kaikeyi}}, manipulated by her
Servant {{c|Manthara}}, invokes two old boons from {{c|Dasharatha}}: that
Her son {{c|Bharata}} be crowned instead, and that {{c|Rama}} be exiled to
The forest for fourteen years. {{c|Dasharatha}} is honor-bound to grant

And carries her to {{p|Lanka}}. The demon-king {{c|Ravana}} rules from

Heads, twenty arms, enormous magical power, and a fortified island

Capital protected by demon armies. {{c|Rama}} and {{c|Lakshmana}} search for
{{c|Sita}} desperately. They meet {{c|Hanuman}}, the monkey-general, who
Becomes {{c|Rama}}'s most devoted ally. {{c|Hanuman}} is himself an avatar of

Lieutenant. They march to the southern coast of {{p|India}}. They
Build a stone bridge across the sea to {{p|Lanka}}—the bridge of
{{p|Ram Setu}} or {{p|Adam's Bridge}}, still visible today as a chain of

Shoals in the {{p|Palk Strait}} between {{p|India}} and {{p|Sri Lanka}}. Over
This bridge the army crosses. The siege of {{p|Lanka}} begins. Great
Battles are fought. {{c|Ravana}}'s demon generals fall one by one.
{{c|Lakshmana}} kills {{c|Indrajit}}, {{c|Ravana}}'s warrior son. Finally {{c|Rama}}

Confronts {{c|Ravana}} in single combat. {{c|Ravana}} is terrible; his ten
Heads regenerate; his twenty arms wield divine weapons. But {{c|Rama}}
Uses the divine arrow given to him by the sage {{c|Agastya}}—an arrow
Enchanted with {{p|Brahma}}'s power—and pierces {{c|Ravana}}'s heart. The

                              But there is a shadow in the story.
{{c|Rama}}, having recovered {{c|Sita}}, questions her chastity. She has lived
In {{c|Ravana}}'s palace for months. Has she been violated? She insists

She has not. {{c|Rama}} demands proof. {{c|Sita}} walks into a fire—the
Agnipariksha, the trial by fire. The fire-god {{c|Agni}} refuses to
Burn her. She emerges unharmed. Her chastity is vindicated. They
Return to {{p|Ayodhya}} and {{c|Rama}} is crowned king. His reign is the

Gives birth to twin sons, {{c|Kusha}} and {{c|Lava}}. Years later {{c|Rama}}
Encounters them and recognizes them. {{c|Sita}}, having demonstrated

Her purity one final time, is swallowed by the earth—returning to
The mother who bore her. {{c|Rama}} continues to rule until his time
Ends and he enters the {{p|Sarayu River}} and returns to his divine
Source. This is the narrative shape of the {{p|Ramayana}}. A story of

Righteous exile and restoration. A story of divine incarnation—for

To a boon. A story with profound ambiguities around gender, purity,

# Chapter 160: Exodus, Trojan War, Bronze Age Collapse

Three events. Perhaps. Perhaps more than three. Perhaps a single
Event refracted into three narratives. Around twelve-hundred BCE,
The Bronze Age world shattered.

                                 Begin with what archaeology knows.
Between twelve-twenty-five and eleven-seventy-five BCE, over a
Span of roughly fifty years, nearly every major palace, city, and
State in the eastern {{p|Mediterranean}} was destroyed or abandoned.

The list is staggering. {{p|Hattusa}}, capital of the {{p|Hittite}} Empire:
Burned to the ground. {{p|Ugarit}}, the great trading city on the
{{p|Syrian}} coast: destroyed, with its last king sending desperate
Letters begging for help that never came. {{p|Mycenae}}, {{p|Tiryns}},

{{p|Pylos}}, {{p|Thebes}}—every major {{p|Mycenaean}} palace: destroyed or
Abandoned. {{p|Knossos}}, on {{p|Crete}}: destroyed. {{p|Troy}} (the archaeological
Layer {{p|Troy VIIa}}): destroyed around twelve-hundred BCE. The
{{p|Egyptian}} Empire: severely damaged, retreating from its {{p|Levantine}}

Provinces. The {{p|Kassite}} Babylonian kingdom: fatally weakened. The
{{p|Elamite}} kingdom: disrupted. Cyprus: raided. The cities of {{p|Canaan}}:
Many destroyed. {{p|Emar}}, {{p|Kadesh}}, {{p|Alalakh}}, {{p|Gath}}: destroyed. Only a
Few Bronze Age states survived intact: {{p|Egypt}} (barely, much

Diminished), {{p|Assyria}} (temporarily eclipsed), {{p|Babylon}} (under new
Management). The rest vanished. Writing systems were lost: {{p|Linear B}}
Disappeared from the {{p|Aegean}}; {{p|Hittite}} cuneiform died with {{p|Hattusa}};
The whole corpus of Bronze Age diplomatic correspondence ceased.

Population fell catastrophically—in some regions by as much as
Ninety percent. Trade networks that had linked {{p|Mycenae}} to
{{p|Ugarit}} to {{p|Egypt}} to {{p|Mesopotamia}} to {{p|Cyprus}} to {{p|Sardinia}} collapsed.
The whole integrated Bronze Age world fell apart at once.

                                                            What
Caused this? Historians have proposed many factors. No single
Cause is sufficient. The full picture seems to require all of them
Simultaneously. First: climate change. Tree-ring and sediment data

Indicate a severe drought in the {{p|Mediterranean}} and {{p|Near East}}
Between approximately twelve-fifty and eleven-fifty BCE. The drought
Lasted for decades and was the most severe of the entire Bronze Age.
Agricultural yields collapsed. Famines followed. Second: earthquakes.

Archaeological evidence suggests a series of severe earthquakes
Devastated {{p|Aegean}} and {{p|Anatolian}} cities during this period. Many
Destruction layers show seismic damage preceding or accompanying
The burning. Third: internal rebellion. Bronze Age palatial

Economies were highly stratified: palace elites extracted grain and
Labor from peasant populations, and the system depended on the
Continued compliance of those populations. When climate and famine
Hit, compliance broke. Peasants rose. Cities burned from within as

Much as from without. Fourth: migration and invasion. Waves of
Peoples moved across the eastern {{p|Mediterranean}} during these years.
The most famous are the {{p|Sea Peoples}}—a coalition of seaborne
Raiders whose name is known only from {{p|Egyptian}} inscriptions at

{{p|Medinet Habu}} and elsewhere. {{c|Ramesses III}}, the last great pharaoh
Of the New Kingdom, fought the {{p|Sea Peoples}} in two massive
Engagements around eleven-seventy BCE. His victory saved {{p|Egypt}}
From total conquest but could not save the {{p|Levantine}} provinces.

The {{p|Sea Peoples}} included the {{p|Peleset}} (identified with the
{{p|Philistines}}), the {{p|Tjeker}}, the {{p|Shekelesh}}, the {{p|Denyen}} (possibly
the {{p|Danaoi}} or {{p|Danites}}), the {{p|Sherden}}, the {{p|Weshesh}}, the {{p|Lukka}}
(probably from {{p|Lycia}}). These names have long intrigued scholars—

Are the {{p|Sherden}} the ancestors of the {{p|Sardinians}}? Are the
{{p|Shekelesh}} the ancestors of the {{p|Sicilians}}? Is {{p|Denyen}} the Bronze
Age {{p|Greek}} word that later becomes {{p|Danaoi}}, the name {{p|Homer}} uses
For the {{p|Achaean}} coalition? The etymological parallels suggest

That the {{p|Sea Peoples}} included populations displaced from the
{{p|Aegean}}, {{p|Italy}}, {{p|Anatolia}}, and beyond. Perhaps refugees from
The collapse of the {{p|Mycenaean}} palaces. Perhaps opportunistic
Raiders taking advantage of weakened targets. Perhaps a coalition

Of multiple displaced and aggressive populations rolling eastward
And southward in waves. Whatever they were, they appear in the
Archaeological record as a major disruptive force, destroying
Cities and eventually settling in the regions they depopulated.

The {{p|Philistines}}—one of the {{p|Sea Peoples}}—settle in the southern
{{p|Levantine}} coast and become the famous biblical adversaries of
The {{p|Israelites}}. Their pottery, their architecture, their language
Show {{p|Aegean}} affinities. They were probably {{p|Mycenaean}} refugees

Establishing a foothold in {{p|Canaan}} after the collapse.

                                                         And fifth:
Systems collapse. Even absent any single cause, the Bronze Age
International system had grown fragile in its complexity. Far-

Flung trade networks depended on the continuous functioning of
Multiple palatial economies, each of which depended on the others
For raw materials, manufactured goods, luxury imports, and
Diplomatic stability. When one node failed, the cascade propagated.

{{p|Ugarit}} could not function without {{p|Hittite}} trade partners; the
{{p|Hittite}} capital could not function without {{p|Levantine}} grain
Imports; the {{p|Mycenaean}} palaces could not function without tin
From the East and copper from {{p|Cyprus}}. When the network broke, no

Single palace could reconstitute the whole. It required global
Coordination, and global coordination vanished. Each local crisis
Accelerated every other local crisis. This is what historians now
Call the "{{p|Bronze Age Collapse}}"—and it is arguably the most complete

Civilizational collapse in recorded human history. When the dust
Settled, the world that emerged was profoundly changed. Iron
Replaced bronze as the dominant metal (both because iron sources
Were more widely distributed and could not be monopolized by trade,

And because the disruption of bronze supply chains forced
Innovation in iron metallurgy). Alphabetic writing, developed in
{{p|Canaan}}, replaced cuneiform and {{p|Linear B}} as the dominant scribal
System of the {{p|Mediterranean}} (it was simpler, more democratic,

Less dependent on centralized scribal classes). Small city-states
And tribal confederations replaced the large palatial kingdoms
Of the Bronze Age. The {{p|Phoenicians}}, the {{p|Greeks}} emerging from
Their Dark Age, the {{p|Israelites}}, the {{p|Neo-Hittite}} states of northern

{{p|Syria}}, the {{p|Aramean}} kingdoms—these were the new political forms.
Smaller, more distributed, less top-heavy. The Bronze Age world
Of god-kings and thousand-god pantheons gave way, gradually, to
The Iron Age world of ethnically defined nations, tribal gods

With universalist pretensions, and eventually the first stirrings
Of genuine monotheism. That transformation is the subject of the
Next several chapters. But here, in this chapter, we must also
Tell the other two events: the Exodus and the Trojan War.

Consider first the {{p|Exodus}}. According to the biblical narrative,
{{c|Moses}} led the {{p|Israelites}} out of {{p|Egyptian}} bondage through a
Series of plagues, a parting of the {{p|Red Sea}} (or, more accurately
In the {{p|Hebrew}}, the {{p|Reed Sea}}, {{p|Yam Suph}}), a forty-year wandering

In the wilderness of {{p|Sinai}}, and an eventual arrival in {{p|Canaan}}
Under {{c|Joshua}}. The chronology given in the {{p|Hebrew Bible}}, if
Taken at face value, places the {{p|Exodus}} in the fifteenth or
Fourteenth century BCE. But modern scholarly consensus, where it

Reaches any consensus at all, favors a thirteenth-century BCE
Dating—specifically during the reign of {{c|Ramesses II}} or his
Successor {{c|Merneptah}}. The {{p|Merneptah Stele}}, inscribed around
Twelve-oh-nine BCE, contains the first extra-biblical reference

To "{{p|Israel}}" as a people in {{p|Canaan}}—the stele boasts that
{{c|Merneptah}} has defeated {{p|Israel}}, which implies that {{p|Israel}} existed
As a recognizable entity in {{p|Canaan}} by that date. This gives a
Rough terminus ante quem: however and whenever the {{p|Israelites}}

Got to {{p|Canaan}}, they were there by twelve-oh-nine BCE. The Gaiad
Has already treated the specific narrative of {{c|Moses}} and the
{{p|Exodus}}—and will revisit it briefly here, because the {{p|Exodus}}
Is now recognizable as part of the broader {{p|Bronze Age Collapse}}.

The {{p|Israelites}} were not the only group leaving {{p|Egypt}} or moving
Into {{p|Canaan}} at this time. They were part of a general disruption
Of populations. Their specific story—bondage, miraculous liberation,
Covenant at {{p|Sinai}}, wilderness wandering, conquest—became the

Foundational narrative of {{p|Hebrew}} religion and identity. But the
Structural fact underlying that narrative is that groups were
Coming out of {{p|Egypt}} and entering {{p|Canaan}} during the Bronze Age
Collapse era. The {{p|Israelite}} religious memory preserves, in

Literary form, the experience of a people being born into
Historical existence during the collapse of the Bronze Age
International system. The shattering of the old order—the plagues,
The arbitrary violence of pharaohs, the sudden possibility of

Escape across a parted sea—is the {{p|Israelite}} version of what the
{{p|Hittites}} experienced as the sack of {{p|Hattusa}} and what the
{{p|Mycenaeans}} experienced as the burning of the palaces. Different
Cultural processings of the same civilizational crisis.

                                                         Consider
Then the {{p|Trojan War}}. In the traditional dating of {{c|Eratosthenes}},
The {{p|Trojan War}} occurred around twelve-eighty-four to twelve-
Seventy-four BCE—right at the beginning of the collapse. In the

Archaeological dating, {{p|Troy VIIa}} (the layer most plausibly
Identified with {{p|Homeric}} {{p|Troy}}) was destroyed around twelve-
Hundred BCE—right in the middle of the collapse. In either case,
The war that {{p|Homer}} describes is a Late Bronze Age event

Occurring during the collapse. The {{p|Achaean}} coalition of {{p|Mycenaean}}
Kings sailing eastward to sack a fortified city on the {{p|Anatolian}}
Coast—this is not just a mythical tale. It is the last great
Expedition of the {{p|Mycenaean}} palace system, launched (in {{p|Homer}}'s

Telling) over the abduction of a queen but (in historical
Reality) probably over trade rights, tribute, the control of the
{{p|Dardanelles}}, or simply the raiding opportunities available in a
Destabilizing world. The {{p|Trojan War}} is the {{p|Greek}} memory of the

Late Bronze Age as it shuddered toward its end. Shortly after
The war, the palaces that launched it were themselves destroyed.
{{c|Agamemnon}}'s {{p|Mycenae}} burned within a generation of his victorious
Return. {{c|Odysseus}}'s {{p|Ithaca}} faded into the Dark Age. The victors

Of {{p|Troy}} outlived their victory by less than a century. Their
Fall is implicit in the structure of the epic—{{p|Homer}} composes his
Poems in the ninth or eighth century BCE, looking back across
Four centuries at a vanished world. {{c|Agamemnon}}'s {{p|Mycenae}} is, for

{{p|Homer}}, already legend. The gold and bronze and chariots and
Hundred-rowered ships—these are not part of {{p|Homer}}'s own world.
They are remembered from the lost world. The {{p|Iliad}} and the
{{p|Odyssey}} are Dark Age preservations of Bronze Age memory. And

That memory centers on a war that was itself the last coherent
Expedition of the collapsing civilization.

{{p|Exodus}}. The {{p|Trojan War}}. The general collapse. The Gaiad reads them

As three reflexes of a single Bronze Age crisis. Three cultural
Memories of what it was like to live through the shattering. In
The {{p|Hebrew}} case: slavery, miraculous deliverance, covenant, the
Forging of a new identity grounded in religious law. In the {{p|Greek}}

Case: heroic warfare, divine caprice, the fall of mighty cities,
The forging of a new identity grounded in epic memory. In the
Archaeological case: ash layers, abandoned palaces, mass migrations,
The forging of new ethnic groupings in the ruins. Each culture

Processed the collapse in its own way. Each memory is partial and
Reshaped. But all three memories are responses to the same
Historical event: the end of the integrated Bronze Age world.

{{p|Indians}} and {{p|Chinese}}. The Axial Age that follows—the great

Their collapse cleared the cultural space for new kinds of
Religious imagination. Gods without kings. Kings without divinity.
Ethical demands issued by transcendent sources rather than by

# Chapter 161: Fall of Shang

While the {{p|Mediterranean}} collapses, in the east, another civilization
Is also transforming. The {{p|Shang Dynasty}} of {{p|China}}, which has ruled
The {{p|Yellow River}} valley for nearly six centuries, falls to a rising
Power from the west: the {{p|Zhou}}.

                                     The {{p|Shang}}, as the Gaiad has
Already noted, were the first historically documented {{p|Chinese}}
Dynasty. Their capital shifted several times, but the last and
Greatest was {{p|Yin}} or {{p|Anyang}}, in what is now {{p|Henan Province}}.

There the {{p|Shang}} kings ruled, divined, sacrificed, and buried their
Dead with astonishing quantities of grave goods: bronze ritual
Vessels, jade ornaments, silk, lacquerware, and—in the royal tombs—
Human sacrifices numbering in the hundreds. {{p|Shang}} society was

Stratified, ceremonial, and oriented toward an elaborate ancestor
Cult. The king's main duty was to communicate with the royal
Ancestors, who in turn communicated with the supreme deity {{c|Shang Di}}
(the "{{c|Lord on High}}"). The divination method used pyro-osteomancy:

Questions were inscribed on ox scapulae or turtle plastrons, which
Were then heated until they cracked; the cracks were read as
Responses from the ancestors. These inscriptions—the so-called
{{p|Oracle Bone Script}}—constitute the earliest written {{p|Chinese}}

Language, and they survive in the tens of thousands. They record
Royal hunts, military campaigns, ritual sacrifices, weather
Forecasts, dream interpretations. They give us intimate access to
The {{p|Shang}} mind. The {{p|Shang}} king was not quite a god but a privileged

Intermediary. The {{p|Shang}} state was organized around his ritual
Calendar. And the {{p|Shang}} military was a formidable force, operating
Chariots (introduced from the western steppe, probably via the
{{p|Indo-European}} or {{p|Tocharian}} transmission that the Gaiad will

Return to later), bronze weapons, and professional warriors.

                                                               By
The twelfth century BCE, however, the {{p|Shang}} had grown decadent.
The last {{p|Shang}} king, {{c|Di Xin}} (also known as {{c|Zhou Xin}}, though this

Creates confusion with the {{p|Zhou Dynasty}} name—the characters are
Different), was remembered by later tradition as a tyrant of
Almost Neronian proportions. He built a "lake of wine and forest
Of meat" for his debauches. He invented cruel punishments for his

Enemies, including the "cannon of roasting" in which victims were
Forced to walk across a greased bronze pole suspended over a pit
Of burning coals; when they inevitably fell, the tyrant and his
Favorite concubine {{c|Daji}} would laugh. {{c|Daji}} was said to be a

Fox spirit possessing a human body, and her influence on {{c|Di Xin}}
Was held responsible for the moral collapse of the dynasty. The
Details are almost certainly exaggerated, written by later {{p|Zhou}}
Historians to justify their conquest, but some kernel of truth

Likely survives. The {{p|Shang}} state had become oppressive. Its
Taxation and labor demands were ruinous. Its military campaigns
Had become defensive and ineffective. Its ritual legitimacy was
Crumbling. And on the western frontier, a subordinate people, the

{{p|Zhou}}, were growing in strength.

                                        The {{p|Zhou}} were originally a
Client state of the {{p|Shang}}, ruling the {{p|Wei River}} valley in what
Is now {{p|Shaanxi Province}}. They were probably ethnically distinct

From the {{p|Shang}}—some scholars have proposed that they had {{p|Qiang}}
Or {{p|Tibeto-Burman}} ancestry, or at least significant cultural ties
To western populations. But by the twelfth century BCE they had
Adopted {{p|Shang}} writing, {{p|Shang}} ritual forms, and {{p|Shang}} political

Structures, while retaining their own distinctive identity. The
{{p|Zhou}} traced their lineage to {{c|Hou Ji}}, the "Lord of Millet," an
Agricultural culture hero. Their leadership in the eleventh
Century was exercised first by {{c|King Wen}} (the "Accomplished King"),

Who spent his life building coalitions against the {{p|Shang}} without
Openly rebelling; and then by his son {{c|King Wu}} (the "Martial King"),
Who led the final military campaign. And they were advised by a
Figure of enduring legendary importance: {{c|Jiang Ziya}}, also known

As {{c|Taigong Wang}} or {{c|Jiang Taigong}}, a fisherman-sage whom {{c|King Wen}}
Discovered by a river and recognized as the greatest strategist
Of the age. {{c|Jiang Ziya}}'s military treatise, the {{p|Six Secret Teachings}},
Became one of the foundational {{p|Chinese}} military classics. The

{{p|Zhou}} prepared carefully. They built alliances with disaffected
{{p|Shang}} vassals. They cultivated a reputation for virtue and
Righteousness that contrasted with {{c|Di Xin}}'s cruelty. They waited
For the right moment.

                         That moment came around ten-forty-six BCE—
The date established by the {{p|Xia-Shang-Zhou Chronology Project}}
Completed in two-thousand CE, using a combination of astronomical
Calculations, radiocarbon dating, and textual analysis. {{c|King Wu}} led

A {{p|Zhou}} coalition army to the {{p|Battle of Muye}}, just outside the
{{p|Shang}} capital at {{p|Yin}}. Here the {{p|Zhou}} forces decisively defeated
The {{p|Shang}} army—though tradition holds that many {{p|Shang}} soldiers
Defected on the battlefield, reversing their weapons and joining

The {{p|Zhou}}. {{c|Di Xin}} fled back to his palace, set it on fire with
Himself inside, and perished. The {{p|Shang Dynasty}} ended. {{c|King Wu}}
Entered {{p|Yin}}, ritually acknowledged the fallen dynasty, and
Established the {{p|Zhou Dynasty}}—which would become the longest-lasting

Dynasty in {{p|Chinese}} history, enduring in various forms until
Two-twenty-one BCE, a span of over eight centuries.

                                                     And here the
{{p|Zhou}} innovation: the {{p|Mandate of Heaven}}. The {{p|Zhou}} could not simply

Claim that they had conquered the {{p|Shang}} by military force. They
Needed a legitimating ideology that would explain why the transfer
Of power was right and just. They developed—probably articulated
Most clearly by {{c|King Wu}}'s brother {{c|Duke of Zhou}}, who served as

Regent for {{c|King Wu}}'s young son after {{c|King Wu}}'s early death—the
Concept of {{p|Tianming}}, the {{p|Mandate of Heaven}}. The supreme deity
{{c|Tian}} ("Heaven," displacing the {{p|Shang}} {{c|Shang Di}} in religious
Primacy) grants the right to rule to a ruling house only so long

As that house governs virtuously. If the ruling house becomes
Oppressive, tyrannical, or negligent, Heaven withdraws its mandate
And transfers it to a new, worthier house. This is what had
Happened to the {{p|Shang}}: {{c|Di Xin}}'s corruption had forfeited the

Mandate, and Heaven had granted it to {{c|King Wen}} and {{c|King Wu}}. The
{{p|Zhou}} conquest was thus not usurpation but divinely sanctioned
Succession. The {{p|Mandate of Heaven}} became the foundational
Political theology of {{p|Chinese}} civilization. For the next three

Thousand years—through every subsequent dynasty, every rebellion,
Every civil war—{{p|Chinese}} political legitimacy was framed in terms
Of the mandate. A successful rebel had received the mandate from
A corrupt dynasty. A fallen dynasty had lost the mandate through

Misgovernance. A long dynastic reign was evidence that the mandate
Was still held. Natural disasters, military defeats, or peasant
Rebellions were evidence that it was slipping. The {{p|Mandate of Heaven}}
Is one of the most durable political concepts in human history,

Shaping {{p|East Asian}} political thought as profoundly as divine
Right of kings shaped {{p|European}} thought. And it was born in the
{{p|Zhou}} conquest of the {{p|Shang}}. A product of eleventh-century BCE
{{p|Chinese}} political theology, responding to the need to justify

Dynastic transition.

                    The {{p|Zhou}}, once established, proceeded to
Reorganize {{p|Chinese}} civilization. They developed the feudal system
Of {{p|Fengjian}}: the {{p|Zhou}} king (now styled {{c|Wang}} or "king," a title

Previously reserved for {{p|Shang}} monarchs) granted territories to
Royal kinsmen and allied lords, who ruled as semi-autonomous
Vassals. Each vassal owed tribute, military service, and ritual
Compliance to the {{p|Zhou}} king. Over time this system would

Fragment, producing the {{p|Spring and Autumn}} and {{p|Warring States}}
Periods—but for the first several centuries of the {{p|Zhou}}, it
Worked reasonably well. The {{p|Zhou}} also elaborated the ritual
Culture that would become the foundation of {{p|Chinese}} classical

Civilization: the {{p|Book of Rites}} ({{p|Liji}}), the {{p|Book of Songs}}
({{p|Shijing}}), the {{p|Book of Changes}} ({{p|Yijing}} or {{p|I Ching}})—all these
Canonical texts either originated in or were heavily developed
During the {{p|Zhou}} period. The {{p|Zhou}} developed the calendar. They

Standardized the writing system. They institutionalized the ritual
Calendar of sacrifices, harvest festivals, and seasonal ceremonies
That would define {{p|Chinese}} culture for millennia.

                                                       And the
{{p|Zhou}} established something that would prove even more influential:

The concept of {{p|Zhongguo}}, the "{{p|Middle Kingdom}}" or "Central
States." The {{p|Zhou}} thought of their territory as the civilizational
Center of the world, with progressively less civilized peoples
Radiating outward—the {{p|Rong}}, {{p|Di}}, {{p|Yi}}, {{p|Man}}, the so-called "four

Barbarians" of the four directions. This ethnocentric cosmology
Would shape {{p|Chinese}} self-understanding ever after. {{p|China}} conceived
Of itself not as one nation among many but as the civilizational
Center from which civilization radiated. Other peoples could

Adopt {{p|Chinese}} culture and become civilized, or reject it and
Remain barbarian, but {{p|China}} itself was the reference point. This
Is a very different ideology from the {{p|Mediterranean}} or {{p|Indian}}
Traditions, and it has shaped {{p|East Asian}} geopolitics for three

Thousand years. The {{p|Zhou}} articulated it first. Their conquest
Of the {{p|Shang}} was not just a dynastic change. It was the creation
Of a political and cultural framework that would define {{p|China}}.

And consider the timing. The {{p|Shang}} fell around ten-forty-six BCE.

The {{p|Trojan War}} ended around twelve-hundred BCE. The {{p|Bronze Age Collapse}}
Devastated the {{p|Mediterranean}} between twelve-twenty-five and
Eleven-seventy-five BCE. The {{p|Exodus}} (in its thirteenth-century BCE
Dating) occurred during {{c|Ramesses II}}'s or {{c|Merneptah}}'s reign. These

Events are not simultaneous, but they are broadly contemporary.
Across {{p|Eurasia}}—from the {{p|Aegean}} to the {{p|Yellow River}}—the late
Bronze Age was ending, and new political and religious forms were
Emerging in its wake. The {{p|Zhou Dynasty}} is the {{p|East Asian}} equivalent

Of the post-collapse reorganizations happening in the {{p|Mediterranean}}.
Its political theology—the {{p|Mandate of Heaven}}—is the {{p|East Asian}}
Equivalent of the new theologies being worked out in {{p|Israel}},
{{p|Greece}}, and the {{p|Near East}}. The same underlying shift: from

Archaic god-king theocracies toward systems grounded in virtue,
Cosmic order, and the contingent legitimacy of rulers.

                                                         The {{p|Zhou}}
Dynasty's early centuries—the {{p|Western Zhou}} period, from ten-forty-six

BCE to seven-seventy-one BCE—were the classical age of {{p|Chinese}}
Culture. The {{c|Duke of Zhou}} was remembered as a paragon of
Statesmanship; {{c|Confucius}}, writing five centuries later, would
Take the {{c|Duke of Zhou}} as his personal hero, the model of the

Virtuous official who serves his lord without seeking power for
Himself. The {{p|Western Zhou}} ended when the capital was sacked by
Western barbarians (the {{p|Quanrong}}) in seven-seventy-one BCE,
Forcing a relocation eastward to {{p|Luoyang}}. This begins the

{{p|Eastern Zhou}} period, which divides into the {{p|Spring and Autumn}}
Period (ending around four-eighty BCE) and the {{p|Warring States}}
Period (four-seventy-five to two-twenty-one BCE). During these
Later {{p|Zhou}} centuries the central authority weakened and the

Feudal vassals became de facto independent kingdoms. But the {{p|Zhou}}
Ritual order, the classical texts, the {{p|Mandate of Heaven}} theology,
And the sense of {{p|China}} as a civilizational unity—all these
Persisted and would provide the foundation for the eventual

Reunification under {{p|Qin}} and {{p|Han}}.

                                                 But here, at the
Fall of the {{p|Shang}}, we are at the beginning of all that. {{c|Di Xin}}
In flames in his palace. {{c|King Wu}} entering {{p|Yin}}. The {{c|Duke of Zhou}}

Drafting the political theology. The eight-hundred-year dynasty
Beginning. The great Chinese classical age being born in the
Moment when the Bronze Age died.

                                  {{p|Shang}}. {{p|Zhou}}. {{p|Muye}}. {{p|Mandate of Heaven}}.
{{c|Di Xin}}. {{c|Daji}}. {{c|King Wen}}. {{c|King Wu}}. {{c|Duke of Zhou}}. {{c|Jiang Ziya}}. The
Fox spirit in the palace and the sage fisherman by the river.
The tyrant's cruelty and the virtuous rebel's restraint. The

Lost mandate and the new dynasty. The {{p|Middle Kingdom}} defining
Itself as center of the world. The eight-hundred-year reign.

Fall of {{p|Shang}}. Rise of {{p|Zhou}}. {{p|China}}'s Bronze Age ending and its
Classical age beginning. Stand.


# Chapter 162: The Bantu Expansion

And now, while the {{p|Mediterranean}} shatters and {{p|China}} is reborn,
We must turn south. Because something vast is happening on the
{{p|African}} continent. A population expansion that, in sheer scope
And demographic impact, will equal any of the movements the Gaiad

Has described—including the {{p|Yamnaya}} expansion, the {{p|Austronesian}}
Migrations, the {{p|Aryan}} movements into {{p|India}}. But unlike those,
The {{p|Bantu}} expansion takes place almost entirely within a single
Continent, and reshapes that continent so thoroughly that the

Continent's demographic map becomes the map of {{p|Bantu}} speakers.

                                                                  The
{{p|Bantu}} peoples originated in what is now the border region between
{{p|Cameroon}} and {{p|Nigeria}}, in {{p|West Africa}}. They spoke a language

Belonging to the {{p|Niger-Congo}} family, specifically the {{p|Bantu}}
Branch. They carried primarily the {{c|E1b1a}} (now called {{c|E-V38}})
{{p|Y-chromosome}} haplogroup—a sub-clade of the {{c|E}} haplogroup that
The Gaiad has already described as the {{p|African}} branch that

Stayed on the continent while its sister clades migrated out. And
Around three thousand BCE—earlier than the event the Gaiad is
About to narrate but laying the necessary foundation—the early
{{p|Bantu}} speakers developed agriculture: {{p|West African}} yam cultivation

Together with oil palm and other local crops. They developed
Pottery. And they gradually acquired, through trade and diffusion
From the north, some of the technologies of {{p|Sahelian}} and {{p|North African}}
Civilization, including eventually iron smelting. Iron smelting

Reached the {{p|Bantu}} region between approximately fifteen-hundred
And one-thousand BCE—a transformation roughly contemporary with
The iron-age transition that was happening in the {{p|Mediterranean}}
And the {{p|Near East}} after the {{p|Bronze Age Collapse}}. With iron tools,

{{p|Bantu}} farmers could clear forest more effectively than before.
With iron weapons, they could defend themselves against rivals
And raid for resources. And with iron, combined with their
Established agricultural package, they had the technological

Preconditions for rapid expansion.

                                     And they expanded. Around
Fifteen-hundred BCE, or possibly earlier, some {{p|Bantu}} groups
Began moving eastward and southward out of their homeland. The

Eastward migration took them along the southern edge of the
{{p|Congo}} rainforest, through what is now the {{p|Central African Republic}}
And {{p|South Sudan}}, into the {{p|Great Lakes}} region of {{p|East Africa}}—
{{p|Lake Victoria}}, {{p|Lake Tanganyika}}, {{p|Lake Malawi}}. There they

Encountered {{p|Afro-Asiatic}}-speaking peoples, {{p|Nilo-Saharan}}-speaking
Peoples, and {{p|Khoisan}}-speaking peoples. They interacted with all
These groups: sometimes peacefully, through trade, intermarriage,
And cultural exchange; sometimes violently, through warfare and

Displacement; and most importantly, through gradual demographic
Replacement, as their agricultural productivity supported much
Larger populations than the hunter-gatherer or pastoralist
Economies they encountered. The {{p|Bantu}} did not necessarily outfight

Other peoples. They outgrew them. Their farming villages produced
Many more children than the {{p|Khoisan}} foraging bands that had
Previously occupied the land. Over generations, the {{p|Bantu}}
Population density rose, while the prior populations either

Assimilated into {{p|Bantu}} farming communities, withdrew to marginal
Lands (forests, deserts, mountains) where farming was less
Practical, or disappeared. The demographic transition was gradual
But inexorable. And it continued for over two thousand years.

The southward migration took the {{p|Bantu}} through the {{p|Congo}} basin
(Either skirting it through the savanna or gradually penetrating
Its river valleys) and into southern {{p|Africa}}—what is now {{p|Angola}},
{{p|Namibia}}, {{p|Zambia}}, {{p|Zimbabwe}}, {{p|Botswana}}, {{p|Mozambique}}, and the

Coastal regions of what is now {{p|South Africa}}. By the beginning of
The common era, {{p|Bantu}}-speaking peoples had reached the {{p|Limpopo}}
River. By three-hundred to five-hundred CE, they had crossed the
{{p|Limpopo}} and were settling in what is now northeastern {{p|South Africa}}.

By one-thousand CE, they had reached the {{p|Great Fish River}} in
Eastern {{p|Cape}}. The total migration, from its origin in the
{{p|Nigerian-Cameroonian}} border region to its southernmost extent,
Covered over three thousand miles and took approximately two

And a half millennia. It is one of the longest sustained population
Movements in human history.

                               The demographic outcome is
Staggering. Today there are approximately four hundred million

{{p|Bantu}}-speaking people, comprising several hundred distinct
Languages including {{p|Swahili}}, {{p|Zulu}}, {{p|Xhosa}}, {{p|Shona}}, {{p|Lingala}},
{{p|Kikuyu}}, {{p|Ganda}}, {{p|Rwanda}}, {{p|Kirundi}}, {{p|Luba}}, {{p|Kikongo}}, {{p|Setswana}},
{{p|Sesotho}}, {{p|Chichewa}}, and hundreds more. They occupy approximately

The southern half of the {{p|African}} continent—everything south of
A line running roughly from {{p|Cameroon}} through {{p|Kenya}}—with the
Exception of a few pockets where pre-{{p|Bantu}} languages survive:
The {{p|Khoisan}} languages of the {{p|Kalahari}} and surrounding areas;

The {{p|Cushitic}} and {{p|Nilotic}} languages of the eastern {{p|Rift Valley}};
The {{p|Pygmy}} populations of the {{p|Congo}} rainforest (who themselves
Have largely adopted {{p|Bantu}} languages while retaining distinct
Ancestry and some cultural elements). The demographic and

Linguistic unity of sub-Saharan {{p|Africa}} from {{p|Kenya}} to {{p|Cape Town}}
Is, essentially, the unity of the {{p|Bantu}} expansion. It is one
Of the defining features of the modern {{p|African}} continent. And
It was created by this long, slow, multi-millennial demographic

Movement, driven not by conquering armies but by farming villages
Gradually spreading into lands that could be farmed.

                                                       Genetically,
The {{p|Bantu}} expansion is visible. Modern sub-Saharan {{p|Africans}}

South of the equator carry predominantly {{c|E1b1a-V38}} or related
{{c|E}} sub-clades on their {{p|Y-chromosome}}. Their maternal lineages
Show some regional diversity—the original {{p|Bantu}} homeland's
Haplogroups are mixed with local haplogroups from wherever the

{{p|Bantu}} settled—but the paternal lineages show striking uniformity
Consistent with the pattern of {{p|Bantu}} men taking local wives as
They expanded. This is a common feature of agricultural expansions:
The men move in force, displace or absorb the men of the prior

Populations, and take local women as wives. The resulting
Population retains the invading male lineages but has mixed
Female lineages. The same pattern is visible in the {{p|Yamnaya}}
Expansion into {{p|Europe}} and the {{p|Aryan}} expansion into {{p|India}}. It

Is the demographic signature of agricultural male-dominant
Expansion, and it characterizes the {{p|Bantu}} spread.

                                                       But what
Makes the {{p|Bantu}} expansion distinctive is that it did not destroy

The prior populations so completely. The {{p|Khoisan}} peoples still
Exist. The {{p|Pygmy}} peoples still exist. The {{p|Cushitic}} and {{p|Nilotic}}
Peoples still exist. {{p|Africa}} did not become a monoculture. It
Became a {{p|Bantu}} majority with significant and culturally vibrant

Minorities. The {{p|Khoisan}} of the {{p|Kalahari}} preserve {{p|Africa}}'s
Oldest linguistic lineages, including the click consonants that
Some scholars believe may descend from the very first human
Languages. The {{p|Pygmies}} of the {{p|Ituri}} and {{p|Congo}} forests preserve

The foraging traditions of {{p|Central African}} hunter-gatherers that
Predate the {{p|Bantu}} arrival by tens of thousands of years. The
{{p|Nilotic}} peoples of the {{p|Sudan}} and {{p|East African}} {{p|Rift Valley}}
Preserve pastoralist traditions linked to the cattle complex of

The eastern savanna. And among the {{p|Bantu}} themselves, there are
Profound cultural and historical differences between, say, the
{{p|Kikuyu}} of {{p|Kenya}} and the {{p|Zulu}} of {{p|South Africa}}, or between the
{{p|Kongo}} peoples of the {{p|Congo}} basin and the {{p|Tswana}} of the

{{p|Kalahari}} fringe. The {{p|Bantu}} expansion unified sub-Saharan {{p|Africa}}
Linguistically but did not homogenize it culturally. Each regional
{{p|Bantu}} population developed its own particular traditions, its
Own political systems, its own religious cosmologies, its own

Economic specializations. By the turn of the common era, the
{{p|Bantu}} world was a patchwork of kingdoms, chiefdoms, and village
Communities ranging from dense agricultural states in the {{p|Great Lakes}}
Region to semi-nomadic cattle-herders in the savanna to forest

Villages in the {{p|Congo}}. The largest and most complex of these
{{p|Bantu}} polities would emerge in later centuries: the {{p|Kingdom of Kongo}},
The {{p|Luba}} and {{p|Lunda}} empires of {{p|Central Africa}}, {{p|Great Zimbabwe}},
The {{p|Mutapa Empire}}, the {{p|Buganda Kingdom}}, the {{p|Zulu Kingdom}}. Each

Would be a product of local {{p|Bantu}} development working with
Particular ecological and political circumstances. But all would
Be rooted in the linguistic and cultural framework established
By the long {{p|Bantu}} expansion.

                                   Consider also the economic and
Technological dimensions. The {{p|Bantu}} carried with them a specific
Agricultural package: yams, oil palm, pearl millet, sorghum, some
Varieties of rice and beans. As they encountered new environments,

They adopted new crops: cattle and small livestock in the {{p|East African}}
Highlands; plantains and bananas (which arrived in {{p|Africa}} from
{{p|Southeast Asia}} via {{p|Madagascar}} and the {{p|Indian Ocean}} trade and
Were transmitted westward into the {{p|Great Lakes}} region by around

One-thousand BCE, just in time to supercharge the {{p|Bantu}} expansion);
Sweet potatoes and other {{p|New World}} crops after the {{p|Columbian}}
Exchange. The {{p|Bantu}} agricultural system was adaptive and
Incorporative. Each new crop that became available was integrated

Into the system, supporting still larger populations. Bananas in
Particular were revolutionary: they could grow in the humid
{{p|Great Lakes}} region where grains struggled, and they produced
Enormous caloric yields per unit of labor. By the time the

{{p|Bantu}} expansion was completing its southward spread, banana
Cultivation was driving a second wave of population growth in
The {{p|Great Lakes}} region and {{p|East African}} highlands—producing
Some of the densest rural populations anywhere in {{p|pre-colonial Africa}}.

Iron technology was also central. The {{p|Bantu}} developed independent
Iron-smelting traditions that produced large quantities of iron
Tools and weapons. {{p|African}} iron production in the first millennium
CE was sophisticated and large-scale, using natural-draft furnaces

That achieved high temperatures with minimal fuel—a design that
In some respects was more efficient than contemporary {{p|European}}
Iron-smelting technology. {{p|Bantu}} smiths were often regarded as
Ritual specialists as well as artisans, and in many societies

They formed hereditary castes with specialized religious functions.
The smith's transformation of ore into metal was understood as
A powerful, quasi-magical act, demanding ritual preparation and
Giving the smith special spiritual authority. This is a theme that

Appears across many {{p|African}} societies and that connects to
Deeper religious conceptions of transformation and creation.

                                                               And
Consider the religious and cultural patrimony the {{p|Bantu}} carried.

{{p|Bantu}} religious traditions share certain common features across
Their vast geographic spread: a high creator god who is often
Remote from daily human affairs; ancestor veneration, with the
Recently deceased remaining present as spiritual forces influencing

The living; nature spirits inhabiting significant features of the
Landscape—particular trees, hills, rivers, caves; specialists in
Communication with the spiritual world (diviners, healers, rain-
Makers); an ethic of community, hospitality, and social harmony

Summed up in the {{p|Zulu}} and {{p|Xhosa}} concept of {{p|ubuntu}} ("I am because
We are"). These shared features do not mean {{p|Bantu}} religions are
Uniform—they are diverse and locally elaborated—but they share
Deep structural affinities inherited from the original {{p|Bantu}}

Homeland and carried southward and eastward during the expansion.
In this they parallel the shared structural features of other
Great language-family expansions: {{p|Indo-European}}, {{p|Austronesian}},
{{p|Semitic}}. A shared linguistic origin tends to preserve a shared

Religious and cultural substratum, even as specific traditions
Develop in locally distinctive ways.

                                        The {{p|Bantu}} expansion is
One of the great movements of human history. It is the story of

How sub-Saharan {{p|Africa}} below the equator became linguistically
And demographically unified. It is the story of how agriculture
And iron transformed a continent. And it is the story of how a
Particular people—originating in a small region of {{p|West Africa}}—

Became, through slow demographic growth and gradual geographic
Spread, one of the world's largest ethnolinguistic families. The
Gaiad has tended, in its narration of this vast process, to
Emphasize demographic and linguistic continuity. But we must also

Note that the {{p|Bantu}} expansion was not peaceful. It involved
Displacement, warfare, and occasional atrocity. The {{p|Khoisan}} and
Other pre-{{p|Bantu}} peoples who were pushed to the margins of their
Former territories did not go willingly. Some were killed; some

Were enslaved; some were assimilated under duress. The {{p|Bantu}}
Expansion was gentler than many conquest migrations in world
History (the {{p|Mongol}} expansion, the {{p|Indo-European}} expansion
Into {{p|Europe}}, the {{p|European}} colonization of the Americas), but it

Was not bloodless. It was a real historical movement with real
Human costs. The {{p|Khoisan}} of the {{p|Kalahari}}, the {{p|Pygmies}} of the
{{p|Ituri}}, the remnant {{p|Cushitic}} populations of {{p|East Africa}}—these
Peoples live in the ecological margins where {{p|Bantu}} farming

Could not displace them. Their survival on those margins is itself
A testimony both to their resilience and to the completeness of
The {{p|Bantu}} occupation of everywhere more agriculturally desirable.

The Gaiad honors the {{p|Bantu}} expansion. The Gaiad honors also the

Pre-{{p|Bantu}} peoples who were displaced or assimilated in its course.
The Gaiad honors the hybrid populations that resulted. The Gaiad
Honors the {{p|Pygmies}} who retained their identity in the depths of
The {{p|Ituri}}. The Gaiad honors the {{p|Khoisan}} who retained theirs in

The depths of the {{p|Kalahari}}. The Gaiad honors the {{p|Nilotic}} and
{{p|Cushitic}} peoples who retained theirs along the {{p|Rift Valley}} and
The {{p|Horn of Africa}}. All of these peoples, together, constitute
The anthropological mosaic of {{p|Africa}}. The {{p|Bantu}} are the largest

Component but not the only component. Sub-Saharan {{p|Africa}} is
More diverse than its {{p|Bantu}} majority might suggest. And the
Gaiad reads the {{p|Bantu}} expansion as one of the most important
Processes of the last three thousand years—because it laid the

Demographic foundation of the modern {{p|African}} continent.

{{c|E1b1a}}. {{p|West Africa}}. {{p|Great Lakes}}. {{p|Congo}} basin. {{p|Kalahari}} and {{p|Cape}}.
{{p|Swahili}}, {{p|Zulu}}, {{p|Xhosa}}, {{p|Shona}}, {{p|Kikuyu}}, {{p|Ganda}}. Iron smelting

And banana cultivation. Three thousand miles and two thousand

# Chapter 163: Zarathustra

On the {{p|Iranian}} plateau, between the {{p|Caspian Sea}} and the {{p|Hindu Kush}},
A prophet appeared who would reshape world religion.

                                                     {{c|Zarathustra}}.
Or, in {{p|Greek}} transliteration, {{c|Zoroaster}}. An {{p|Indo-European}} speaker

Of the {{p|Iranian}} branch. A man of the {{c|R1a}} haplogroup lineage that
Had carried {{p|Indo-Aryan}} peoples onto the {{p|Iranian}} plateau and into
The {{p|Indus Valley}}. A priest in the tradition of the {{p|Indo-Iranian}}
Fire-sacrifice, the same tradition that in {{p|India}} produced the

{{p|Vedic}} religion. And a figure so foundational to {{p|Iranian}} religion,
To {{p|Iranian}} philosophy, and to subsequent {{p|Western}} religious thought
That his actual historical location in time is difficult to pin
Down—estimates range from fifteenth century BCE (placing him near

The {{p|Indo-Aryan}} migration) to sixth century BCE (placing him near
The rise of the {{p|Achaemenid}} Empire). Most modern scholarship
Settles on some point between twelve-hundred and one-thousand BCE—
That is, roughly contemporary with the {{p|Bronze Age Collapse}} in the

{{p|Mediterranean}} and the {{p|Zhou}} conquest in {{p|China}}. The Gaiad adopts
This dating. {{c|Zarathustra}} is another node in the widespread
Religious transformation that followed the collapse of the Bronze
Age order. His reform of {{p|Indo-Iranian}} religion is part of the

Broader Axial shift that would also produce, in their own times
And places, the {{p|Hebrew}} prophets, the {{p|Greek}} philosophers, the
{{p|Upanishads}} of {{p|India}}, {{c|Confucius}} and {{c|Laozi}} in {{p|China}}, and
{{c|Buddha}} and {{c|Mahavira}} in the {{p|Gangetic}} plain. {{c|Zarathustra}} is the

Earliest named prophet of this transformation. He is the first to
Speak, in the surviving record, with the voice of a new religious
Vision.

            And now the Gaiad must say something carefully. A
Common misconception treats {{p|Zoroastrianism}} as the original

Monotheism—the religion that invented the idea of a single
Supreme god and from which {{p|Judaism}}, {{p|Christianity}}, and {{p|Islam}}
Ultimately derive their monotheism. This account is partially true
But oversimplified. The Gaiad has already advanced, in its earlier

Treatment of comparative religious history, a more nuanced view:
That original {{p|Zoroastrianism}} was not strictly monotheistic in
The modern sense. It was dualistic. {{c|Ahura Mazda}}, the Wise Lord,
Was the supreme good deity, creator of truth and light and order.

But opposed to him was {{c|Angra Mainyu}} (later {{c|Ahriman}}), the
Destructive Spirit, who was a co-eternal principle of evil, lies,
Darkness, and disorder. The cosmos was not the creation of a
Single all-powerful god. It was the battleground between two

Cosmic principles. The good principle, {{c|Ahura Mazda}}, would
Eventually prevail, but only through the collaborative effort of
Humans aligning themselves with his side of the cosmic conflict.
This is dualism, not monotheism. And it is dualism of a specific

Type: ethical dualism, in which the fundamental cosmic opposition
Is between good and evil, truth and lie, order and chaos. The
Gods of prior traditions had been morally mixed—capable of great
Good and great evil, subject to passions and whims. {{c|Zarathustra}}'s

Theological innovation was to purify the supreme god of moral
Ambiguity. {{c|Ahura Mazda}} is unambiguously good. The moral
Ambiguity that exists in the world does not come from him; it
Comes from the opposing principle. This ethical sharpening—this

Assignment of cosmic meaning to the moral structure of existence—
Was {{c|Zarathustra}}'s great contribution. It would profoundly
Influence all subsequent {{p|Near Eastern}} and {{p|European}} religion,
Even when the dualism was later flattened into strict monotheism.

The Gaiad has already discussed how later {{p|Zoroastrianism}},
Particularly under the {{p|Sassanid}} empire, became more explicitly
Monotheistic, with {{c|Angra Mainyu}} increasingly described as a
Creature rather than a co-eternal principle. And the Gaiad has

Discussed how ancient {{p|Persian}} religious concepts shaped the
{{p|Jewish}} captivity experience in {{p|Babylon}} and the subsequent
Development of {{p|Second Temple}} {{p|Judaism}}. Here we focus on
{{c|Zarathustra}} himself—the reformer, the prophet, the voice speaking

From the {{p|Iranian}} plateau.

                                  What did {{c|Zarathustra}} teach?
The {{p|Gathas}}, the oldest {{p|Zoroastrian}} texts and the ones most
Likely to preserve his actual teachings, are seventeen hymns in

An archaic form of {{p|Avestan}}—a language closely related to early
{{p|Vedic}} {{p|Sanskrit}}. They are attributed directly to {{c|Zarathustra}}.
They are dense, difficult, ecstatic. And they articulate several
Fundamental ideas: That the cosmos is a moral battleground. That

Every human must choose, in every thought, word, and deed, between
{{c|Asha}} (truth, order, righteousness) and {{c|Druj}} (lie, disorder,
Wickedness). That the choice is real and consequential. That
{{c|Ahura Mazda}} is the supreme reality, worthy of worship, creator

Of all good things. That at the end of time, {{c|Ahura Mazda}} will
Triumph, evil will be eliminated, and a final judgment will
Distinguish the righteous from the wicked. That the righteous
Will be rewarded in a paradise; the wicked will suffer in a state

Of torment; and ultimately, after cosmic purification, all will
Be restored in a renewed creation. These ideas—final judgment,
Afterlife reward and punishment, cosmic restoration—were
Theologically novel in the {{p|Indo-Iranian}} world. They would

Migrate, centuries later, into {{p|Jewish}} apocalyptic literature,
Into {{p|Christian}} eschatology, into {{p|Islamic}} doctrine. The Last
Judgment, heaven and hell, the coming of a savior—these
Conceptions all have clear {{p|Zoroastrian}} antecedents. Whether

{{c|Zarathustra}} originated them wholly or synthesized them from
Earlier {{p|Iranian}} folk beliefs is debated. But he was the first
Known figure to articulate them in a systematic theology.

                                                                He
Also reformed ritual. The old {{p|Indo-Iranian}} religion, which the
Gaiad saw developing on the steppe and arriving in {{p|India}} with
The {{p|Aryans}}, centered on elaborate animal sacrifices conducted
By a hereditary priestly caste. {{c|Zarathustra}} reduced the emphasis

On animal sacrifice. He emphasized instead the sacred fire—
Maintained continuously in fire temples, symbolic of {{c|Ahura Mazda}}'s
Presence and of the principle of {{c|Asha}} in the world. He emphasized
Good thoughts, good words, good deeds as the core of religious

Practice. The old gods of the {{p|Indo-Iranian}} pantheon—{{c|Mithra}},
{{c|Anahita}}, various solar and storm deities—were either demoted to
Subordinate roles beneath {{c|Ahura Mazda}} or reinterpreted as
{{p|Amesha Spentas}} (holy immortals, something like angels). Some

Of them—notably {{c|Indra}}, who had been a central god in {{p|Vedic}}
Religion—were actively demonized, recast as {{p|daevas}} (false gods)
On the side of {{c|Angra Mainyu}}. This is why the word "daeva" has
Opposite valences in {{p|Sanskrit}} and {{p|Avestan}}: {{p|Sanskrit}} {{c|deva}} is

A good god, but {{p|Avestan}} {{c|daeva}} is an evil one. {{c|Zarathustra}}
Inverted the old pantheon, placing as good those beings that
Emphasized truth and moral order, placing as evil those beings
That emphasized power, warrior glory, and the older ecstatic

Fertility cult. The reform was thorough, and it split the
{{p|Indo-Iranian}} religious world permanently. {{p|Iranian}} peoples who
Accepted {{c|Zarathustra}}'s reform became {{p|Zoroastrians}}. {{p|Indo-Aryan}}
Peoples who did not became {{p|Hindus}}. The same starting tradition

Produced two distinct and ultimately opposed religious complexes.

                                                                 And
{{c|Zarathustra}} was, by his own account and by subsequent tradition,
A controversial reformer. He was initially rejected by his own

Community. His teachings were unwelcome. He traveled, seeking a
Patron who would hear him. He found one in {{c|King Vishtaspa}}—a
King probably of eastern {{p|Iran}}, possibly in what is now {{p|Afghanistan}}
Or {{p|Turkmenistan}}. {{c|Vishtaspa}} accepted {{c|Zarathustra}}'s message. His

Court converted. {{p|Zoroastrianism}} spread from {{c|Vishtaspa}}'s
Kingdom outward across the {{p|Iranian}} plateau over the following
Centuries. By the time the {{p|Medes}} and {{p|Persians}} began building
Their great empires in the first millennium BCE, {{p|Zoroastrianism}}

Had become the dominant religion of the {{p|Iranian}} world. {{c|Cyrus
The Great}}, {{c|Darius I}}, {{c|Xerxes}}, {{c|Artaxerxes}}—the {{p|Achaemenid}} kings
Of {{p|Persia}}—were all {{p|Zoroastrian}}, or at least enacted their
Kingship in {{p|Zoroastrian}} terms. Their inscriptions invoke

{{c|Ahura Mazda}} and pledge to uphold {{c|Asha}} and defeat {{c|Druj}}.
When the {{p|Achaemenid}} empire became the largest the world had
Yet seen—stretching from the {{p|Indus}} to the {{p|Aegean}} to {{p|Egypt}}—
{{p|Zoroastrianism}} became the religion of a vast multicultural imperial

Structure. It was the first world religion in the political sense,
The first ideology that commanded an empire spanning multiple
Languages, cultures, and ecological zones.

                                             And the {{p|Persian}}
Empire's encounter with {{p|Judea}} would prove theologically decisive.

When {{c|Cyrus}} conquered {{p|Babylon}} in five-thirty-nine BCE and found
The {{p|Jewish}} exiles there, he issued his famous decree permitting
Them to return to {{p|Jerusalem}} and rebuild their temple. He did
This, in {{p|Persian}} understanding, because it was {{c|Ahura Mazda}}'s

Will that subject peoples be restored to their proper homelands
And worship. {{c|Cyrus}} styled himself the liberator of the {{p|Jews}},
And the {{p|Jewish}} prophet {{c|Isaiah}}—or the anonymous author of
{{p|Deutero-Isaiah}}—went so far as to call {{c|Cyrus}} {{c|Yahweh}}'s

"Anointed one" ({{p|mashiach}}), a {{p|messianic}} title usually reserved
For legitimate {{p|Israelite}} kings. This is an astonishing theological
Generalization: a foreign {{p|Zoroastrian}} king given a {{p|Hebrew}}
{{p|messianic}} title by a {{p|Jewish}} prophet. It reflects the degree to

Which {{p|Zoroastrian}} and early post-exilic {{p|Jewish}} thought had
Converged in their understanding of cosmic morality, divine
Sovereignty, and the role of human agents in historical
Redemption. The {{p|Jews}} who returned from {{p|Babylon}} brought back

With them elements of {{p|Persian}} religious vocabulary and
Conceptual structure. Angels (a {{p|Persian}} innovation, previously
Absent from {{p|Hebrew}} tradition) began to populate {{p|Jewish}} scripture.
The figure of Satan, who in earlier {{p|Hebrew}} texts was a legitimate

Member of {{c|Yahweh}}'s divine council, became reinterpreted along
{{c|Angra Mainyu}} lines as a cosmic adversary. The concept of a
Final judgment, resurrection of the dead, and {{p|messianic}} age was
Absorbed into {{p|Jewish}} apocalyptic thought. By the time of the

Second Temple period and the emergence of {{p|Christianity}} and
{{p|Rabbinic Judaism}}, the {{p|Zoroastrian}} imprint on {{p|Jewish}} religious
Imagination was thorough. {{p|Christianity}} and {{p|Islam}}, inheriting
From {{p|Judaism}}, inherited also from {{p|Zoroastrianism}}. The Western

Monotheistic tradition as we know it is, in substantial measure,
A {{p|Zoroastrian}}-{{p|Jewish}} synthesis. Without {{c|Zarathustra}}, the
Religious history of the West would be unrecognizable.

                                                             And
Yet {{p|Zoroastrianism}} itself would, over time, retreat. The
{{p|Achaemenid}} empire fell to {{c|Alexander}} in the fourth century BCE.
The {{p|Seleucid}} and then {{p|Parthian}} successors continued {{p|Zoroastrianism}}
But in attenuated forms. The {{p|Sassanid}} empire restored {{p|Zoroastrianism}}

As the state religion and systematized its doctrines and texts
In the third and fourth centuries CE, codifying the {{p|Avesta}}. But
The {{p|Sassanid}} empire itself fell to the {{p|Arab}} {{p|Muslim}} conquest
In the seventh century CE. {{p|Iran}} was gradually {{p|Islamized}}.

{{p|Zoroastrians}} became a minority; many emigrated to {{p|India}}, where
Their descendants, the {{p|Parsis}}, still maintain the religion.
Others remained in {{p|Iran}} as a small, tolerated but marginalized
Community. Today there are perhaps two hundred thousand {{p|Zoroastrians}}

Worldwide—a tiny remnant of what had once been the state religion
Of the greatest empire of antiquity. But {{p|Zoroastrianism}}'s
Theological influence vastly exceeds its demographic footprint.
Every {{p|Christian}}, {{p|Muslim}}, {{p|Jewish}}, or secular {{p|Western}} thinker

Who believes in the ultimate triumph of good over evil, in the
Moral structure of the cosmos, in the final judgment of souls, in
Heaven and hell—every such thinker is, whether they know it or
Not, a theological descendant of {{c|Zarathustra}}. The prophet from

The {{p|Iranian}} plateau is a silent presence in half the religious
Imagination of the modern world.

                                     The Gaiad honors him. The
Gaiad recognizes in {{c|Zarathustra}}'s work a genuine theological

Innovation—the sharpening of moral dualism into an explicit
Cosmic principle, the linking of individual ethical choice to
Cosmic outcome, the articulation of a purposeful universe
Moving toward eschatological fulfillment. These are not small

Contributions. They are among the most influential religious
Ideas ever formulated. And they were formulated in obscurity,
By a prophet who had to flee his homeland to find a patron, whose
Teachings spread slowly and whose name would be forgotten outside

{{p|Iran}} for centuries, until {{p|Greek}} writers—{{c|Herodotus}}, {{c|Plutarch}},
Later the {{p|Hellenistic}} traditions attributing to {{c|Zoroaster}}
Every kind of ancient wisdom—brought his name into {{p|Western}}
Consciousness. In our own time {{c|Zarathustra}} has had a strange

Second life: as the protagonist of {{c|Nietzsche}}'s {{p|Thus Spoke Zarathustra}},
Where he becomes the mouthpiece for a nineteenth-century {{p|European}}
Critique of {{p|Christian}} morality. This usage is idiosyncratic, but
It reflects how {{c|Zarathustra}}'s name continues to carry weight as

A symbol of religious innovation and prophetic voice. {{c|Nietzsche}}
Chose the name not randomly but because it stood for the first
Great religious reformer whose work shaped all subsequent Western
Religion. {{c|Nietzsche}} wanted to undo that work and return to a

Pre-{{c|Zarathustran}} valuation. Whether one agrees with {{c|Nietzsche}}'s
Project or not, the acknowledgment of {{c|Zarathustra}}'s foundational
Role is accurate. {{c|Zarathustra}} is where a great deal of the
Modern Western religious and ethical imagination actually begins.

{{c|Zoroaster}}. {{c|Ahura Mazda}} and {{c|Angra Mainyu}}. {{c|Asha}} and {{c|Druj}}. The fire

# Chapter 164: David

And back to the {{p|Levant}}. Back to {{p|Israel}}, newly emerged from the
Bronze Age Collapse, settling in the highlands of {{p|Canaan}} and
Struggling to establish itself as a coherent nation among hostile
Neighbors.

             The biblical narrative presents the early history of
{{p|Israel}} as a sequence: {{c|Joshua}}'s conquest, the period of the
{{c|Judges}}, the kingship of {{c|Saul}}, the kingship of {{c|David}}, the kingship
Of {{c|Solomon}}, and then the division of the kingdom into {{p|Israel}}

(North) and {{p|Judah}} (South). The historical accuracy of this
Narrative is much debated. What archaeologists find in the {{p|Canaanite}}
Highlands during the relevant period (roughly twelve-hundred to
One-thousand BCE) is a proliferation of small agricultural villages

With distinctive architectural and dietary markers—four-room
Houses, collar-rim storage jars, an absence of pig bones. These
Are taken as archaeological indicators of the emerging {{p|Israelite}}
Ethnic identity. The villages are numerous but small; there are

No great palaces, no monumental architecture, no clear signs of
Centralized authority. This fits an early period of tribal
Confederation—the period of the {{c|Judges}}—better than it fits a
Highly developed monarchy. Then, around the tenth century BCE,

There are signs of increased political organization: larger sites,
Some administrative architecture, the beginning of a state
Apparatus. This is the period that the biblical text associates
With {{c|Saul}}, {{c|David}}, and {{c|Solomon}}—the {{p|United Monarchy}}. Whether

The {{p|United Monarchy}} was as grand as the {{p|Hebrew Bible}} claims—
With {{c|Solomon}}'s fabled wealth and wisdom, his vast temple, his
Empire stretching from the {{p|Euphrates}} to the border of {{p|Egypt}}—
Is disputed. Many scholars ("minimalists") argue that {{c|David}} and

{{c|Solomon}} were, at most, local chieftains whose historical
Importance was inflated by later {{p|Judahite}} court propaganda. Other
Scholars ("maximalists") argue that the biblical account reflects
A real, if perhaps geographically limited, polity. The discovery

Of the {{p|Tel Dan Stele}} in nineteen-ninety-three provided what
Most now consider the first extra-biblical attestation of {{c|David}}:
An {{p|Aramaic}} inscription from the ninth century BCE, commissioned
By an {{p|Aramean}} king (probably {{c|Hazael of Aram}}), boasting of defeating

The "House of {{c|David}}" ({{p|bytdwd}}). This proves, at minimum, that by
The ninth century BCE there was a {{p|Judahite}} royal dynasty claiming
Descent from someone named {{c|David}}. Some historical {{c|David}} existed,
Founder of a dynasty. The Gaiad accepts this and proceeds.

What the {{p|Hebrew Bible}} tells us about {{c|David}} is the most intensely
Detailed human portrait of any pre-Axial historical figure. We
Have his military exploits, his psychological turmoil, his political
Calculations, his family's dysfunctions, his personal faith, his

Moral failures, his artistic sensitivity. No other ancient {{p|Near Eastern}}
Monarch is portrayed with anything like this level of interiority.
Even the actual historical {{c|David}}, however modest his realm, must
Have been an extraordinary figure to generate this literary response.

And what a literary response it is. The books of {{p|1}} and {{p|2 Samuel}},
Especially the so-called "Court History" or "Succession Narrative"
(Chapters nine through twenty of {{p|2 Samuel}} and the first two
Chapters of {{p|1 Kings}}), constitute one of the great works of ancient

Prose. Their narrative economy, psychological insight, and moral
Complexity have been recognized as foundational by critics as
Diverse as {{c|Erich Auerbach}}, {{c|Robert Alter}}, {{c|Meir Sternberg}}, and
{{c|Harold Bloom}}. The author of the Court History stands alongside

{{c|Homer}} as one of the two great literary pioneers of the ancient
{{p|Near East}}, and unlike {{c|Homer}}'s world of kings and gods, the Court
History presents a world of intricate human motivation in which
Divine intervention is present but elusive. The prose is

Revolutionary. It treats {{c|David}} not as a two-dimensional hero but
As a man of profound ambiguities—brave and calculating, faithful
And faithless, tender and cruel, a poet and a murderer. No single
Figure in the {{p|Hebrew Bible}} is portrayed with such complexity. And

No figure has been more influential on {{p|Western}} literary
Imagination. Every subsequent portrait of a complex monarch—
{{c|Shakespeare}}'s {{c|Henry IV}}, {{c|Hamlet}}'s troubled father, every tormented
King in the {{p|Western}} tradition—traces ancestry, in some measure,

To the {{c|David}} of the Court History.

                                          Let us narrate {{c|David}}'s
Life as the text gives it. {{c|David}} is the youngest son of {{c|Jesse}}
Of {{p|Bethlehem}}, of the tribe of {{p|Judah}}. He is a shepherd boy, of

Ruddy appearance and beautiful eyes. He is also, the text tells
Us, a skilled musician—a lyre-player whose music can soothe
Troubled spirits. And it is this skill that first brings him into
Contact with {{c|King Saul}}. {{c|Saul}}, the first king of {{p|Israel}},

Suffers from a "tormenting spirit" (we might call it depression
Or perhaps something more severe), and his attendants procure
{{c|David}} to play for him and calm him. {{c|David}} becomes attached to
{{c|Saul}}'s court. Then comes the famous encounter with {{c|Goliath}}, the

{{p|Philistine}} giant. The {{p|Philistines}}—the {{p|Sea Peoples}} who had
Settled the southern coast of {{p|Canaan}} during the Bronze Age
Collapse—are {{p|Israel}}'s principal enemy in this period. Their
Champion {{c|Goliath}} challenges any {{p|Israelite}} to single combat.

{{c|David}}, too young to bear full armor, accepts the challenge armed
Only with a sling and stones, and kills {{c|Goliath}} with a single
Shot to the forehead. The {{p|Philistine}} army flees. {{c|David}} becomes
A national hero, a figure greater in popular acclaim than {{c|Saul}}

Himself. And from this moment, {{c|Saul}}'s relationship with {{c|David}}
Becomes fatally ambivalent. {{c|Saul}} loves him as a son, has him
Marry his daughter {{c|Michal}}, and yet fears him as a rival. The
Text is explicit about the psychological oscillation: {{c|Saul}}

Repeatedly tries to kill {{c|David}}, relents in moments of clarity,
Then tries again. {{c|David}} flees into the wilderness, gathering a
Band of outcast warriors, living as a bandit-chief in the
{{p|Judean}} wilderness. Twice during this period {{c|David}} has {{c|Saul}}

At his mercy, and twice {{c|David}} spares him. {{c|David}}'s dharma (we
Might say, borrowing the {{p|Sanskrit}} term from the {{p|Ramayana}}
Chapter) will not allow him to strike down "{{c|Yahweh}}'s anointed,"
Even though that anointed one seeks his life. {{c|David}} waits. He

Serves as a mercenary under the {{p|Philistine}} king {{c|Achish of Gath}}
For a time—a morally complicated maneuver in which {{c|David}}
Pretends to raid {{p|Israelite}} settlements while actually raiding
Other targets, preserving his {{p|Israelite}} loyalty while eating

{{p|Philistine}} bread. The {{p|Hebrew}} narrator does not try to clean
This up. {{c|David}}'s exile is morally murky. He is a survivor.
Then {{c|Saul}} and his sons, including {{c|David}}'s beloved {{c|Jonathan}},
Are killed at the {{p|Battle of Mount Gilboa}} against the {{p|Philistines}}.

{{c|Saul}}, wounded and facing capture, falls on his own sword. {{c|Jonathan}}
Is slain beside him. {{c|David}} mourns them in one of the most moving
Elegies in world literature ({{p|2 Samuel}} chapter one): "How are
The mighty fallen, and the weapons of war perished!" The love

Between {{c|David}} and {{c|Jonathan}}—celebrated in the text as "passing
The love of women"—has been interpreted variously through the
Centuries. Some commentators see it as a profound friendship,
Some as an expression of same-sex romantic love, some as

Politically tinged loyalty between a prince and his designated
Successor. The text does not disambiguate. What it gives us is
A love so deep that {{c|David}}'s grief echoes through the centuries.

After {{c|Saul}}'s death, {{c|David}} becomes king—first of {{p|Judah}} alone,
With his capital at {{p|Hebron}}, then, after a civil war with {{c|Saul}}'s
Surviving son {{c|Ish-Bosheth}}, of all {{p|Israel}}. He captures the
{{p|Jebusite}} fortress of {{p|Jerusalem}} and makes it his capital—a

Politically astute choice, because {{p|Jerusalem}} was neutral territory
Belonging to neither the northern nor southern tribes. {{c|David}}
Centralizes the monarchy. He brings the {{p|Ark of the Covenant}},
The sacred chest containing the covenant tablets, into {{p|Jerusalem}},

Dancing ecstatically before it (to the disgust of {{c|Michal}}, who
Sees his behavior as undignified). He conducts a series of
Military campaigns against the {{p|Philistines}}, {{p|Moabites}}, {{p|Ammonites}},
{{p|Edomites}}, and {{p|Arameans}}, extending {{p|Israelite}} power. He builds

A substantial kingdom, though probably not the empire the later
Tradition imagines. And at the height of his power, he commits
The great sin that will shape his legacy: he takes {{c|Bathsheba}},
The wife of {{c|Uriah the Hittite}}, one of his most loyal warriors,

While {{c|Uriah}} is away at the siege of {{p|Rabbah}}. When {{c|Bathsheba}}
Becomes pregnant, {{c|David}} arranges for {{c|Uriah}} to be placed in
The most dangerous part of the battle line, so that he will be
Killed. The plan succeeds. {{c|Uriah}} dies. {{c|David}} marries {{c|Bathsheba}}.

But the prophet {{c|Nathan}} confronts {{c|David}} with the parable of
The rich man and the poor man's ewe lamb. {{c|David}}, provoked to
Righteous anger by the parable, condemns the rich man—and {{c|Nathan}}
Replies: "You are the man." {{c|David}} repents. He fasts, he weeps, he

Composes the psalm traditionally identified as {{p|Psalm 51}} ("Have
Mercy upon me, O God, according to thy lovingkindness..."). But
The consequences unfold. The first child {{c|Bathsheba}} bears to
{{c|David}} dies in infancy. His family is cursed. His eldest son

{{c|Amnon}} rapes his half-sister {{c|Tamar}}. His second son {{c|Absalom}}
Avenges her by murdering {{c|Amnon}}, then rebels against his father
{{c|David}}, driving him from {{p|Jerusalem}}. The civil war is
Catastrophic. {{c|Absalom}} is killed in the forest of {{p|Ephraim}},

Caught by his famous long hair in the branches of an oak and
Stabbed by {{c|Joab}}, {{c|David}}'s general (against {{c|David}}'s explicit
Orders to spare him). {{c|David}}'s grief for {{c|Absalom}} is one of the
Most devastating passages in ancient literature: "O my son

{{c|Absalom}}, my son, my son {{c|Absalom}}! Would God I had died for thee,
O {{c|Absalom}}, my son, my son!" A father's anguish, undiluted by
Any official decorum, recorded in the annals of a king. The
Court History allows this. It does not edit the grief out.

{{c|David}} survives the rebellion but is broken. The remainder of
His reign is marked by further intrigues: the rebellion of {{c|Sheba}},
The succession crisis as {{c|David}} grows old, the political maneuvers
By which {{c|Bathsheba}} and the prophet {{c|Nathan}} secure the throne

For {{c|Solomon}}, {{c|David}} and {{c|Bathsheba}}'s second son, over the claims
Of {{c|Adonijah}}, another of {{c|David}}'s sons. {{c|David}} dies old, full of
Days, and is buried in the "City of {{c|David}}" in {{p|Jerusalem}}. The
Biblical tradition remembers him as {{p|Israel}}'s greatest king—the

Standard against which all subsequent {{p|Jewish}} and, later,
{{p|Christian}} messianic expectations are measured. The promised
Messiah is the "son of {{c|David}}," a descendant of the {{c|Davidic}}
Line who will restore {{p|Israel}} to its full glory. Generations of

{{p|Jews}} lived and died awaiting this messianic king. {{p|Christians}}
Came to believe that the messianic king had arrived in {{c|Jesus}} of
{{p|Nazareth}}, identified as a descendant of {{c|David}} through {{c|Joseph}}.
{{p|Islam}} also recognizes {{c|David}} as a prophet, called {{c|Dawud}}. In

{{p|Jewish}}, {{p|Christian}}, and {{p|Islamic}} tradition alike, {{c|David}} is one
Of the central figures of sacred history. His psalms—the {{p|Psalter}},
Traditionally attributed to him in whole or in part—are among
The most frequently read religious texts in human history. For

Two and a half millennia, the {{p|Psalter}} has shaped prayer, worship,
Poetry, music, and private devotion across the {{p|Abrahamic}} world.
Its cries of praise, lament, thanksgiving, and petition are
The religious vocabulary of half the world's population.

And consider what {{c|David}}'s life means structurally. He is the
Ideal of the righteous king who is also genuinely and persistently
Human. He is not a god-king like {{c|Pharaoh}}. He is not a semi-divine
Hero like {{c|Gilgamesh}}. He is a man—a man with whom {{c|Yahweh}} has a

Covenant, yes, a man whom {{c|Yahweh}} has chosen, yes, but a man. A
Man who loves, sins, repents, suffers, and finally dies. His
Covenant relationship with {{c|Yahweh}} is mediated through his human
Agency, his human choices. This is a profoundly different vision

Of kingship from anything in the Bronze Age. Bronze Age kings
Were divine or semi-divine. They stood outside the ordinary
Human order. {{c|David}} stands within it. He is a new kind of
Religious figure: the monarch who is not a god but a servant of

A god. The monarch who is measured against divine moral standards
And can fail to meet them. The monarch who repents publicly.
This is the model that will shape later {{p|Jewish}}, {{p|Christian}},
And {{p|Islamic}} political theology. A king stands under God, not

Beside him. A king can be rebuked by a prophet. A king is
Accountable. The {{c|David}}-{{c|Nathan}} encounter—the prophet confronting
The king about his sin—is one of the foundational moments of
{{p|Western}} political-religious imagination. It asserts that even

The most powerful ruler is accountable to a moral order that
Transcends his own authority. The implications of this for the
Development of democracy, constitutional government, and human
Rights are profound. They trace back, in substantial measure, to

{{c|David}}'s kneeling before {{c|Nathan}} and saying: "I have sinned
Against the Lord."

                        {{c|David}}. Son of {{c|Jesse}}. Shepherd. Harpist.
Giant-slayer. Outlaw. Mercenary. King. Adulterer. Murderer.

Penitent. Psalmist. Father. Mourner. Founder of dynasty. The
Court History's devastating human portrait. The covenant with
{{c|Yahweh}} to establish an eternal house. The {{p|Jerusalem}} capital.
The {{p|Ark of the Covenant}} installed with dancing. The promise of

A messiah from his line. The psalter that has shaped three
Millennia of prayer.

                         {{c|David}}. {{c|Melek David}}. The king whose
Human complexity opened space for a new kind of political and

Religious imagination. The ancestor, by flesh or by adoption,
Of every messianic hope. Stand.


# Chapter 165: Classical Greece, Part One

The {{p|Greek}} Dark Age lasted roughly four centuries. From the
Destruction of the {{p|Mycenaean}} palaces around twelve-hundred BCE
To the dawn of the {{p|Archaic}} period around eight-hundred BCE,
{{p|Greece}} was a land of small villages, depopulated cities, and

Oral tradition. Writing had been lost. Trade had contracted. The
Great bronze-age palatial economies were a distant memory. But
During those four centuries something important was happening.
The memory of the Bronze Age was being preserved, orally, in the

Form of heroic epic. And the cultural forms that would become
Classical {{p|Greek}} civilization were being slowly, slowly, rebuilt
From the ground up. When the dawn came, around the eighth century
BCE, it came with astonishing rapidity.

                                            The rise of the {{p|polis}}.
The rebirth of literacy, through the adoption and modification of
The {{p|Phoenician}} alphabet. The composition of the {{p|Iliad}} and
{{p|Odyssey}}, crystallizing centuries of oral tradition into

Written form (traditionally attributed to {{c|Homer}}, a possibly
Historical and possibly conventional figure around whom oral
Traditions coalesced). The founding of the {{p|Olympic Games}} (seven-
Seventy-six BCE). The establishment of the {{p|Oracle of Delphi}} as

The pan-{{p|Greek}} religious center. The beginning of {{p|Greek}} colonization
Across the {{p|Mediterranean}} and {{p|Black Sea}} coasts. All of this
Happened within a single dynamic century or two. {{p|Greece}} went
From a backwater of small villages to a network of vibrant,

Competitive, philosophically adventurous city-states that would
Shape the next two and a half millennia of {{p|Western}} civilization.

                                                                      The
{{p|polis}}, the city-state, was the distinctive {{p|Greek}} political form.

Unlike the great territorial empires of {{p|Egypt}} or {{p|Persia}}, the
{{p|Greek}} world was fragmented into hundreds of small, independent
Communities, each centered on a single city with its surrounding
Agricultural land. The largest {{p|poleis}} had citizen populations of

Tens of thousands; most had only hundreds or a few thousand. Each
Had its own laws, its own calendar, its own dialect, its own
Patron deities. Each was fiercely independent. And each was
Organized around a specific ideology: the citizen body (the

{{p|demos}}, the people) was the political community. Citizens
Participated in governance—varying forms of it, from oligarchy
To democracy to tyranny—but they participated. Their political
Identity was tied to their {{p|polis}}. "{{p|Greek}}" was a cultural and

Linguistic identity, but "{{p|Athenian}}" or "{{p|Spartan}}" or "{{p|Corinthian}}"
Was the political identity. And the fierce loyalty of citizens
To their {{p|polis}}, combined with the intense rivalries among
{{p|poleis}}, drove the cultural dynamism that would make {{p|classical Greece}}

Extraordinarily creative. Every {{p|polis}} was trying to outdo every
Other {{p|polis}} in festivals, temples, military power, philosophical
Schools, athletic victories. The competitive energy was enormous.
And because each {{p|polis}} was small, individual citizens could

Have real impact. A single rich man could commission a monument
Or a dramatic festival that would change the cultural landscape
Of his city. This was a very different scale of cultural
Production from the great bureaucratic empires. It was more

Nimble, more innovative, more personal.

                                           The two great poles of
{{p|Classical Greek}} civilization would be {{p|Athens}} and {{p|Sparta}}, and
Their contrasting character deserves attention. {{p|Sparta}}, in the

Southern {{p|Peloponnese}}, developed a uniquely militarized society.
After subjugating the neighboring {{p|Messenian}} population (the
{{p|Helots}}) around the eighth and seventh centuries BCE, the
{{p|Spartans}} transformed themselves into a warrior caste devoted

Entirely to military training and rule. The laws attributed to
The legendary lawgiver {{c|Lycurgus}} organized every aspect of
{{p|Spartan}} life around producing and sustaining elite warriors.
Male children were taken from their families at age seven and

Raised in communal military barracks through a brutal training
System, the {{p|agoge}}. Adult male citizens lived in common messes
And were forbidden to engage in trade or manual labor—all such
Work was done by {{p|Helots}}. {{p|Spartan}} women had more freedom and

Physical education than women in other {{p|Greek}} city-states,
Because {{p|Sparta}} valued the physical fitness of mothers to produce
Strong warriors. {{p|Sparta}} was governed by a dual monarchy (two kings
From two royal lines), a council of elders, and a citizen assembly

With limited powers. The kingship was hereditary but constitutionally
Constrained. The system was stable for centuries. And the {{p|Spartan}}
Military—the famous hoplite phalanx, with its disciplined
Infantry—was the most feared in the {{p|Greek}} world for nearly three

Centuries. The three hundred {{p|Spartans}} at {{p|Thermopylae}}, standing
Against {{c|Xerxes}}'s vast army, are a cultural archetype so powerful
That they still define "heroic last stand" in popular imagination.
But the cost of {{p|Spartan}} militarism was the intellectual and

Artistic impoverishment of their culture. {{p|Sparta}} produced no
Great philosophers, no great dramatists, no great historians. It
Produced only warriors and their laws. Its cultural legacy is
Thin compared to its military prestige.

                                          {{p|Athens}}, by contrast,
Developed in the opposite direction. {{p|Athens}} in the {{p|Archaic}} period
Was governed by aristocratic families—the {{p|Eupatridae}}—but a
Series of reforms gradually democratized the political system.

{{c|Solon}}, around five-ninety-four BCE, enacted sweeping legal reforms:
He cancelled debts that had reduced poor citizens to slavery, he
Opened political participation to all citizens regardless of birth
(Though still ranked by wealth), and he laid the foundation for

Later democratic development. After {{c|Solon}}, {{p|Athens}} underwent a
Period of tyranny under {{c|Peisistratos}} and his sons (tyranny in the
{{p|Greek}} sense meant one-man rule that bypassed constitutional forms
But was not necessarily cruel—{{c|Peisistratos}} was widely considered

A good ruler). After the overthrow of {{c|Hippias}}, the last of the
{{c|Peisistratid}} tyrants, in five-oh-eight BCE, {{c|Cleisthenes}} instituted
Radical democratic reforms: he reorganized the citizen body into
Ten new tribes based on geographical deme membership (breaking the

Power of aristocratic kinship networks), he established the
{{c|Boule}} (council of five hundred citizens selected by lot) as the
Principal executive body, and he gave the {{p|Ecclesia}} (the citizen
Assembly) supreme legislative power. With these reforms, {{p|Athens}}

Became the first substantial democracy in human history. "The
People" ({{p|demos}}) ruled ({{p|kratos}})—hence {{p|demokratia}}. Now, to be
Clear, {{p|Athenian}} democracy was limited. Only adult male citizens
Could participate. Women, foreign residents ({{p|metics}}), and slaves

Were excluded. Perhaps fifteen percent of {{p|Athens}}'s total population
Had full political rights. And those fifteen percent participated
Directly—not through elected representatives, but through actual
Attendance at the Assembly and service in executive bodies, often

Selected by lot. This was direct democracy, radically different
From modern representative democracy. It worked because the
Citizen body was small enough (a few thousand) that direct
Participation was feasible, and because citizens had time—afforded

By slave labor and a prosperous economy—to engage in political
Life. It was democracy for a leisured minority, enabled by the
Exploitation of a disenfranchised majority. The contradiction is
Real and has been justly criticized. But even acknowledging that

Contradiction, the {{p|Athenian}} political experiment was unprecedented.
No prior society had given political power so directly to so
Large a fraction of its male population. The consequences for
Cultural and intellectual life were transformative. Because any

Citizen could propose legislation, could serve on juries, could
Speak in the Assembly, the premium on rhetorical skill was enormous.
Education in rhetoric, logic, and persuasion became essential for
Citizens who wanted influence. This created the market for the

{{p|Sophists}}—traveling teachers of rhetoric and wisdom who charged
Fees for instruction. And this in turn set the stage for {{p|Socrates}}
And the philosophical revolution. But that comes in the next
Chapter. Here we are laying the foundations.

                                               {{p|Athens}} under
Democracy became a cultural hothouse. Its fifth century BCE—
Between the {{p|Persian Wars}} at its beginning and the {{p|Peloponnesian War}}
At its end—was one of the most creative periods in human history.

{{p|Athens}} produced the playwrights {{c|Aeschylus}}, {{c|Sophocles}}, {{c|Euripides}},
And {{c|Aristophanes}}—whose tragedies and comedies founded the
{{p|Western}} dramatic tradition. It produced the historians {{c|Herodotus}}
And {{c|Thucydides}}—who invented history as a literary and analytical

Genre. It produced the sculptor {{c|Phidias}}, whose statues of {{c|Athena}}
And {{c|Zeus}} were the most celebrated of antiquity. It produced the
Architect {{c|Ictinus}}, whose {{p|Parthenon}} atop the {{p|Acropolis}} remains
One of the most influential buildings ever designed. It produced

The politician {{c|Pericles}}, whose leadership and oratory defined
The golden age. And it produced {{c|Socrates}}, whose radically
Different philosophical method would reshape all subsequent
Western thought. All within a single city of perhaps fifty

Thousand adult male citizens. All within roughly a century. The
Cultural output per capita is staggering. Nothing comparable had
Ever happened before, and few comparable cultural efflorescences
Have happened since. And it all depended on the democratic

Political structure that gave ordinary citizens the opportunity,
And the obligation, to think and speak and contribute.

                                                          But
We are getting ahead of ourselves. Before the fifth-century golden

Age came the sixth-century preparation. In the sixth century BCE,
{{p|Greek}} philosophy began. It began not in {{p|Athens}} but in {{p|Ionia}}—
The {{p|Greek}}-speaking cities of the western {{p|Anatolian}} coast,
Including {{p|Miletus}}, {{p|Ephesus}}, {{p|Samos}}, and others. The {{p|Ionians}} were

At the eastern frontier of the {{p|Greek}} world, in close contact
With the older civilizations of {{p|Lydia}}, {{p|Phrygia}}, {{p|Mesopotamia}}, and
{{p|Egypt}}. They absorbed mathematical, astronomical, and religious
Ideas from those civilizations. And they began to ask new kinds

Of questions. {{c|Thales of Miletus}}, traditionally dated to the early
Sixth century BCE, is commonly identified as the first philosopher.
He asked: what is everything made of? And he gave an answer:
Water. All things, he proposed, are ultimately water in different

Forms. The specific claim is less important than the kind of
Question and the kind of answer. {{c|Thales}} was not asking "what
Gods created the world?" (the mythological question). He was
Asking "what is the fundamental substance of the world?" (the

Philosophical question). And he was answering it not by citing
Divine authority or ancient tradition but by reasoning about
Observed nature. Water becomes ice. Water becomes steam. Water
Supports life. Water is found everywhere. Perhaps, he reasoned,

Water is the primary substance, and other apparent substances
Are just transformations of it. This reasoning was rough by
Modern standards. But it was a new kind of reasoning. It was
Reasoning from observation toward general principles, seeking

Naturalistic explanations for observed phenomena. {{c|Thales}} founded
What would later be called the {{p|Milesian School}}—a tradition of
{{p|Ionian}} natural philosophy that included {{c|Anaximander}} (who
Proposed that the primary principle was not water but an

Unbounded and indeterminate stuff, the {{p|apeiron}}), {{c|Anaximenes}}
(Who proposed air as the primary substance), and later thinkers
Who variously proposed fire, number, being itself, atoms. These
Thinkers are called the {{p|Pre-Socratics}}, and their work between

The sixth and fifth centuries BCE laid the foundation for all
Subsequent {{p|Western}} philosophy. They asked: what is the nature
Of reality? What is it made of? How does it change? Is change
Real or illusory? Is the world one or many? What is the soul?

What is knowledge? What is the good? These questions have not
Been answered definitively in two and a half millennia. But the
Asking of them—in the form of rational argument accountable to
Observation and logic—began in {{p|Ionia}} in the sixth century BCE.

{{c|Pythagoras of Samos}}, a contemporary of the later {{p|Milesians}},
Emigrated to {{p|Croton}} in southern {{p|Italy}} and founded a semi-
Religious, semi-philosophical community there. {{p|Pythagorean}}
Teachings emphasized the fundamental role of number in reality.

{{c|Pythagoras}} is credited (probably anachronistically) with the
{{p|Pythagorean theorem}}, with the discovery of musical ratios, and
With the concept that mathematics expresses the deep structure
Of the cosmos. The {{p|Pythagoreans}} also taught the transmigration

Of souls (a doctrine that may have entered {{p|Greek}} thought from
{{p|Indian}} or {{p|Egyptian}} sources or may have developed independently).
Their legacy would shape {{p|Plato}}'s philosophy profoundly. The
Idea that the ultimate structure of reality is mathematical—an

Idea so central to modern physics—traces its Western origin to
The {{p|Pythagoreans}}. {{c|Heraclitus of Ephesus}}, in the early fifth
Century BCE, developed a philosophy of flux and opposites. "One
Cannot step into the same river twice," he famously said. All

Things flow; stability is illusion; reality is the tension of
Opposites. {{c|Parmenides of Elea}}, working roughly contemporaneously
In southern {{p|Italy}}, developed exactly the opposite view: change
Is impossible, being is eternal and unchanging, what appears to

Change is mere illusion. These two opposed positions—the {{c|Heraclitean}}
And the {{c|Parmenidean}}—frame a dialectic that still structures
{{p|Western}} philosophy. Every metaphysical debate about the nature
Of change, of identity, of permanence, of becoming, traces back

To these {{p|pre-Socratic}} arguments. And meanwhile, while these
Philosophers were working out the structure of reality, the
Historians were inventing the investigation of human affairs.
{{c|Herodotus of Halicarnassus}} traveled across the {{p|Mediterranean}}

World collecting stories, customs, and accounts. His {{p|Histories}}—
The first prose historical work in the {{p|Greek}} tradition—covers
The {{p|Persian Wars}} but also includes ethnographic accounts of
{{p|Egypt}}, {{p|Persia}}, {{p|Scythia}}, {{p|Babylon}}. He is curious, discursive,

Often credulous, but brilliantly humane. He treats non-{{p|Greek}}
Cultures with more respect than most later {{p|Greek}} writers. He
Preserves information about foreign civilizations that would
Otherwise be lost. He is the father of history in both senses:

The first historian and the origin of much {{p|Greek}} knowledge
About foreign peoples. {{c|Thucydides}} of {{p|Athens}}, coming a generation
Later, wrote a different kind of history. His {{p|History of the
Peloponnesian War}} is analytical, pitiless, and deeply concerned

With causation, motive, and the underlying dynamics of political
Power. He invented the technique of putting speeches into the
Mouths of historical actors—speeches that render the thinking
Of political leaders with a depth that bald narrative cannot

Achieve. {{c|Thucydides}} is the ancestor of hard-nosed political
Realism in historical writing. His account of the {{p|Melian Dialogue}}—
In which the {{p|Athenians}} justify their destruction of {{p|Melos}} with
The frigid maxim that "the strong do what they can and the weak

Suffer what they must"—is one of the most uncompromising statements
Of political power ever written. Between {{c|Herodotus}}'s humane
Inclusiveness and {{c|Thucydides}}'s analytical realism, {{p|Greek}}
Historiography staked out its enduring range.

                                                 And all of this—the
{{p|Ionian}} philosophers, the rise of democracy, the first historians,
The tragic and comic drama, the architectural and sculptural
Achievements—all of this happened against the backdrop of the

{{p|Persian Wars}}. In four-ninety BCE, {{c|Darius I}} of {{p|Persia}} sent an
Expeditionary force against {{p|Athens}} in retaliation for {{p|Athenian}}
Support of the {{p|Ionian}} revolt. The {{p|Persians}} were defeated at the
{{p|Battle of Marathon}} by an {{p|Athenian}}-{{p|Plataean}} coalition under the

Generalship of {{c|Miltiades}}. Ten years later, {{c|Xerxes I}} led a far
Larger invasion—perhaps the largest military operation the world
Had yet seen. His army crossed the {{p|Hellespont}} on a bridge of
Boats. {{p|Leonidas}} and his three hundred {{p|Spartans}}, along with

Several thousand allies, delayed him at {{p|Thermopylae}}. {{p|Athens}}
Was burned. But at the naval {{p|Battle of Salamis}} (four-eighty BCE),
The {{p|Greek}} fleet under {{c|Themistocles}} defeated the {{p|Persian}} navy.
The following year, at the {{p|Battle of Plataea}}, the combined {{p|Greek}}

Land forces defeated the remaining {{p|Persian}} army. {{c|Xerxes}}
Withdrew. The {{p|Persian}} threat was defeated. The {{p|Greeks}}—by which
We mean the shifting coalition of city-states that happened to
Cooperate for this crisis—had preserved their independence from

The greatest empire on earth. This victory had enormous
Psychological impact. The {{p|Greeks}} believed, not without reason,
That they had preserved the possibility of free citizens
Governing themselves against the imperial monarchies of the

{{p|Near East}}. The wars became part of {{p|Greek}} self-definition: we
Are not {{p|Persians}}; we do not bow; we govern ourselves. The
Rhetoric of {{p|Greek}} freedom versus {{p|Persian}} despotism became a
Civilizational touchstone that would shape {{p|Western}} political

Thought for the next two millennia. It was not entirely accurate—

Practices—but it was psychologically real and culturally
Productive. Out of the crisis of the {{p|Persian Wars}}, out of the

Victorious self-understanding that followed, came the classical
Age. And the classical age is what we will explore next.

# Chapter 166: Assyria and Babylon

Before we follow {{p|Greece}} into its golden age, we must turn east
One more time and trace the {{p|Near East}} through the centuries
That followed the {{p|Bronze Age Collapse}}. Because while {{p|Israel}}
Was forming its monarchy and {{p|Persia}} was preparing to become an

Empire, the old {{p|Mesopotamian}} powers—{{p|Assyria}} and {{p|Babylon}}—were
Engaged in a long, alternating struggle for dominance that would
Reshape the {{p|Near East}} and profoundly affect the religious
Development of {{p|Israel}} and the wider world.

                                                     Begin with {{p|Assyria}}.
The {{p|Assyrian}} state, centered on the {{p|Tigris}} river in northern
{{p|Mesopotamia}}, had existed in various forms since the early second
Millennium BCE—the Gaiad has touched on the {{p|Old Assyrian}} and

{{p|Middle Assyrian}} periods. But {{p|Assyria}} reached its greatest
Extent and power in what is called the {{p|Neo-Assyrian Empire}}—
Flourishing roughly from nine-eleven BCE to six-oh-nine BCE, about
Three centuries of ruthless imperial power. The {{p|Neo-Assyrian}} kings

Built the first true empire of the {{p|Iron Age}}. Their method was
Militaristic, methodical, and brutally effective. They developed
Standing professional armies, siege engines, iron weapons in
Massive quantities, and a doctrine of terror: rebellious cities

Were not merely conquered but obliterated, their inhabitants flayed
Alive, impaled on stakes, or deported en masse to distant regions
Where they could not rebuild resistance. The {{p|Assyrian}} royal
Inscriptions boast of these atrocities in matter-of-fact detail:

"I flayed all the chief men, and I covered the walls of the city
With their skins." "I built a pillar over the city gate, and I
Flayed all the chief men... I stuck some on stakes... I cut off
The limbs of the officers... I cut off the hands and feet of

Some, and I cut off the ears and noses of others." The Gaiad does
Not dwell on these horrors gratuitously. But they are part of the
Historical record, and they affected everyone who lived in the
{{p|Near East}} during the {{p|Neo-Assyrian}} period. The threat of

{{p|Assyrian}} conquest was existential and total. Peoples prayed in
Genuine terror at the approach of {{p|Assyrian}} armies. Their fear
Is recorded in {{p|Hebrew}} scripture, in {{p|Egyptian}} inscriptions, in
{{p|Elamite}} and {{p|Babylonian}} records. {{p|Assyria}} was the boot on the

{{p|Near East}}'s neck for three centuries.

                                              And the {{p|Neo-Assyrian}}
Kings were builders as well as destroyers. They constructed new
Capitals—{{p|Nimrud}}, {{p|Dur-Sharrukin}} (near modern {{p|Khorsabad}}),

Eventually {{p|Nineveh}}—with massive palaces decorated by narrative
Bas-reliefs depicting royal lion hunts, military campaigns, and
Court ceremonies. Many of these reliefs are preserved in the
{{p|British Museum}} and elsewhere and are among the finest examples

Of ancient {{p|Near Eastern}} art. The {{p|Assyrian}} kings collected
Libraries: {{c|Ashurbanipal}}'s library at {{p|Nineveh}}, with its tens of
Thousands of cuneiform tablets, preserved an enormous corpus of
{{p|Sumerian}}, {{p|Akkadian}}, and earlier {{p|Babylonian}} literature that

Would otherwise have been lost. It is thanks to this library that
We have the complete text of the {{p|Epic of Gilgamesh}}, recovered
From its ashes by nineteenth-century archaeologists. The
{{p|Neo-Assyrian}} kings conducted their terror campaigns with one

Hand and their cultural patronage with the other. The two
Activities were not, to them, contradictory.

                                                 The sequence of
{{p|Neo-Assyrian}} kings is worth marking. {{c|Ashurnasirpal II}}

(Eight-eighty-three to eight-fifty-nine BCE) consolidated the
Empire and moved the capital to {{p|Nimrud}}. {{c|Shalmaneser III}}
(Eight-fifty-nine to eight-twenty-four BCE) pushed {{p|Assyrian}}
Power westward and fought a major but indecisive battle at

{{p|Qarqar}} against a coalition including the {{p|Israelite}} king
{{c|Ahab}}—this is the first recorded {{p|Assyrian}} contact with
{{p|Israel}}. {{c|Tiglath-Pileser III}} (seven-forty-five to seven-twenty-seven
BCE) introduced military and administrative reforms that made

{{p|Assyria}} unstoppable: a permanent professional army, standardized
Provincial administration, systematic deportation of conquered
Peoples. {{c|Sargon II}} (seven-twenty-two to seven-oh-five BCE)
Completed the conquest of {{p|Israel}} (the northern kingdom), which

Fell in seven-twenty-two BCE. The ten tribes of the north were
Deported to various corners of the {{p|Assyrian}} empire and
Disappeared from history—becoming the "Lost Tribes of {{p|Israel}}"
About which so much later speculation has been generated. Foreign

Populations were imported into the former {{p|Israelite}} territory;
These settlers mixed with remaining {{p|Israelites}} to form the
{{p|Samaritans}}, who would play such an ambiguous role in later
{{p|Jewish}} history. Only the southern kingdom of {{p|Judah}} survived,

And even it survived only as a vassal of {{p|Assyria}}. {{c|Sennacherib}}
(Seven-oh-five to six-eighty-one BCE) invaded {{p|Judah}} in seven-oh-one
BCE, devastated forty-six cities, and besieged {{p|Jerusalem}}, where
{{c|King Hezekiah}} desperately held out. The {{p|Jerusalem}} siege was

Famously lifted, according to the {{p|Hebrew Bible}}, when an "angel of
{{c|Yahweh}}" killed a hundred and eighty-five thousand {{p|Assyrian}}
Soldiers in a single night—a miraculous deliverance celebrated
In {{p|Isaiah}} and {{p|2 Kings}}. The {{p|Assyrian}} records, predictably, do not

Mention this disaster, but the fact that {{c|Sennacherib}} did indeed
Withdraw from {{p|Jerusalem}} without taking it is attested. What really
Happened—plague, political urgency at home, strategic recalculation—
Is unclear. But {{p|Jerusalem}} survived. {{p|Judah}} survived. And the

Memory of this deliverance shaped {{p|Judean}} religious imagination,
Reinforcing the belief that {{c|Yahweh}} protected his holy city, that
Even the mighty {{p|Assyrian}} army could not take what {{c|Yahweh}} had
Set apart. {{c|Esarhaddon}} (six-eighty-one to six-sixty-nine BCE)

Completed the unfinished business of his predecessors by conquering
{{p|Egypt}} itself—an astonishing {{p|Assyrian}} triumph, though {{p|Egyptian}}
Rule would be briefly restored after his death. {{c|Ashurbanipal}}
(Six-sixty-eight to six-twenty-seven BCE) was the last great

{{p|Assyrian}} king. He ruled the empire at its maximum extent, from
{{p|Egypt}} to the {{p|Zagros Mountains}}. He was intellectually cultivated,
Collected the great library at {{p|Nineveh}}, and conducted complex
Diplomatic and military operations across his vast realm. But

Even under {{c|Ashurbanipal}} the empire was strained. A civil war
Against his brother {{c|Shamash-shum-ukin}} (who ruled as {{p|Assyrian}}
Viceroy in {{p|Babylon}} but rebelled) exhausted {{p|Assyrian}} resources.
After {{c|Ashurbanipal}}'s death, the empire collapsed with stunning

Speed. Within twenty years of his passing, every major {{p|Assyrian}}
City had been destroyed and the empire had ceased to exist.

                                                                The
Agents of {{p|Assyria}}'s destruction were the {{p|Medes}} and the {{p|Neo-Babylonians}}.

The {{p|Medes}}, an {{p|Iranian}} people from the {{p|Zagros}} mountains (the
Predecessors of the {{p|Persians}} in {{p|Iranian}} political development),
Were the {{p|Assyrians}}' northern rivals. The {{p|Neo-Babylonians}}, under
{{c|Nabopolassar}} and then his son {{c|Nebuchadnezzar II}}, had rebelled

Against {{p|Assyrian}} control of {{p|Babylon}} and established an
Independent kingdom. In six-twelve BCE, a combined {{p|Mede}}-{{p|Babylonian}}
Army assaulted {{p|Nineveh}} and destroyed it. The city burned. Its
Population was massacred. The great library was buried in the

Ashes (where it would remain until its rediscovery in the nineteenth
Century CE). {{c|Sinsharishkun}}, the {{p|Assyrian}} king, perished in the
Flames. {{p|Assyrian}} imperial power ended abruptly. A few remnant
{{p|Assyrian}} forces tried to regroup at {{p|Harran}}, but they were defeated

In six-oh-nine BCE. The {{p|Neo-Assyrian Empire}} was finished. And
Consider what this meant. A state that had terrorized the {{p|Near East}}
For three centuries vanished within a single generation. The
{{p|Hebrew}} prophet {{c|Nahum}} celebrated the fall of {{p|Nineveh}} in ecstatic

Verse ({{p|Book of Nahum}}, probably composed very shortly after the
Event): "Woe to the bloody city! It is all full of lies and
Robbery... Nineveh is laid waste; who will bemoan her?" Every
Subject people of the {{p|Assyrian}} empire shared this sentiment.

{{p|Assyria}} had been an entity that stood for imperial cruelty on
A cosmic scale, and its destruction was felt as a moral
Vindication by all who had suffered under it. Including the
Surviving {{p|Judeans}} who now watched their former oppressor fall.

And in {{p|Assyria}}'s place rose {{p|Babylon}}. The {{p|Neo-Babylonian Empire}},
Under {{c|Nebuchadnezzar II}} (six-oh-five to five-sixty-two BCE),
Inherited most of the former {{p|Assyrian}} territory, including the
{{p|Levant}}. {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}} was one of the great figures of the

Ancient {{p|Near East}}. He defeated the {{p|Egyptian}} pharaoh {{c|Necho II}}
At the {{p|Battle of Carchemish}} in six-oh-five BCE, establishing
{{p|Babylonian}} supremacy over {{p|Syria}} and {{p|Palestine}}. He rebuilt
{{p|Babylon}} into a wonder of the ancient world. The city's walls

Were legendarily massive—the {{p|Ishtar Gate}}, with its glazed
Blue tiles and gold-decorated animal reliefs, is now partially
Reconstructed in the {{p|Pergamon Museum}} in {{p|Berlin}} and remains
Visually stunning. The {{p|Hanging Gardens of Babylon}}—one of the

Seven wonders of the ancient world, though their historicity is
Disputed—were attributed to {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}}'s construction for
His {{p|Median}} queen {{c|Amytis}} who missed her mountainous homeland.
{{p|Babylon}} was enormous: perhaps two hundred thousand inhabitants,

With monumental architecture on a scale matched at the time only
By certain {{p|Egyptian}} and {{p|Chinese}} centers. And {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}}
Inherited {{p|Babylon}}'s ancient intellectual traditions. The
{{p|Neo-Babylonians}} refined {{p|Mesopotamian}} astronomy to an astonishing

Degree. They kept detailed records of celestial phenomena over
Centuries, developed mathematical models of planetary motion,
Invented the zodiac, and developed the first accurate lunar
Calendar. {{p|Babylonian}} astronomy would be inherited by the {{p|Greeks}}

(Particularly through {{c|Hipparchus}} and later {{c|Ptolemy}}) and become
The foundation of all {{p|Western}} astronomy until {{c|Copernicus}}. The
Names of the zodiac signs—{{c|Aries}}, {{c|Taurus}}, {{c|Gemini}}, and so on—
Are {{p|Babylonian}} in origin, translated through {{p|Greek}} and {{p|Latin}}.

The seven-day week with days named after the seven visible
Celestial bodies ({{p|Sun}}, {{p|Moon}}, {{p|Mars}}, {{p|Mercury}}, {{p|Jupiter}},
{{p|Venus}}, {{p|Saturn}}) originated in {{p|Babylonian}} astronomical religion.
The division of the circle into three hundred sixty degrees, of

The hour into sixty minutes, of the minute into sixty seconds—
All of this is the heritage of {{p|Babylonian}} mathematics. Every
Time we check the time, we are using a time-keeping system inherited
From {{p|Neo-Babylonian}} astronomer-priests working in the sixth

Century BCE. It is a remarkably persistent cultural gift.

                                                             But
For the {{p|Judean}} population, {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}} would be remembered
For another reason entirely. In five-ninety-seven BCE, {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}}

Besieged {{p|Jerusalem}} because its king {{c|Jehoiakim}} had rebelled
Against {{p|Babylonian}} suzerainty. The city surrendered. {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}}
Deported the {{p|Judean}} king (now {{c|Jehoiachin}}, {{c|Jehoiakim}}'s successor),
The royal family, the nobility, and the skilled artisans to

{{p|Babylon}}. This is the first deportation. Ten years later, in
Five-eighty-seven BCE, {{p|Judah}} rebelled again under the new puppet
King {{c|Zedekiah}}. This time {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}} was less lenient. His
Army besieged {{p|Jerusalem}} for eighteen months. When the city fell,

It was sacked systematically. The temple of {{c|Solomon}}—the central
Religious institution of {{p|Judahite}} faith, built four centuries
Earlier by the third king of {{p|Israel}}—was destroyed. Its gold
And bronze fittings were carried back to {{p|Babylon}} as spoils.

The royal family was killed or blinded. {{c|Zedekiah}}'s sons were
Slain before his eyes, then his eyes were put out, and he was
Led in chains to {{p|Babylon}}. The remaining population of {{p|Jerusalem}}
Was deported. This is the second deportation, the great {{p|Babylonian}}

Exile. {{p|Judah}} was ended as a political entity. Its royal dynasty
(The line of {{c|David}}) was broken. Its temple was gone. Its people
Were scattered—some in {{p|Babylon}}, some fleeing to {{p|Egypt}}, some
Remaining in the devastated homeland as peasants under {{p|Babylonian}}

Administration. By every conventional measure of ancient {{p|Near Eastern}}
History, this should have been the end of {{p|Judahite}} civilization.
It should have followed the pattern of the northern kingdom of
{{p|Israel}} a century and a half earlier: deported, scattered,

Assimilated, lost to history. But it did not. {{p|Judah}}—unlike most
Of the peoples {{p|Assyria}} and {{p|Babylon}} had deported—managed to
Preserve its religious and cultural identity through the exile.
This preservation is one of the most remarkable cultural

Phenomena in ancient history. How did it happen? Partly because
{{c|Nebuchadnezzar}}'s deportation policies, unlike {{p|Assyria}}'s, did not
Attempt to disperse deported populations. The {{p|Judean}} exiles
Were settled together in {{p|Babylonian}} territory, maintaining their

Community. Partly because they had, in their scripture, a religious
Foundation that did not depend on a specific temple or land. The
{{p|Torah}}—or the early sources that would become the {{p|Torah}}—taught
Them that they had a special covenant with {{c|Yahweh}} regardless of

Their political fortunes. Partly because the shock of exile
Prompted intense religious reflection. The prophets of the exile—
{{c|Ezekiel}}, {{p|Second Isaiah}}, {{c|Jeremiah}} (who had warned them of the
Exile before it happened)—reframed the disaster as divine

Discipline rather than divine abandonment. {{c|Yahweh}} had not been
Defeated by {{c|Marduk}}, the {{p|Babylonian}} god. {{c|Yahweh}} had allowed the
Exile because {{p|Judah}} had strayed from the covenant, and {{c|Yahweh}}
Would eventually restore them. This theological reframing kept

The community coherent. And partly because during the exile the
{{p|Judean}} religious scholars began the massive project of editing
And compiling their scriptures. Much of what we now call the
{{p|Hebrew Bible}} was edited into its enduring form during or shortly

After the {{p|Babylonian}} exile. The {{p|Pentateuch}}, the {{p|Deuteronomic}}
History, the prophetic books—all received their definitive
Editorial shape during this period. The exile was the furnace in
Which {{p|Judaism}} as a text-centered religion was forged. Rather

Than being destroyed by the exile, {{p|Judahite}} religion was
Transformed by it. When the opportunity to return came—after
{{c|Cyrus the Great}} of {{p|Persia}} conquered {{p|Babylon}} in five-thirty-nine
BCE and issued his decree permitting the exiles to return—what

Returned was no longer the old {{p|Judahite}} religion. It was something
New: {{p|Second Temple Judaism}}, a religion of text and law and
Synagogue as much as of temple and sacrifice, a religion that
Could survive anywhere its people happened to live, a religion

Equipped to weather further catastrophes (as it would need to
Do). The {{p|Babylonian}} exile was the crucible. It forged the
Template of diasporic religion that would shape {{p|Judaism}} for
Millennia and influence every subsequent religion of the Book.

And as for {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}}'s own fate, he died in five-sixty-two
BCE. His successors were weak. The empire declined rapidly. In
Five-thirty-nine BCE, {{c|Cyrus the Great}} of {{p|Persia}} marched on
{{p|Babylon}}. The city fell, perhaps without a siege—some accounts

Suggest the {{p|Babylonian}} population welcomed {{c|Cyrus}} as a liberator
From the unpopular last king {{c|Nabonidus}}. {{p|Babylon}} became a
{{p|Persian}} province. And thus the two great {{p|Mesopotamian}} powers—
{{p|Assyria}} and {{p|Babylon}}—ended. First {{p|Assyria}} destroyed, then

{{p|Babylon}} conquered. The {{p|Near East}} entered a new phase: the
Age of the {{p|Persian}} empire, which would rule from the {{p|Indus}}
To the {{p|Aegean}} for the next two centuries.

                                                     {{p|Assyria}}.
{{p|Babylon}}. The three centuries of {{p|Neo-Assyrian}} terror. The deportation
Of the northern tribes. The destruction of {{p|Nineveh}} in six-twelve.
The brief {{p|Neo-Babylonian}} supremacy. {{c|Nebuchadnezzar}} rebuilding
{{p|Babylon}} with his blue-glazed gates and fabled gardens.

The {{p|Jerusalem}} exile of five-eighty-seven. The forging of
Diasporic {{p|Judaism}} in the furnace. {{c|Cyrus}} of {{p|Persia}} as liberator.
The {{p|Judean}} return and the building of the {{p|Second Temple}}.

The zodiac and the seven-day week, inheritances of {{p|Babylonian}}
Astronomer-priests. The library of {{c|Ashurbanipal}}, preserving
{{p|Gilgamesh}} in its ashes. The template of text-centered religion.

# Chapter 167: The Babylonian Exile and the Birth of Monotheism

The previous chapter told the story of the {{p|Babylonian}} exile as
Political and cultural catastrophe. This chapter tells it as
Theological crucible. Because what emerged from the exile was
Something new in the religious imagination of humanity: genuine,

Full-throated monotheism.

                            To understand this, we must be
Careful about terms. The Gaiad has argued, in earlier chapters,
That true monotheism is historically rare—rarer than popular

Accounts suggest. The Bronze Age world was populated by
Polytheisms. The Axial Age produced dualisms ({{p|Zoroastrianism}}),
Monisms ({{p|Upanishadic}} philosophy), and various forms of divine
Hierarchy. True monotheism—the claim that there is only one god,

That no other gods exist even as subordinate or defeated beings—
Is distinctive. And the claim that emerged from {{p|Babylonian}}-exile
{{p|Judaism}} is specifically this: not merely that {{c|Yahweh}} is the
Greatest god, nor that {{c|Yahweh}} is the only god worth worshipping,

But that {{c|Yahweh}} is literally the only god—that all other "gods"
Are delusions, wood and stone, nothing. This is a radical claim.
It is not present in the earliest layers of the {{p|Hebrew Bible}}.
It emerges in the later layers, especially during and after the

Exile. Consider the evidence. Early biblical texts speak of {{c|Yahweh}}
As greater than other gods, as chief among them, as jealous of
{{p|Israelite}} loyalty, but they acknowledge that other gods exist.
"Who is like you, O {{c|Yahweh}}, among the gods?" asks {{p|Exodus}} 15:11.

This is henotheism: the worship of one god while acknowledging
Others. The first commandment itself—"You shall have no other
Gods before me"—presupposes that there are other gods; the
Command is exclusivity of worship, not denial of existence. Early

Prophetic and historical texts in the {{p|Hebrew Bible}} operate
Within this henotheistic framework. But the great prophets of
The exile period—especially {{p|Second Isaiah}} (the anonymous prophet
Whose oracles are preserved in chapters forty through fifty-five

Of the {{p|Book of Isaiah}}, composed during the {{p|Babylonian}} exile)—
Move beyond henotheism to explicit monotheism. "I am {{c|Yahweh}},
And there is no other; besides me there is no god" ({{p|Isaiah}} 45:5).
"I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god"

({{p|Isaiah}} 44:6). "The idols of the nations are silver and gold,
The work of human hands; they have mouths, but do not speak; they
Have eyes, but do not see" ({{p|Psalm}} 115:4-5). This is not henotheism.
This is a new claim: that only {{c|Yahweh}} is real, that other gods

Are illusions, that worship of them is folly. The theological
Stakes of this claim are enormous. If only {{c|Yahweh}} is real, then
{{c|Yahweh}} is not only {{p|Israel}}'s god but the god of all nations
(Even if they do not know it). If only {{c|Yahweh}} is real, then

{{c|Yahweh}} controls history universally, not just {{p|Israel}}'s portion.
If only {{c|Yahweh}} is real, then the cosmic moral structure applies
To all humanity. And if only {{c|Yahweh}} is real, then the religious
Diversity of the nations is not a competition among different

Deities but a universal confusion about the one true god. The
Missional implications of this theology are profound—universal
Monotheism tends toward universal religion. But the first
Articulation of this monotheism was defensive, not missionary.

It emerged as the {{p|Judahite}} response to crushing defeat.

                                                                How
Did the exile produce this theological leap? The Gaiad proposes
Several interacting factors. First, the experience of exile itself.

Ancient religions were typically territorial: each god had a land,
A people, a specific place of dwelling. To be deported from your
Land was typically to be separated from your god. If {{c|Yahweh}} was
A territorial god of {{p|Judah}}, then the exiles in {{p|Babylon}} had

Left him behind. The exilic community had to choose: either
{{c|Yahweh}} had been defeated by {{c|Marduk}} (and therefore abandoned them),
Or {{c|Yahweh}} had power extending beyond {{p|Judah}}. They chose the
Second option. They developed the theology that {{c|Yahweh}} was

Everywhere—in {{p|Babylon}} as in {{p|Jerusalem}}, in {{p|Ur}} as in {{p|Bethel}}.
This is deterritorialization, and it is a major theological
Development. Once you claim that your god is not tied to a
Specific land, you are already moving toward universalism. Second,

The experience of living in the heart of {{p|Babylonian}} religion
Forced confrontation with the question of divine power. The
{{p|Babylonians}} worshipped {{c|Marduk}}, {{c|Nabu}}, {{c|Ishtar}}, and other deities
With massive institutional structures—temples, priesthoods,

Festivals, elaborate cosmogonies. The exiles saw {{p|Babylonian}}
Religion up close, in all its grandeur. They could have assimilated.
They could have decided their god was inferior to {{c|Marduk}} and
Converted. Some probably did—the silent drift of assimilation

Is a normal feature of exile populations. But those who remained
{{p|Judean}} had to articulate why they should. And the most powerful
Articulation turned out to be: the {{p|Babylonian}} gods are illusions,
Stone and wood, incapable of the real power that only {{c|Yahweh}}

Possesses. This denigration of rival gods sharpened into
Metaphysical denial of their existence. Not merely inferior, but
Unreal. Third, the {{p|Zoroastrian}} influence. The Gaiad has already
Discussed how {{p|Zoroastrianism}}'s theological categories influenced

{{p|Jewish}} thinking during this period. The concept of cosmic moral
Dualism, of a supreme good creator, of a final judgment—these
{{p|Persian}} ideas entered {{p|Jewish}} thought in the sixth and fifth
Centuries BCE. And the {{p|Persian}} conception of {{c|Ahura Mazda}} as the

Universal creator was, despite {{p|Zoroastrian}} dualism, closer to
Monotheism than was {{p|Babylonian}} polytheism. The exilic {{p|Jews}}
Were influenced, absorbing and transforming {{p|Persian}} theological
Ideas. The relationship between {{p|Zoroastrianism}} and emerging

{{p|Jewish}} monotheism is complex—probably mutual influence rather
Than one-way transmission—but there is no doubt the two traditions
Cross-pollinated during the exile and post-exile period. Fourth,
The editorial project of the exile. With the temple destroyed,

The monarchy ended, and the priesthood dispersed, {{p|Judean}} religion
Had to find new institutional forms to survive. One of those
Forms was scripture. The exilic and post-exilic scholars undertook
A massive project of compiling, editing, and canonizing the sacred

Texts of {{p|Israel}}. Different written and oral traditions were
Combined. Inconsistencies were smoothed (though often incompletely).
Theological positions were articulated. The {{p|Pentateuch}}—the first
Five books of the {{p|Hebrew Bible}}—took shape in something close to

Its final form during this period. And the editors, influenced
By the monotheizing tendency of the prophets, conformed earlier
Material to the new theological framework wherever they could.
The result is a biblical text that shows traces of earlier

Henotheism alongside later monotheism, creating a document of
Genuine theological evolution. You can see the seams, if you know
Where to look. But the overall direction is toward ever-sharper
Monotheistic formulation. And fifth, the rise of the synagogue.

With the temple destroyed, {{p|Jewish}} religious life had to be
Organized around something else. The synagogue—the gathering
Place where scripture was read, prayers were said, and community
Was maintained—probably originated during the {{p|Babylonian}} exile.

It was not a temple. It did not have sacrifices. It did not require
A priest (though scribes and teachers played leading roles). It
Could be established anywhere there was a minimally sized {{p|Jewish}}
Community. This institutional innovation democratized {{p|Jewish}}

Religious life and made it portable. Diasporic {{p|Jews}} could maintain
Their faith wherever they were. And the theological content of
Synagogue worship—the recitation of {{p|Torah}}, the prayers addressing
{{c|Yahweh}} as universal creator—reinforced the monotheistic framework.

Take these factors together, and you have the conditions for the
Birth of monotheism: deterritorialization, confrontation with rival
Deities in their own heartland, {{p|Persian}} theological influence,
Scriptural canonization, and institutional innovation. All these

Factors converged during the sixth century BCE to produce, from
What had been the henotheistic religion of a small {{p|Near Eastern}}
Kingdom, the first fully monotheistic religion in recorded human
History. And this monotheism, once born, would prove to be one

Of the most consequential theological innovations ever made. It
Would, over the next thousand years, reshape the religious
Landscape of the entire {{p|Mediterranean}} and {{p|Near Eastern}} worlds.
Through its {{p|Christian}} and {{p|Islamic}} extensions, it would reshape

The religious landscape of most of the planet.

                                                Now, a crucial
Clarification. The Gaiad, in its earlier treatments, has been
Critical of monotheism as a theological stance. The Gaiad's own

Theological sympathies are with a reflective polytheism—one that
Honors the plurality of divine manifestations, that resists the
Collapse of cosmic complexity into singular transcendence, that
Preserves the ecological and cultural richness of the pre-

Monotheistic religious imagination. The Gaiad has argued that
Monotheism, when it became triumphalist, led to religious
Persecution, to the destruction of local traditions, to the
Psychological and cosmological impoverishment of religious life.

This critique stands. But the Gaiad also acknowledges that
Monotheism, in its origin, was not primarily a triumphalist
Project. It was a survival strategy. The {{p|Judean}} exiles did not
Invent monotheism to conquer other religions. They invented it

To hold themselves together under conditions that should have
Destroyed them. Their theological radicalism was a response to
Historical crisis, not an aggressive expansionist ideology. The
Later aggressive monotheisms—{{p|Christian}} imperialism, {{p|Islamic}}

Conquest, {{p|Protestant}} mission—represent developments of the
Original insight, and often problematic developments. But the
Original insight itself was a courageous theological act, a
Refusal to accept that divine reality was as fragile as the

Political structures that had supported the earlier religion. And
The Gaiad honors the courage of that act, even while maintaining
Reservations about its later forms.

                                        And consider who took the
Lead in this theological transformation. The exilic and post-exilic
Period produced some of the most remarkable religious personalities
In world history. {{c|Ezekiel}}, the priest-prophet, who saw visions of
{{c|Yahweh}}'s glory in {{p|Babylon}} itself, confirming that {{c|Yahweh}} was

Not bound to {{p|Jerusalem}}. His visions of the chariot-throne of
{{c|Yahweh}} moving across the heavens are among the strangest and
Most influential in religious literature, founding the tradition
Of {{p|Jewish}} mysticism (the {{p|Merkavah}} or chariot mysticism that

Would feed into later {{p|Kabbalah}}). {{c|Jeremiah}}, the weeping prophet,
Who had warned against rebellion before the fall of {{p|Jerusalem}}
And who witnessed its destruction; who was carried down to {{p|Egypt}}
Against his will by the refugees and died there in exile. His

Emotional intensity, his psychological interiority, his sense of
Being called against his will to speak uncomfortable truths—all
These make {{c|Jeremiah}} a prototype of the dissenting religious
Prophet. {{p|Second Isaiah}}, whom we have already mentioned, whose

Anonymous oracles from the exile are among the most exalted
Religious poetry ever composed, whose declarations of {{c|Yahweh}}'s
Sole divinity and whose "servant songs" (with their haunting
Imagery of a suffering servant through whose pain the nations

Will be redeemed) would shape {{p|Christian}} and {{p|Jewish}} messianic
Hope for millennia. {{c|Ezra}}, the scribe-priest who returned from
{{p|Babylon}} and led the post-exilic community in reformulating its
Religion around the {{p|Torah}}, organizing public readings of scripture,

Enforcing marital purity laws, and building the institutional
Structures of post-exilic {{p|Judaism}}. {{c|Nehemiah}}, the court official
Who obtained {{p|Persian}} permission to rebuild {{p|Jerusalem}}'s walls
And then governed the restored community, protecting it from its

Neighbors. These figures, working in succession, built the religion
That would become rabbinic {{p|Judaism}} and, through it, the matrix
From which {{p|Christianity}} and {{p|Islam}} would emerge. They are
Arguably the most influential religious teachers, per capita, of

Any period in human history. All of them active in a small exiled
Or recently-returned population of perhaps fifty thousand people
In the sixth and fifth centuries BCE. The theological productivity
Of that small community is staggering.

                                         And the return itself, when
It came, was complicated. {{c|Cyrus}}'s edict of five-thirty-eight BCE
Permitted the {{p|Judean}} exiles to return to {{p|Jerusalem}} and rebuild
The temple. But not all exiles returned. Many had built lives in

{{p|Babylon}}; some were doing well; some had assimilated. The returnees
Were a minority of the exilic population, and they found a devastated
Country, hostile neighbors (especially the {{p|Samaritans}}, who had
Their own claim to {{c|Yahweh}}-worship), and the challenging task of

Rebuilding. The {{p|Second Temple}} was completed in five-sixteen BCE,
Eighty years after the destruction of the first temple, under the
Leadership of {{c|Zerubbabel}} (a descendant of the {{c|Davidic}} line) and
The high priest {{c|Joshua}}. But the {{p|Second Temple}} was a smaller,

Less glorious structure than {{c|Solomon}}'s original. And the political
Situation of the restored community was limited: they were a
{{p|Persian}} province ({{p|Yehud Medinata}}), governed by a {{p|Persian}}-appointed
High priest. There was no independent {{p|Judean}} king. The {{p|Davidic}}

Dynasty remained a religious memory and a future hope, not a
Current political reality. This tension—between the theological
Claim that {{c|Yahweh}} had chosen the {{c|Davidic}} line eternally and the
Political fact that no {{c|Davidic}} king ruled—would generate messianic

Expectations for centuries. Who would be the promised son of
{{c|David}} who would restore {{p|Israel}}'s sovereignty? Generations waited.
{{p|Jewish}} apocalyptic literature developed in response, envisioning
A future divine intervention that would end the current evil age

And establish a new, {{p|messianic}} age. {{p|Daniel}} (composed probably
In the second century BCE under {{p|Seleucid}} oppression, though set
Fictively in the {{p|Babylonian}} exile) is the great apocalyptic text
Of this tradition. The {{p|Book of Enoch}}, the {{p|Book of Jubilees}}, and

Other pseudepigraphic texts developed the tradition further.
And it was from this apocalyptic milieu that {{p|Christianity}} would
Eventually emerge in the first century CE—as one expression of
A messianic ferment that had been building for six hundred years

Since the exile.

                  The theological heritage of the exile is thus
Astonishing. Genuine monotheism. Deterritorialized religion. The
Rise of scripture as the central religious object. The synagogue

As a portable institution of worship. Messianic and apocalyptic
Hope as structuring features of religious imagination. The
Interiorization of divine-human relationship into covenant, law,
And personal piety. The articulation of a universal cosmic drama

Of sin, exile, and ultimate redemption. All of these conceptual
Innovations emerged from the {{p|Babylonian}} captivity and its
Aftermath. They would become the conceptual furniture of most of
The world's subsequent religious thought. When we read theological

Texts from {{p|Augustine}} to {{p|Rumi}} to {{p|Maimonides}} to {{p|Calvin}}, we
Are reading descendants of the theological breakthrough of the
Sixth-century BCE {{p|Judean}} exiles. Their radicalism under duress
Produced, in the space of a century, a new religious grammar

That would shape the religious imagination of billions of people
Over the next twenty-five centuries.

                                            And again, the Gaiad's
Assessment is complex. The Gaiad honors the exilic communities for

Their courage and creativity. The Gaiad also maintains that
Monotheism, as it developed, created problems that polytheistic
Traditions did not have—problems of exclusivity, of intolerance,
Of the flattening of cosmic complexity. But the Gaiad acknowledges

That monotheism emerged from a specific historical situation in
Which it was the most viable theological response. It was not
Born as imperialist ideology. It was born as survival theology.
The later imperialist uses of monotheism are distortions of an

Originally defensive and preservationist project. The Gaiad
Reads the {{p|Babylonian}} exile as one of the pivotal moments in
The history of religion—as genuinely pivotal as the Axial Age
Breakthroughs happening roughly contemporaneously in {{p|Iran}},

{{p|India}}, {{p|China}}, and {{p|Greece}}. These parallel religious
Transformations all share certain features: the critique of
Sacrificial religion, the articulation of universal ethical
Demands, the interiorization of religious life into personal

Conduct and thought, the development of textual canons. The
Axial Age is a world-historical transformation, not confined to
Any one civilization. And the {{p|Jewish}} monotheism forged in
{{p|Babylonian}} exile is the {{p|Judean}} branch of that global

Transformation—a distinctive branch, with distinctive consequences,
But part of a pattern that characterizes the whole of Eurasian
Religious history in the middle of the first millennium BCE.

                                                                {{p|Babylonian}}
Exile. {{p|Second Isaiah}}. {{c|Ezekiel}}'s chariot vision. {{c|Jeremiah}}'s tears.
The birth of scripture-centered religion. The synagogue as
Portable temple. The theological leap from henotheism to
Monotheism. {{c|Yahweh}} as sole god, not merely greatest. The {{p|Persian}}

Influence. {{c|Cyrus}}'s edict. The return and rebuilding. The
{{p|Second Temple}}. The messianic hope deferred. The apocalyptic
Literature that would flower. The conceptual framework that
Would shape three Abrahamic religions.

The exile as crucible. The crushing defeat that forged a new
Theological imagination. The catastrophe that produced the
Vocabulary of half the world's religions.

# Chapter 168: The Persian Empire

{{c|Cyrus the Great}}. The name reverberates through the ancient world
With a distinctive resonance. A conqueror, yes, but also—almost
Uniquely among ancient conquerors—remembered by the conquered
Themselves with gratitude. The {{p|Jews}} called him {{c|Yahweh}}'s anointed.

The {{p|Babylonians}} welcomed him as liberator. The {{p|Greeks}}, writing
Generations later, portrayed him as a model of just kingship
(Even as their city-states faced {{p|Persian}} expansion). {{c|Cyrus}}'s
Reputation was such that even his successors' failures could not

Erase it. And the empire he founded, the {{p|Achaemenid}} empire, was
The largest the world had yet seen—stretching from the {{p|Indus Valley}}
To the {{p|Aegean}} Sea, from the {{p|Caucasus}} to {{p|Egypt}}'s southern border.
Over thirty million people under a single political authority.

Multiple languages, multiple religions, multiple ecological zones,
Multiple histories. The {{p|Persian}} achievement was to hold all this
Together in a functional imperial system for two hundred years.
How did they do it? That is the subject of this chapter.

{{c|Cyrus}} was born around five-ninety BCE in {{p|Anshan}}, in what is now
Southwestern {{p|Iran}}. His father {{c|Cambyses I}} was king of the
{{p|Persians}}, a tributary of the {{p|Medes}}. His mother, by some accounts,
Was the daughter of {{c|Astyages}}, king of the {{p|Medes}}—making {{c|Cyrus}}

A {{p|Median}} prince through his maternal line. The {{p|Persians}} and
{{p|Medes}} were closely related {{p|Iranian}} peoples with shared language
And culture; in the political geography of the early sixth century
BCE, the {{p|Medes}} dominated. {{c|Cyrus}} inherited his father's throne

Around five-fifty-nine BCE and began a remarkable career of
Conquest. He rebelled against {{p|Median}} overlordship around five-
Fifty-three BCE, and within a few years had defeated {{c|Astyages}}
And absorbed the {{p|Median}} kingdom. He then turned westward against

{{p|Lydia}}. {{c|King Croesus}} of {{p|Lydia}}—proverbial for his wealth, and the
Ruler of {{p|Anatolia}} west of the {{p|Halys River}}—had reportedly
Consulted the {{p|Oracle of Delphi}} about whether to make war on
{{p|Persia}}. The oracle's answer was famously ambiguous: "If you

Cross the {{p|Halys}}, you will destroy a great empire." {{c|Croesus}}
Crossed. The empire destroyed turned out to be his own. {{c|Cyrus}}
Defeated him at {{p|Thymbra}} in five-forty-seven BCE and annexed
{{p|Lydia}}, including the {{p|Greek}}-speaking cities of the {{p|Ionian}} coast.

Then he turned east and consolidated his rule over the {{p|Iranian}}
Plateau and {{p|Central Asia}}. Then, finally, in five-thirty-nine BCE,
He marched on {{p|Babylon}}. As noted in the previous chapter, {{p|Babylon}}
Fell with little resistance—the unpopular {{c|Nabonidus}} was deposed,

The {{p|Babylonian}} priests welcomed {{c|Cyrus}}, and the city was
Incorporated into the {{p|Persian}} empire without destruction. {{c|Cyrus}}
Then issued his famous edict permitting exiled peoples to return
To their homelands and rebuild their temples—an edict which

Benefited the {{p|Jews}} most famously but which was in fact a general
Policy. {{c|Cyrus}} wanted his subjects to be grateful, loyal, and
Stable, and he understood that religious and cultural autonomy
Was cheaper than coercion. The {{p|Cyrus Cylinder}}, a clay cylinder

Inscribed with his proclamation on taking {{p|Babylon}} and now held
In the {{p|British Museum}}, is sometimes called the "first charter of
Human rights"—a slightly overheated description, but the cylinder
Does record an explicit policy of religious tolerance and

Restoration of displaced peoples. For its time, this was remarkable.
{{c|Cyrus}} died in battle around five-thirty BCE, fighting against
The {{p|Massagetae}} in {{p|Central Asia}}. His tomb at {{p|Pasargadae}}—a
Simple stone structure surmounted by a gabled roof—still stands

And is one of the most moving monuments of antiquity. Its
Inscription is said to have read: "O man, I am {{c|Cyrus}} the son of
{{c|Cambyses}}, who founded the empire of the {{p|Persians}} and was king
Of {{p|Asia}}. Grudge me not therefore this monument." Simplicity, even

Humility, inscribed on the tomb of a world conqueror.

                                                          His son
{{c|Cambyses II}} continued the expansion, conquering {{p|Egypt}} in
Five-twenty-five BCE. The last native {{p|Egyptian}} pharaoh, {{c|Psamtik III}},

Was defeated at {{p|Pelusium}} and then captured. {{c|Cambyses}} briefly
Ruled {{p|Egypt}} as pharaoh before dying under unclear circumstances
In five-twenty-two BCE. His death triggered a crisis: {{c|Cambyses}} had
Apparently had his own brother {{c|Bardiya}} secretly killed before the

{{p|Egyptian}} campaign, and an impostor claiming to be {{c|Bardiya}} seized
The throne. This impostor was overthrown by a faction of {{p|Persian}}
Nobles led by {{c|Darius}}, a distant relative of the royal family.
{{c|Darius}} became king and spent the next several years putting down

Rebellions across the empire. His account of these events is
Preserved in the {{p|Behistun Inscription}}—a massive rock relief
Carved high on a cliff in western {{p|Iran}}, written in three
Languages ({{p|Old Persian}}, {{p|Elamite}}, and {{p|Akkadian}}) and featuring

{{c|Darius}} himself triumphing over nine defeated rebels. The
{{p|Behistun Inscription}} was what, in the nineteenth century CE,
Allowed scholars to decipher cuneiform—because its trilingual
Structure functioned like the {{p|Rosetta Stone}} did for {{p|Egyptian}}

Hieroglyphics. Without {{p|Behistun}}, our knowledge of ancient
{{p|Mesopotamia}} and {{p|Persia}} would be vastly poorer. {{c|Darius}}, in
Addition to successfully defending his reign, was a brilliant
Administrator. He reorganized the empire into twenty {{p|satrapies}}

(Provinces), each under a {{p|satrap}} (governor) responsible to the
Central government. He established a standardized tax system,
Weights and measures, a royal postal service with relay stations
(The famous "{{p|royal roads}}" with horse stations every day's ride,

Allowing messages to travel from {{p|Susa}} to {{p|Sardis}} in a week), and
A single imperial currency (the gold {{p|daric}}). He built the great
Ceremonial capital at {{p|Persepolis}}, whose ruins still impress
Visitors with their scale and architectural sophistication. The

{{p|Apadana}} audience hall at {{p|Persepolis}}, with its thirty-six
Massive columns rising over twenty meters, was surrounded by
Reliefs showing delegations from all the empire's peoples
Bringing tribute—{{p|Medes}}, {{p|Elamites}}, {{p|Babylonians}}, {{p|Egyptians}},

{{p|Lydians}}, {{p|Ionians}}, {{p|Indians}}, {{p|Scythians}}, {{p|Arabs}}, and many more.
The imagery was ideological: the {{p|Great King}} ruled many peoples,
And each people brought him its distinctive gifts. Diversity under
A single imperial canopy. The visual rhetoric of {{p|Persian}} rule

Was pluralistic: the king was honored in the distinctive styles
Of each subject culture, and each subject culture retained its
Own traditions under his protection. This was not assimilation
Imperialism. It was federation imperialism. Each people kept its

Language, its religion, its local rulers (often), its cultural
Practices—as long as it paid tribute, provided troops when
Required, and accepted ultimate {{p|Persian}} authority. For most
Subject peoples, {{p|Persian}} rule was much lighter than the rule

Of {{p|Assyria}} or {{p|Babylon}} had been. The {{p|Persians}} did not conduct
Mass deportations. They did not demand religious conformity. They
Taxed regularly and expected loyalty, but they did not terrorize.
And the result was an empire that was, by the standards of the

Time, remarkably stable and prosperous for most of its population.

                                                                      {{c|Darius}}
Extended the empire further, adding the {{p|Indus Valley}} in the east
And {{p|Thrace}} in the west. He attempted to expand into {{p|Greece}} but

Was defeated at {{p|Marathon}} in four-ninety BCE. {{p|Greece}} would remain
Beyond {{p|Persian}} reach, though the empire retained significant
{{p|Greek}}-speaking territories in {{p|Anatolia}} and {{p|Cyprus}}. {{c|Darius}} died
In four-eighty-six BCE, having reigned for thirty-six years and

Having consolidated the largest empire in history to that point.
His son {{c|Xerxes I}} attempted another invasion of {{p|Greece}} in four-
Eighty BCE, which also failed (after initial success) due to the
{{p|Greek}} naval victory at {{p|Salamis}} and land victory at {{p|Plataea}}.

{{c|Xerxes}} is a complicated figure in the historical record—portrayed
In the {{p|Hebrew Bible}} as {{c|Ahasuerus}}, husband of {{c|Esther}} and
Protagonist of that remarkable novella of {{p|Jewish}} court intrigue;
Portrayed by {{c|Herodotus}} as an arrogant tyrant whose hubris

Brought his army to disaster at {{p|Salamis}}; portrayed in {{p|Persian}}
Inscriptions as a mighty king completing his father's constructions
At {{p|Persepolis}}. Each perspective captures something. {{c|Xerxes}} was
The last of the great conquering {{p|Persian}} kings. After him, the

Empire ceased expanding and focused on maintaining what it had.

Subsequent {{p|Achaemenid}} kings—{{c|Artaxerxes I}}, {{c|Darius II}}, {{c|Artaxerxes II}},
{{c|Artaxerxes III}}, {{c|Arses}}, {{c|Darius III}}—mostly managed to hold the
Empire together despite periodic rebellions (especially in {{p|Egypt}},

Which repeatedly revolted and was repeatedly reconquered). Court
Intrigue and succession disputes weakened the central authority
Over time. The empire grew more decadent, more reliant on
Mercenary troops, more dependent on bribery to buy the loyalty of

Its various satraps and subject peoples. By the mid-fourth century
BCE, the {{p|Persian}} Empire was still vast but increasingly hollow.
It would prove vulnerable to the decisive blow that {{c|Alexander}} the
Great would deliver in the following decades—but that is for

A later chapter. For now, we are concerned with the empire as it
Existed during its classical period: vast, diverse, administratively
Sophisticated, and unprecedented in human history for its scale
And its (relative) administrative rationality.

                                                   Consider some
Specific features of {{p|Persian}} rule that have had lasting influence.
The {{p|Royal Road}} that ran from {{p|Susa}} to {{p|Sardis}}—some twenty-five
Hundred kilometers, with postal stations at regular intervals—was

The prototype of imperial road systems that would later include
The {{p|Roman}} {{p|viae}} and the {{p|Inca}} {{p|Qhapaq Ñan}}. The idea that an
Empire should have systematic communication and transport
Infrastructure was not obvious; it had to be invented, and the

{{p|Achaemenids}} invented it on a scale nobody had previously attempted.
The {{p|satrapy}} system was the first mature imperial provincial
Administration. Each {{p|satrapy}} had its {{p|satrap}} (civil governor), its
Military commander (deliberately separated from the {{p|satrap}} to

Prevent consolidation of local power), and its royal secretary
(Who reported directly to the central court on the actions of
The other two). This three-way balance of authority prevented
Any single official from accumulating enough power to rebel. The

{{p|Roman}} and later imperial administrations would borrow variations
On this structure. The standardized coinage, weights, and measures
Facilitated trade and commerce across the empire's vast territory.
Economic integration rose to a level that would not be matched

In the region until the early modern period. Cultural contacts
Flourished. {{p|Greek}} doctors and sculptors worked in {{p|Persepolis}}.
{{p|Phoenician}} sailors worked in the {{p|Persian}} navy. {{p|Jewish}} scribes
Worked in the royal court. {{p|Indian}} traders brought goods from the

{{p|Ganges}} to {{p|Babylonia}}. The empire was a zone of exchange in which
Ideas and goods moved across what had previously been separate
Civilizational spheres. Some scholars have argued that the
{{p|Persian}} Empire was the cradle of the first genuinely global

Economy—the first time that commercial networks linked the entire
{{p|Old World}} in sustained exchange. And the cultural consequences
Were profound. The spread of {{p|Aramaic}} as a lingua franca across
The empire. The dissemination of {{p|Babylonian}} astronomy to {{p|Greeks}}

And {{p|Indians}}. The transmission of {{p|Zoroastrian}} religious concepts
Into {{p|Jewish}} thought (as we have already discussed). The mixing of
Artistic styles—{{p|Persian}} architecture shows influences from {{p|Egypt}},
{{p|Mesopotamia}}, {{p|Ionia}}, and {{p|Central Asia}}, synthesized into its own

Distinctive imperial style. The {{p|Persian}} Empire was not just a
Political unit. It was a cultural matrix through which the
Achievements of multiple civilizations flowed and mixed.

                                                             And
The religious dimension is worth returning to. The {{p|Achaemenid}}
Kings were {{p|Zoroastrian}}—or at least their inscriptions invoke
{{c|Ahura Mazda}} and frame their kingship in {{p|Zoroastrian}} terms.
But they did not impose {{p|Zoroastrianism}} on their subjects. In

{{p|Babylon}}, {{c|Cyrus}} participated in the {{p|Marduk}} festival and presented
Himself as {{c|Marduk}}'s chosen servant. In {{p|Egypt}}, {{c|Cambyses}} and his
Successors styled themselves as pharaohs and patronized {{p|Egyptian}}
Temples. In {{p|Jerusalem}}, {{c|Cyrus}} permitted the rebuilding of the

Temple and supported it with royal funds. The {{p|Persians}} understood
That religious legitimacy in a multi-ethnic empire required
Adopting the religious forms of each subject culture. This was
Not cynicism; it was genuine imperial theology. {{c|Ahura Mazda}} was

Thought to be the universal creator, whose purposes were served
By the continuance of proper worship wherever it occurred, under
Whatever local name. The {{p|Persian}} king, as {{c|Ahura Mazda}}'s agent
In the world, had the duty to support all legitimate cults within

His realm. This theological framework made religious tolerance
Into a positive imperial virtue rather than a pragmatic concession.
And the consequence was that the {{p|Persian}} Empire was, for most
Of its subjects, a religiously comfortable home. It was not

Paradise—there were rebellions, there were failed reconquests,
There were occasional episodes of imperial brutality. But compared
To what had come before, and compared to many empires that would
Follow, the {{p|Persian}} Empire was a relatively humane political

Structure. Its subjects were not, on the whole, eager to be freed
From it. When {{c|Alexander}} conquered the empire in the fourth
Century BCE, he did not find populations widely hostile to their
Existing rulers. He found populations who accepted {{p|Persian}}

Rule as reasonably fair and effective. His conquest had to be
Imposed militarily, not welcomed as liberation. This is itself
A testimony to the quality of {{p|Persian}} governance.

                                                             {{p|Persia}}.
{{p|Achaemenid}}. {{c|Cyrus}}, {{c|Cambyses}}, {{c|Darius}}, {{c|Xerxes}}, and their
Successors. The first genuinely global empire. The {{p|satrapy}} system
And the {{p|Royal Road}}. The standardized coinage and the standardized
Weights. The religious tolerance grounded in imperial theology.

The cultural exchange across the largest territory ever unified.
The transmission of {{p|Zoroastrian}} concepts into {{p|Jewish}} thought
And the transmission of {{p|Babylonian}} astronomy into {{p|Greek}} science.
The foundation on which {{p|Hellenistic}}, {{p|Roman}}, {{p|Parthian}}, {{p|Sassanid}},

And {{p|Islamic}} political traditions would later build.

                                                             The
{{p|Persian}} Empire. The first great imperial synthesis of the
Ancient world. The foundation beneath {{p|Western}} and {{p|Eastern}}

Political imagination alike. The conquering empire that was also
The civilizing empire.

Stand.
