# Chapter 195: Charlemagne and Byzantium

And in the {{p|West}}, while the usurper's hand
Kept the throne empty, the {{c|2660|Franks}} grew, and grew;
{{c|2818|Clovis I}} took up, in the {{x|Nicene}} band,
The faith; the {{c|2649|Goths}} kept {{x|Arian}}, as true.{{fn|Clovis I of the Franks was baptized a Nicene Christian, a Catholic, around the year 500, unlike the Goths, the Vandals and the Burgundians, who were Arians.}}

His heirs, the {{c|2819|Merovingians}}, would then reign
Three hundred years, but weak, and ruled by none;
The {{c|2820|Mayors of the Palace}} were the brain;
And {{c|2821|Charles Martel}}, the {{c|2822|Hammer}}, Mayor, won

At {{c|2823|Tours}}, seven-thirty-two, stopped the march
Of {{x|Umayyads}}, who fell back to {{p|Spain}};
{{c|2824|Pepin the Short}} then asked the {{c|2825|Pope}}: the arch
Of kingship: should the one who ruled now reign?

The {{c|2825|Pope}} said yes; and {{c|2826|Pepin}} took the crown;
{{c|2827|Carolingians}} began; and his son
Was {{c|2677|Charlemagne}}, {{c|2828|Charles the Great}}, whose renown
Would build what {{p|Europe}} had not seen, as one.

{{c|2677|Charlemagne}} reigned forty-six years. He fought
the {{c|2829|Saxons}} for their grove, and in it felled
the Irminsul, and set the priests he brought
at their own altars, where their feasts were held.

Seven-seventy-four: he took the land
of {{p|Italy}} from {{c|2678|Lombards}}; and in {{p|Spain}}
he pressed the {{c|2764|Muslims}} back with his own hand
and held the frontier as his own domain.

He did not break the {{c|2830|Lombards}}; took the crown,
{{c|2831|Rex Langobardorum}};{{fn|Charlemagne conquered the Lombard kingdom in 774 and took the title Rex Langobardorum. Its crown is the one later called the Iron Crown of Lombardy.}} and its crown, too,
Held it for twenty-six years, in renown,
The realm of {{c|2665|Theodoric}}'s heirs, come through.

He brought from {{p|York}} {{c|2832|Alcuin}}, and there, to lead
His school; old learning lived again and rose;
The script was made that we today can read,
And monks would copy old books, and keep those

That would be lost. On {{x|Christmas}}, on the day,
Eight hundred, {{c|2834|Leo III}} crowned his head
{{c|2835|Imperator Romanorum}},{{fn|Pope Leo III crowned Charlemagne in St Peter's on Christmas Day 800. The papacy held the imperial throne to be vacant because a woman, Irene, ruled in Constantinople.}} to stay,
In {{p|St. Peter's}}; and the throne, so long dead

Since {{c|2665|Theodoric}}, filled. The {{c|2825|Pope}} would place
The crown; the claim was not the {{c|2825|Pope}}'s: he held
{{p|Rome}}, {{p|Italy}}, {{p|Gaul}}, {{p|Germany}}, by grace
Of conquest and of law; and he excelled,

{{c|2678|Lombards}}' heir, who were {{c|2665|Theodoric}}'s own;
And this enraged {{p|Constantinople}}, where
{{c|2836|Irene}} ruled, claimed the {{p|Roman}} throne alone;
The {{c|2838|Byzantines}} would not let a {{c|2837|Frank}} share

The crown: for since {{c|2383|Justinian}}, they'd kept
The {{p|West}} by the usurper's right, no right,
And kept the throne empty, where none had stepped;
No {{c|2837|Frank}} would have the crown they'd seized by might.

{{p|Byzantium}} itself was shaken: then
{{c|2839|Iconoclasm}} rose, seven-twenties,
When {{c|2834|Leo III}}, emperor, bade men
Break holy icons, for all their plenties.

The fight was over who would hold the face:
the throne, that broke the icons in its name,
or monks, who kept them in the holy place
as windows, for a century of flame.

{{p|Nicaea}}, seven-eighty-seven, said
The {{x|Second Council}}: honored, not adored;
It came back, till eight-forty-three, then fled;
And all the icons came back, were restored;

{{x|Triumph of Orthodoxy}}'s feast is kept
In Lent. {{c|2840|Cyril}}, {{c|2841|Methodius}}, then went
To the {{c|2842|Slavs}}, and made letters where they stepped,
The {{c|2843|Glagolitic}}, made with this intent:

That they would pray in their own tongue; and so
{{p|Rus}}, {{p|Moravia}}, {{p|Bulgaria}}'s land,
Prayed not in {{x|Latin}}, {{x|Greek}}; and so would go
The faith in each one's tongue, as they had planned.

{{c|2677|Charlemagne}} died; his grandsons split the whole
At {{c|2845|Verdun}}, eight-forty-three: {{c|2846|West}} and {{c|2847|East}},
{{p|Middle Francia}}, which broke apart; the toll
Of {{c|2848|Vikings}}, seven-ninety-three, released

At {{p|Lindisfarne}}, slew monks, and from the sea,
From {{p|Scandinavia}}, three hundred years,
From {{p|Ireland}} to {{p|Russia}}, they would be
Raiders, then settlers, kings, and turned, by tears.

So honor {{c|2677|Charlemagne}}: the empty throne
Was filled again, eight hundred, at the feast;
The {{c|2678|Lombards}}' heir, {{c|2665|Theodoric}}'s, his own;
The monks' icons, windows, kept in the {{p|East}}.
