# Chapter 138: H, L, T — The Indus Valley

Three houses share the {{p|Indus}}: {{c|1874|H}}, and {{c|1878|L}},
And {{c|1886|T}}; not near in blood, but in one land
They built one people's cities, and they dwell
Together in the valley that they planned.

{{p|Harappa}} and {{p|Mohenjo-daro}} stood,
{{p|Dholavira}}, {{p|Rakhigarhi}}: the stream
Of {{p|Indus}} and {{p|Saraswati}}, the good
Brown water ran, and fed the farmer's dream.

Two thousand years their cities stood and grew,
With forty thousand souls in each, and more;
The streets were laid in grids, and ran straight through,
And faced the four directions, door by door.

The bricks were one in size, the weights were one,
The drains ran hidden under street and lane,
And no one built so well beneath the sun
For three millennia, nor would again.

The houses had their water, and the baths
Were great and public, holy, and were clean;
And on the seals, and on the market paths,
Their writing ran, in signs that all had seen.

They spoke the speech the {{p|Dravidian}} south
Still speaks today, the mother of its tongue;
Their names were spoken by the mother's mouth,
Their songs were sung, as all good songs are sung.

Their god was {{x|Pashupati}}, lord of beasts,
Who sat in yoga, horned, upon the seal;
And he is {{c|1340|Shiva}} now; their bathing feasts,
Their goddess and their lingam, where men kneel.

The {{c|1923|Priest-King}} in his robe of trefoil stars,
The {{c|1924|Dancing Girl}} in bronze, with hand on hip,
The terracotta mothers, and the jars,
The seals of trade upon the merchant ship.

Of India's houses, {{c|1874|H}} is the great,
The largest, {{c|1925|Haplogroup H}}; and his hand
Built {{p|Mohenjo-daro}}'s streets, set the weight,
And lit the lamps, and sealed the goods of land.

And {{c|1878|L}}, of {{c|1926|Haplogroup L}}, is the strong
In the south and west, {{p|Dravidian}} kin;
His sons went to the {{p|Caucasus}} along,
And {{p|Central Asia}}'s steppes they settled in.

And {{c|1886|T}}, of {{c|1927|Haplogroup T}}, took the sea,
The sailor house, whose ships went far and wide
To {{p|Arabia}}, the {{p|Swahili}} quay,
The {{p|Horn of Africa}}, and the far tide.

He joined the {{p|Indus}} to the {{p|Arab}} shore,
And to the {{p|Horn}}, in one long loop of trade,
Before the kings, and many years before
Their realms arose; and so the bond was made.

Then {{p|Saraswati}} dried, and the monsoon
Shifted its rains, and slowly, year by year,
The cities emptied, not at once, but soon
The people scattered, far, and far, and near.

They did not die, but scattered to the plain,
And stayed, and mixed, and in {{p|Karachi}} still,
{{p|Mumbai}} and {{p|Delhi}}, walk their sons again,
Their sons, of {{c|1874|H}} and {{c|1878|L}} and {{c|1886|T}}, who fill

The whole subcontinent from shore to shore;
Their cities sleep, their streets are laid in stone,
Their god sits on the seal, as long before,
And still their blood is in the nation's bone.

So honor {{c|1874|H}}, and {{c|1878|L}}, and {{c|1886|T}}, the three
Who built the {{p|Indus}} cities, grid and bath,
The seal, the weight, the drain, the trade by sea,
And left their sons to walk the long-worn path.
