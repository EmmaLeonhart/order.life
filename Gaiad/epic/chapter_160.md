# Chapter 160: Exodus, Trojan War, Bronze Age Collapse

And then the world of bronze was overthrown:
In fifty years, the palaces and towns
Of all the eastern sea were burned, or flown,
Abandoned; and the kings put down their crowns.

{{p|Hattusa}}, {{p|Hatti}}'s seat, burned to the ground;
And {{p|Ugarit}}, the trading town, was razed;
Its last king wrote for help, and none was found;
His letter in the kiln was left unglazed.

{{p|Mycenae}}, {{p|Tiryns}}, {{p|Pylos}}, {{p|Thebes}}, the same;
{{p|Knossos}} on {{p|Crete}}; and {{p|Troy}}, the town, was done;
{{p|Emar}}, {{p|Alalakh}}, {{p|Kadesh}}, {{p|Gath}}: the flame
Took {{p|Canaan}}'s cities, {{p|Cyprus}}' too, each one.

And {{p|Egypt}} lived, but less, and lost its lands
Abroad; {{p|Assyria}} waned; and the throne
Of {{p|Babylon}} was weak in {{p|Kassite}} hands;
And {{p|Elam}} shook; and few came through alone.

{{p|Linear B}} was lost; the {{p|Hittite}} hand
In wedge-script died with {{p|Hattusa}}'s fall;
The letters that the kings sent, land to land,
Were ended; and the trade was ended, all:

The roads that tied {{p|Mycenae}} to the {{p|Nile}},
To {{p|Ugarit}}, to {{p|Cyprus}}, and away
To far {{p|Sardinia}}: they broke; the while
The people fell, nine-tenths in some, that day.

For five things came at once: the first, the drought,
From twelve hundred fifty, a hundred years,
The worst the age of bronze had known; without
The harvests, famine came, and with it fears.

The second, earthquakes, shook the towns, and brought
The walls down, with the burning, or before;
The third, the peasants, who for grain had wrought
For lords, rose up when there was grain no more.

The fourth, the peoples moving in their waves,
The {{p|Sea Peoples}}, a host of raiders, came,
And {{c|2140|Ramesses III}}, the last who saves
{{p|Egypt}}, fought twice, and won, and carved their name

At {{p|Medinet Habu}}, upon the stone:
The {{p|Shekelesh}}, the {{p|Tjeker}}, {{p|Peleset}},
The {{p|Denyen}}, {{p|Sherden}}, {{p|Weshesh}}, and their own
{{p|Lukka}} from {{p|Lycia}}, in battle met.

The {{p|Sherden}} were {{p|Sardinia}}'s; and those
{{p|Shekelesh}}, {{p|Sicily}}'s; the {{p|Denyen}}, they
Were {{p|Danaoi}}, the {{p|Greeks}}; for when the blows
Fell on {{p|Mycenae}}, they too sailed away,

And raided, settled; and the {{p|Peleset}},
The {{p|Philistines}}, from the {{p|Aegean}} shore,
Took {{p|Canaan}}'s southern coast, and settled, set
Their pots and halls as at their home before.

The fifth: the whole was tied, and each one stood
Upon the rest: the tin, the copper, grain;
{{p|Ugarit}} leaned on {{p|Hatti}}, as it should,
And {{p|Hatti}} on the {{p|Levant}}'s grain, or wane;

So when one fell, the next fell, one by one,
And none could raise the whole; and so it broke,
The greatest falling that was ever done,
Of all the kingdoms, all the palace folk.

And out of it came iron, which was found
In many lands, and could not be held tight;
And out of {{p|Canaan}} came the letters, sound
By sound, the alphabet, that all could write;

And small towns, leagues of tribes, and peoples came:
{{p|Phoenicians}}, {{p|Arameans}}, {{p|Israel}}'s line,
The {{p|Hittite}} towns of {{p|Syria}}, the same;
And {{p|Greeks}} in dark, that waited for their sign.

And in those years did {{c|1006|Moses}} lead the sons
Of {{c|2019|Jacob}} out of {{p|Egypt}}, from the land
Of {{p|Goshen}}, where the king had bound the ones
Who kept their fathers' altars, by his hand.

The plagues came down on {{p|Egypt}}; and they fled;
The sea of reeds, the {{p|Yam Suph}}, parted wide;
They crossed it, and the chariots, instead,
Were drowned behind them, in the closing tide.

At {{p|Sinai}} then the covenant was made;
And forty years they wandered in the waste;
Then {{c|2141|Joshua}} led them in, unafraid,
To {{p|Canaan}}, where the falling towns were placed.

And {{c|2093|Merneptah}}, the son of {{c|2033|Ramesses}}, set
A stone, and wrote that {{p|Israel}} was laid
Low by his hand; and so in {{p|Canaan}} yet
Was {{p|Israel}}, a people, and it stayed.

They were not all who left; for many came
To {{p|Canaan}} in the breaking of the world;
But theirs the story of the house, the name,
Of {{c|2019|Jacob}}'s sons, when their flag was unfurled.

And in those years the {{p|Trojan War}} was fought,
The last great sailing of the {{p|Greeks}}; for when
The war was won, and {{p|Troy}} was burned to naught,
The kings came home, and lost their homes again:

Then {{c|1337|Agamemnon}}'s own {{p|Mycenae}} burned
Within a generation of the war;
{{c|2112|Odysseus}}' isle, where he had returned,
Grew small and dark, and was a king's no more.

So three things happened in the age's end:
The breaking, and the war, the going out;
And from the ruin, kingdoms that would mend
Into small nations, with new gods about.

The god-kings fell, because the house they ruled
Was broken, and no palace held the rite;
And so the rite went out, no longer schooled
By kings, to households, and to those who write.

So honor those who lived when bronze was done:
{{c|2140|Ramesses III}}, who held the sea at bay;
The sons of {{c|2019|Jacob}}, {{c|1006|Moses}}' own, each one;
And {{p|Troy}}, and those who burned and sailed away.
