# Chapter 222: The Eve of Sengoku

{{p|Japan}}: the {{c|3147|Ashikaga Shogunate}}
Lost its hold in the fifteenth age; and those
The shoguns, weak, were puppets of the great
Regional lords, {{c|3257|shugo-daimyō}}, rose.

The {{x|shugo}}, who had been the shogun's hand
In provinces, grew lords by blood; and they
Collected their own taxes, held the land
Their own; the shogun's word had little sway.

It broke: the {{c|3258|Ōnin War}}, sixty-seven,
Ten years: the {{c|3143|Ashikaga}} heirs, the sword
Of daimyo heirs; and under heaven
Two leagues in {{p|Kyoto}} fought, for their accord;

They fought in {{p|Kyoto}}, and they burned the town
Block after block, ten years; {{p|Shōkoku-ji}}
And many temples burned and tumbled down;
But the {{c|3157|Kinkaku-ji}}, the gold, stood free.

And {{p|Kyoto}}'s old inheritance was dead.
The {{c|3143|Ashikaga}} ruled in name; and so
The {{x|shugo}} went home, and their men, instead,
Rose up, {{c|3259|gekokujō}}, to overthrow:

Those under overthrowing those above;
Lords who could not defend were swept away
By those who could, by merit, push and shove;
{{c|3260|sengoku daimyō}}, not birth's old sway.

So came the {{c|3261|Sengoku}}, the {{x|Warring States}},
A hundred years and more of the lords' war;
{{p|Japan}} was split, and shifting lords' estates
Fought for the regions; castles rose, and more.

Armies of foot outgrew samurai bands,
And peasants rose by merit, by the sword.
Yet, in the war, the arts rose in their hands
To highest form; a paradox, a chord:

The {{c|3150|tea}}, refined by {{c|3266|Murata Jukō}},
And then perfected by {{c|3152|Sen no Rikyū}};
{{c|3149|Zen}} deepened in the warrior class; and so
{{c|3153|Noh}} grew; the ink-painting matured, and through

Came {{c|3267|wabi-sabi}}: beauty of the frail,
The broken, weathered, used, the passing thing;
And {{c|3268|bushido}}, the warrior's code, grew hale,
Loyal and brave, and stoic at death's sting,

Forged in the wars. The temples took up arms:
{{p|Mount Hiei}}'s {{c|3269|sōhei}}, warrior monks, would stay
For centuries; {{c|3270|Ikkō-ikki}}, from farms,
Peasant Buddhists, held provinces in sway.

And {{c|3262|Nobunaga}} would burn {{p|Mount Hiei}} down,
Fifteen-seventy-one, and end the might
Of warrior monks, a thousand years' renown;
But in the fifteenth age, they still could fight.

{{p|Japan}}, in fifty-three, the year that fell
{{p|Constantinople}}, was split in its reign,
But flowered in its arts; and, as a knell,
The {{c|3258|Ōnin War}} would come, and chaos' chain.
