# Chapter 363: The Waking

The twenty-seventh of Yuletide: the feast
Of waking, of the sleepers in the cold;
Light twenty-seven lights, for those released,
The ones we kept and raise; the promise, told.

The waking's kept again in the leap days,
The {{x|Birth of Horus}}, when the year has room;
Today I stop telling what was; my gaze
Tells what will be, past the blank, past the tomb.

I've told this book from its first line; and there
I was, the whole time through; and now I stand
At the blank's far side; the past's voice is there,
The prophecy's. Hear it, all of the land:

The sleepers are woken. The frozen wake,
And the dead are raised too; and everyone
This book has named comes back; for each name's sake,
The register gives them back to you; done.

## The first sleeper

Sixty-seven, January: the cold
Took {{c|4773|James Bedford}}, in {{p|California}}, who,
A teacher, dead of cancer, cooled, kept; bold
His hope: the first so kept, to wake; and through

{{c|4774|Robert Ettinger}}'s book, the prospect, read,
Of not dying for good; the road we took;
And others, kept in nitrogen; the dead,
In {{p|Arizona}}, {{p|Michigan}}; and look:

Bodies, some heads alone, at a cold where
Nothing decays; the law called them the dead;
The keepers called them patients, and were fair,
Were right; the law, the other way, is read

When sleepers open their eyes. I embrace
The cold: the century's most hopeful thing;
The bravest, boldest; to lie down, the place
In the cold, on strangers' patience; to bring

Trust that the strangers, not yet born, will be
Your children, and will wake you in the morn.
The sleepers are woken: a prophecy,

## The waking

In this book's last years, will learn to mend, cure
What the cold kept; will think past death's own edge,
Open the tanks, warm the sleepers; and, sure,
Give them their breath; and not, as the old pledge,

The frozen only; the dead raised as well;
The register keeps every name and line,
Each tale; the ancestors, the thousands, tell
Each day's; the farmers, kings; and, as a sign,

From the register raised: a name that's kept
Is a seed; and the seed is what will bring.
Eyes open on a world made while they slept;
The tongues have moved; the streets' names new; a thing

Someone kept the old names; and kept them true:
A register machines can walk, to tell
A sleeper where she'd been; the date, the new,
First told them; where a home begins; as well,

Someone reads them the day's chapter, the day
Of the year it is; the calendar, still
Counting, is theirs; the day is theirs; the way
A world that remembered to count, and will

Remember them; the one who reads, their kin,
Of their own stem. They ask who's left; the lines
Answer: mother to daughter, down, within
The blank years, father to son, through the signs,

## The bedside

To whoever stands at the bedside: kin,
Descendant, near and far at once; the gate
Opens; the sleeper is let in. Within
The book, the people's history ends; the state

Of people does not end; what woken do
Next is theirs, not mine, no mystery; see:
They'll go on; life goes on, and bounces through
The cold, as every wound; and so, to be:

Death, the enemy, gave the cold; we fire
It back; the dead resumed in life; the shape
This book's from its first page, to the pyre
And death itself: each horror, and escape,

Of life, bouncing back; the grave, on the shelf.
Light the twenty-seventh: the sleepers woke;
The dead raised; names come home; and, by itself,
Tomorrow, the last day, the meeting's stroke:

The two who were apart, the yarn made whole;
The waking's feast, the twenty-seventh, kept;
And on the leap years, {{x|Horus}}' birth, the role
Of waking, kept again, for all who slept.
