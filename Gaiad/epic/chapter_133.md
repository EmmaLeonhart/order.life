Of fish, the tide pools, and the bells and bowls

And then. Some seventy-four thousand years
Ago (the earth's own chronicle, again,
Recorded it, as in its rock appears),
{{p|Sumatra}}, by a land-bridge joined, and when

{{p|Toba}}, a mountain there, erupted. Not
A small one, nor the ordinary flow
Of cinder-coned and ordinary hot
Outburst: the largest blast earth had to show

In twenty-five million years. And its throw,
Three thousand cubic kilometers cast
Of rock and ash, fell over all below,
On {{p|Asia}}, and the continent so vast,

Shadowing it. It dimmed the sky; the rim
Of the bright sun was blocked for years, and all
The temperature dropped fast, and, cold and grim,
That had been warming felt the winter's thrall.

Into a sudden cold, the hammer-blow,
Like a fist on the living systems, fell,
The stun of the eruption, closing slow,
On south and east {{p|Asia}}. The forests' knell

Was rung: they died. Food sources failed. The whole
Of green, in mist and fog of ash, would stop;
Photosynthesis ceased; and so the toll,
Through seasons of growth, with no harvest crop,

Fell on the settled life of those who bore
The lineage {{c|1850|D}}: all collapsed. The spread,
The first-wave migrants' body and their core,
Through tens of stations, settlements that led

{{p|Iran}} to {{p|Indonesia}}, starved and died,
And lay in the cold; and beneath the ash,
Pneumonia-stricken, and the light denied,
The coastal settlements lay; and the cache

Of inland camps lay buried deeper yet.

Of years behind {{p|Toba}}, had paid the debt
Of death; and then the barriers that rose

To life were absolute. And groups that came
Within the second wave found stations there,

But emptied, bare of people. Lands that wear

The first of these, the one the story holds:
