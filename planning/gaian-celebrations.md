# Real celebrations of the Gaian holidays: a plan

Emma, 2026-10-12: "If I actually have enough money for it ... try to do real celebrations ... really following the calendar."

This is a plan for what each named holiday in `content/gaian_days.json` would look like if it were kept for real, in calendar order. It covers the 73 entries that carry a `summary`. Where two entries share a day, each has its own section, and the second says how it folds into the first.

## What a celebration is for

In Lifeism a holiday is a seasonal rite in the sense of the doctrine's "Practice" section: a time set apart for gratitude, mourning, reverence, commitment and contemplation, and for renewing the vows (protect agency, cultivate truthfulness, reduce harm, build flourishing, treat death as an enemy). A celebration is meant to make the year feel inhabited: every Gaian date falls on the same weekday every year, so a holiday kept once can be kept the same way forever.

Every celebration below reads that day's Gaiad chapter aloud (chapter N on day N). Alone, read it aloud anyway, slowly, standing or seated at a lit candle. In a gathering, one person reads, or the chapter is passed around a sentence at a time, and there is a short silence after. A printed copy is better than a phone.

Two tiers:

- **Small:** alone or with a few people, almost no money. Something to do, something to eat, the reading, and usually a light.
- **Full:** a gathering, when there is money. Cost classes are rough: **low** is under about US$100 for the whole gathering, **medium** about $100 to $500, **high** over $500 (venue hire, catering, travel, or a commissioned object).

Some ground rules that hold throughout:

- **The weekday matters.** Monday is beginning. Friday, Saturday and Sunday are the three Sabbaths: Friday for gathering and unification, Saturday for rest, Sunday for aspiration. Most holidays fall on a Sunday, so most full celebrations are Sunday daytime events that look forward.
- **Borrow with credit, never as our own.** Many holidays track a Jewish, Muslim, Christian, Shinto, Buddhist, Chinese, Hindu, Roman or Gaelic observance. Where we use a food or custom from that tradition, say aloud where it comes from. Sacraments and commanded rites (the Eucharist, the Passover seder, the shofar service, a sukkah as a commandment, a Shinto priest's purification) stay theirs: Lifeists may attend them as respectful guests where the community welcomes visitors, and do not perform them as Lifeist rites.
- **Suffering is not a virtue.** Fasts and vigils below are always optional, light, and skipped by anyone for whom they would do harm.
- **Mourning is anti-death, not death-romance.** On the extinction and atrocity days we mourn because death and cruelty are enemies, and we end by naming something that pushes back against them.
- **Gregorian counterparts drift.** The Gaian date is fixed; the Gregorian holiday it tracks moves a few days around it from year to year. Keep the Gaian date.

## Sagittarius

### Sagittarius 1: New Year's Day, Aster Day (Gregorian New Year's Day, 1 January)

Monday, day 1, chapter 1. Monday is beginning: Aster made the world on a Monday.

- **Small:** At first light, hold a ball of yarn and read chapter 1. Drink a glass of warm milk (or oat milk) for the milk Aster stirred into the void. Write one vow for the year on a slip of paper and tie it with a length of the yarn. Light one candle and let it burn through breakfast.
- **Full (low to medium):** A morning gathering. Each guest gets a short length of red or blue yarn from a shared ball; after the reading everyone ties their piece onto a single long green cord, which is kept and brought out every New Year. Serve a milk-based breakfast: rice pudding, milk tea, star-shaped cookies.
- *Buy or prepare:* three balls of yarn (red, blue, green), candles, milk, rice, a star cookie cutter; a printed chapter 1 for each reader.
- **Chapter tie:** Aster spins the universe from yarn and the red and blue threads are tied together with green, as every life is a thread in that reunion.

### Sagittarius 8: Coming of Age Day (Gregorian Seijin no Hi, second Monday of January)

Monday, day 8, chapter 8.

- **Small:** Anyone who has come of age in the past year (the Japanese custom is twenty) reads chapter 8 aloud to someone older. The older person tells them one thing they wish they had known. Share a plain meal: rice and miso soup.
- **Full (medium):** A short ceremony for the year's new adults, credited to Japan's Seijin no Shiki. Each says, in their own words, which path they are choosing for now and that they keep the right to change it (agency is sacred). Older members give a small practical gift: a good pen, a tool, a book. Formal dress, kimono or furisode if anyone owns one; nobody should buy or rent for this. Photographs outdoors after.
- *Buy or prepare:* gifts, a celebratory lunch, a photographer if one is available.
- **Chapter tie:** Bios, Viros and Dedos choose three different ways to live, and the one who refuses protection dies: choices at the threshold of adulthood matter.

### Sagittarius 14: Ides of Sagittarius

Sunday, day 14, chapter 14.

- **Small:** After dark, go outside and find whatever planets are up (a free sky app tells you which). Read chapter 14 by flashlight with a red filter. Eat something round for each world you saw.
- **Full (low to medium):** A stargazing night. Borrow or rent a telescope, or join a local astronomy club's public night. A "solar system" potluck: each guest brings a dish for one world in the chapter (Titan, Europa, Mars, the Moon) and says why they chose it. Hot drinks outdoors.
- *Buy or prepare:* telescope rental or club fee, red flashlights, folding chairs, blankets.
- **Chapter tie:** the daughters of Nolah and Nemla travel inward from Pluto world by world; we look up at the same worlds.

## Capricorn

### Capricorn 7: Kinen-sai (Gregorian Imbolc, 1 February; Groundhog Day, 2 February; Kinen-sai, 4 February)

Sunday, day 35, chapter 35.

- **Small:** Light a candle in a window at dusk, as is done for Imbolc in the Gaelic tradition (credit St Brigid's Day and the older Imbolc). Look for the first green shoots or buds outside. Eat seeds and porridge. Read chapter 35.
- **Full (low):** A late-winter walk to a pond, shore or aquarium, to see living water creatures. Back indoors, plant seeds in pots, one per guest, to take home. Serve a seed and grain meal: oat bread, butter, seeded crackers, soup. If anyone wants the Shinto prayer for the year's harvest, they can attend a shrine's Kinen-sai as a guest.
- *Buy or prepare:* seed packets, small pots and soil, aquarium tickets if going.
- **Chapter tie:** Cyath builds the first reefs and Lobopus's line splits into three houses: the first great building of the sea, prayed for as the year's first growth.

### Capricorn 10: Ash Wednesday (1st possible) (Gregorian Ash Wednesday, when it falls here)

Wednesday, day 38, chapter 38.

The five Ash Wednesdays (Capricorn 10, 17, 24, Aquarius 3 and 10) are the five possible Wednesdays for the Christian date. Assumption: each is kept small every year, and the one that matches the Christian Ash Wednesday that year is kept full.

- **Small:** Burn a scrap of last year's paper vows in a dish. When cool, mark 命 (life) on your forehead with the ash, as the site describes. Read chapter 38. A plain supper.
- **Full (low):** An evening gathering. Old vows, notes and last year's dried plant cuttings are burned in a metal bowl outdoors; the ash is mixed with a little oil and each person marks 命 on another's forehead. Say plainly that this borrows the Christian Ash Wednesday with gratitude, and that the Lifeist meaning is commitment to life, not dust-to-dust. Simple soup and bread.
- *Buy or prepare:* a metal bowl, olive oil, paper for vows, soup.
- **Chapter tie:** from Acorn and Oak to Pentus, the echinoderm line tries body plan after body plan; ash marks the start of a season of trying again.

### Capricorn 14: Lupercalia, Valentine's Day (Gregorian Valentine's Day, 14 February; Lupercalia, 15 February)

Sunday, day 42, chapter 42.

- **Small:** Give someone you love a handwritten card and say why. Read chapter 42 together. Eat something red, and something from the sea if you eat fish.
- **Full (medium):** A wolf-themed dinner for friends and couples, crediting Rome's Lupercalia (the she-wolf who nursed Romulus and Remus) and St Valentine; the old Roman running and striking is not reenacted. Toasts to love of every kind, romantic and not. Reef-colored table: corals, blues, gold.
- *Buy or prepare:* dinner, wine or juice, cards and envelopes for every guest to write one.
- **Chapter tie:** after extinction, Leviathan rules in peace and the reef cities bring the first golden age of life; love is what builds after loss.

### Capricorn 17: Ash Wednesday (2nd possible)

- **Full (low):** As on Capricorn 10, if this is the year's matching Wednesday. Add a reconciliation: anyone in a feud with another person is invited, without pressure, to send one message toward peace.

- **Chapter tie:** Asteros and Echinos go to war and Ophis and Britta marry across the feud; the ash season is for ending feuds.

### Capricorn 21: Terminalia (Gregorian Terminalia, 23 February)

Sunday, day 49, chapter 49.

- **Small:** Walk the edge of your home, garden or block. Touch a boundary marker, a fence post, a curb. Read chapter 49 at the edge. Eat bread and honey, as the Romans offered to Terminus, god of boundaries (credit).
- **Full (low):** Neighbors walk a shared boundary together and set or repaint a small marker stone, garlanded. A picnic at the boundary afterward, food from both sides. This is a day to thank neighbors, not to argue property lines.
- *Buy or prepare:* a stone or painted marker, a garland, honey cakes, picnic food.
- **Chapter tie:** Euthy crosses the boundary from sea to land to save her eggs, and plant and fungus make the first covenant on the far side.

### Capricorn 24: Ash Wednesday (3rd possible)

Wednesday, day 52, chapter 52. The JSON gives `day_of_year` 80 for this entry; Capricorn 24 is day 52, which fits the weekly sequence of Ash Wednesdays (see Data notes). If day 80 (Aquarius 24, chapter 80, Dicyno the breadbasket) was meant instead, the same plan applies, with bread baked and given away.

- **Small:** Ash 命 on the forehead, the chapter, a plain supper.
- **Full (low):** As on Capricorn 10, if this is the matching Wednesday. Because the chapter is about inventing organs, add a short thanks for the body: each person names one part of their body they are grateful for that day.
- *Buy or prepare:* as on Capricorn 10.
- **Chapter tie:** Vater, Hestracon and their descendants build liver, stomach, bone and jaw one invention at a time; Lent begins with gratitude for the body that was built.

### Capricorn 28: Lantern Festival (Gregorian Lantern Festival, 15th of the first Chinese lunar month)

Sunday, day 56, chapter 56.

- **Small:** Make a paper lantern from a jar and tissue paper and put a tea light in it. Eat tangyuan (sweet rice balls, credit the Chinese Yuanxiao custom) or any small round sweet. Read chapter 56 by the lantern's light.
- **Full (medium):** An evening lantern walk through a park or along a street, everyone carrying a lantern. Lantern riddles (灯谜), written on slips and hung for guests to solve, credited to the Chinese festival. Tangyuan cooked together. If a local Chinese community holds a Lantern Festival, go as guests and spend money at its stalls.
- *Buy or prepare:* paper lanterns or jars and tissue, LED tea lights, glutinous rice flour, black sesame or red bean filling.
- **Chapter tie:** Arboreus divides the plant world and the first scale-trees reach up; lanterns are hung on trees and lit against the end of winter.

### Aquarius 21: St. Cyno's Day (Gregorian St Patrick's Day, 17 March; Nowruz and Shunki Kōrei-sai, about 21 March)

### Aquarius 26: Good Permian Friday (Gregorian Good Friday, when it falls here)

Friday, day 82, chapter 82. Friday is the gathering Sabbath.

The five Good Fridays (Aquarius 26, Pisces 4, 12, 19, 26) each mourn one mass extinction and lead into one of the five Easters. Assumption: all five are kept every year, since each Easter has its own meaning.

- **Small:** At three in the afternoon, put out the lights and sit in silence for a few minutes. Read chapter 82. Eat a simple meatless meal. Write down one species that is endangered now.
- **Full (low):** An afternoon gathering in a darkened room with a single candle. Read chapter 82 slowly, with a pause after the figure "96 percent". Then each person names a species alive today that is at risk, and the group chooses one conservation group to give to. Credit the Christian Good Friday, which this day accompanies, and invite any Christians present to keep their own service first.
- *Buy or prepare:* one large candle, a simple bread and soup meal, a donation.
- **Chapter tie:** the Siberian Traps kill nearly all life in the sea; the deepest mourning of the year.

### Aquarius 28: Easter of Fish (I) (Gregorian Easter Sunday, when it falls here)

Sunday, day 84, chapter 84. "Easter is the Technology Holiday"; this is the Day of Fish, for the resurrection of the forgotten.

- **Small:** Eat fish (or a fish-shaped bread) for lunch. Sprout a seed in a glass. Read chapter 84. Look up one forgotten person, species or invention and say its name aloud.
- **Full (medium):** A morning gathering at water, a pond, river or the sea. Painted eggs (credit Christian and older spring custom), a fish meal, and a short sharing: each person brings the story of something forgotten that they want brought back into memory (a relative, a lost craft, an extinct animal). Technology Holiday element: a demonstration or show-and-tell of one tool that rescues lost things (photo restoration, DNA sequencing, an archive).
- *Buy or prepare:* eggs and dyes, fish or fish-shaped bread, seeds, a projector or laptop.
- **Chapter tie:** after the extinction, moss covers bare rock, dormant seeds sprout and teleost fish fill the healing sea.

## Virgo

- **Full (medium):** An evening meal opening the Days of Awe. Apples and honey and a round loaf, credited to Rosh Hashanah; Jewish friends are invited to keep their own holiday first. Answer the chapter: the Japanese converts grieved that their ancestors were lost; Lifeism says no one is lost to us for lack of a rite, and each person names an ancestor.

Things noticed in `content/gaian_days.json` while writing this, recorded here and not changed:
