# Modern Period — Proposed Per-Chapter Map (ch 253–364)

**Status:** DRAFT allocation, 2026-06-26. This is the missing layer between
`thematic-spine.md` (the three axes) and per-chapter notes / drafting. It turns
the "unstable bracket sketch" in `chapter-allocation.md` into 110 concrete
human-scale slots (253–362) plus the two cosmic-finale chapters (363–364).

**Generation is still gated on Leo (2026-08-12).** This file is planning, not
drafting. Its job is to make Leo drafting fast and non-generic.

## How to read each slot

`Ch NNN — Working title  [axis tags]`
→ *Society-claim:* the genuine thing this chapter says about how society works
  (the editorial filter: a chapter that records without claiming is not a
  Modern chapter).

Axis tags: **A1** invented tradition · **A2** suppressed syncretism ·
**A3a** bureaucratic absorption · **A3b** material improvement ·
**A3c** the unresolved fork. `CHRON` = chronicle-form. `TABLEAU` = global
moment-in-time. Numbers are **proposed and movable** — clusters and sequence
matter more than exact slot. ~ marks a soft/optional slot that can be cut or
merged if the budget tightens.

## Default calls on the open structural questions

These are MY proposed defaults so the map can exist. Override any of them.

1. **Chronicles: 3 total — Popes (253) + Ottoman sultans + Qing emperors.**
   Rationale: Popes carries antiquity→Modern (fixed); Ottoman and Qing are the
   two lineages where "the succession IS the thread" and each frames its 20th-c
   collapse. **Tsars folded into focal chapters** (the Revolution is more
   powerful as event-narrative than as a list). Mughals folded into focal
   Akbar/Aurangzeb chapters, not a chronicle.
2. **Tableaux: 2 — ch 329 (1945, positive peak) + a 1914 tableau (the dark
   mirror, "the lights go out").** Ch 362 performs a third de-facto tableau (the
   present). Kept sparse per intentionality-over-coverage.
3. **Africa / SE Asia line (user's own rule applied):** dominant-state cases
   stay in Modern — **Ethiopia (Solomonic), Songhai, Vietnam, Siam, the
   Indonesian/Malay sultanates, Joseon Korea**. Frequently-conquered
   no-coherent-state regions → **deferred to Book of Leo**, not allocated here.

## BLOCK I — Early Modern: parallel modernization (ch 253–292, ~40)

Shape: the world modernized *everywhere*; Europe accelerated but did not
uniquely modernize; the late-18th-c revolutionary wave is what diverges the
Euro-Atlantic. The gunpowder empires are treated as modernizing states, not
despotisms, and sorted by the Thesis-2 variable (did they construct a unified
identity, or keep a mismatched ruling class?).

- **Ch 253 — Chronicle of the Popes** [CHRON] *(anchor)*
  → Peter → Alexander VI / Tordesillas. An institution outlives every person in
  it; continuity is manufactured by succession, and the pope who "lit up the
  world" did it by dividing it on a map.
- **Ch 254 — The world was modernizing everywhere** [A1]
  → The opener thesis: modernity is a global parallel phenomenon, not a European
  export. Sets the frame that reframes every chapter that follows.
- **Ch 255 — Osman's house** [CHRON, A1]
  → Ottoman sultans Osman I → Mehmed VI. A Turkish house rules an Arab and
  Balkan majority for six centuries and never becomes them — the lineage that
  refuses to fuse.
- **Ch 256 — The Arab world that wasn't** [A1]
  → The Ottoman non-Arabization counterfactual named directly: had the ruling
  house Arabized, a unified Arab superstate; its refusal is why the Arab world
  is a fragmented state-system today.
- **Ch 257 — The Balkan in-between** [A2]
  → Ottoman-era religious coexistence in the Balkans — the millet world where
  Muslim and Christian villages shared saints and festivals — set up as the
  thing 19th-c nationalism will tear apart.
- **Ch 258 — Shah Abbas builds an Iran** [A1, A2]
  → The Safavids impose Twelver Shi'ism on a Sunni-majority Iran: an identity
  externally constructed that nonetheless *took*. Invented tradition succeeding
  at its political task — and hardening the Sunni-Shia line that still runs.
- **Ch 259 — Akbar's open door** [A2] *(high-water of suppressed syncretism)*
  → The Ibadat-khana debates, Din-i Ilahi, Hindu-Muslim fusion at its peak. A
  Genghis-descended emperor who is interesting precisely because he is so
  un-Mongol, so Persianate, so syncretic.
- **Ch 260 — Aurangzeb shuts it** [A2]
  → The reversal: fusion was a choice and the next generation un-chose it. The
  Mughal failure to fuse Hindu and Muslim into one people — the vacuum the
  British will step into.
- **Ch 261 — The dominant minority** [A1]
  → The early-modern pattern named as a pattern: Mughal/Ottoman/Qing all ruled
  as a ruling identity that did not match the ruled. What's modern about it.
- **Ch 262 — Nurhaci's heirs** [CHRON, A1]
  → Qing emperors Nurhaci → Puyi. Steppe outsiders who sinicize, rule a Han
  majority as a distinct Manchu caste — and dissolve as a people when the
  dynasty falls rather than fragmenting territorially.
- **Ch 263 — The Manchu gods** [A2]
  → The ruling-class religion kept deliberately distinct from Han practice — the
  Manchu-language rites, the shamanic court cult. Identity maintained by NOT
  fusing, the inverse of Meiji.
- **Ch 264 — The last steppe** [A2] *(Zunghar Genocide, 1755–58)*
  → A dynasty of former-steppe-people-turned-sedentary-rulers extinguishes the
  last steppe-nomadic polity. The end of pastoralism as a world-historical
  force — and the irony is the shape of the chapter.
- **Ch 265 — Muhammad Ali's Egypt** [A1]
  → Convergent modernization at the supposed periphery: a modern army and
  state-led industry carved out of nominal Ottoman overlordship. Modernization
  was happening even where the textbook says it wasn't.
- **Ch 266 — Sakoku** [A1]
  → Tokugawa Japan looks frozen — sealed, static, non-modernizing — and the
  appearance is the setup for the lie Meiji will expose. A coherent single-bloc
  people, unlike the dominant-minority empires.
- **Ch 267 — Joseon's Confucian state** [A1]
  → Korea as the most thoroughly Confucianized state — bureaucracy as a moral
  cosmology. Foreshadows Donghak (the peoples-based reaction) later.
- **Ch 268 — Solomon's line in the highlands** [A1]
  → Ethiopia's Solomonic dynasty: a dominant-state Africa that invents its own
  ancient continuity (descent from Solomon and Sheba) and stays sovereign.
- **Ch 269 — Songhai and the sahel** [A1]
  → West African dominant-state modernity (Songhai, the sahelian empires):
  literate, administered, trade-state Islam — the Africa the conquered-periphery
  story erases.
- **Ch 270 — The sultanates of the islands** [A2]
  → The Indonesian/Malay maritime sultanates: Islam arriving by trade and fusing
  with local courtly Hindu-Buddhist forms — syncretism as the normal state of a
  trading world.
- **Ch 271 — Vietnam and Siam hold** [A1]
  → The mainland SE-Asian dominant states that kept coherent identities and
  sovereignty where their neighbors were absorbed — the line between Modern and
  the Book of Leo drawn in practice.
- **Ch 272 — Nanak's path** [A2→A1] *(Sikhism, confirmed inclusion)*
  → Guru Nanak's syncretic founding (neither Hindu nor Muslim) that hardens,
  under pressure, into the Khalsa — a people forged from a fusion by persecution.
- **Ch 273 — The book printed** [A3a] *(seed of bureaucratic absorption)*
  → Print as the first machine that absorbs a human function (the scribe) and
  standardizes a people into readers of one fixed text. The proto-computing arc
  begins here, not with electronics.
- **Ch 274 — Wahhabism: a return that is an invention** [A1]
  → The revivalist move dissected: presenting a brand-new purification as the
  recovery of pure early Islam. The template for every "return to tradition."
- **Ch 275 — The census eye** [A3a]
  → The statistical state: populations rendered legible, counted, categorized.
  Government learns to see its people as rows. Bureaucracy as proto-computing.
- **Ch 276 — Citizens, suddenly** [A1] *(American Revolution, Euro-Atlantic
  social transformation — NOT Americas coverage)*
  → Popular sovereignty invents "the people" as the source of authority. The
  social transformation, not the territory; the citizen-soldier nation is born.
- **Ch 277 — The French remake the calendar** [A1]
  → The Revolution tries to manufacture a whole new tradition from scratch —
  new calendar, new cult, new festivals — and shows both the power and the
  limits of inventing tradition by decree.
- **Ch 278 — Napoleon exports the nation** [A1]
  → Nationalism weaponized and spread across Europe; the model of the
  unified-people state that the gunpowder empires never matched, now loose in
  the world.
- **Ch 279 — The divergence named** [A1]
  → The pivot chapter: Europe pulls ahead not by uniquely modernizing but by a
  specific social transformation the parallel-modernizers did not undergo. The
  whole Block-I thesis stated plainly before the 19th c. opens.
- ~~**Ch 280 — reserve / spillover**~~ → flex slot for whichever Block-I thread
  overruns (likely an extra Ottoman or Mughal beat).

*(Block I runs ~253–280; the early-modern material is dense and several of
these may want splitting — hence the buffer to 292 is held loosely below.)*

## BLOCK II — The long 19th century (ch 281–320, ~40)

Shape: industrialization and the bureaucratic-absorption arc ramp; colonialism
reframed as *divergence from a parallel start*, not unique European genius; and
invented tradition reaches its flowering as every rising nation manufactures an
ancient self.

- **Ch 281 — The engine and the clock** [A3a, A3b]
  → Industrialization as double-edged from the start: the factory both lifts
  material life and decomposes human labor into measurable units (Taylorism's
  seed). Both registers at once, unresolved.
- **Ch 282 — The organized hands** [A3b] *(labor, in scope as peoples-based)*
  → Labor organizing as a people, not an ideology: the human counter-pressure
  that will make the post-WWII compression possible. Distinct from Marxism-qua-
  ideology (excluded).
- **Ch 283 — Colonialism as divergence** [A1]
  → Empire reframed: not Europe uniquely modernizing but Europe cashing a
  temporary acceleration into domination over parallel-modernizers who started
  from the same place.
- **Ch 284 — Germany invents Germany** [A1]
  → Unification by manufactured ancient continuity — folk, forest, language as
  primordial nation. The purest case of inventing the tradition you claim to
  inherit.
- **Ch 285 — Italy, made; Italians, to be made** [A1]
  → "We have made Italy; now we must make Italians." The state precedes the
  people and sets out to manufacture them — invented tradition stated by its own
  architects.
- **Ch 286 — The Balkans break** [A2]
  → 19th-c nationalism shatters the Ottoman coexistence of Ch 257. People who
  were "both" are forced to be "one"; the shared-saint world becomes a map of
  enemies.
- **Ch 287 — Meiji: the catch-up** [A1] *(Meiji Restoration, very important)*
  → The late-comer that succeeds where the gunpowder empires failed. Names the
  open question rather than answering it: *why* Japan, and not the Ottomans?
  The uncertainty is the content.
- **Ch 288 — State Shinto** [A1] *(keystone invented-tradition chapter)*
  → A national religion constructed from local, diverse, syncretic shrine
  practice — the manufactured imperial cult presented as timeless antiquity. The
  unification job done right.
- **Ch 289 — Shinbutsu bunri** [A2] *(the casualty of Ch 288)*
  → The forced separation of Buddha and kami after a millennium of fusion. The
  cost of Ch 288 named directly: a real syncretism destroyed to manufacture a
  pure one. Ch 288–289 are a matched pair — the thesis and its price.
- **Ch 290 — Kang Youwei's church that wasn't** [A1]
  → The failed invention: a Confucian church modeled on Christianity that never
  took. Invented tradition can fail — and the failures map the limits of the
  move as sharply as the successes.
- **Ch 291 — Donghak** [A1/A2] *(peoples-based new religion, Korea)*
  → An indigenous syncretic faith rising as a people against both foreign
  pressure and the Confucian state — religion as the form a people's resistance
  takes.
- **Ch 292 — Tenrikyo and the founders** [A1] *(peoples-based, Japan)*
  → New religions as peoples forming in the modern churn — covered because they
  are communities, not because the doctrines are endorsed. The "peoples not
  ideologies" rule in action.
- **Ch 293 — A people without a land** [A1] *(Judaism / the Zionist turn)*
  → An ancient people meets the nation-state age and the question of whether
  peoplehood requires a state. The modern pressure that turns a diaspora toward
  territory.
- **Ch 294 — The reactionary binary** [A1]
  → Reactionary Christianity reframing the gender binary as the foundation of
  the faith — a 19th/20th-c invention presented as eternal doctrine. Invented
  tradition in the intimate register.
- **Ch 295 — Sinicization of Islam** [A2]
  → Centuries of Chinese Muslim fusion (Hui mosque-as-temple architecture,
  Confucian-Islamic scholarship) — the syncretic normal that a later state will
  violently reverse. Plants what Ch ~348 detonates.
- **Ch 296 — The mismatched modern army** [A3a]
  → Conscription and the legible citizen: the nation-state learns to mobilize
  its whole population as a countable resource. The census becomes the draft.
- **Ch 297 — Punch cards** [A3a] *(Hollerith, 1890 census)*
  → Mechanized counting: the census becomes a machine. The direct technological
  ancestor of both the modern corporation and the death camp — named here, paid
  off at Ch ~324.
- **Ch 298 — Vaccination, sanitation, the mortality crash** [A3b]
  → The genuine, dramatic good: childhood death stops being normal. The Gaiad is
  not a doom narrative — this is one of the largest improvements in human life,
  full stop.
- ~~**Ch 299–308 — 19th-c continuation / colonial-encounter beats**~~ [mixed]
  → Reserve cluster (~10 slots) for: the scramble for Africa as divergence-
  cash-out; India under the Raj after 1857; the Ottoman 19th-c reform
  (Tanzimat) as too-late unification; the opium wars as the Qing's insufficient
  acceleration made concrete; one Latin-American-Catholic-indigenous-fusion beat
  *iff* not pre-empted by Lehi. **To be specified before Leo** — each must clear
  the "says something about society" filter or be cut.
- **Ch 309–320 — run-up to the wars** [A1, A2]
  → The nationalisms of Ch 284–286 hardening toward catastrophe; the alliance
  systems; the last imperial summer. Cluster ends on the eve of 1914. (~12 slots,
  to be specified; keep sparse — intentionality over coverage.)

## BLOCK III — The World Wars (ch 321–335, ~15; centered on movable 329)

- **Ch 321 — 1914: the lights go out** [TABLEAU]
  → The dark-mirror tableau to 329. A global moment caught whole: the dynastic
  world walking into the fire it built. (Movable, like 329.)
- **Ch 322 — The dynasties fall** [A1]
  → Romanov, Habsburg, Hohenzollern, Ottoman, Qing — the houses of Block I and
  II collapse almost together. The age of the dynastic ruling-identity ends.
- **Ch 323 — The Russian remaking** [A1] *(Tsars fold here — focal, not chron)*
  → Revolution as the violent attempt to manufacture a new kind of people
  overnight. The Tsarist line ends not in a list but in an event.
- **Ch 324 — IBM and the machine that counted the dead** [A3a] *(keystone)*
  → The Hollerith arc (Ch 297) pays off: mechanized identification enabling
  industrial-scale extermination. The same punch card ran the census and the
  camp. Technology doesn't choose; political order does.
- **Ch 325 — Armenia, and the nation forged from it** [A1] *(confirmed
  inclusion)*
  → The Armenian Genocide framed as the industrial violence that made the
  Turkish nation-state possible — Atatürk's desperate, too-late unification of
  what remained of Anatolia. Pairs with the Ottoman fragmentation thread.
- **Ch 326–328 — the second war** [A3a, A1]
  → The Holocaust as the bureaucratic-absorption arc at its horizon; fascism's
  invented-tradition mythology; the Pacific war as the Meiji imperial cult
  turned outward. (~3 slots.)
- **Ch 329 — 1945: the peace tableau** [TABLEAU, A3b] *(drafted; soft anchor)*
  → The rare positive global peak — the moment equality actually began to rise.
  In dialogue with Ch 362 (the moment we're still hoping it will again).
- **Ch 330 — The trials** [A1] *(notes exist; partly unreliable — rewrite)*
  → War-crimes tribunals: the attempt to put the fascist beast on trial, and the
  incompleteness of the reckoning. International law as a people's invented
  tradition, built in real time.
- ~~**Ch 331–335 — immediate postwar settlement**~~ [A3b]
  → Reserve (~5): the institutions of the compression being built; the UN/
  Bretton-Woods order as the political balance that made broad gains possible.
  To be specified.

## BLOCK IV — The postwar arc: rise and break (ch 336–355, ~20)

Shape: the compression of inequality (the great positive peak) and then its
breaking — Axis 3b giving way to Axis 3c. Decolonization, the material-
improvement arc, the computing arc, and the suppressed-syncretism reversals all
run through here.

- **Ch 336 — The compression** [A3b] *(keystone positive chapter)*
  → 1945–1975: organized labor + industrial productivity + war-chastened
  governments producing broad, simultaneous gains across many places. One of the
  rare moments living standards rose for almost everyone at once.
- **Ch 337 — Partition** [A2] *(the Akbar thread, Ch 259–260, pays off)*
  → Hindu-Muslim syncretism's final defeat: a subcontinent cut in two, millions
  moved and killed, the fusion of Ch 259 declared impossible. The cost of
  unified identity at its starkest.
- **Ch 338 — Decolonization, where it worked and where it didn't** [A3b, A1]
  → The dominant-state nations reclaiming sovereignty; the invented-tradition
  task each new state faces (manufacture a people from colonial borders). Mixed
  ledger, honestly kept.
- **Ch 339 — The green revolution** [A3b]
  → Mass famine becomes rare. The unglamorous, enormous good — and the quieter
  cost (monoculture, dependency) that keeps it double-edged.
- **Ch 340 — Electrification and the plumbed house** [A3b]
  → The concrete texture of daily life transformed — light, water, the
  refrigerator. Modernity as the thing you stop noticing because it worked.
- **Ch 341 — The mainframe absorbs the office** [A3a]
  → Post-war computing swallows administrative function wholesale. The
  bureaucratic-absorption arc goes electronic; the clerk becomes the program.
- **Ch 342 — ARPANET** [A3a]
  → The internet born as bureaucratic/military infrastructure, not as
  liberation. What it was built to be, before the story we tell about it.
- **Ch 343 — The break** [A3c] *(the fork named)*
  → Post-1980: the labor-government balance that made the compression breaks;
  inequality diverges again. Not technology's fault — the political order
  changed. The pivot from Axis 3b to Axis 3c.
- **Ch 344 — Credit scores and the algorithmic self** [A3a]
  → Risk modeling renders the person as a number to be priced. Bureaucratic
  absorption reaches into individual fate.
- ~~**Ch 345–355 — late-century threads**~~ [mixed]
  → Reserve cluster (~11): the reactionary invented-tradition revival (1950s
  nostalgia as political myth, Ch ~294 grown to power); the syncretism reversals
  (Sinicization-of-Islam violently undone, Ch 295 detonating); one
  Latin-American or African post-colonial syncretism beat; the founding of
  Israel as the Ch 293 thread landing. **To be specified before Leo**; keep the
  filter strict.

## BLOCK V — Run-in and handoff (ch 356–362, 7)

- **Ch 356 — Surveillance capitalism** [A3a]
  → The absorption arc privatized: attention and behavior themselves rendered
  legible and sold. The census eye (Ch 275) is now in everyone's pocket,
  pointed back at them.
- **Ch 357 — The reactionary present** [A1]
  → Invented-tradition movements alive right now — manufactured pasts deployed
  against the present. Axis 1 brought to the reader's own moment, unresolved.
- **Ch 358 — The syncretism struggle, current** [A2]
  → Whatever fusion is being purged as the chapter is drafted. Axis 2 in the
  present tense — left open, not concluded.
- **Ch 359 — Climate** [A3c]
  → The unresolved fork in its largest form: the same industrial capacity that
  lifted billions now threatens them. No resolution offered — the book refuses.
- **Ch 360 — The machine that thinks** [A3a] *(AI / LLMs, elevated density)*
  → The current phase of the absorption arc: cognitive-bureaucratic labor itself
  absorbed. The clerk, then the program, now the mind. The arc of Ch 273→275→
  297→341→356 reaching the reader's exact present.
- **Ch 361 — The fork** [A3c] *(the run-in's final tension-setter)*
  → All three axes held open at once: the same tools could concentrate power or
  distribute it; we are still at the fork. Sets up everything 362 refuses to
  resolve.
- **Ch 362 — 2026 → the time-skip** [TABLEAU, A3c] *(handoff anchor; see
  `ch362-handoff.md`)*
  → The last present-tense look at us. Holds every live thread unresolved and
  performs, in the verse itself, the step out of recorded history into
  handed-off time. The register is "still in it" — not triumph, not doom.

## BLOCK VI — Cosmic finale (ch 363–364, structurally separate)

- **Ch 363 — Cryonic revival** *(conclusion of the human arc)*
  → The only mid-term prediction the Gaiad commits to, because the practice
  requires it. The implied gap before this chapter (what people did in between)
  is deliberately not narrated — the refusal is the point.
- **Ch 364 — Milkomeda** *(conclusion of the entire Gaiad; referenced in ch 1)*
  → The galactic merger / Aster–Andromeda reunion. The ouroboros closes; the
  cosmic love story that opened the book lands. Load-bearing since ch 1.
