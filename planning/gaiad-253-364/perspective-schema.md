# Perspective schema — Japan + Britain as the anchor countries

**Terminology (Emma, 2026-07-07, final formulation):** "Japan is its own thing.
The **anchor countries** are **Japan and Britain** — and by extension **Shinto
and Anglicanism**." Two corrections this locks in: (1) *anchor countries*, not
"perspective countries" — and Japan is not an "exemption" from the no-Europe
test below; anchors are simply outside the test's domain, the test applies only
to subjects. (2) The anchors carry a religious dimension by extension: the
Modern Period's two anchor religions are Shinto and Anglicanism. This is also
the resolution of the original anglican-focus directive: order.life's two
religious poles are the Shinto-derived material already in the site's identity
and the Anglican material now being added — both flow from the anchors.

**Source: Emma, 2026-07-07, verbatim schema statement.** This is the development of
the "anglican focus = scope of modernity" directive (`anglican-focus.md`) into an
organizational structure for ch 253–362. Every point below is Emma's; the gap
analysis at the bottom is session scaffolding responding to her open question.

## The schema (Emma's points, all deliberate)

1. **England focus already existed; doubling down on it.** Not a new direction —
   an intensification of one already present.
2. **England and Japan are the countries of our perspective.** The Modern Period
   is narrated from these two vantage points, not from a neutral global camera.
3. **Scoping down a bit.** The schema narrows Modern Period coverage, it does not
   add to it.
4. **Countries often enter the story as they get colonized by the Anglophone
   world.** Colonization-by-Anglosphere is the recurring structural mechanism by
   which the rest of the world comes on stage.
5. **Exceptions retained at strength:** the Protestant Reformation in Germany is
   covered pretty strongly, and the French Revolution likewise. These are
   continental-European anchors that stand outside the England/Japan lens.
6. **Arab Revolt chapter — needed.** Structurally it is **England → Arab world**
   (the Anglophone power dismembering the Ottoman Empire; pairs with the existing
   Ottoman-fragmentation / non-Arabization-counterfactual material in
   `chapter-allocation.md`).
7. **Russia: open — unsure how much to cover.** (See gap analysis.)
8. **Spanish perspective: needed, a bit.** Not a full third vantage — "a bit."
9. **Open question (Emma's):** are there places clearly missed by this structure?
   Acknowledged as "a bit of a weird organizational structure."
10. **It builds into things like global capitalism** — the British Empire very
    much developed global capitalism. The schema is not just geographic: the
    England-perspective carries the story of capitalism as modernity's operating
    system (East India Company, joint-stock form, City of London, gold standard,
    free-trade imperialism).

## Refinement: gunpowder empires and the no-Europe test (Emma, 2026-07-07, later)

- **Gunpowder empires stay in, but they are basically the LAST polities to get
  their own chapters un-framed by colonialism** ("no colonial" chapters).
- **Why parallel protagonists fails as organization:** "them as parallel
  protagonists is just too difficult. They exist, Peter the Great exists. But
  organization is based on reactions to European colonialism."
- **The criterion (Emma's formulation):** only the *origins* of the gunpowder
  empires are divergent enough and big enough that you can tell their story
  never mentioning Europe — "or maybe more accurately, never mentioning Europe
  isn't contrived."

**Session restatement — the no-Europe test:** a non-European polity gets a
standalone (non-reaction-framed) chapter iff telling its story without
mentioning Europe is not contrived. Ottoman rise, Safavid founding/Shi'ism
imposition, Mughal founding/Akbar, Qing conquest (and the Zunghar genocide as
Qing-internal steppe history) pass at their origins; after roughly the origins
era, a Europe-free telling of any of them is contrived, so all later
non-European material is narrated in the reaction-to-European-colonialism
frame. Peter the Great / Russia explicitly named as existing but NOT getting
parallel-protagonist treatment. Japan is not subject to the test at all — it is
an anchor country ("its own thing"), and the test applies only to subjects.

**Implications:**
- The standalone gunpowder chapters sit EARLY in the Modern bracket
  (origin era, ~1500s–1600s), likely early Virgo right after ch 253.
- The open chronicle-slot question in `chapter-allocation.md` (Ottoman sultans /
  Qing emperors / Mughal emperors / Tsars chronicles) is now effectively
  answered in the negative: full-line chronicles of those dynasties would be
  parallel-protagonist treatment, which this refinement rejects. Their
  chronicle-ness is spent at origins only.
- The gunpowder thesis-1 ("modernized in parallel but insufficiently") is
  retained as content but told from the reaction frame, not from inside.

## Why the schema coheres (session note)

England and Japan are structurally twin choices: both island empires off a
continent; both the archetypal invented-tradition cases already in the spine
(Anglican national church / Meiji State Shinto); one is the metropole that
colonized the world, the other the catch-up case that then ran its own
colonization. The schema generalizes cleanly: **countries enter as they are
colonized/contacted by one of the two perspective empires** — India, Africa,
the Arab world, Opium-War China via England; Korea, Taiwan, Manchuria, and
(in WWII) Southeast Asia via Japan. Ch 329 (1945 tableau) is the hinge where
the two perspective empires collide and one absorbs the other's sphere.

## Gap analysis — what the lens reaches and what it misses (session scaffolding)

**Reached naturally by the lens:**
- China — Opium Wars / Hong Kong (England →), Sino-Japanese wars (Japan →);
  Qing already confirmed via gunpowder empires.
- Korea — colonized by Japan; Donghak already on the peoples list.
- India — the central England → case; Sikhism already confirmed.
- Arab world / Ottoman dismemberment — Arab Revolt chapter (point 6).
- Sub-Saharan Africa, Anglophone parts — Egypt, Boer War, Nigeria, Kenya.
- Persia/Iran — Great Game, Anglo-Persian Oil; Safavids already confirmed.
- Southeast Asia — largely via Japan's WWII occupation (Indonesia, Indochina,
  Malaya, Burma); Philippines is a three-lens node (Spain → US → Japan).
- USA — as the Anglophone world's own successor-metropole (post-1890s; earlier
  Americas stay in Lehi).

**Russia (Emma's open question) — recommendation:** cover Russia at lens
*intersections* rather than standalone: Crimean War and the Great Game
(vs England), the Russo-Japanese War 1905 (vs Japan — and load-bearing for the
Japan perspective: the first modern defeat of a European great power), and the
Cold War as the Anglosphere's rival. That likely makes a Tsars chronicle
unnecessary and keeps the scoping-down intact. The Russian Revolution itself is
reachable as "the rival operating system to British-built global capitalism"
(point 10) if wanted — otherwise it stays peripheral by design.

**Genuinely missed by the lens (candidates to consciously accept or patch):**
- **Post-1890s Latin America** — Lehi owns pre-1890s; afterwards it is neither
  Anglophone-colonized nor perspective-country territory. Partial patch: US
  informal empire (dollar diplomacy, United Fruit) is Anglophone-colonization
  in the loose sense, and serves the global-capitalism thread.
- **Non-Anglophone Africa** — Congo (Belgium), Francophone West Africa,
  Ethiopia-vs-Italy. Existing planning already leans Ethiopia → Modern
  ("dominant state" rule); the rest may fall to Leo under the
  indigenous-persistence expansion or be consciously dropped.
- **Continental interior Europe** — Italy, Austria-Hungary, the Balkans,
  Poland, Scandinavia: only reachable via the world wars. Under
  intentionality-over-coverage this may be acceptable loss.
- **French empire beyond the Revolution** — Algeria, Indochina-before-Japan.
  Indochina gets picked up by the Japan lens in WWII; Algeria likely dropped.
- **Ottoman core / Turkey** — NOT missed: Armenian Genocide + Atatürk already
  confirmed; the Arab Revolt chapter completes the picture from the England side.

**Spain (point 8) — where "a bit" naturally sits:** ch 253 already ends on
Tordesillas (Spain/Portugal dividing the world); the Armada 1588 is the moment
the England perspective *becomes* the perspective (hegemony contested); the
Spanish-American War 1898 is the Anglophone world absorbing Spain's remnants,
handing the Philippines toward the Japan lens. Three touchpoints, no standalone
Spanish arc — matches "a bit."
