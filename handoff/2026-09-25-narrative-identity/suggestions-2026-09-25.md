## 1. Practice — what keeps spirituality from slipping again

Emma, today: spirituality is a regulator for her, and she lost it for about 90 days in survival mode.
Asked what would keep it, her answer: **"daily practice and Stanley Park walks more."**

- **A daily practice tied to the Gaian calendar** — the calendar already pairs every day with a Gaiad
  chapter; the practice could be as small as reading that day's chapter.
- **The Stanley Park walk as a regular rite.** Today's walk was where it came back: the tide pools,
  Siwash Rock, the seawall, then the cedar forest. Her words: sea-facing Shinto *"shifted towards a
  forest"* and *"Stanley Park really exemplifies all that ... even though it doesn't really have shrines
  proper."* The walk goes sea to forest, which is that shift, walked in order.
- **Siwash Rock** (Skalsh the Unselfish, turned to stone by Q'uas the Transformer) was where she felt
  connected to her ancestors, Empress Jingū and the sea.

## 2. Material for the Gaiad's modern section (253–364)

She said the modern part is the one she didn't know what to do with. Candidates from today:

- **Invented tradition, made explicit.** Hers: *"I'm inventing tradition, and pretty soon it's gonna be
  the way things always were."* Precedents: the Kojiki from Tenmu's court; Meiji's separation of kami and
  buddhas (1868); Kuroda Toshio on "Shinto" as a later construct; Hobsbawm. Already one of the three
  themes in `planning/gaiad-253-364/thematic-spine.md`.
- **The Qing's two layers.** Public Confucian state cult (Temple of Heaven, Altar of the Sun) over a
  private Manchu rite: the Tangse (堂子) and the shamanic sacrifices in Kunning Palace (坤寧宮). The
  Qianlong emperor codified the Manchu rites in 1747. In 1673 Han officials were barred from the Tangse,
  and afterwards *"Han officials didn't know what went on, and Manchu officials couldn't explain it
  either"* (Yeh Kao-shu, NCCU 2025). Emma: *"I was always drawn to the Qing."*
- **The Aga Khan.** A family that deliberately turned religious office and ancestry into international
  recognition over several generations. Emma: *"very, very intentional on the part of the family."*
- **Meiji ending the Festival of the Deva and Naraka (六道冥官祭).** Performed at every accession from
  Go-Yōzei to Kōmei and at shogun investitures; dropped when the Tsuchimikado lost their office. A
  modernising state standardising religion away. (Source: shinto.miraheze.org.)
- **Jinja Honchō (1946)** taking over the organising role of the Jingi-in as a private body — a
  non-state body imposing one ontology. Emma: *"The ontology is our imposition."*
- **Placement fix, hers:** the Daijōsai chapter is great but belongs *"closer to Yom Kippur than to
  Ninamesai"*; the Niiname-sai slot becomes the real Daijōsai of Emperor Heisei (November 1990).

## 3. Scripture and doctrine notes from today

- **Religions' primary materials, hers:** Shinto's is paper because it is bureaucratic; **Lifeism's is
  the spreadsheet, the database — "a tracked order of things."**
- **The Book of Lehi found in Stanley Park,** her idea, as a frame for the book's satirical structure.
- **The Norse sagas are comparable to the Kojiki** in mythopoetic quality and in giving genealogies;
  the Gaiad can do for Europe what the Kojiki did for Japan. *"The big issue is just the lack of a
  European department of divinities to organize things."*
- **The Gaiad as 記紀 (kiki), with its own Engishiki.** Her databases cover genealogy and deities (*"all
  deities are seen as having genealogy"*) and religious buildings — *"Wikidata does not need to be the be
  all end all."*

## 4. Organisation: the Department of Divinities and 武李宮内庁

- **Department of Divinities** as the name of the religious organisation — the standard translation of
  神祇官 (Jingikan), set up in the 701 Taihō Code beside the 宮内省. *Her DoD/DoW joke:* also rendered
  "Department of Worship."
- **武李宮内庁, Bureus Household Agency** — her chosen name. A family office, not a lineage
  association, which *"may control a lineage association."* 宮内 means "within the palace", with no
  claim to be imperial.
- **Order of Life IDs** for persons-and-deities (one category) and religious buildings. Permanent,
  human-readable, on the FamilySearch model (`XXXX-XXX`), click-to-copy everywhere. Our suggestion,
  not decided: Crockford base-32, with the category in the URL path (`/p/…`, `/b/…`) rather than in the
  ID, so an ID never encodes something that could change.
- **shrines.order.life** for the Shinto wiki scripts, with temples.order.life redirecting there — hers.
